All three public sources explicitly present commentary for Psalm 8:1–2; the first two retain the discussion of God's displayed glory and his use of weak instruments. Unlike a title-only variance, these witnesses have **actual prose word variants** in the expository discussion.

## Exact small public-rendering examples

| Within Psalm 8:1–2 exposition | CCEL rendered wording | 1828-lineage reprint transcription |
| --- | --- | --- |
| God’s glory in the lower world | `How brightly God's glory shines` | `How bright God's glory shines` |
| God’s glory in the upper world | `How much more brightly it shines` | `How much brighter it shines` |
| Opening of point II | `How powerfully he proclaims it` | `How powerfully he discovers it` |

These are **public-display word comparisons**, not new silent normalizations or an exact alignment of the original CCEL XML and original CrossWire zCom raw. They show that this Psalm has independently observed *edition/transcription-level lexical variation*, including a different lexical verb, rather than just punctuation changes. They do **not** establish which variant is the historically original wording, whether a particular variant reflects compositorial decisions or transcription errors, or whether CrossWire 2.2 chose either of these exact renderings. The observed stock/source difference in the private comparator may be a distinct variation altogether.

The larger Psalm 7 source-only expository paragraph and Acts 17 paragraph remain separate; no classification from this Psalm 8 note transfers to them.
