# Luke — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

**Luke** is generally believed to have been a physician and a companion of the apostle Paul.

Henry considers Luke's style and familiarity with Jewish rites and customs evidence that **he was Jewish**. At the same time, he considers Luke's Greek name and knowledge of the Greek language evidence of **a Gentile background**.

These apparently conflicting observations are retained as the commentator's own statements, rather than harmonized by an undocumented editorial decision.

Luke first appears in **Acts 16:10–11**, where he joined Paul at Troas. Henry says that he accompanied Paul to Jerusalem and was also with him during his travels and imprisonment in Rome.

The Gospel appears intended to replace the numerous deficient and unreliable accounts then circulating.

Its purpose is to provide an authentic and inspired account of **Jesus' life, miracles and teaching**, based on the testimony of people who heard and saw those events themselves.
