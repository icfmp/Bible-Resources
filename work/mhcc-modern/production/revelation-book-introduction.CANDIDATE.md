# Revelation — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

John's Revelation consists of **two principal parts**.

**First**, it concerns “the things that are”: the church's condition at the time.

It includes the apostle's vision of **Jesus Christ**, his command to write what he sees (**Revelation 1:9–20**), and the messages to the **seven churches of Asia**.

Those messages undoubtedly addressed the actual condition of each church then.

But their teachings, warnings, encouragements, praise, rebuke and promises are useful to **the whole church in every age**.

**Second**, Revelation describes “the things that will take place afterward”: prophecy concerning the church's future from the time John saw these visions.

This book is intended to promote **spiritual growth**.

It warns careless sinners, directs awakened people toward salvation, strengthens weak believers, comforts tempted and suffering Christians, and especially encourages **martyrs faithful to Christ** while enduring cruel persecution from Satan and his followers.
