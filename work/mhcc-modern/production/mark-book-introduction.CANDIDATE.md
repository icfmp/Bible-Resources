# Mark — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

Mark was the nephew of Barnabas (**Colossians 4:10**) and the son of **Mary of Jerusalem**, whose house was a gathering place for the apostles and early Christians (**Acts 12:12**).

Because Peter called Mark his son (**1 Peter 5:13**), it is thought that Mark was converted through Peter's ministry.

Mark was thus closely associated with the disciples of Jesus, even if he had not personally been one of them.

He wrote in **Rome**.

Some suppose Peter **dictated the Gospel to him**. Henry considers it more generally attested, however, that **Peter preached in Rome** and that Mark, his companion, understood his preaching accurately and was asked to write down its details.

Peter's own humility is evident in the passages that mention him.

Mark's careful account includes many events Peter personally witnessed, and the details suggest the testimony of an eyewitness.

The Gospel emphasizes **Christ's miracles more than his teaching discourses**.

Though it repeats many events found in Matthew, reading them again is valuable: **each evangelist records the events from the perspective that most affected him**.
