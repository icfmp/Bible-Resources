# Acts — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

The book of Acts connects **the Gospels with the Epistles**.

It records important events involving the apostles **Peter and Paul** and the Christian church from the Savior's ascension until Paul's arrival in Rome, a period of **about thirty years**.

**Luke** wrote the book. He witnessed many of the events he describes and accompanied Paul to Rome.

But Acts is **not a complete history** of the Christian church during those years, or even a complete biography of Paul.

Henry identifies **two major purposes** in its narrative.

**First**, it records how the Holy Spirit's gifts were communicated at Pentecost and how the apostles' miracles confirmed the truth of Christianity. The gifts showed that Jesus' promises had really been fulfilled.

**Second**, it demonstrates **the right of Gentiles to be received into the church of Christ**.

Much of Acts consists of public addresses and sermons delivered by different people.

Their words and manner fit the speakers and the circumstances in which they were spoken.

Henry judges that most of the speeches preserve **the substance** rather than necessarily every word originally spoken.

All, however, direct attention to **Jesus as the Christ, the anointed Messiah**.
