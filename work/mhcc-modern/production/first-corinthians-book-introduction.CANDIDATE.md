# 1 Corinthians — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

The church at Corinth included **some Jews but more Gentiles**.

Paul had to confront Jewish religious superstition on one hand and Gentile believers' sinful conduct on the other.

The church's peace was disturbed by **false teachers** who undermined the apostle's influence.

This produced two opposing groups.

One insisted strongly upon **Jewish ceremonies**.

The other tolerated excesses opposed to the gospel, encouraged by the luxury and sins widespread in Corinth.

Paul wrote this letter for **two purposes**.

**First**, he intended to correct the disorderly conduct and abuses that had been reported to him.

**Second**, he wanted to give satisfactory answers to questions upon which Corinthian believers had requested his judgment.

It is remarkable how Paul combined **Christian gentleness with firmness**, moving from general truths directly toward correction of the church's errors and sins.

He explained God's truth and will about different matters with powerful arguments and an energetic style.
