# Romans — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

The apostle Paul's purpose in writing to the Romans appears to have been to answer the objections of unbelieving Jews while teaching those who believed, to strengthen Christians and bring idolatrous Gentiles to faith.

He also wanted to show that a **Gentile convert had the same standing in God's favor as a Jewish believer**.

These purposes are brought together as Paul answers the unbelieving Jew and defends the believing Gentile.

The letter explains plainly how a sinner may be accepted by God and **justified in his sight**: solely through grace, by faith in **the righteousness of Christ**, **without distinction of nation or people**.

It also answers the objections of Jewish Christians who tried to combine the law and the gospel as joint conditions for acceptance before God and exclude Gentiles from the Messiah's saving blessings.

The letter concludes by urging **holiness through practical instruction**.
