## God creates heaven and earth (verses 1–2)

The Bible's opening verse gives us a satisfying and useful account of how heaven and earth began.

Humble Christians understand this by faith better than the most learned people's speculations can explain it. Looking at heaven and earth, we see the power of their great Creator.

Our own nature and place in creation should remind us of our duty. As Christians, we must always keep heaven before our eyes and the earth beneath our feet.

The Son of God, who is one with the Father, was with him when he made the world. More than that, Scripture repeatedly tells us that the world was made *by* the Son, and that nothing was made without him.

What high thoughts we should have of the great God we worship, and of the great Mediator in whose name we pray!

Here, at the very beginning of Scripture, we also meet the Holy Spirit, whose work in human hearts is described so often throughout the Bible.

At first the world had nothing pleasing to see. It was without form and empty, full of confusion and darkness.

God's work of grace in a person's soul is also a new creation.

A soul without grace, not yet born again, is disordered and confused, filled with every kind of evil. It is empty of all that is good because it is without God. It is dark—indeed, darkness itself.

That is our natural condition until almighty grace changes us.

## The creation of light (verses 3–5)

God said, “Let there be light.” He willed it, and light appeared at once.

What power there is in God's word!

In the new creation of the soul, light comes first too. The blessed Spirit enlightens our understanding, and through that enlightenment works upon our will and affections.

People who were darkness because of sin become light in the Lord through grace.

Without the coming of the Son of God to give us understanding, fallen humanity would have remained in darkness forever (1 John 5:20).

God approved the light he had called into being. He also separated it from darkness.

What fellowship can light have with darkness?

In heaven there is perfect light, without any darkness. In hell there is complete darkness, without a glimmer of light.

Day and night both belong to the Lord. Let us use them both for his honor: working for him each day, resting in him each night, and meditating on his law both day and night.

## God separates the earth from the waters and makes it fruitful (verses 6–13)

The earth was empty. But God spoke, and it was filled with his riches. They still belong to him.

He allows us to use them, but they come from God and should be used in his service and for his honor.

At his command, the earth produces grass, plants and fruit.

We should give God the glory for every benefit we receive from what the earth produces.

If, through grace, we have a share in him who is the Fountain, we can still rejoice in him when the streams of earthly mercy run dry.

## God makes the sun, moon and stars (verses 14–19)

On the fourth day, God made the sun, moon and stars. All are his works.

Scripture speaks of the stars as they appear to our eyes. It does not tell us their number, nature, location, size or movements.

The Bible was not written to satisfy our curiosity or turn us into astronomers. It was written to lead us to God and make us holy.

The lights of heaven serve their Creator faithfully. They shine in their appointed seasons without fail.

We too have been set as lights in the world to serve God. But do we fulfill the purpose for which we were created as faithfully as they do?

We do not.

Our light does not shine before God as his lights shine before us. We burn our Master's candles but neglect our Master's work.

## God creates living creatures (verses 20–25)

God commanded fish and birds to come into being, and he himself carried out that command.

Insects are more numerous than birds and beasts, and just as remarkable. They seem to have been included in this day's work.

The Creator's wisdom and power are no less wonderful in an ant than in an elephant.

God's providence preserves everything he has made, and every creature's fruitfulness comes from his blessing.

## God makes humanity in his image (verses 26–28)

Human beings were made last. This was both an honor and a kindness to them.

Yet they were created on the same day as the animals. Their bodies were formed from the same earth, and while they live in those bodies, they share that earth with the animals.

God forbid that by indulging our bodily desires we should become like the beasts that perish!

Human beings were made different from everything that came before. In them, flesh and spirit—earth and heaven—were brought together.

God said, “Let us make man.”

When humanity was created, it was meant to glorify the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

We are baptized into that great name, and we owe our very existence to the One whose name it is.

It is especially the human soul that bears God's image.

Humanity was created upright (Ecclesiastes 7:29). The mind understood divine things clearly and truly, without error or misunderstanding.

The will agreed immediately and entirely with God's will.

Every affection was rightly ordered. There were no sinful appetites or uncontrolled passions. Thoughts could readily settle upon what was best.

How holy and happy our first parents were when they bore God's image!

And how terribly that image has been defaced in humanity.

May the Lord renew it in our souls by his grace!

## God appoints food (verses 29–30)

God gave people plants and fruit for food, including grain and all the produce of the earth.

Let God's people entrust their needs to him. They need not be consumed by worry over what they will eat or drink.

The God who feeds his birds will not let his little children starve.

## God approves the completed creation (verse 31)

When we examine our own work, we discover with shame that much of it has been very bad.

But when God looked upon everything he had made, it was all very good.

It was good because everything was exactly as its Creator intended.

All God's works, throughout his dominion, bless him. So let my soul bless the Lord!

Let us thank God for the gospel of Christ. And as we think about his almighty power, let us, as sinners, flee from the wrath to come.

If we are created anew in God's image, in holiness, we will one day enter the “new heavens and new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.”
