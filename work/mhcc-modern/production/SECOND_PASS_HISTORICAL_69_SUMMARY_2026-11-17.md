### What the second pass discovered despite perfect first-pass structural coverage

- **Real textual omissions existed:** Hebrews 11:39–40 had lost Henry's substantial final application and 1 Peter 3 had lost the resurrection assurance at the end of its original exposition. Both are now restored in modern derivative manuscripts.
- **Doctrinal claims had been weakened:** Henry's frequent explicit eternal-judgment wording was repeatedly modernized into vague references to “judgment”, “danger” or “misery”. These differences have been restored where detected, with the precise context documented in the chapter's Git change.
- **Editorial voice had leaked into Henry's translated prose:** in James 5, modern critical explanation of the author's six-thousand-year reckoning was removed from the translated body into notes; in 1 John 5, a modern textual-critical label was likewise moved into an editorial note. Neither changes biblical text, authorial source authority or critical apparatus.
- **Qualified historical interpretations are still treated as interpretations:** e.g. Jude's conjecture about Moses' burial, Henry's readings of Antichrist, papal Rome, Turkish power, the Reformation and the Millennium. They are Henry's positions, not newly settled claims of Scripture.
- **Bible references and source editorial structures remain a separate release gate.** Matching a source K section does not prove that every source `<reference>`, footnote, book-introduction slot, quotation, paragraph, heading or clause has been faithfully handled.
