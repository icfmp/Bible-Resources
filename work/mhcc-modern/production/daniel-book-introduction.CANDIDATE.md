# Daniel — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

Daniel came from **a noble family**, perhaps even **Judah's royal family**.

According to the historical chronology given in Henry's introduction, Daniel was taken captive to Babylon **in the fourth year of Jehoiachin, in 606 BC**, while still young.

He was educated in the learning of **the Chaldeans** and held high office under both **the Babylonian and Persian empires**.

He suffered persecution for his faith but was miraculously delivered.

Henry estimates that Daniel was **about ninety-four years old** at the time of his last visions.

The book is partly **historical**, describing events affecting Daniel and the Jewish people in Babylon.

It is, however, chiefly **prophetic**.

Its visions and prophecies concern the four great world empires, **the coming and death of the Messiah**, the restoration of the Jews and the conversion of the Gentiles.

Some prophetic passages are very difficult to explain.

Nevertheless, throughout the book we find encouragement to **faith and hope**, examples to follow and teaching that directs our thoughts to **Jesus Christ on the cross and upon his glorious throne**.
