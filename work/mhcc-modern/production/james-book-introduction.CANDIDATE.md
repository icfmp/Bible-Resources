# James — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

James's letter is among the New Testament's most instructive writings.

It chiefly addresses **errors that had arisen among Jewish Christians**. Rather than providing the extensive doctrinal explanations found in some other apostolic letters, it sets out an excellent summary of the practical duties of believers.

The leading truths of Christianity appear throughout.

Read carefully, the letter **fully agrees with Paul's teaching concerning grace and justification**. It strongly calls for hopeful patience, the obedience of faith and the practical exercise of love. Its warnings, rebukes and encouragements suit the different people addressed.

Its truths must be maintained, and its rules of conduct remain applicable in every age.

**Christ has no dead, fruitless branches. Faith is not an idle grace:** wherever true faith exists, it produces the fruit of good works.
