# Song of Solomon — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

Henry reads this book as **a divine allegory of the love between Christ and his church of true believers**.

Its images arise from the relationship and affection between a bridegroom and his promised bride.

Scripture often uses marriage as a picture of an especially close, firm and secure relationship.

Henry points readers to **Psalm 45; Isaiah 54:5–6; 62:5; Jeremiah 2:2; 3:1; Ezekiel; Hosea; Matthew 9:15; 25:1; Revelation 21:2, 9; and Ephesians 5:27**.

He believes every kind of believer and every circumstance that believers experience can be recognized somewhere in this song when the book is carefully compared with other Scriptures, with the Holy Spirit's help in response to prayer.

However, he warns that expositors and translators have often **misunderstood its figurative language**.

We must particularly consider the important differences between the customs of **Europe and the ancient Near East**.

The limited understanding of eastern customs possessed by many earlier translators and interpreters prevented them, in some cases, from rendering the expressions correctly.

Changes in European languages during the previous two or three centuries also affected how particular phrases were understood.

**Expressions from other ages must not be judged uncritically according to modern assumptions**.

Nevertheless, Henry concludes that the book's main themes, rightly understood, are consistent with the sincere Christian's affections and spiritual experience.
