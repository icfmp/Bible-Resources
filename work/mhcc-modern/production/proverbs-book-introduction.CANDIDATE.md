# Proverbs — Separate book introduction (Modern English candidate)

Henry explains the purpose of Proverbs by expanding the opening verses of the book.

**First**, these are the proverbs of **Solomon, David's son and king of Israel**.

**Second**, they concern **knowledge of wisdom**, devotion toward God, moral instruction and discipline, and understanding counsel that is wise and prudent.

**Third**, they teach how to acquire **practical wisdom**. True wisdom becomes visible in the way a person lives, especially in **righteous conduct toward other human beings**.

**Fourth**, they give simple people discernment through sound principles and a correct understanding of good and evil. They give the young knowledge so they need not go wrong through **ignorance**, and discretion through considering God's commands so they need not go wrong through **stubbornness**.

Henry contrasts this teaching with proverbs from other nations, many of which he says are grounded in **selfishness, cunning, pride, injustice, national contempt and hostility**.

Solomon's proverbs instead rest upon **godliness, charity, justice, benevolence and genuine prudence**.

Their complete moral purity, Henry concludes, shows that **they are the word of God**.
