# Psalms — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

**David wrote most of the Psalms**, though some were plainly written by others and the authorship of some is uncertain.

All were written through the inspiration of the **Holy Spirit**.

No part of the Old Testament is cited or referred to more often in the New Testament.

Every Psalm either points directly to **Christ—his person, character and offices**—or can lead believers to think about him.

The Psalms give words to the hearts of believers in every condition: mourning over sin, longing for God or rejoicing in him; bearing affliction, struggling with temptation or celebrating the hope or experience of deliverance; contemplating God's perfections, thanking him for his mercy, meditating on his truths or delighting in his service.

They provide a **divinely appointed measure of spiritual experience** by which believers can examine themselves.

As true religion grows stronger within the heart, the Psalms become increasingly valuable.

Through the psalmists' words, the Spirit helps us **pray**.

When we become familiar with them, we find help approaching the throne of grace to confess sin, ask for mercy or give thanks.

Whatever holy affection fills the heart—desire, hope, sorrow or joy—we can find worthy language for it in this book.

Through the generations, the church has offered its prayers and praises to God using these inspired words.
