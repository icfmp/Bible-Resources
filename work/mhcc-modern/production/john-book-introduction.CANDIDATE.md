# John — Book introduction (Modern English production candidate)

The apostle and evangelist **John** seems to have been the youngest of the twelve disciples. Jesus gave him special affection and confidence; he is therefore called **the disciple Jesus loved**.

John was deeply attached to his Master and, according to Henry, ministered successfully in Jerusalem and outlived the city's destruction, in agreement with Jesus' words in **John 21:22**.

Traditional history, as Henry recounts it, says that John later lived mainly in **Ephesus**, after the death of Jesus' mother. Toward the end of **Domitian's reign**, he was exiled to **Patmos**, where he wrote the book of Revelation. Under **Nerva**, he was released and returned to Ephesus.

Henry says it was *thought* that John wrote his Gospel and letters there, **around AD 97**, and died not long afterward. These are the commentator's historical claims and dating suggestions, not independently established facts.

The Gospel's purpose is to teach Christians the **true divine nature, office and character of Jesus Christ**, who came to teach and redeem humanity.

John therefore selected events displaying Christ's **divine power and authority** and teachings that most clearly revealed his divine nature and **the atoning power of his death for the world's sins**.

John only briefly described, or omitted, many events already reported in the other Gospels. In doing so, Henry says, he confirmed the truth of those accounts and made room for additional teaching and events of great importance.
