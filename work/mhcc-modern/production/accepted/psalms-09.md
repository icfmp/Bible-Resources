# Psalm 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David praises God for protecting his people. | 1–10 |
| And for cause to praise him. | 11–20 |

## David thanks God for protecting his people (verses 1–10)

For our praise to be acceptable to God, we must offer it sincerely **with our whole hearts**.

When thanking him for a particular mercy, remember the earlier mercies already received.

Our greatest joy must be **in the Giver, not merely the gift**.

The victories of the Redeemer should be celebrated as the victories of all the redeemed.

The strongest of God's enemies are powerless before his almighty strength.

We can be confident that the Lord's judgments are true and free of all injustice.

His people may flee to him through faith as their **Refuge**.

They can depend upon his power and promises for safety, so that no lasting harm comes upon them.

Those who recognize God as faithful and truthful will rejoice in his promises and rest upon them.

Those who know him as **their everlasting Father** will entrust their souls to him as their greatest concern.

They will trust him continually, persevering in faithful service throughout life.

Who would refuse to seek the One who has **never abandoned those who seek him**?

## Reasons to call others to praise (verses 11–20)

People who truly believe God deserves praise do not want merely to praise him more faithfully themselves.

They also want **others to join them**.

The day is coming when God will show that he has not forgotten the cries of humble people—their prayers or the testimony of their blood.

No suffering can bring us so low, or so close to death, that God cannot raise us up.

If he has already rescued us from **spiritual and eternal death**, we can confidently look to him for help in every distress.

God's Providence often brings wicked people to ruin through **the very plans they made to destroy others**.

The drunkard destroys his health.

The spendthrift impoverishes himself.

The quarrelsome person brings trouble upon his own life.

These examples show how sin contains the seeds of its punishment.

The ultimate destruction of those who continue in sin comes through their own choices.

Sin originally entered human life through the wicked one.

Those who stubbornly remain under his dominion face his dreadful end.

The true condition of nations and individuals can be measured by a simple question:

**Do their actions show that they remember God, or that they have forgotten him?**

David urged God's people to wait patiently for deliverance, even when it appears long delayed.

God will prove that he has **never forgotten them**.

How strange that human beings—creatures made of dust and destined for dust—often need severe suffering before they recognize who they are!
