# Colossians 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Colossians exhorted to be heavenly-minded; | 1–4 |
| to mortify all corrupt affections; | 5–11 |
| to live in mutual love, forbearance, and forgiveness; | 12–17 |
| and to practise the duties of wives and husbands, children, parents, and servants. | 18–25 |

## Seek what is above, where Christ reigns (verses 1–4)

Because Christians are free from the ceremonial requirements of the Mosaic law, they should pursue **gospel obedience all the more carefully**.

Heaven and earth pull our affections in different directions.

We cannot make both **the chief purpose** of our lives.

Love for the one weakens attachment to the other.

Regenerated people are **dead to sin** because its dominion has been broken through grace.

Its remaining power is gradually reduced as God's Spirit works in the soul.

At last, when believers are perfected in heavenly glory, **sin's power will disappear completely**.

To be dead in this sense means that the Holy Spirit enables us to **put sinful desires to death**, value earthly things less and long for heavenly blessings more.

We have not yet seen Jesus Christ with our own eyes.

But our comfort is that our life is **secure with him**.

The Holy Spirit brings the living water of Christ's grace into believers' souls through faith.

Christ lives within them by his Spirit, and they live for him in everything they do.

When Christ returns, all the redeemed will be gathered together.

Those whose lives are now hidden with him will **appear with him in glory**.

Since we look forward to such happiness, why should we set our deepest affections upon a world that passes away?

## Put to death sinful desires and worldly greed (verses 5–11)

We must oppose the sinful habits and impulses that draw us toward the world's values.

Treat them like **weeds or destructive pests** that spread and ruin everything around them.

They must be **put to death, restrained and continually resisted**.

Make no provision for satisfying sinful desires.

Avoid situations that tempt us, including **sensual indulgence, attachment to worldly pleasures and greed**.

**Greed is idolatry** because it gives earthly things the love and devotion that belong to God.

Sin must be put to death because **if we do not destroy it, it will destroy us**.

The gospel transforms not only bodily desires but also **the higher faculties of the mind and soul**.

It strengthens the proper rule of reason and conscience over appetite and passion.

Country of origin, social position and the circumstances of life do not establish different standards of holiness.

Everyone is called to be holy.

For the Christian, **Christ is all**—the only Lord and Savior, the foundation of every hope and the source of all true happiness.

## Clothe yourselves with compassion, forgiveness and gratitude (verses 12–17)

Christian duty includes more than avoiding harm.

We must **do all the good we can for other people**.

Those chosen and loved by God ought to be **humble and compassionate toward everyone**.

While sin remains in our hearts, disagreement and conflict may still occur.

But believers must **forgive one another**, imitating the forgiveness through which we ourselves have been saved.

Let **God's peace rule in our hearts**.

It is his own work among his people.

Thanksgiving to God helps make our character agreeable and useful to others.

The gospel is **the word of Christ**.

Many people possess that word but allow it to live within them only **weakly**. It has little authority over their lives.

Our souls flourish when we are filled with **Scripture and the grace of Christ**.

When we sing psalms, our hearts should **feel the truths we sing**, rather than merely pronounce words.

Whatever our occupation, perform everything **in the name of the Lord Jesus**, relying upon him with faith.

Those who do all things in his name will never lack reasons for **gratitude to God the Father**.

## Serve Christ faithfully in every household relationship (verses 18–25)

The New Testament letters that most fully proclaim **God's grace and the glory of Christ** are also among those that explain our **ordinary Christian responsibilities most carefully**.

We must never separate **gospel privileges** from **gospel duties**.

Henry describes wives' duty as submission **to their own husbands**, not to cruel rulers or harsh tyrants.

Their husbands are bound to treat them with **tender, faithful love**.

Children who obey their parents are most likely to prosper.

Parents must likewise be **gentle and compassionate**, while children fulfill their duty of obedience.

Servants should obey their masters in everything **consistent with their duty to God, their heavenly Master**.

Their work must be **just, honest and diligent**, not driven by selfish calculation or outward pretense.

Believers who fear God remain faithful **even when no human supervisor watches**, because they know God sees their conduct.

They should work energetically rather than lazily, and cheerfully rather than resenting the position in which Providence has placed them.

Their encouragement is that through lawful and faithful service **they serve Christ himself**, who will reward them gloriously.

But those who do wrong will be held accountable for the wrong.

This applies **equally to masters who treat servants unjustly**.

The righteous Judge will judge both parties fairly.

**Master and servant stand on the same level before his tribunal**.

How much happier would the world be if genuine Christianity influenced every rank, relationship and condition of life!

But people who profess the gospel while neglecting duties and giving just grounds for complaint to those around them deceive themselves and **bring disgrace upon Christ's message**.
