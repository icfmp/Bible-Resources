# Isaiah 48 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews reproved for their idolatry. | 1–8 |
| Yet deliverance is promised them. | 9–15 |
| Solemn warnings of judgment upon those who persisted in evil. | 16–22 |

## A people proud of their name but lacking holiness (verses 1–8)

The Jews valued their descent from **Jacob** and used **Jehovah's name** as the name of their God.

They took pride in Jerusalem and the temple.

Yet their actual lives lacked holiness.

When our religion is not sincere, we are only **taking the Lord's name in vain**.

God had announced long beforehand what he would do, through prophecy, so that these people would have no excuse to claim credit for what occurred.

He has said and done enough to put an end to human boasting.

That makes the sin and eventual destruction of proud people even more serious.

Sooner or later, every mouth will be silenced before him.

We are all born with a disposition toward disobedience.

Where there is **original sin**, actual sins will follow.

Does not each person's conscience confirm the truth of Scripture on this point?

May the Lord test and refine us, and make us **doers of his word**.

## God promises rescue for the sake of his glory (verses 9–15)

We possess nothing in ourselves that could give God a reason to show us mercy.

He spares sinners for **the praise and honor of his own mercy**.

God sometimes leads people into suffering **in order to do them good**.

He refines them, but **not as completely as silver is refined**.

If the Lord treated us by burning away everything worthless with absolute strictness, **we would all be rejected as dross**.

Instead he accepts his people though their refining is still incomplete.

Many have returned to God during the **furnace of affliction**.

There they became chosen vessels, and a new work of grace began within them.

God's people can take comfort that **he will preserve his own honor**, and for that reason will bring about their deliverance.

When he determines to deliver them, he is never at a loss for instruments.

God has established a gracious plan to save those who come to him, doing so **for his own sake and the glory of his grace**.

## The Spirit teaches, and the Lord warns (verses 16–22)

The **Holy Spirit** equips people for service.

Those sent by God and by his Spirit may speak boldly.

Henry applies this passage particularly to **Jesus Christ**, whom the Father sent and upon whom the Spirit rested **without measure**.

God teaches everyone he redeems.

He teaches them how to profit from affliction and makes them sharers in **his holiness**.

By his grace he leads them along the path of duty.

By his Providence he leads them along the path of deliverance.

God does not delight in afflicting his people.

Had their sins not turned them away, their peace would have been continual and abundant.

Spiritual comfort belongs together with **holiness of life and obedience to God's will**.

Unrepentant people will suffer more deeply when they realize how happy they might have been.

The prophet assures Israel that the exiles will be rescued.

God provides for all those whom he intends to bring home.

He will not leave them without what they need **for their journey**.

Henry applies this provision to **the grace stored up for believers in Jesus Christ**.

Every good thing flows through him, just as water flowed for Israel from the rock—**and that Rock was Christ**.

The prophecy also points, in Henry's explanation, to spiritual redemption and to the church's deliverance from **antichristian tyranny**.

Yet whatever changes occur in history, the Lord's final warning to hardened sinners remains:

**There is no peace for the wicked**.

Guilt and divine judgment bring inward anguish and outward misery to those who continue without repentance.
