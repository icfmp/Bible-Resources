# Isaiah 25 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A song of praise. | 1–5 |
| A declaration of the gospel blessings. | 6–8 |
| The destruction of the enemies of Christ's church. | 9–12 |

## A song of praise for the Lord's protection (verses 1–5)

These words could celebrate the Jews' return from captivity. But Henry sees a greater fulfillment in praise for **Christ's victories over our spiritual enemies** and for the comfort he provides to all believers.

Genuine faith simply believes God's testimony and relies on the truth of his promises.

The Lord weakens proud people who consider themselves invincible, while strengthening those who are humble and look to him for support.

**God shelters his people in every kind of weather.**

He protects believers from arrogant oppressors. Their insults and threats are like the noise of strangers or **the fierce sun at midday**: intense for a moment, but where are they when the sun goes down?

The Lord has always been and will always remain **a refuge for his distressed people**.

He has prepared shelter for them, and he teaches them to run to it.

## The gospel feast and victory over death (verses 6–8)

The New Testament often compares God's welcoming reception of repentant sinners to **a feast**.

The invitation extends to all peoples, **Gentiles as well as Jews**.

The gospel strengthens and cheers the hearts of those who have become conscious of their sin and mourn over it.

A veil covers the nations because humanity lives in spiritual darkness.

The Lord removes that veil through **the light of the gospel and the Holy Spirit's power**, enabling people to recognize and welcome divine truth.

Those long dead in trespasses and sins receive **spiritual life**.

Christ himself, in his resurrection, **triumphs over death**.

Sorrow will end. Those who mourn for sin will receive comfort; those who suffer for Christ will find consolation.

Yet the promise that **God will wipe away every tear** will be **completely fulfilled only in the joys of heaven**.

Hope in that future should help us resist excessive grief now, particularly the kind of weeping that prevents us from continuing to sow and do good.

Sometimes even in this life God removes the public shame borne by his people. But at **the great day** all their reproach will be removed.

Let us patiently endure sorrow and disgrace, knowing their time is short.

## Waiting for the Redeemer and his final victory (verses 9–12)

People who longed for the promised Redeemer received the news of his coming with joy and praise.

Glorified believers will one day enter their Lord's joy with a triumphant song.

**Waiting for Christ is never in vain.** His mercy arrives with abundant compensation for every apparent delay.

The hands once extended upon **the cross to accomplish salvation** will finally be raised in judgment against those who persistently refuse him.

Henry reads **Moab** here as a figure representing all opponents of God's people. Their pride will be brought down through repeated judgments.

The defeat of Moab thus pictures **Christ's final victory** and the overthrow of **Satan's strongholds**.

Therefore, beloved brothers and sisters, remain **steadfast and immovable**, always abounding in the Lord's work, because your work in him is not wasted.
