## Luke's preface (verses 1–4)

Luke did not intend to write about questions on which Christians may safely disagree or remain uncertain.

He wrote about **the things that are, and ought to be, firmly believed**.

The teaching of Christ is what the wisest and best people have confidently entrusted their souls to, with deep satisfaction.

The great events on which our hope depends were recorded from the testimony of those who were **eyewitnesses from the beginning and ministers of the word**.

Their understanding was brought to completeness through **divine inspiration**.

## Zechariah and Elizabeth (verses 5–25)

The parents of John the Baptist were sinners, like everyone else, and were justified and saved in the same way as other believers.

Yet they were distinguished by their genuine godliness and integrity.

They had no children, and Elizabeth's advanced age meant they could no longer expect a child by ordinary means.

While Zechariah burned incense in the temple, the people were gathered outside in prayer.

All prayer offered to God can be accepted and answered only through **Christ's intercession in the heavenly temple**.

We must pray ourselves, earnestly and from the heart, if we hope to share in that intercession.

Even our best prayers receive an answer of peace only through the mediation of Christ, who always lives to intercede.

Zechariah had often prayed for a child.

Those prayers were not forgotten: **prayers offered in faith are kept before God**, and he may answer requests made in youth when we have reached old age.

Gifts received through prayer bring a special kind of happiness.

The promised son would be a means of turning many people to God and preparing them to receive Christ's gospel.

He would go before the Savior with courage, zeal, holiness and detachment from earthly pleasures and ambitions.

The disobedient would be turned toward the wisdom of righteous ancestors—or, more fully, toward the wisdom of **the Righteous One who was coming**.

Zechariah heard the angel's promise but spoke in unbelief.

God rightly made him unable to speak because he had objected to the divine word.

Yet even that correction was kind: it prevented further expressions of unbelief and strengthened his faith.

When discipline helps us trust God's word more fully, we have no reason to complain.

Even sincere believers may dishonor the Lord through unbelief. Their mouths are sometimes closed in confusion when they should be open in grateful praise.

We should recognize the Lord's merciful attention when he looks upon us with compassion and acts for our good.

## Gabriel announces Jesus' birth to Mary (verses 26–38)

Here we learn about **the mother of our Lord**.

We should **not pray to Mary**, but we should praise God for the favor he showed her.

Christ's birth was necessarily miraculous.

The angel greeted Mary as someone specially **chosen and favored by the Most High**, receiving an honor that generations of Jewish mothers had long hoped for.

The greeting troubled her, but the angel assured her that God had shown her favor.

She would bear a son named **Jesus**, the **Son of the Most High**, sharing in divine nature and perfection.

How precious the name **Jesus—Savior**—is to sinners who know their need!

We often fail to run to him because we do not recognize either our own poverty and peril or his immeasurable riches.

A person truly convinced of sin's guilt and the judgment it deserves would continually ask: **Is the Savior mine?**

Such a person would willingly overcome anything that hindered coming to him.

Mary's response expressed humble wonder and **faith**, not a demand for a sign.

Great indeed is the mystery of godliness: **God was manifested in the flesh** (**1 Timothy 3:16**, the cited source wording).

Christ's human nature was brought into being in a way appropriate for its union with the divine nature.

Like Mary, we should bring our desires into agreement with God's word.

In every difficulty, remember that nothing is impossible with God.

When we hear his promises, we should turn them into prayers:

**I am the Lord's willing servant; let it happen to me according to your word.**

## Mary visits Elizabeth (verses 39–56)

People in whom God has begun the work of grace benefit from sharing their experience with one another.

When Mary arrived, Elizabeth recognized the presence of the one who would become mother of the great Redeemer.

Filled with the Holy Spirit, she declared Mary and the child she carried greatly blessed because God had honored and loved them.

Mary, strengthened by Elizabeth's words and herself moved by the Holy Spirit, responded with joy, wonder and gratitude.

Mary knew **she was a sinner who needed a Savior**.

She could rejoice in God only through the salvation provided in the promised Messiah.

Those who recognize their need of Christ and hunger for righteousness and life in him receive the best gifts.

He fills them with spiritual blessings until they are satisfied.

The poor in spirit will find their longing fulfilled, while those who imagine they need nothing will depart empty.

## John the Baptist is born (verses 57–66)

John's birth brought joy throughout the family.

His name, **Johanan**, was associated by Henry with **“gracious”**, because John would introduce the gospel of Christ, in which God's grace shines so clearly.

Zechariah's speech returned.

Unbelief had closed his mouth; believing opened it again.

**He believed, and therefore he spoke.**

When God opens our lips, we ought to praise him.

It would be better to lack speech than to possess it and never use it to glorify God.

The Lord's hand was with John.

God can work in the lives of infants in ways we cannot fully explain.

We should notice what he does and wait to see what follows.

## Zechariah's prophetic song (verses 67–80)

Zechariah prophesied about **the Messiah's kingdom and salvation**.

The gospel brings light.

In John's ministry, the dawn began to break, growing brighter until the fullness of daylight.

The gospel **reveals** what was hidden: the knowledge of God's glory in the face of Jesus Christ.

It **revives** those sitting in death's shadow, like condemned prisoners in a dark dungeon.

It **guides** our feet onto the way of peace, the path that will at last bring us to peace (**Romans 3:17**).

John showed strong faith, vigorous and holy affections, and freedom from excessive fear or love of the world.

Through those years he grew ready for useful work, though he lived privately until he appeared publicly as the Messiah's forerunner.

Let us pursue peace with everyone, while also seeking peace with God and with our consciences.

If God calls us to remain unknown to the world, we can still grow diligently in **the grace of Jesus Christ**.
