# John 19 — Modern English

## Jesus is condemned and crucified (verses 1–18)

Pilate could hardly have imagined how future generations of great and godly people would reverently remember Christ's sufferings.

Jesus willingly endured public ridicule.

Let everyone who believes **look upon Jesus in his suffering**. Behold him with faith and love, and continually fix your attention on him.

If hatred made Jesus' enemies more determined to oppose him, should not love make us more diligent in serving **him and his kingdom**?

Pilate appears to have suspected that Jesus might be a person far above the ordinary human condition.

Even natural conscience can make a person afraid of fighting against God.

Christ suffered for **the sins of Jews and Gentiles**. In God's wise purpose, Jewish leaders planned his death and Gentile authorities carried it out.

If Jesus had not been rejected by human beings, **we would have remained rejected before God**.

The Son of Man was handed over to unreasonable and wicked people.

He was led away **on our behalf so that we might escape**.

He was fastened to the cross like a sacrifice bound upon the altar.

Scripture was fulfilled. He did not die among temple sacrifices but among criminals executed by public authority.

Let us contemplate his suffering with faith.

Was there ever sorrow like his sorrow?

Look upon him bleeding and dying, and let love for him lead you **to live for him**.

## Christ's final hours on the cross (verses 19–30)

John records remarkable circumstances of Jesus' death in fuller detail than elsewhere.

Pilate refused to alter the inscription on the cross at the priests' request. Henry attributes this to God's hidden influence, preserving a statement of **Christ's kingship and authority**.

The Roman soldiers' conduct fulfilled several Old Testament prophecies. Everything God has written must be fulfilled.

Dying, Jesus provided tenderly for his mother.

When God takes one earthly source of comfort from us, he may raise up another from an unexpected place.

Christ teaches everyone to **honor parents during their life and at their death**, meeting their needs and promoting their comfort as far as possible.

Above all, consider his final announcement: **“It is finished.”**

The Father's purposes concerning Christ's suffering had been fulfilled.

All Old Testament **types and prophecies pointing to the suffering Messiah** had reached their fulfillment.

The ceremonial law had reached its end, because the reality it foreshadowed had now arrived.

Transgression was dealt with through the bringing in of **everlasting righteousness**.

The sufferings of his body **and soul** were complete.

**The work of human redemption and salvation was accomplished.**

His life was not taken from him against his will; he **freely gave it up**.

## The soldier pierces Jesus' side (verses 31–37)

The soldiers confirmed that Jesus had died.

He died more quickly than crucified prisoners ordinarily did, a sign that he had laid down his life voluntarily.

The spear opened the source of bodily life; no ordinary human body could have survived such a wound.

Its solemn attestation suggests that the detail had particular meaning.

**Blood and water** flowed from his side.

Henry understands them as signs of the two great blessings believers receive through Christ:

**Blood for justification**, bringing atonement and forgiveness.

**Water for sanctification**, bringing cleansing and holiness.

Both flow from the pierced side of the Redeemer. The crucified Christ provides **merit for our justification and the Spirit's grace for our sanctification**.

This comforts fearful believers: Jesus provides both the pardon they need and the holiness they lack.

The refusal to break Jesus' legs fulfilled Scripture (**Psalm 34:20**), prefigured in the Passover lamb (**Exodus 12:46**).

May we always look toward the Savior whom **our sins have pierced**, sometimes even against the warnings and mercies we had already received.

He gave both blood and water so that in his name we might be justified and sanctified.

## Jesus is buried (verses 38–42)

**Joseph of Arimathea** had been a secret disciple.

Those who follow Christ should confess him openly. Yet someone timid in a smaller trial may show courage in a greater one.

When God has work to do, he can raise up people suited to it.

**Nicodemus**, another secret friend though not a constant public follower, helped prepare Jesus' body for burial.

Grace that begins like **a bruised reed** can grow as strong as **a cedar**.

These wealthy men showed that the dishonor of the cross had not reduced their love for Christ or their respect for his teaching.

We should fulfill today's duties when the opportunity arises, trusting God to carry out his promises in his own time.

Jesus was assigned a grave among the wicked as someone executed for a crime, but **was with the rich in his death**, fulfilling **Isaiah 53:9**.

Those seemingly incompatible circumstances could hardly have been predicted from ordinary human knowledge.

He was placed in a **new tomb**, removing any excuse to confuse the identity of the One who would rise.

Nor should believers be particular about the location of their own burial: Jesus was buried in the tomb conveniently at hand.

The **Sun of Righteousness** had set for a little while, soon to rise with greater glory and **never set again**.
