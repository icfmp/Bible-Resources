## Keep far from sexual temptation (verses 1–14)

Solomon warns young men, whom he addresses as his children, **to refuse lustful desires**.

Some readers understand the adulterous woman as a picture of **idolatry or false doctrine**, because both corrupt beliefs and conduct. But the passage's **direct subject is sin against the seventh commandment**.

Sexual wrongdoing has also repeatedly become a means by which Satan draws people from the worship of God into false religion.

Consider its terrible consequences. Sin's fruit is bitter, whichever aspect we consider. It wounds the person who follows it and leads toward final judgment.

Its natural tendency is **to destroy both body and soul**.

We should therefore avoid not only the act itself but also **everything that might lead to it**.

Those who wish to avoid harm should **keep away from danger**.

If we deliberately put ourselves into temptation and then pray, **Lead us not into temptation**, we mock God.

Such sin damages reputation, wastes time, consumes property, destroys health and fills the mind with horror.

A person may feel merry now, but sooner or later sorrow will follow.

Someone convinced of sin's guilt blames **personal folly** rather than inventing excuses.

Repeated acts of sin become entrenched habits.

Through an extraordinary act of divine mercy, true repentance can prevent the final ruin of such a sinner.

But Henry warns that **this is uncommon**; many people die as they have lived.

What words could express the misery of a person who has destroyed himself and enters eternity tormented by an accusing conscience?

## God's provision of marriage and his knowledge of all our ways (verses 15–23)

**Lawful marriage** is a God-appointed means of avoiding destructive sexual sins.

Yet marriage is not lived rightly unless the spouses attend to God's word, seek his direction and blessing, and act with genuine affection toward one another.

Always remember that hidden sins may escape human notice, but **our ways are before the Lord**.

He not only sees our conduct; **he examines every step**.

Those who foolishly choose a path of sin may justly be left by God to follow their own choices until those choices bring destruction.
