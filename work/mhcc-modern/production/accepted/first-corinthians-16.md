# 1 Corinthians 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A collection for the poor at Jerusalem. | 1–9 |
| Timothy and Apollos commended. | 10–12 |
| Exhortation to watchfulness in faith and love. | 13–18 |
| Christian salutations. | 19–24 |

## The collection for believers in Jerusalem (verses 1–9)

Good examples from other Christians and congregations ought to encourage us toward generous action.

It is wise to **set aside resources for good purposes**.

Those rich in the present world should be **rich in good works (1 Timothy 6:17–18)**.

Diligence alone will not make anyone prosperous without God's blessing (**Proverbs 10:4, 22**).

What better encouragement toward charity for God's people than remembering that **everything we possess is God's gift**?

Acts of mercy are genuine fruit of love for God and are appropriate ways of serving him **on the Lord's Day**.

Ministers carry out their proper work when they encourage and assist efforts of charity.

A Christian minister should remain concerned for the people among whom he has worked faithfully and fruitfully for a long time.

Every plan should be made with submission to **God's Providence (James 4:15)**.

Opposition and enemies do not necessarily discourage faithful, successful ministers.

Such difficulty may instead **increase their zeal and courage**.

They are often more troubled by the hardness of their hearers' hearts and the backsliding of professing Christians than by attacks from outsiders.

## Timothy and Apollos should receive honorable treatment (verses 10–12)

Timothy was doing **the Lord's work**.

Therefore, to needlessly trouble him was to grieve the Holy Spirit, and to despise him was to dishonor the One who sent him.

Those engaged in Christ's work deserve **kindness, respect and consideration**.

Faithful ministers should not compete through jealousy.

They should protect one another's **reputation and usefulness** and show sincere concern for one another's welfare.

## Watchfulness, firmness and active love (verses 13–18)

A Christian is always exposed to spiritual danger.

Therefore, believers must **remain watchful**, established in the gospel's faith and unwilling to abandon it.

Faith in Christ alone enables them to stand firm when temptation comes.

But Christian courage must always be accompanied by **love governing the heart and visible in conduct**.

There is a great difference between **steadfastness in Christ** and **agitated, uncontrolled excitement**.

Paul commended some people who served Christ and cared for the church.

Those who serve believers, protect the churches' honor and help remove unjust reproaches deserve grateful respect and love.

The Corinthians should willingly recognize everyone who worked with Paul and assisted him in his service.

## Greetings and final appeal to love Christ (verses 19–24)

Christianity does not abolish courtesy.

True religion encourages **kind, considerate and helpful behavior** toward everyone.

People who use their faith as an excuse for bitter, unfriendly conduct misrepresent Christianity.

Christian greetings should not be empty compliments.

They express **real goodwill** and ask God's grace and blessing upon others.

Every Christian household should resemble **a small Christian church**.

Where two or three gather in Christ's name and he is present among them, there is a church.

Paul ends with a solemn warning.

Many people constantly pronounce **Christ's name** without genuinely loving him in their hearts.

No one sincerely loves Christ while refusing his commands or despising his law.

Some people are Christian in name only.

They do not love Jesus Christ the Lord sincerely and therefore do not belong to God's people or enjoy his favor.

**Whoever does not love the Lord Jesus Christ remains without the remedy of salvation**.

Let us never be satisfied with religious profession alone.

Ask whether we possess **love for Christ, desire for his salvation, gratitude for his mercy and obedience to his commands**.

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ contains every good gift needed for this life and eternity.

Wishing that grace upon a friend is wishing **the greatest possible good**.

We cannot desire anything better, and we should not desire anything less for our brothers and sisters in Christ.

True Christianity makes us seek the blessings of **both worlds** for those we love.

Paul had spoken plainly and rebuked the Corinthians' faults with necessary severity.

But he departed **in love**, affirming his affection for them in Christ.

May our love be with everyone who belongs to Christ Jesus.

Examine yourselves.

Do all things seem worthless when compared with **Christ and his righteousness**?

Do you knowingly indulge some sin or neglect a duty you know God requires?

Such serious and honest questions help reveal the spiritual condition of our souls.
