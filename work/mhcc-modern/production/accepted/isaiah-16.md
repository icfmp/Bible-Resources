# Isaiah 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Moab is exhorted to yield obedience. | 1–5 |
| The pride and the judgments of Moab. | 6–14 |

## Moab is called to submit and show mercy (verses 1–5)

God warns sinners how they can turn from the path of destruction. Here he gives such counsel to **Moab**.

The people were to send Judah the tribute they had previously agreed to pay.

Henry draws a wider lesson: **turn away from sin through righteousness**, and God may prolong peace.

He also applies the command to **submission to Jesus Christ**.

Give him your best gift—indeed, **offer yourselves as a living sacrifice**.

When you approach God, the supreme Ruler, come in the name of **the Lamb of God**.

Those refusing Christ's rule resemble a bird wandering away from its nest, exposed to the next bird of prey.

Anyone unwilling to reverence God becomes vulnerable to fear of everything else.

Moab was called to show kindness to the people of Israel.

Those who hope to receive help when they themselves suffer must **show compassion to other sufferers**.

The promise concerning **Hezekiah's throne** belongs, in an even greater sense, to **the kingdom of Jesus Christ**.

Serving him may not bring earthly riches, honor or public approval.

It may even expose believers to poverty and contempt.

Nevertheless, they gain **peace of conscience and eternal life**.

## Moab's pride and judgment (verses 6–14)

Those who refuse all counsel cannot readily be helped.

Henry regards **pride as especially destructive to souls**.

Proud people often become easily angered, and their pride and anger lead to lies and fruitless schemes.

Moab's countryside was famous for fields and vineyards, yet invading armies would ruin them.

God can quickly turn laughter into mourning and joy into sorrow.

We should rejoice confidently **in God**, but receive earthly blessings with humble awareness that they may soon disappear.

The prophet felt sorrow at the destruction of such a beautiful and fruitful land.

Moab's false gods could provide no help.

But the God of Israel, the only true God, would fulfill everything he had declared.

Moab's coming judgment was close, and the people were urged to prepare.

Even the sternest warnings of God's wrath disclose **a path of escape to those who take them seriously**.

The only lasting escape is to **submit to the Son of David and dedicate ourselves to him**.

When the appointed time comes, the pride, prosperity and multitude of the wicked will all pass away.
