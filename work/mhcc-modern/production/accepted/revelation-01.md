# Revelation 1 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## The revelation of Jesus Christ and its blessing (verses 1–3)

This book is **the revelation of Jesus Christ**. Indeed, all Scripture comes through him and ultimately concerns him.

Its chief purpose is to reveal God's intentions concerning **the church and the nations in relation to it**, continuing to the end of the world.

These events are certain to happen, and their fulfillment would begin soon.

Although Jesus is himself **God**, possessing light and life in himself, as **Mediator** he receives instructions from the Father to reveal to humanity.

Through Christ we know what to expect from God and what God requires from us.

The book pronounces a blessing on people who **read and listen to its prophecy**.

But merely hearing or reading is insufficient. Keep its message **in memory, thought, affection and practice**.

Even when the prophetic meaning is difficult, its mysteries reveal something of God, awaken reverence and purify the soul.

No other part of Scripture speaks more fully of the gospel or gives a stronger warning against sin.

## Grace and peace from the Triune God (verses 4–8)

There is no true peace without **grace**. Where grace comes first, real peace follows.

John's greeting is an act of adoration to **the Holy Trinity**.

**The Father** is the eternal and unchanging Lord who is, was and is to come.

**The Holy Spirit** is described as **the seven Spirits**, the one perfect Spirit of God working through a diversity of gifts and operations.

**Jesus Christ** is the faithful Witness, the Firstborn from the dead and the Ruler over the earth's kings.

He knows all God's purposes and will raise his people by his own power.

Every ruler's plans are subject to him and every ruler must answer to him.

Sin stains the soul with **guilt and corruption**. Only **Jesus' blood** can remove that stain.

He shed his blood to satisfy divine justice, obtaining pardon and purification for his people.

He has made believers **kings and priests**.

As kings they overcome the world, put sin to death, govern their own hearts, resist Satan and prevail through prayer.

As priests they enter God's presence and offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable through Christ.

For these blessings, glory and rule belong to him forever.

Jesus will judge the world.

In the day of his appearing, everyone will see how wise and happy his friends were and how miserable his enemies are.

Henry warns that Christ's coming will terrify those who **wound and crucify him by apostasy** and astonish the ungodly world.

Remember his return often.

He is **the Beginning and the End**, the Almighty, the eternal and unchanging One from whom all things come and for whom they exist.

Anyone hoping to share his everlasting glory must willingly receive and honor him now as **Savior**, knowing he will return as **Judge**.

## John sees the vision on the Lord's Day (verses 9–11)

John was suffering **not for wrongdoing but for bearing witness to Jesus Christ**, Emmanuel and Savior.

The Spirit of glory rested upon this persecuted apostle.

He received his vision on **the Lord's Day**, the first day of the week, which Henry identifies as **the Christian Sabbath** in remembrance of Christ's resurrection.

Those who call Jesus their Lord should honor him on his day.

Henry urges dedicating the Lord's Day wholly to spiritual duties, rather than spending its hours in sensual amusements or worldly pleasures.

John was in a serious, reverent and heavenly frame of mind through the Spirit's influence.

Believers seeking fellowship with God should turn thoughts and desires away from earthly distractions.

Sometimes Christians are **prevented by necessity, not by choice**, from joining public worship.

Even then they may find divine comfort through **private prayer, meditation and the Spirit's presence**.

Neither confinement nor external hardship can separate them from Christ's gracious voice and power.

John then heard a loud voice like a trumpet calling him.

## The glorious Son of Man among his churches (verses 12–20)

The churches receive **light from Jesus Christ and his gospel** and are called to shine before others.

The **golden lampstands** picture their purity and value.

Not only ministers but all church members should live so that people glorify God.

Jesus stood among the lampstands, showing that he remains with his churches, filling them with **light, life and love**.

His long robe **may** signify righteousness and priestly mediation.

The golden sash **may** picture the preciousness of his love for his people.

His white hair **may** signify majesty, holiness and eternity.

His eyes like flame **may** show his knowledge of hearts and coming events.

His feet like refined bronze **may** show the firmness and excellence of his purposes and deeds.

His voice like rushing waters **may** reveal the irresistible power of his word.

The **seven stars** represent the ministers of the churches, whom Christ sustains and guides.

The sword represents his justice and the searching power of his word (**Hebrews 4:12**).

His face shone brighter than mortal eyes could bear.

Overwhelmed by this glory, John fell down.

We can be content to walk by faith during earthly life.

Jesus comforted John with **“Do not fear.”**

He revealed himself as **the First and the Last**, truly God.

He was once dead—the same One the disciples saw crucified—but now lives eternally, having overcome death.

He possesses supreme authority over **death and the unseen world**, judging with a sentence no one can overturn.

Listen to his voice, receive his love and obey his word.

What will he withhold from those for whom he gave his life?

Commit yourselves entirely to the One who rules all things wisely.
