# Joshua 14 — Modern English

## The inheritance of the nine and a half tribes (verses 1–5)

The Israelites had to settle the land they had conquered. Conquering Canaan would have served little purpose if they did not go on to live there.

Yet each person was not free to choose any place that appealed to him. God would choose their inheritance.

Let us consider both the mercies God has already given us and the inheritance he has promised us—the eternal home in heaven.

Does God show favoritism? Is it not better to have our earthly happiness and suffering assigned by the infinite wisdom of our heavenly Father than by our own ignorance?

Christ has revealed the great mystery of godliness, and Jesus Christ has purchased our redemption. Those who have received such grace should gladly entrust their earthly affairs to the Father's wise arrangement.

## Caleb receives Hebron (verses 6–15)

Caleb asked Joshua, “Give me this mountain”—that is, Hebron. God had promised it to him before, and Caleb wanted Israel to see how highly he valued that promise.

Those who live by faith prize what God has expressly promised more than what they receive merely through his providential care.

The Anakim still possessed Hebron. By asking for it, Caleb showed that he was not afraid of those enemies. He also intended to encourage Israel to continue its conquests.

Caleb lived up to his name, which Henry explains as meaning **“all heart.”** Hebron was granted to Caleb and his descendants because he had followed the Lord God of Israel wholeheartedly.

Happy are we if we follow the Lord in the same way. Exceptional faithfulness will be crowned with exceptional favor.
