# Luke 4 — Modern English

## The temptation of Jesus (verses 1–13)

Being alone in the wilderness gave the tempter an apparent advantage over Jesus.

No friend was present to pray for or advise him in the hour of testing.

Jesus **knew his own strength**, so he could allow that situation. We know our own weakness and must not deliberately put ourselves in temptation's way.

Made like his brothers and sisters, Jesus lived in dependence upon God's **providence and promise**.

God's word is our **sword**, and faith in it is our **shield**.

God has countless ways to provide for his people. We can therefore trust him while remaining in the path of duty.

Satan's promises are deceptive.

If he has influence over worldly power and honor, he uses them as bait to entice people toward destruction.

Any opportunity to gain wealth or status through sin is a price offered for the soul; reject it.

Our true riches, honor and happiness are found through worshiping and serving God.

Christ would not worship Satan. When the Father delivers the kingdoms of the world to Jesus, he will not allow the worship of the devil to continue in them.

Satan also tempted Jesus to **bring about his own death** by throwing himself down on the strength of an unauthorized claim to divine protection.

This would have been presumption, not faith.

Do not abandon Scripture because Satan or other people misuse it.

Study it more diligently and use it to defend yourself against every kind of attack.

Let the word dwell richly within us; it is our life.

Christ overcame the devil not only **for himself but for us**.

He allowed the tempter to use every approach, yet Satan found nothing sinful within Jesus for the temptation to take hold of.

Resist the devil, and he will flee.

He withdrew only for a season.

Henry contrasts this earlier temptation, aimed at drawing Jesus into sin, with Satan's later persecution, aimed at bringing him to suffering.

The serpent would bruise Christ's **heel** yet have his own **head** crushed (**Genesis 3:15**).

We are never entirely beyond Satan's reach while we remain in this fallen world.

## Jesus teaches in Nazareth (verses 14–30)

Jesus taught in the synagogues, places where people gathered to read, explain and apply Scripture, and to pray and praise God.

The Holy Spirit's gifts and graces rested upon him **without measure**.

Christ releases sinners from **the guilt of sin** and, through his Spirit and grace, from **sin's corrupting power**.

Through the word of his gospel he brings light to people in darkness.

Through the power of grace he opens the eyes of the spiritually blind.

He proclaimed **the acceptable year of the Lord**.

Let sinners respond to the Savior's invitation while freedom is being announced.

Jesus' name was **Wonderful**.

Nothing shows this more clearly than the astonishing grace in his words and the transforming power accompanying them.

How remarkable that he spoke such gracious words to people so lacking in grace!

Prejudice often supplies an excuse for rejecting the humbling message of the cross.

People whose hearts are offended by God's word may blame **the preacher's manner** rather than admit opposition to the truth itself.

The teaching of **God's sovereignty and right to act as he wills** angers proud people.

They refuse to seek God's favor on his terms and resent those who receive the mercy they neglect.

Many still reject Jesus though they hear his words.

May we instead honor him as **the Son of God and Savior of humanity**, showing our faith through obedience rather than dishonoring him through sin.

## Jesus drives out evil spirits and heals people (verses 31–44)

Jesus' teaching deeply affected his hearers.

Its power reached human consciences.

His miracles showed him as **the conqueror of Satan and the healer of disease**.

When Christ grants someone a renewed life through recovery from sickness, that life should be devoted more fully to his glory and service.

Our work should include spreading the good news about Christ, praying for those suffering in body or mind, and using our influence to bring sinners to him for healing.

He expelled evil spirits from many who were possessed.

We were not placed in the world simply to serve ourselves.

We are here to glorify God and **do good to our generation**.

People came looking for Jesus.

Even a desert is no longer desolate when **Christ is with us**.

He continues to accompany believers through his **word and Spirit**.

His blessings will be made known to more nations, until people throughout the earth who once served Satan recognize Jesus as **the Christ, the Son of God**, and find redemption through his blood—**the forgiveness of sins**.
