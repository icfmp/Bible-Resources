# Job 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| Bildad reproves Job. | 1–7 |

## Bildad rebukes Job (verses 1–7)

Job had said much that deserved thoughtful consideration.

But Bildad, like an impatient and angry debater, dismissed it all by asking how long Job would continue talking this way.

People often misunderstand someone's meaning and then rebuke that person as though they had committed wrongdoing.

Even in discussions about religion, people too readily answer with harshness and treat others' arguments with contempt.

Bildad's speech shows that he already held a poor opinion of Job.

Job agreed that God never perverts justice.

But this did not mean his children were rejected by God or that they had died because of some extraordinary sin.

**Extraordinary suffering is not always the punishment of extraordinary wickedness.**

At times it is instead **the testing of extraordinary grace**.

When we judge another person's case, we should lean toward the charitable interpretation.

Bildad offered Job hope that an upright person would eventually experience a good outcome.

God does enrich his people with growing grace and comfort.

What begins small can grow toward completeness, like early dawn brightening toward midday.

## Bildad describes the fate of hypocrites (verses 8–19)

Bildad spoke truly about hypocrites and the eventual destruction of their hopes and pleasures.

He appealed to earlier generations and the testimony of ancient teachers.

Those who speak out of sincere hearts and personal experience of divine things can teach most effectively.

Consider a plant growing in marshy soil.

It appears vividly green, yet quickly withers when conditions dry up.

This is a picture of religious profession sustained **only by prosperity**.

A spider's web may be intricately woven, but it can be swept away at once.

So it is with the confidence of someone who claims religion while lacking God's grace in the heart.

A person who merely performs religious forms may flatter himself, assume he is safe, remain untroubled about salvation and deceive others with empty confidence.

A garden tree may flourish and fasten its roots around a rock, yet later be cut down and forgotten.

It pictures wicked people who seem firmly established before being suddenly removed.

**Bildad's teaching about false confidence is true in general**, but it cannot fairly be applied to Job if restricted to what happens in **this life**.

## Bildad applies the argument to Job (verses 20–22)

Bildad insisted that people receive the outcome appropriate to their character.

He and Job's other friends therefore concluded that Job's present suffering proved his wickedness.

God will not finally reject an upright person.

Such a person may be brought low temporarily but will never be abandoned forever.

Sin does bring ruin upon people and their families.

But **calling Job ungodly and wicked because he suffered was unfair and unloving**.

The friends' mistake was failing to distinguish **the present life of trial and discipline** from **the future state of final judgment**.

May we seek the portion and confidence of the righteous, carry their cross and die their death.

Until then, let us avoid hurting others with hasty judgments and troubling ourselves needlessly over other people's opinions.
