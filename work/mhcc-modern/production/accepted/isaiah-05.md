# Isaiah 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The state and conduct of the Jewish nation. | 1–7 |
| The judgments which would come. | 8–23 |
| The executioners of these judgments. | 24–30 |

## God's carefully tended vineyard (verses 1–7)

**Jesus Christ is God's beloved Son and our beloved Savior**.

The Lord's care for Israel is pictured through the tending of a vineyard.

One day we will answer to him for all the advantages and opportunities we have received.

He planted the finest vines, gave Israel an excellent law and instituted appropriate forms of worship.

The **temple** was like a vineyard's tower, where God gave signs of his presence. His **altar**, where sacrifices were offered, represented the appointed means of grace.

God expects **fruit** from people who receive such privileges.

Good intentions and promising beginnings are worth something, but they are insufficient by themselves. There must be genuine fruit: **thoughts, desires, words and actions guided by the Spirit**.

Instead, the vineyard produced wild grapes—the products of corrupted human nature.

Where God's grace does not bear fruit, sin will.

When people who profess faith misuse their privileges and reject grace, the responsibility is their own.

They may lose their distinct standing among God's people.

A vineyard left unpruned becomes overgrown with thorns. When errors and sinful habits are never corrected, the same thing happens to a community.

God may withdraw his Spirit from people who persistently resist him, or remove the preaching of the gospel from places that have long dishonored it.

Consider the contrast between the fruit he seeks and the fruit our hearts produce.

He seeks **humility, gentleness, love, patience, freedom from worldly obsession, prayer and praise**.

Sin produces **pride, anger, discontent, malice, contempt for God, curses and irreverent speech**.

May we bear fruit patiently and finally receive everlasting life.

## Warnings against greed, pleasure and moral confusion (verses 8–23)

The Lord pronounces judgment on people who devote their hearts to wealth.

Owning a house and a field and buying another is **not in itself sinful**.

The fault lies in **never recognizing that one has enough**.

**Greed is idolatry.**

Many envy the prosperous person whom God calls miserable because of greed.

The Lord has many ways to empty even crowded cities. People who seek ultimate satisfaction in possessions will be disappointed.

He also warns those who live only for sensual pleasure.

**Music is not wrong in itself**, but becomes sinful when it diverts the heart from God.

People may continue pursuing pleasure even while divine judgments are unfolding around them.

Death lowers everyone, whether high or low.

In judging pride, God reveals both **his power and his holiness**.

Woe to those who deliberately pursue sinful desires, mock the Holy One of Israel and dare to challenge him.

They **reverse the distinction between good and evil**.

They prefer their own reasoning to God's revelation, and their personal schemes to his commands.

They treat profitable wrongdoing as prudent and duties requiring self-denial as foolish.

Drunkenness is no harmless pastime; it exposes the person to divine judgment.

Even judges may corrupt justice to protect their interests.

One sin frequently requires **another sin to hide it**.

## The approaching instruments of judgment (verses 24–30)

Those who live in wickedness should not expect an easy life.

Sin weakens a nation's **roots** and destroys its **blossoms**—its strength and beauty.

When people reject God's word and law, they should not be surprised if he abandons them to judgment.

Under divine wrath, even mountains tremble and the powerful are afraid.

God can readily find instruments to punish people who continually provoke him.

Henry points to **the Chaldeans**, and later **the Romans**, as instruments used in the historical destruction of Judah and Jerusalem.

Those refusing to listen to God's warnings through his prophets would instead hear the threatening voices of their enemies.

They would find no refuge wherever they turned.

If God is displeased with us, no created source of comfort can finally make us secure.

Let us seek a well-founded assurance that, when every earthly support fails, **God himself will remain the strength of our hearts and our portion forever**.
