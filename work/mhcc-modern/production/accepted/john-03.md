# John 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ's discourse with Nicodemus. | 1–21 |
| The baptism of John of Christ John's testimony. | 22–36 |

## Jesus teaches Nicodemus about the new birth (verses 1–21)

Nicodemus was afraid, or ashamed, to be seen with Christ.

He therefore came to Jesus **at night**.

When faith is unpopular, there are many people who, like Nicodemus, hesitate to identify themselves openly with Jesus.

Yet Jesus welcomed him and taught us to encourage **even weak beginnings of faith**.

Nicodemus later acknowledged Christ publicly.

He was a ruler, but he did not come to discuss public affairs.

He came to talk about **the condition and salvation of his own soul**.

Jesus spoke immediately about the necessity and character of **regeneration, the new birth**, leading him to the true source of holiness.

Birth marks the beginning of life.

To be born again is to begin **a genuinely new life** after having lived wrongly or without proper purpose.

We need **a new nature, new principles, new affections and new goals**.

At our first birth we were corrupted by sin.

We must therefore become **new creatures**.

**No stronger expression** could have been chosen to describe so great a change in a person's state and character.

Something is done **within us and for us** that we cannot accomplish by our own efforts.

A new life begins that will endure forever.

Without it, we cannot expect to enjoy the benefits of Christ, either now or in the future.

Nicodemus misunderstood Jesus as though regenerating an immortal soul meant physically reshaping the body.

Yet he admitted his lack of understanding, demonstrating a desire to learn.

Jesus made clear that the Author of this new birth is **the Holy Spirit**.

Neither human intelligence nor human power can produce it.

Our corrupt nature makes the change essential.

In view of God's holiness, our own depravity and the eternal happiness promised to believers, we should not be surprised that it is so necessary.

Jesus compared the Spirit's work with **water**.

Henry says it is also **probable** Jesus was referring to baptism.

But **this does not mean everyone baptized is saved, or that only baptized people can be saved**.

What is indispensable is **the new birth performed by the Spirit**, of which baptism is a sign.

The same Greek word can refer to **wind and Spirit**.

God governs the wind's movement; the Spirit likewise gives his influence **where, when, to whom and in whatever measure he chooses**.

We may not see the origin of that work, but we can recognize its effects when a soul grieves over sin and longs for Christ.

Nicodemus still struggled to understand.

Spiritual truths seem foolish to the natural human mind.

Some people conclude that what they cannot believe cannot be proved.

Jesus' teaching about heavenly things invited Nicodemus to seek deeper understanding.

Jesus is fully able to disclose God's will, for **he came down from heaven and yet is in heaven**.

Here is the mystery of **his two natures in one person**.

He is the Son of Man, yet remains divine.

We learn these things through revelation from above, received by faith.

Jesus also taught that salvation comes through looking to him, like the Israelites bitten by poisonous serpents who survived through looking at the **bronze serpent** (**Numbers 21:6–9**).

Sin is deadly.

Whatever attraction it may seem to possess, in the end it **bites like a serpent**.

The remedy is set before us in the gospel.

The One we have offended has become our **peace**.

We receive healing by **believing in Christ**.

Those who despise their disease or refuse the remedy through Jesus are responsible for their own ruin.

**Look to Christ crucified and live.**

Unless God's grace enables us to do this, we remain wounded by Satan and under the sentence of death.

Jesus came to save us through forgiveness, delivering us from condemnation under the law.

Here is **genuinely good news**.

God's love is revealed in giving **his Son for the world**.

How astonishing that the great God should love such an undeserving world!

Our duty is to believe in Jesus Christ, whom the Father gave as **Prophet, Priest and King**.

We must submit to him to be taught, governed and saved.

His promise is that **whoever believes in him will not perish but have everlasting life**.

Through Christ God reconciles sinners to himself.

There is no other Savior.

Those who believe in Jesus are **not condemned**, however great their past sins.

They are not dealt with according to their guilt.

Unbelief, by contrast, rejects God's most precious gift and the only remedy for sin.

Those who reject the Son remain **condemned already**, under the law's charge for their sins and without the gospel's pardon.

Such unbelief springs from the heart's hostility toward God and some form of love for sin.

People performing the deeds of darkness often refuse Christ's light because it would expose their wrongdoing.

**Christ is hated because sin is loved**.

The renewed heart does the opposite.

It welcomes the light, wants to know God's will and obeys even when obedience conflicts with worldly advantage.

The Holy Spirit fills the heart with **God's love**, making that love the governing principle of life.

While a sinner is burdened by unforgiven guilt, there can be little more than fear of divine judgment.

But when the sinner sees the righteous ground of **forgiveness through Christ** and trusts it personally, that fear gives way to sincere love and communion with God.

Works are good when **God's will is their rule, God's glory is their goal, and God's strength enables them**.

Regeneration is therefore the most important concern of life.

What benefit would abundant food, fine clothes, pleasure, success or long years give us if we died **without being born again** and finally heard the Judge say he never knew us?

## John gladly gives way to Christ (verses 22–36)

John the Baptist was content with **his own assigned place and task**.

Jesus had come to perform a greater work.

John knew Christ would increase in honor and influence while fewer people followed John.

The Messiah's government and peace would **have no end**.

John also knew the fundamental difference between them.

Christ came from heaven as **the Son of God**.

John was a sinful and mortal man who could speak only about the more ordinary matters of religion.

Jesus' words were **the words of God**.

The Holy Spirit was given to him **without measure**, unlike prophets who received particular gifts for appointed tasks.

**Eternal life is obtained only through faith in the Son of God**.

Those refusing to believe in him cannot share in salvation.

God's wrath remains upon them **forever**, as Henry explicitly warns.
