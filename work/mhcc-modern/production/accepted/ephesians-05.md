# Ephesians 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortation to brotherly love. | 1, 2 |
| Cautions against several sins. | 3–14 |
| Directions to a contrary behaviour, and to relative duties. | 15–21 |
| The duties of wives and husbands are enforced by the spiritual relation between Christ and the church. | 22–33 |

## Imitate God's forgiving love (verses 1 and 2)

God has forgiven believers **for Christ's sake**.

Therefore we should **imitate God**, especially in his love and willingness to forgive.

This is how beloved children should resemble their heavenly Father.

God's love shines most clearly in **the sacrifice of Jesus Christ**.

We should continually consider the greatness of that love and shape our conduct by it.

## Live as children of light (verses 3–14)

Sinful sexual desires must be opposed and put away.

We should fear and detest the sins Paul lists.

The warnings address not only obvious acts of wrongdoing but also practices many people consider unimportant.

Corrupt speech is not useful.

It **pollutes those who speak and harms those who listen**.

Christian joy ought to be expressed through words that contribute to **God's glory**.

A greedy person makes **money into an idol**, transferring to wealth the hope, confidence and happiness that belong to God alone.

Those who surrender themselves to sinful desires or love of the world do not belong to **the kingdom of grace** and will not enter **the kingdom of glory**.

Yet even people guilty of dreadful sins can become **children of obedience** when they repent and believe the gospel.

God's wrath is then turned from them.

How dare we treat lightly the very sins that call down his just judgment?

Sinners resemble people walking in darkness.

They do not understand where they are going or the consequences of their actions.

But God brings about a wonderful change through grace.

Believers must **walk as children of light**, with knowledge and holiness.

Works of darkness are unfruitful, whatever profit they seem to offer, because they end in ruin for those who refuse repentance.

We may share in other people's sins in many ways: **through praise, advice, agreement or hiding their wrongdoing**.

If we participate in their sins, we should fear sharing in their judgment.

Henry also says we should **rebuke wrongdoing rather than quietly approve it**.

A good person may be ashamed even to speak about sins that others shamelessly commit.

It is not enough merely to recognize that a behavior seems shameful.

We must understand that it violates **God's holy law**.

Like the prophets and apostles, we should call those asleep and dead in sin to **wake up and rise**, so that **Christ may give them light**.

## Use your time well and seek the Spirit's fullness (verses 15–21)

Another safeguard against sin is **careful, thoughtful living**.

Without watchfulness, a person cannot preserve purity of heart and conduct.

**Time is a gift entrusted to us by God**.

When used outside his purposes, it is wasted.

If we have wasted much of our past, we should become more diligent in the time that remains.

Many dying people would gladly give the whole world to recover a little time.

Yet how little we value it now, and how freely we spend it on worthless things!

People often complain about the wickedness of the age.

It would be better if their concern moved them to **redeem the time**.

Neglecting our souls and remaining ignorant of duty reveal profound foolishness.

**Drunkenness** rarely comes alone.

It leads people into further sins and provokes God's displeasure.

The person ruled by drink becomes a terrible example to family and community and rushes toward ruin.

When exhausted or sad, we should not try to lift our spirits through intoxicating drink, which can deepen the misery.

Instead, seek through **earnest prayer to be filled with the Holy Spirit**, and avoid grieving our gracious Comforter.

All God's people have reason to sing with joy.

Even when we are not singing aloud, we should remain **continually thankful**.

There is never a shortage of reasons for gratitude—**even during hardship**—because God's dealings have a gracious purpose for his people.

He guides believers away from sin and teaches them to **submit to one another** in the duties he has commanded, for his glory and their mutual good.

## Marriage reflects Christ's union with the church (verses 22–33)

Henry explains the wife's duty as **submitting to her husband in the Lord**, honoring and obeying him from a principle of love.

He describes the husband's duty as **loving his wife**.

The example for that love is **Christ's love for the church**: genuine, pure and constant despite her shortcomings.

Christ **gave himself for the church** so that he might sanctify her in this life and glorify her in the world to come.

He grants believers a principle of holiness and delivers them from **sin's guilt, pollution and rule** through the work of the Holy Spirit.

**Baptismal water is the outward sign** of that inward work.

The church and its members will not be entirely without fault until the day of heavenly glory.

But **only those being sanctified now will be glorified then**.

Paul's reference to Adam's words has a **literal meaning about marriage**.

It also has an additional spiritual meaning, as marriage provides **a picture of the union between Christ and his church**.

Human husbands and wives will both have weaknesses and failures in the present life.

Those imperfections do not cancel their married relationship.

All the responsibilities of marriage can be summarized by **unity and love**.

As we worship and rejoice in **Christ's gracious, self-giving love**, let husbands and wives learn their corresponding duties toward one another.

Such faithfulness can prevent many evils and spare families a great deal of pain.
