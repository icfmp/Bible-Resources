# 2 Kings 18 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Hezekiah's good reign and removal of idolatry (verses 1–8)

Hezekiah was a true son of David.

Other kings had done what was right, but had not followed David's example as closely.

We must not assume that when times and people become bad, matters must inevitably grow worse.

After many evil kings, God raised up someone who resembled David himself.

The **bronze serpent** had been carefully preserved as a memorial of God's kindness to Israel in the wilderness.

Yet burning incense to it was foolish and wicked.

Attempts to help worship by means not authorized in Scripture interrupt the exercise of faith and lead toward superstition and other serious evils.

Fallen human nature corrupts such practices.

True faith has no need of these artificial aids.

God's word, considered daily and accompanied by prayer, provides all the outward assistance needed.

## Sennacherib attacks Judah (verses 9–16)

Sennacherib's invasion brought great suffering upon Judah.

Through it, God tested Hezekiah's faith and corrected the people.

Hidden unbelief, hypocrisy and spiritual lukewarmness among many required correction.

For sincere believers, such trials purify faith and hope and lead to simple dependence upon God.

## Rabshakeh speaks against the Lord (verses 17–37)

Rabshakeh tried to persuade Judah that resistance was pointless.

He asked, **“What is this confidence you are trusting?”**

How good it would be if sinners applied a similar question to themselves and sought peace with God!

It is wise to submit to the Lord because resistance against him cannot succeed.

What basis for confidence do people have when they stubbornly oppose him?

Rabshakeh's speech was clever and carefully constructed.

Yet it was full of pride, malice, falsehood and blasphemy.

Hezekiah's officers remained silent.

There is a time to remain silent as well as a time to speak.

Some people are so determined to mock what is holy or reject reason that presenting religious truths to them is like **throwing pearls before pigs**.

Their silence made Rabshakeh even prouder and more confident.

Sometimes the wisest response is to let people continue their insults rather than argue.

A firm expression of abhorrence can be sufficient witness against such behavior.

We must leave the whole matter with the Lord, who holds every heart in his hands, entrusting ourselves to him with humility, hope and earnest prayer.
