# Numbers 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The ashes of a heifer. | 1–10 |
| Used to purify the unclean. | 11–22 |

## The ashes of the red heifer (verses 1–10)

The heifer was to be burned completely.

Henry sees this as a picture of Jesus Christ enduring great suffering in body and soul when he offered himself as a sacrifice.

By bearing God's just anger against human sin, Christ made atonement.

The ashes were preserved for ceremonial purification.

They could not themselves wash away moral guilt.

But they pointed toward the true cleansing of sin accomplished by our Lord's death.

The benefits of Christ's blood are set before us through God's word and the sacraments.

They are like a continuing fountain of saving merit to which believers may come by faith, again and again, for the cleansing of their consciences.

## Cleansing people made unclean through death (verses 11–22)

Why did touching a dead body produce ceremonial uncleanness?

Because death is the wages of sin.

Sin brought death into the world, and death continues to reign through sin's power.

The Mosaic law could not conquer death or abolish it.

But the gospel reveals life and immortality and opens the way to a better hope.

The ashes of the heifer represented Christ's saving merit.

The flowing water represented the power and grace of the Holy Spirit, whom Scripture compares to rivers of living water.

The Spirit applies Christ's righteousness to us, bringing genuine cleansing.

People who expect to benefit from Christ's righteousness while refusing the Spirit's sanctifying influence are deceiving themselves.

The ashes did not accomplish their purpose without water.

Likewise, Christ's saving work is not to be separated from the Spirit's transforming work.

Without their connection to Christ's sacrifice, these regulations would be difficult to understand.

Reading them alongside the New Testament makes their spiritual meaning clear.

They reveal the fallen human condition and the defiling power of sin.

They also warn us to keep away from evil influences and corrupting companionship.
