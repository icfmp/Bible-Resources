## Jesus speaks about divorce (verses 1–12)

Wherever Jesus went, people gathered to hear his teaching.

**Preaching was his constant practice**.

Jesus explained that Moses permitted divorce because of **human hardness of heart**.

That is hardly a reason to use the permission casually.

God himself joined man and woman together and fitted them to be **companions, comforts and helpers** to one another.

The bond God established should not be dissolved lightly.

Anyone eager to send away a spouse should consider what it would be like if **God dealt with that person in the same way**.

## Jesus receives children (verses 13–16)

Parents and caregivers brought small children to Jesus so that he would touch and bless them.

The text gives no indication they were ill or needed bodily healing.

They may not even have been old enough to understand formal instruction.

Yet those responsible for them believed **Christ's blessing would benefit their souls**, so they brought them.

Jesus commanded that the children be allowed to come and that no one hinder them.

As soon as children can understand the Savior's words, they should be taught to come to him.

Adults must likewise receive God's kingdom **as children receive care and instruction from parents, caregivers and teachers**.

## Jesus and the wealthy young man (verses 17–22)

The young ruler was earnest and wanted to know what he should do **now** to obtain eternal happiness.

Most people ask only for this life's pleasures and advantages (**Psalm 4:6**).

He sought **good conduct here** so he could obtain the highest good in the coming life.

Jesus helped his understanding and directed his conduct.

But the encounter ended in sorrow.

The man wanted to know what more he needed to do for eternal life.

Jesus tested whether he truly valued that life enough to bear a **present cross for a future crown**.

The young man wanted to follow Christ without giving up the wealth he loved.

He left in grief because he could not hold on to both ultimate treasures.

**No one can serve both God and wealth** (**Matthew 6:24**).

## Wealth as an obstacle (verses 23–31)

Jesus explained the great danger facing those who possess abundant worldly goods.

People determined to acquire more wealth rarely appreciate **Christ and his grace** properly.

He also taught about the great salvation belonging to those with few possessions who nevertheless give up what they have for him.

One of a godly person's hardest trials is surrendering attachment even to **dear friends and relatives** when love for Jesus requires it.

Even people who gain immeasurably through Christ must still expect to suffer for him until reaching heaven.

Let those with little learn contentment.

Let those with much **watch against love of wealth**.

We should ask for grace to surrender everything if Christ requires it and to use faithfully everything we are permitted to keep.

## Jesus foretells suffering and teaches humble service (verses 32–45)

Christ's persistent determination to finish the saving work assigned to him astonished his disciples and should continue to astonish us.

Worldly honor is attractive, and even Jesus' own disciples were repeatedly dazzled by it.

We need wisdom and grace **to know how to suffer with him**.

We may trust him to determine how much heavenly glory he will give us.

Jesus explained that worldly rulers commonly **abuse their authority**.

If he granted every request we make, our motives would soon reveal desire for fame, rank and power, along with unwillingness to drink from his cup or share his baptism of suffering.

Sometimes **receiving exactly what we ask** would ruin us.

But Christ loves his people and gives them **only what is good for them**.

## Bartimaeus receives sight (verses 46–52)

Bartimaeus had heard about Christ's miracles.

When he learned that Jesus was nearby, he hoped to regain his sight.

Those seeking Christ's healing should recognize him as **the promised Messiah**.

His gracious invitation encourages us to believe that those who come to him will receive the help they truly need.

We must put aside **the garment of self-sufficiency**.

Like someone casting off a long garment that might hinder running, we should lay aside every weight and the sin that clings most closely to us (**Hebrews 12:1**).

Bartimaeus asked that his eyes might be opened.

The ability to work and earn one's bread is a real gift.

People given healthy bodies and senses should not make themselves practically disabled through foolishness or idleness.

Jesus restored his sight.

His words pointed to **Bartimaeus's faith in the Son of David, in Christ's pity and power**—not merely to repeated words or cries.

Jesus himself moved that faith into action.

When gospel preaching or Christian Scripture comes within reach, **Christ is passing by**.

That is our opportunity to come to him.

But spiritual healing is not the end of our duty.

When Christ opens our eyes, we should **continue following him**, learning from his teaching and honoring him in our lives.

People with spiritual sight recognize such beauty in Christ that they desire to follow eagerly.
