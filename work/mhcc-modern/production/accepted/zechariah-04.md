## The lampstand and the two olive trees (verses 1–7)

The prophet's spirit wanted to listen, but his body was weak.

We should ask God to awaken us whenever he speaks and then **rouse ourselves to pay attention**.

The church is like a **golden lampstand** set in this dark world to give light through God's revelation.

Two olive trees stood beside it, supplying a continuous stream of oil.

The Lord brings his gracious purposes for his church to completion **without depending upon human skill or labor**.

Sometimes he employs human instruments, but he does not need them.

The oil pictured the abundance of grace by which he brings light and holiness to the church's ministers and members.

No human power can produce that grace or stop it from being given.

The temple's construction would therefore reach a happy conclusion.

All the difficulties were pictured as **a great mountain**.

Through faith, they would be brought down and made level.

**Christ is our Zerubbabel**.

He has encountered mountains of obstacles, but nothing is too difficult for him.

What began through God's grace can safely be entrusted to that same grace.

He will not abandon the work of his own hands.

## Do not despise small beginnings (verses 8–10)

The precise fulfillment of Scripture's prophecies testifies to their divine origin.

God often chooses people who appear weak or unlikely to accomplish great things.

Therefore **do not despise the dawning light**.

It will grow brighter until the day reaches its fullness.

Those who feared the temple could never be completed would rejoice when they saw **Zerubbabel with the plumb line**, directing and supervising its completion.

The all-wise and almighty Providence that rules the earth cares particularly for the church.

Everyone carrying responsibility for the work must look to the Lord, remain aware of his Providence, depend upon his guidance and submit to the way he orders events.

Fix your faith upon **Christ**.

See him carrying out his work according to his glorious plan and each day bringing his spiritual building closer to completion.

## The oil supplied through the Lord's anointed (verses 11–14)

Zechariah wanted to understand the two olive trees.

Henry identifies them with **Zerubbabel and Joshua**, the prince and the priest.

Both had received gifts and grace from the Holy Spirit.

They lived at the same time and served together in God's work.

They foreshadowed Christ's offices of **King and Priest**.

Those two offices are united in the one person of Jesus Christ, **God and man**, from whose fullness all grace is received and shared.

The two leaders helped build God's temple.

Christ builds God's church spiritually.

He is not only the Messiah, the Anointed One.

He is also **the Good Olive Tree** of his church, supplying its needs from his inexhaustible fullness.

The Holy Spirit is the anointing believers receive.

Through the Spirit, all the golden oil of grace flows from Christ to believers and keeps their lamps burning.

Let us seek, through the Savior's intercession and generosity, the supplies of grace that have always been sufficient for his saints in every trial and task.

Let us wait on him through his appointed ordinances, praying to be **sanctified completely—in body, soul and spirit**.
