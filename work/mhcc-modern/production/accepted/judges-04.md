# Judges 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Israel again revolts, and is oppressed by Jabin. | 1–3 |
| Deborah concerts their deliverance with Barak. | 4–9 |
| Sisera defeated. | 10–16 |
| Sisera put to death by Jael. | 17–24 |

## Israel falls under Jabin's oppression (verses 1–3)

The land had enjoyed peace for **eighty years**.

That long period should have strengthened Israel's faith.

Instead, prosperity made the people feel secure and indulge their sinful desires.

In that way, the prosperity of fools becomes their destruction.

Jabin and his general, Sisera, oppressed Israel severely.

This enemy was nearer than the former oppressors.

Distress finally made the Israelites cry out to the Lord, because they could see no other possibility of relief.

People who ignore God while prosperous may find themselves compelled to seek him when trouble comes.

## Deborah calls Barak to deliver Israel (verses 4–9)

Deborah was a **prophetess**.

The Holy Spirit had instructed her in divine knowledge.

She judged Israel as God's messenger, correcting abuses and setting right the people's grievances.

At the Lord's direction, she told Barak to gather an army and confront Jabin's forces.

Barak insisted that Deborah accompany him.

She agreed.

She would not send him into danger while refusing to go there herself.

Those who call others to do their duty in God's name should be prepared to assist them.

Barak cared more about assurance of mind and success in his calling than about receiving personal honor.

## Barak defeats Sisera (verses 10–16)

Sisera placed his confidence chiefly in his chariots.

But if we have reason to believe that God goes before us, we can proceed cheerfully and courageously.

Do not be dismayed by difficulties in resisting Satan, serving God or suffering for him.

Has the Lord not gone ahead of you?

Then follow him faithfully.

Barak went down from the mountain to the plain, although Sisera's iron chariots would have an advantage there.

Barak left the favorable ground because he was trusting in God's power.

Salvation belongs to the Lord alone (Jeremiah 3:23).

His trust was not disappointed.

When God goes before us in spiritual conflict, we must still exert ourselves.

And when he graciously grants success over the enemies of our souls, we should use that victory as encouragement to remain watchful and resolute.

## Sisera is killed by Jael (verses 17–24)

Sisera's chariots had been his pride and confidence.

Such confidence in created things is doomed to disappointment.

What we lean upon may prove like a broken reed that not only gives way but wounds us.

An idol can soon become a burden (Isaiah 46:1).

God can make us weary of what we once desperately wanted.

Henry thinks Jael **probably intended to show kindness** to Sisera at first.

He believes that afterward a special divine impulse led her to recognize Sisera as a determined enemy of the Lord and his people and to kill him.

This is Henry's interpretation of the exceptional historical event, not a model for private violence.

We must break off associations with God's enemies if we want the Lord to be our God and his people our people.

Sisera intended to destroy Israel with many iron chariots.

Yet he himself was killed with a single iron tent peg.

Thus God uses weak things to confound the strong.

Israel would have avoided much harm if the people had earlier removed the Canaanites as God had commanded and enabled them.

Yet it is better to become wise late, through painful experience, than never to learn wisdom at all.
