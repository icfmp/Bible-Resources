# Romans 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Objections answered. | 1–8 |
| All mankind are sinners. | 9–18 |
| Both Jews and Gentiles cannot be justified by their own deeds. | 19, 20 |
| It is owing to the free grace of God, through faith in the righteousness of Christ, yet the law is not done away. | 21–31 |

## Paul answers objections (verses 1–8)

The Mosaic law could not save people **while they remained in their sins** or rescue them from sin by its own power.

Yet Israel's privileges gave the Jewish people many means through which salvation could be learned and received.

Their appointed worship, education in knowledge of the true God and the blessings given to Abraham's descendants were all means of grace.

Undoubtedly God used them to convert many people.

Most importantly, **the Scriptures had been entrusted to Israel**.

Possessing God's word and his appointed worship is a people's chief blessing.

God's saving promises belong to **those who believe**.

Therefore, the unbelief of some—or even many—who outwardly profess faith cannot make his faithfulness fail.

He will fulfill his promises toward his people and carry out his judgments upon unbelief.

The fact that God will judge the world should silence every accusation that he is unjust.

The unbelief and wickedness of some Jews demonstrate humanity's need for **God's righteousness through faith**, and also God's justice in punishing sin.

The notion that we should **do evil so that good may result** is more common in sinners' hearts than openly on their lips.

Few people admit such a principle plainly.

But true believers know that **duty belongs to us, while outcomes belong to God**.

We must not commit one sin or tell even **one lie** because we hope—or even feel certain—that God may use it to bring glory to himself.

Anyone who thinks and acts that way deserves condemnation.

## All humanity is under sin (verses 9–18)

Everyone is under sin in **two respects**.

We are under its **guilt**, like people carrying an unbearable burden.

We are also under its **power**, enslaved to practice evil.

Old Testament passages describe the fallen, corrupt condition of all people until grace restrains or changes them.

Despite the advantages some people enjoy, the same descriptions apply to many who call themselves Christians.

Their beliefs and actions show that **they do not fear God**.

Where there is no fear of God, we cannot expect true goodness.

## Jews and Gentiles cannot be justified by law (verses 19 and 20)

It is useless to seek justification through obeying the law.

Everyone must admit guilt.

**“Guilty before God”** is a dreadful verdict.

No one can be justified by a law that condemns them for breaking it.

The corruption of our fallen nature makes justification through our own actions forever impossible.

## God provides righteousness through Christ (verses 21–26)

Must sinful humanity remain forever under divine judgment?

Is the wound incurable?

**No. Blessed be God, another way has been opened.**

It is **the righteousness of God**—righteousness that he has appointed, provided and accepted.

It is received through **faith in Jesus Christ**, the anointed Savior.

Justifying faith receives Christ in **all three of his anointed offices: Prophet, Priest and King**.

It trusts him, accepts him and clings to him.

Jewish and Gentile believers are equally welcome to God through Christ.

**There is no difference**.

Christ's righteousness is given to all who believe.

It is not merely offered from a distance but **placed upon them like a crown or robe**.

This comes through entirely free grace and mercy.

Nothing in us deserves such gifts.

It comes **freely to us**, but **Christ purchased it and paid the price**.

Faith therefore looks particularly to **Christ's blood**, through which he made atonement.

In this salvation God declares **his righteousness**.

His hatred of sin is evident because nothing less than Christ's blood could satisfy for it.

And God's justice does not require that a debt be paid a second time once **the Surety has paid it** and God has accepted that payment as full satisfaction.

## Faith excludes boasting without abolishing God's law (verses 27–31)

God has arranged justification and salvation so that **human boasting is excluded from beginning to end**.

If our own works saved us, people would have reason to boast.

Justification by faith excludes such boasting completely.

But believers are not left lawless.

**Genuine faith is active and produces obedience**.

Faith justifies, however, **not by being a meritorious act or a good work**, but by **uniting the sinner to Jesus Christ**.

Through that relationship the believer receives pardon and justification **for the Savior's sake**.

A person refusing that union remains under condemnation.

The law still serves a purpose.

It convicts us concerning **past sins** and directs us concerning **future obedience**.

We cannot be saved by law as a covenant that promises life on condition of perfect obedience.

Yet we acknowledge and obey its rightful demands **as a rule of life in the Mediator's hand**.
