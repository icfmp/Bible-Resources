# 2 Samuel 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Ammon's violence to his sister. | 1–20 |
| Absalom murders his brother Ammon. | 21–29 |
| David's grief, Absalom flees to Geshur. | 30–39 |

## Amnon assaults his sister (verses 1–20)

From this point onward, David faced one trouble after another.

Adultery and murder had been his own sins.

Now similar sins among his children began the punishment God had announced.

David had been **too indulgent toward his children**.

He could trace their wrongdoing back to failures in his own conduct.

That recognition must have made the pain of their sins still more difficult to bear.

No one should expect kindness from someone willing to attempt sexual seduction or abuse.

**It is better to suffer the greatest wrong than to commit even the smallest sin.**

## Absalom murders Amnon (verses 21–29)

Several things made Absalom's offense especially serious.

He arranged for Amnon to be killed when Amnon was least prepared to die.

He also led his servants into sharing the guilt of murder.

Servants have been taught badly when they obey a wicked master **against God's commands**.

Children whom parents indulge without correction often become a heavy sorrow to their godly parents.

Such parents neglect their duty to God because of misguided affection for their children.

## David mourns; Absalom escapes (verses 30–39)

**Jonadab** was guilty in connection with both Amnon's sin and his death.

People who advise us to do wrong turn out to be false friends.

After a while David longed to see Absalom again.

Instead of recoiling from him as a murderer, he began desiring his return.

This was a weakness in David.

Henry adds an important qualification.

God must have seen something in David's heart that distinguished him from **Eli**.

Otherwise we might conclude that David too had honored his sons above God.
