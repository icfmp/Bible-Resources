# Leviticus 3 — Modern English

## The fellowship offering from the herd (verses 1–5)

Peace offerings acknowledged God as the giver of everything good.

The offering was divided between the altar, the priest and the person who brought it.

It was called a **peace offering** because God and his people seemed to share a feast together, as a sign of friendship.

These offerings could be presented as **requests for mercy**.

When someone was asking God for a blessing, that person might add a peace offering to the prayer.

Christ is our Peace and our Peace Offering.

Only through him can we receive a peaceful answer to our prayers.

But peace offerings could also be given as **thanksgiving** for mercy already received.

We should continually offer God sacrifices of praise through Christ, our Peace.

Such praise pleases the Lord more than the sacrifice of an ox or bull.

## The fellowship offering from the flock (verses 6–17)

The people were forbidden to eat either the fat or the blood of these sacrifices.

The fat meant the fat of the internal organs.

Like the blood, it belonged to God as his portion of each sacrifice.

God would not allow the blood that made atonement to be treated as an ordinary thing (Hebrews 10:29).

Nor will he allow us, even while enjoying the benefits of the atonement, to claim any share of the honor for having accomplished it.

The command taught Israel to distinguish between ordinary things and holy things.

It also kept the people separate from idol worshipers.

The blood poured out and the fat burned in solemn sacrifice were meant to impress upon them the sense that some great spiritual mystery was being shown.

Christ, the Prince of Peace, made peace through the blood of his cross.

Through him, believers are reconciled to God.

Having God's peace in their hearts, they also become eager to pursue peace with all people.

May the Lord multiply grace, mercy and peace to everyone who desires to bear the name of Christian.
