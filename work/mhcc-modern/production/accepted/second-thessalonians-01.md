# 2 Thessalonians 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle blesses God for the growing state of the love and patience of the Thessalonians. | 1–4 |
| And encourages them to persevere under all their sufferings for Christ, considering his coming at the great day of account. | 5–12 |

## Faith, love and patience grow under affliction (verses 1–4)

Where saving grace is genuine, it will also **grow**.

The path of the righteous is like **light growing brighter and brighter** until the perfect day.

When we observe this growth, **all the glory belongs to God**.

As faith becomes stronger, love should also increase, because **faith works through love**.

The Thessalonians' patience and faith deserved to be held up as an example to other Christians.

Their sufferings were not proof that God's grace had failed.

Rather, trials permitted by Providence and persecution inflicted by people **increased the exercise of their faith and patience**, enabling them to remain firm through trouble.

## Christ's return brings justice, rest and glory (verses 5–10)

If our religion is worth anything, it is worth everything.

People who cannot bring themselves to suffer for it either lack genuine religion or have not learned how precious it is.

Yet **suffering does not earn heaven**, any more than good works earn it.

Patient endurance can **prepare us to enjoy the promised joy**, but it does not purchase that joy.

One of the clearest signs of a person moving toward destruction is **a spirit of persecution and hostility toward God's people**.

The Lord will bring judgment upon those who trouble his servants.

There remains **a rest for God's people**, free from sin and sorrow.

God's righteousness guarantees that good and evil will receive their final judgment.

This assurance should terrify hardened sinners and sustain believers.

Faith that looks toward judgment can begin to understand some of Providence's difficult purposes, which otherwise appear confused and inexplicable to unbelievers.

Jesus will appear from heaven in **the glory and power of the heavenly world**.

His light will expose and his power will consume those who are like worthless chaff.

This appearing will be dreadful for people who refuse to know God—especially those who **reject his revealed gospel or profess to believe it while refusing obedience**.

The truth of the gospel is given to us so that we may **obey its commands**.

Sinners may be spared for a long time, but their final punishment will certainly come.

They have chosen to do sin's work and will receive sin's wages.

In this life God often judges sinners through created instruments.

At the final day, destruction will come **from the Almighty himself**.

How dreadful to meet the full power of his anger!

But that day will be joyful for **believers who receive and obey Christ's gospel**.

Jesus Christ will be admired and glorified **by his saints and within his saints**.

His grace and power will be displayed in everything he has purchased, accomplished and bestowed upon them.

How magnificent the glory he will give his people!

Yet how much more splendid is **the Giver of that glory**.

God's justice in judging wicked people will be acknowledged.

But **the glory of his mercy in saving believers** will be an even greater cause of wonder and worship.

Even angels will marvel.

The most ordinary believer will receive happiness **beyond the widest human imagination**, for Christ will be glorified in all those who believe, without exception.

## Pray for God to complete the work of faith (verses 11 and 12)

Faith in Christ's coming should make us **pray more**, both for ourselves and for other people.

Every good quality in a believer springs from **God's gracious good pleasure**.

That is why it is called **grace**.

God has many merciful purposes for his people.

Paul prayed that he would **complete their work of faith through divine power**, so that other acts of obedience would also grow.

God's power not only begins faith but **continues and sustains it**.

This is the purpose of the grace of **our God and Lord Jesus Christ**, revealed to and at work within believers.
