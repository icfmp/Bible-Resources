# Ezekiel 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A vision of the burning of the city. | 1–7 |
| The Divine glory departing from the temple. | 8–22 |

## Fire from among the cherubim (verses 1–7)

The fire taken from between the wheels, beneath the **cherubim**, recalls **Ezekiel 1:13**.

Henry understands it as a sign of **God's anger about to fall upon Jerusalem**.

The vision shows that divine judgment is not arbitrary.

The fire of God's wrath, which brings judgment upon a nation, is **just and holy**.

It also points toward the great day when **the earth and all its works will be consumed**.

## The glory of the Lord departs (verses 8–22)

Ezekiel saw how **divine Providence governs events in the world**.

When God withdraws from a people who have provoked his displeasure, **angels above and events on earth** serve to accomplish his purpose.

The **Spirit of life, the Spirit of God**, directs every creature in heaven and on earth so that each contributes to the divine plan.

God's departure from a rebellious people takes place **by degrees**.

Even when the Lord is ready to leave, **he would return if the people repented and prayed**.

May this warn sinners to **seek the Lord while he may be found** and call upon him while he is near.

And may it cause us all to **walk humbly and carefully with our God**.
