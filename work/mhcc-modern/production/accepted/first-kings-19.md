# 1 Kings 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

| God's answer to Elijah. | 14–18 |

## Elijah flees into the wilderness (verses 1–8)

Jezebel sent Elijah a threatening message.

What should have convinced and humbled her instead made her sinful heart harder and angrier against God.

Even great faith does not remain equally strong at all times.

Elijah might still have served Israel usefully, and he had every reason to trust God's protection while doing God's work.

Yet he fled.

His desire to die was not the calm longing of grace expressed by Paul when he wished to depart and be with Christ.

God allowed Elijah to experience his own weakness.

This taught him that whenever he had acted bravely and powerfully, his strength had come **from the Lord**, not from himself.

By nature he was no better than his ancestors.

God knows what services and trials he intends for us, even when we do not.

He will ensure that his grace is sufficient for what he calls us to face.

## God appears to Elijah (verses 9–13)

God asked, **“What are you doing here, Elijah?”**

This was a rebuke.

We should regularly ask ourselves whether we are where duty calls us.

Am I in the place where God wants me, where my work lies and where I can be useful?

Elijah complained about Israel's stubbornness in sin.

He believed he was the only faithful person left.

Despairing of success has prevented many good undertakings.

Yet if Elijah had come to seek God, he would discover that God intended to meet him.

The strong wind, earthquake and fire did not move Elijah to cover his face.

The quiet voice did.

Hearts touched by grace are often more deeply moved by **God's tender mercies** than by terrifying displays of power.

The gentle voice of the One who speaks from the **cross and mercy seat** is especially powerful in taking hold of the heart.

## God answers Elijah's complaint (verses 14–18)

The Lord repeated his question.

Elijah once more expressed his discouragement.

Where should God's prophets take their complaints except to their Master?

The Lord answered.

He announced judgment on Ahab's wicked house and on Israel's sins.

He also showed that Elijah was **not alone**, contrary to what he had believed.

A helper would be raised up for him.

In this way God addressed every part of Elijah's complaint.

God's faithful people are often **hidden ones** (Psalm 83:3).

At times the visible church almost disappears.

It is like wheat buried in chaff or gold mixed with dross, until the day when everything will be sifted, refined and separated.

The Lord knows those who belong to him, even when we do not see them.

In heaven, we may not meet some people we expected to find there.

But we will meet others whom we never imagined would be there.

God's love is often far broader than human generosity and charity.

## Elisha is called (verses 19–21)

Elijah found Elisha as God had directed.

He found him **not in a school of prophets**, or occupied with reading, praying or sacrifices, but **plowing a field**.

Idleness honors no one, and farming is no disgrace to anyone.

An honest earthly occupation does not prevent a person from receiving a heavenly calling.

The Holy Spirit moved Elisha's heart, and he became willing to leave everything to follow Elijah.

When Christ exercises his saving power, his people become willing.

No one would come to him unless drawn in this way.

It was a discouraging time for anyone to begin serving as a prophet.

Someone consulting only human advantage would hardly have wanted Elijah's mantle.

Yet Elisha left everything cheerfully to accompany him.

Likewise, when Jesus called people to follow him, they willingly left close friends and profitable work and took up difficult duties out of love for his name.

May the power of his grace work deeply in us.

May we submit to him without reservation and make our calling and election sure.
