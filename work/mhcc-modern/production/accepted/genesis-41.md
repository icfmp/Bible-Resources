## Pharaoh's dreams (verses 1–8)

Pharaoh's dreams became the means by which Joseph was released from prison.

Henry says that, since God no longer speaks to us in this way, we need not pay much attention to our dreams or be eager to tell them. Recounting foolish dreams can produce little more than foolish conversation.

But these particular dreams were clearly sent by God: Pharaoh awoke deeply troubled.

## Joseph interprets Pharaoh's dreams (verses 9–32)

God's time to deliver his people is always the most suitable time.

If the chief cupbearer had arranged Joseph's release earlier, Joseph would probably have returned to the land of the Hebrews.

Then he would not have received such blessing himself, or become such a blessing to his family.

When Joseph was brought before Pharaoh, he gave God the honor.

Pharaoh had dreamed that he was standing beside the Nile and saw both fat and thin cows emerging from it.

Henry observes that Egypt's plenty depends on the Nile's overflow rather than on rainfall.

See how many different ways Providence distributes its gifts! Yet our dependence remains on the same First Cause, who makes every creature—rain or river—what it is to us.

See how changeable the comforts of this life are. We cannot be certain that tomorrow will resemble today, or next year this one.

We must learn both how to live with little and how to live with abundance.

Notice God's goodness in sending seven years of plenty before seven years of famine, so that provisions could be stored.

The earth produces more in some seasons and less in others. Yet when all is considered together, those who gather much have nothing left over, and those who gather little lack nothing (Exodus 16:18).

Notice, too, how quickly worldly pleasures and possessions pass away.

The enormous harvests of the good years were swallowed up by the famine years. What seemed so plentiful was barely sufficient to keep the people alive.

But there is bread that lasts to eternal life. That is the bread worth working for.

Those who make earthly things their highest good will find little satisfaction in remembering that they once enjoyed them.

## Joseph advises Pharaoh and is raised to power (verses 33–45)

Joseph gave Pharaoh wise counsel. A clear warning should be followed by good advice.

God has warned us in his word of a coming day of trial, when we will need all the grace we can receive.

We should therefore make provision now.

Pharaoh spoke honorably of Joseph, recognizing him as a man in whom God's Spirit was present. Such people deserve to be valued.

Pharaoh gave Joseph the marks of high rank and a name expressing the value he placed on him: *Zaphnath-paaneah*, which Henry explains as “revealer of secrets.”

Some have interpreted the name instead as “savior of the world.” Henry presents that as another proposed translation, not as the only possible meaning.

Joseph's advancement encourages everyone to trust God.

But all the brightest glory, even the glory of heaven, belongs to Christ.

God has entrusted the highest office to his hands and given him all authority in heaven and on earth.

## Joseph prepares for the famine (verses 46–57)

In naming his sons *Manasseh* and *Ephraim*, Joseph acknowledged God's providence.

First, God made him forget his suffering. Second, God made him fruitful in the land where he had been afflicted.

The seven years of plenty came—and then they ended.

We should remember that both our times of prosperity and our opportunities to do good will come to an end.

We must not become complacent when life goes well or lazy about using our opportunities.

The good years will end. Whatever your hand finds to do, do it. Gather while there is time to gather.

Then the shortage came, and famine spread beyond Egypt into other lands.

Joseph had been diligent in storing food during the years of plenty. When famine arrived, he was prudent and careful in distributing it.

He was occupied with important and useful work.

Yet while he was doing all this, his father Jacob was saying, “Joseph is no more!”

How much trouble would vanish if only we knew the whole truth.

These events should turn our thoughts to Jesus.

Throughout the world there is a famine of the bread of life.

Go to Jesus and do whatever he tells you. Listen to his voice and come to him.

He will open his treasures and satisfy hungry souls in every age and nation, without money and without price.

But those who reject this provision must starve; his enemies will be destroyed.
