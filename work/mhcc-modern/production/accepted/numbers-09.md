# Numbers 9 — Modern English

## The Passover and those unable to attend (verses 1–14)

God gave special instructions for observing this Passover.

So far as the record shows, Israel did not celebrate another Passover until arriving in Canaan (Joshua 5:10).

Henry sees this as early evidence that the ceremonial arrangements were not meant to last forever: some were allowed to fall into disuse for many years soon after they were established.

The Lord's Supper, by contrast, was not set aside during the church's earliest years, even though Christians then faced greater persecution and trouble than Israel had experienced in the wilderness.

Indeed, Henry says it was celebrated more frequently during the times of persecution than afterward.

Israel could hardly forget the Exodus while still living in the wilderness.

There was greater risk of forgetting it after settling in Canaan.

These verses also explain what should happen when people were ceremonially unclean at Passover.

Those whose hearts and consciences are polluted by sin cannot truly enjoy communion with God or the Christian Passover until they are cleansed through repentance and faith.

Notice how troubled the Israelites were when they were prevented from taking part in the Lord's offering.

We, too, should feel real sorrow when circumstances keep us from Sabbath worship or the Lord's Supper.

Moses did not decide the difficult case according to his own preference or feelings.

He waited for God's instruction.

Ministers must take their guidance from God's word.

When a case remains difficult, believers should take time to bring it before God in humble, trusting prayer, seeking the Spirit's direction.

God then gave a rule for this case and similar ones.

People who are unwillingly prevented from joining in God's ordinances may still expect his grace and favor amid their difficulties.

But people who deliberately refuse to participate should not presume upon his kindness.

God will not be mocked.

## Israel moves when the cloud moves (verses 15–23)

The cloud was the visible sign of God's presence among Israel.

It taught the people that he remained near them day and night.

As long as the cloud rested above the tabernacle, they stayed in the same place.

Waiting for God's appointed time is never wasted time.

When the cloud rose, they traveled, even if the camp had been comfortable.

We do not know when we will leave the earthly tent of our bodies.

We should therefore remain prepared to move whenever the Lord calls us.

It is safe and good to go where God leads us and rest where he commands us to rest.

The cloud pictured the Holy Spirit's guidance.

We should not now expect the same visible signs of divine presence.

Yet God promises to direct his spiritual Israel by his counsel (Psalm 73:24), to guide them even to death (Psalm 48:14), and to lead all his children by his Spirit (Romans 8:14).

He makes straight the paths of people who acknowledge him in all their ways (Proverbs 3:6).

At every change of circumstance we should be willing to move or remain according to his command.

Our prayer should be, “Father, your will be done. Direct me and everything entrusted to me as you please. Only let me remain yours and follow my duty.”

Applying general biblical instructions to specific situations requires wise counsel and earnest prayer.

We must not confuse our own desires with divine guidance.

When something is obviously wrong—or even doubtful, and yet we strongly desire to do it—what someone calls “the cloud moving” may really be a temptation permitted by Satan.

People can imagine they are following God while actually following stubborn impulses.

The record of God's mercy and the truth of his word lead us reliably through Christ toward eternal peace.

Follow the pillar of cloud and fire: take the **Bible** to heart and humbly receive the word that can save your soul.
