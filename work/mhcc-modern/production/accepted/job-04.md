# Job 4 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Eliphaz rebukes Job (verses 1–6)

Satan was attempting to prove Job a hypocrite by making him suffer.

Job's friends reached a similar conclusion **because Job was suffering and had shown impatience**.

We must keep this distinction in mind if we are to understand their conversation.

Eliphaz spoke with some sympathy for Job, but accused him of being weak and fainthearted.

People often make very little allowance for someone who has taught others.

Even godly friends may call something **a slight touch** when the sufferer feels it as **a deep wound**.

We should help suffering people stop dwelling exclusively on their troubles and turn their thoughts to the merciful God present in them.

What better way to do that than to look at **Jesus Christ**, whose incomparable sorrows teach God's children to put their own sufferings in perspective?

## Eliphaz argues that judgment falls upon the wicked (verses 7–11)

Eliphaz made two arguments.

**First**, he claimed that upright people were never ruined in this way.

But the same outward events happen to the righteous and the wicked in life and death (**Ecclesiastes 9:2**).

The great and lasting distinction appears **after death**.

Some of our worst mistakes arise from drawing false conclusions out of truths that are otherwise undeniable.

**Second**, Eliphaz said wicked people frequently experience sudden ruin.

He appealed to what he had observed.

We, too, can see many such cases.

The general observation, however, did **not establish that Job was wicked**.

## Eliphaz recounts a vision (verses 12–21)

Eliphaz described a frightening vision.

Quiet reflection on our hearts can be a fitting occasion for the Holy Spirit to speak to us (**Psalm 4:4**).

Since humanity fell into sin, communication from heaven has often filled human beings with terror because of the guilt of their consciences.

How could sinful people claim to be **more righteous or more pure than God**, their Creator, Lord and Owner?

Human pride is dreadful, and God's patience is astonishing.

Look at the human condition.

We live in **fragile houses of clay whose foundations are in dust**.

Some people stand upon a larger pile of dust than others, but all of us stand upon earth that will soon receive our bodies.

Some lives end suddenly.

Others are consumed slowly by illness, like clothing eaten by a moth.

No one can resist death through personal power.

Beauty, strength, learning, wealth and influence cannot keep death away.

These advantages die with us or pass to others.

How can weak, sinful, dying creatures accuse God of injustice or claim to be purer than their Maker?

Instead of quarreling with their suffering, they should be astonished that divine judgment has not already overtaken them completely.

Can people cleanse themselves of guilt without their Creator?

God can justify sinful humans only through **the righteousness and gracious help of the promised Redeemer**.

Even angels who once served before God's throne suffered deserved judgment when they sinned.

Though people who live without God may seem unpunished for a time, their judgment is as certain as that of fallen angels.

Yet careless sinners notice so little that they do not prepare for death or consider their final end.
