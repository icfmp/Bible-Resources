# Isaiah 45 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The deliverance of the Jews by Cyrus. | 1–4 |
| God calls for obedience to his almighty power. | 5–10 |
| The settlement of his people. | 11–19 |
| The conversion of the Gentiles. | 20–25 |

## Cyrus is appointed to deliver Israel (verses 1–4)

Cyrus is called **God's anointed**, because God's counsel had appointed and equipped him for an important service.

Henry recounts how, on the night Cyrus entered Babylon, the gates leading from the city to the river were left open and his army advanced through the emptied river channel.

The Lord went before Cyrus, opening the cities he besieged.

He also gave him treasures concealed in secret places.

The true God was **unknown to Cyrus**, yet God had known him in advance and had **called him by name**.

The precise fulfillment of these words should have taught Cyrus that Jehovah was the only true God and that he had prospered **for Israel's sake**.

Through all the changes of states and kingdoms, the Lord works for the good of his church.

## The sovereign Lord cannot be challenged (verses 5–10)

There is no God besides **Jehovah**.

Nothing takes place outside his sovereign rule.

He creates peace, here referring to every kind of good, and he brings **evil in the sense of punishment or calamity, not moral evil or sin**.

God is the source of everything true, holy, good and happy.

Error, moral evil and misery came into the world through **the willful apostasy of his creatures**, under his permission.

Yet he restrains and overrules those evils for his righteous purposes.

This teaching comforted those longing earnestly for Israel's deliverance while waiting patiently for it.

But it chiefly points, in Henry's explanation, to **the redemption of sinners through God's Son** and to the **Spirit's outpouring** that makes the gospel fruitful.

We must not expect salvation without righteousness: the Lord has brought both together.

Oppressors must not resist God's purposes for his people.

And oppressed believers should not murmur as though God treated them unkindly.

Human beings are like **earthen pots and broken pieces of pottery**, made more broken still through conflict with one another.

It is as foolish for us to quarrel with God as for clay to accuse the potter.

Turn God's promises into prayers.

Ask him to cause salvation and righteousness to abound among us.

Rest assured that **the Judge of all the earth will do right**.

## God's people will be established (verses 11–19)

Believers may ask God for what they need.

If a request is truly for their good, he will not withhold what is best.

Yet how often people call God to account, as though he owed them explanations for every part of his conduct!

Cyrus made provision for the returning Jews.

In a far greater way, God will provide for those **redeemed by Jesus Christ**.

The restoration would persuade many observers and bring some to faith.

Everyone who genuinely joins the Lord discovers that **his service is perfect freedom**.

Even when he is his people's Savior, God may sometimes seem to hide his face and permit them to experience his displeasure.

Let them wait for the Lord even then.

There is **a world without end**.

Our ultimate happiness or misery depends on our condition in that world.

The God we serve is the only true God.

Everything he has said is just, plain and sufficient.

He calls us to seek him, and he does not refuse **prayers offered in faith** or disappoint genuine faith in his promises.

He gives grace sufficient, comfort and deep satisfaction of soul.

## The nations are called to God's salvation (verses 20–25)

All nations are invited to approach **Jehovah**, because no other being can save.

He can deliver without anyone's help.

Without him, no one can save.

When the heart submits to **Jesus Christ**, the body willingly bends the knee and obeys him.

People from every nation will come to Christ for blessing.

Those who hate his cause will be brought to shame.

Every believer will rejoice in Christ as **Friend and Portion**.

Everyone must come to him.

May we come now to **the Lord our Righteousness**, obeying his commandments and trusting in his salvation.
