# Proverbs 17 — Modern English

## Verse 1 — Peace at home

Love and peace within a household are essential to the comfort and happiness of human life.

## Verse 2 — Wisdom is greater than birth

A wise servant can be more deserving of honor and family affection than a son who lives in wickedness.

## Verse 3 — Trials expose the heart

God tests our hearts through suffering, often revealing sins that still remain even within a believer.

## Verse 4 — The danger of flattery

People living in sin often welcome flatterers, **especially false teachers** who reinforce them in wrongdoing.

## Verse 5 — Do not mock the poor

Mocking poverty shows contempt for **God's providence and his commands**.

## Verse 6 — The value of faithful parents

It is an honor and blessing for grown children to continue enjoying the presence and guidance of wise, godly parents.

## Verse 7 — Good speech needs a good life

In Proverbs, a fool means a wicked person. Excellent words do not fit such a person, because **conduct contradicts speech**.

## Verse 8 — How gifts rule covetous hearts

People who make money their chief desire may do almost anything to acquire it. How much more should **the gifts of God** influence our hearts toward him!

## Verse 9 — Forgive and overlook offenses

Peace is preserved by giving the most charitable interpretation possible and not continually recalling every wrong spoken or done against us.

## Verse 10 — The wise receive correction

A gentle rebuke enters not only the thoughts but the heart of someone who is wise.

## Verse 11 — Rebellion invites judgment

Satan and his messengers are allowed to confront those who persist stubbornly in evil.

## Verse 12 — Avoid uncontrolled rage

Guard your own passions carefully, and avoid the company of people whose anger is violent and unrestrained.

## Verse 13 — Do not repay good with evil

Returning evil for good is **devilish conduct** and can bring a curse upon one's household.

## Verse 14 — Stop conflict before it begins

The earliest stage of a quarrel is especially dangerous. Resist it at the first sign; if possible, leave the dispute **before it even begins**.

## Verse 15 — Judge impartially

God is offended both when a guilty person is declared innocent and when someone innocent is condemned.

## Verse 16 — Do not neglect true wisdom

It is absurd for human beings to disregard **God's favor and their own true interests**.

## Verse 17 — Faithful friendship and Christ

Changes in wealth or circumstances should not weaken love for friends and relatives. Yet **only Christ deserves unreserved confidence**. In him this promise of faithful friendship finds its greatest fulfillment.

## Verse 18 — Human guarantees and the divine Surety

Never endanger your family by rash financial promises. But Christ's decision to become **Surety for humanity** was a glorious act of divine wisdom: **he was fully able to pay the debt**.

## Verse 19 — Avoid anger and vain display

Those seeking a peaceful conscience should avoid needless provocations to anger. Living above one's means in order to impress others is a road toward ruin.

## Verse 20 — Schemes and uncontrolled speech

Evil plans bring no real advantage. Many people have suffered severely because they could not restrain their tongues.

## Verse 21 — Sorrow from a foolish child

The wickedness and folly of a child cause profound grief to even wise and godly parents.

## Verse 22 — The mercy of cheerfulness

It is God's kindness that he permits us to rejoice, gives reasons for joy, and through his grace can make our hearts capable of enjoying it.

## Verse 23 — Bribes reveal guilt

The wicked can be willing to surrender even money they dearly love in order to escape the consequences of their crimes.

## Verse 24 — Keep wisdom in view

Prudent people continually keep **God's word** before them. Foolish people cannot settle their thoughts or persevere steadily in a good purpose.

## Verse 25 — Children can wound both parents

Wicked children despise **a father's rightful authority** and **a mother's tenderness**.

## Verse 26 — Do not punish duty

It is seriously wrong to blame or penalize someone for doing what duty requires.

## Verses 27 and 28 — Temperance in speech

Wise people show themselves through **calmness of spirit and control over the tongue**. When they speak, they choose words that serve a purpose. God alone sees every heart perfectly and cannot be misled by appearances.
