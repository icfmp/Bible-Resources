# James 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| How to apply to God under troubles, and how to behave in prosperous and in adverse circumstances. | 1–11 |
| To look upon all evil as proceeding from ourselves, and all good from God. | 12–18 |
| The duty of watching against a rash temper, and of receiving the word of God with meekness. | 19–21 |
| And of living according thereto. | 22–25 |
| The difference between vain pretences and real religion. | 26, 27 |

## Trials should produce patience, prayer and humility (verses 1–11)

Christianity teaches us to rejoice during trials. God sends such experiences in love, using them to strengthen the graces already at work within us and prepare us for the crown of life.

During a trial we must let **patience, not passion**, direct what we say and do. When patience has completed its work, it equips us for the Christian race and spiritual warfare.

We should not pray only for an affliction to be removed. More importantly, ask for **wisdom to use the trial rightly**: wisdom to govern our spirits and manage our responsibilities. When we come to God acknowledging our weakness and folly, we may confidently claim his promise to give wisdom to anyone who asks.

Those whose chief concern is their **spiritual and eternal welfare** become wiser through suffering. Their faith and devotion remain steady instead of rising and falling with outward circumstances. Instability may not always cost a person worldly respect, but it cannot please God.

Whatever our situation, we can rejoice in God. People of low standing may rejoice because faith makes them **rich heirs of God's kingdom**. The wealthy may rejoice when humbling trials teach them that worldly possessions fade and that happiness comes from God, not from things destined to perish.

## Evil comes from our sinful desires; good gifts come from God (verses 12–18)

Not every sufferer is blessed. The blessing belongs to those who persevere in the path of duty through trials. Their love for God will be fully rewarded in the world where love is perfected.

God's commands and providential trials reveal what rules a person's heart. Yet **nothing sinful in our thoughts or actions can be attributed to God**.

A refining fire may expose impurities without creating them. Likewise, divine testing reveals the corruption already present in us; God is **not its author**. Blaming our sinful nature, circumstances or lack of self-control upon God falsely makes him responsible for wrongdoing.

The origin of sinful temptation lies **within our own hearts**. Stop its earliest movements, or the evils that follow remain our responsibility.

God takes no pleasure in humanity's death and does not cause its sin.

He is **unchanging**. The sun appears different when clouds pass before it, although the sun itself has not changed. So the variations we experience arise from our condition, not from change in God's goodness.

Every good gift comes from him, especially **new birth through divine grace**. Through his renewing work, a Christian becomes so changed as to seem made anew. We should dedicate every capacity to his service, becoming **firstfruits among his creatures**.

## Listen humbly rather than speaking in anger (verses 19–21)

When suffering, do not accuse God. Open your heart and ears to receive what he teaches.

Those wishing to control their tongues must first govern their passions. **Anger is the worst contribution we can make to a dispute**.

Cast away wrongdoing as a person throws off filthy clothes. This includes corrupt thoughts, desires and affections, not merely visible conduct.

Receive God's word with **humility and teachability**. Welcome even correction of your faults, not only enduring it patiently but receiving it with thanks.

God's word intends to make us wise for salvation. People who attend to it merely for selfish or trivial advantages dishonor the gospel and harm their own souls.

## Be doers of the word rather than forgetful hearers (verses 22–25)

Even if we heard a sermon every day from an angel, **hearing without obedience** could never bring us to heaven.

Those who only hear deceive themselves. At the end, **self-deception** will prove the most dangerous deception of all.

God's word acts as a mirror. It exposes the corruption of our hearts and conduct.

**The law reveals the stains of sin; the gospel shows Christ's blood as the cleansing remedy.** But looking into that mirror helps nothing if we go away, forget the stains, and refuse the cleansing offered.

Study the word for direction and continue in what it teaches. Its promises bring present comfort and future reward.

Every part of revelation has a purpose: leading sinners to Christ for salvation and helping believers walk in freedom by **the Spirit of adoption**, obeying God's holy commands.

Notice the distinction: the blessing does **not** come **because of the merit of our deeds**, but is experienced **in faithful doing**. What counts is not merely talking but walking.

As believers continue in obedience, Christ becomes more precious to them and they become increasingly prepared for their inheritance in light.

## Genuine religion controls speech and practices love (verses 26 and 27)

People who work harder to **appear religious** than to **be religious** have an empty profession.

An uncontrolled tongue, eagerness to expose other people's faults or attempts to belittle their wisdom and godliness betray this emptiness. A slanderous tongue cannot be reconciled with a truly humble and gracious heart.

False religion reveals itself through impurity and lack of compassion. True religion teaches us to act continually **in God's presence**.

A pure life belongs together with **sincere love and charity**. Genuine religion is measured by how these qualities shape the heart and conduct.

What matters in Jesus Christ is **faith working through love**—faith that purifies the heart, puts sinful desires to death and obeys God's commandments.
