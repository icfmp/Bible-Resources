# Hosea 3 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The prophet enters into a new contract, representing the gracious manner in which God will again restore Israel under a new covenant.**

## God's gracious covenant represented (verses 1–3)

People often dislike true religion because they prefer **objects and forms of worship** that allow them to indulge their sinful desires instead of putting those desires to death.

How remarkable that **a holy God** should show goodwill toward people whose **sinful minds are hostile to him**!

Hosea's actions picture God's gracious dealings with humanity after it departed from him.

They illustrate **the covenant of grace** he is willing to make with his people:

**They will belong to him as his people, and he will be their God.**

They must acknowledge the consequences of their wrongdoing and **must not return to folly**.

One sign that affliction is producing good in us is that we are kept from **yielding to the temptations that come with suffering**.

## Israel's waiting and eventual restoration (verses 4–5)

Here the meaning of the prophetic picture is applied directly to Israel.

The people would remain for a long time **like a widow**, deprived of joys and honors.

In the end, however, they would be **received again**.

Those who seek the Lord so as to find him must come to **Christ** and willingly become his people.

We should fear not only **the Lord's greatness**, but also **his goodness**.

We should reverence not only **his majesty**, but also **his mercy**.

Henry notes that even Jewish writers apply this passage to **the promised Messiah**.

He understands it as predicting **a future conversion of Jewish people to Christ**, for which, in his reading, they have been preserved as a distinct people.

Fear of God may first arise from knowing his holiness and just judgment.

But experiencing **mercy and grace through Jesus Christ** leads the heart to reverence a loving and glorious Friend and Father and to fear offending him.
