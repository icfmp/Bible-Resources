# 1 Peter 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The consideration of Christ's sufferings is urged for purity and holiness. | 1–6 |
| And the approaching end of the Jewish state, as a reason for sobriety, watchfulness, and prayer. | 7–11 |
| Believers encouraged to rejoice and glory in reproaches and sufferings for Christ, and to commit their souls to the care of a faithful God. | 12–19 |

## Christ's sufferings call us to holiness (verses 1–6)

The strongest arguments against sin come from **Christ's sufferings**.

He died to destroy sin.

He willingly endured terrible suffering yet **never consented to the least sin**.

Temptation succeeds against people because of the corruption already within them.

True believers no longer make sinful desires the rule of life. They seek to do **God's will**.

Conversion brings a wonderful change to thoughts, judgments, loves, habits and conduct.

A converted person deeply regrets how much life was previously wasted in sin.

One wrongdoing leads easily to another. Peter identifies six related sins.

Christians should avoid not only obvious wickedness but also actions **that lead toward sin or even bear its appearance**.

Henry understands the gospel to have been preached to people **who have since died**.

Although they were slandered as wrongdoers by proud, worldly people, some even killed for faith, the Holy Spirit gave them spiritual life and enabled them to live to God.

Therefore do not be disheartened when the world despises or reproaches those who belong to Christ.

## Keep sober, pray and serve as the end approaches (verses 7–11)

Henry understands the destruction of the **Jewish nation and its religious order**, foretold by Christ, as close at hand.

The same passage naturally directs everyone to reflect upon the approach of **death and judgment**.

Knowing life's end is near should make us self-controlled in worldly matters and earnest in prayer and religion.

Because believers all have faults, love must be willing to **overlook, excuse and forgive the failings of others**.

Otherwise Satan can turn ordinary mistakes into endless conflicts.

But **love does not atone for the sins of the person who shows it** or earn God's forgiveness.

Christian work is both important and difficult. The goodness of our Master and the greatness of his reward require serious diligence.

In every duty, pursue **God's glory** above personal ambition.

Someone living only for reputation, possessions or selfish advantage rests upon goods destined to disappear.

But a person who entrusts everything to God can say **the Lord is my portion**. Glory through Christ alone is solid and everlasting.

## Suffer as Christians, not because of wrongdoing (verses 12–19)

Patience under persecution, trust in God's promises and obedience to the Holy Spirit's word bring glory to God.

By contrast, people who insult Christians for their faith dishonor the Holy Spirit.

It may seem unnecessary to warn believers against serious crimes. But opponents falsely accused them of such conduct, and **even the best Christians need warnings against great sins**.

There is no comfort in troubles brought upon ourselves through wrongdoing and folly.

Jesus had warned of a time of great calamity (**Matthew 24:9–10**), and it was approaching.

If the trials of this life can be so dreadful, how terrible will the final judgment be for the impenitent!

Scripture says the righteous are **scarcely saved**.

This does **not** mean God's purpose or promise of salvation is uncertain.

It means believers pass through immense difficulties: temptations, suffering, conflict outside and fear within.

The hardest obstacles are often not outward troubles but **remaining sinful desires and inward corruption**.

The end of someone who delights in sin and mocks the righteous will be far more dreadful.

The only sure care for our souls is to **entrust them to God through prayer** while continuing patiently to do what is right.

He will govern everything for the believer's final good.
