## The stages of Israel's journey (verses 1–49)

This chapter looks back over Israel's travels through the wilderness.

It is a history worth remembering.

As the people moved toward Canaan, they were constantly being asked to break camp and move again.

That is also the condition of our lives in this world.

We have no lasting city here.

Most of the changes we make are little more than moving from one part of a desert to another.

Israel sometimes went forward and sometimes seemed to go backward.

Yet all the while, the pillar of cloud and fire directed the journey.

God did not lead them along the shortest route, but he led them along the **right** one.

The path by which he brings his people to himself is always the best path, though it does not always look the nearest.

Remembering earlier journeys should also remind us of God's care for us, our families and our country.

He has led us, fed us and preserved us throughout our lives.

Very few periods of life can be remembered without recalling both God's goodness and our own ingratitude or disobedience.

His kindness leaves us without excuse for our sins.

We would not want to live through the same years again unless God's grace enabled us to avoid the sins we committed and make better use of the opportunities we missed.

Our wanderings will soon end, and our eternal condition will then be fixed.

How important the **present moment** is!

Happy are those whom the Lord guides with his counsel now and will finally receive into his glory.

The gospel calls us to that happiness.

Now is the acceptable time. Now is the day of salvation.

Sinners should take hold of the opportunity and flee for refuge to the hope set before them.

Let us use our time to glorify God and serve the people of our generation.

He will lead us safely through the remaining journey into his eternal kingdom.

## Israel is warned about the Canaanites (verses 50–56)

As Israel prepared to cross the Jordan, the people faced a renewed temptation to adopt the idols of Canaan.

God warned that sparing those idols or the idolaters would lead to judgment.

By doing so, they would be raising vipers close to their own hearts.

If they made peace with the Canaanites, even temporarily, those who remained would become like irritants in their eyes and thorns in their sides.

We should expect trouble from every sin we willingly indulge.

Whatever we allow to tempt us will eventually torment us.

The Canaanites were to be driven from the land.

But if the Israelites adopted their wicked practices, the Israelites would also be driven out.

Listen and fear.

If we do not drive out sin, sin will drive us out.

If we do not put our sinful desires to death, those desires will destroy our souls.
