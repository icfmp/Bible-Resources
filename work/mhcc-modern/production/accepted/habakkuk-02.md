## Waiting for the Lord in faith (verses 1–4)

When we are troubled and uncertain about the ways of God's Providence, we must guard against the temptation to become impatient.

After presenting our complaints and requests to him, we should watch for the answer he gives through **his word, Spirit and providential dealings**.

What is the Lord saying to our situation?

God will not disappoint the faith of those who wait to hear his reply.

Every person is concerned with the truths contained in God's word.

The promised blessing may seem delayed for a long time, but it will come at last.

The result will more than repay the patient waiting of his people.

A humble and repentant sinner seeks an interest in this salvation.

Such a person rests upon **God's promise and upon Christ**, through whom that promise is fulfilled.

They not only live by faith, but also **walk and work by faith**, persevering until the end and receiving glory.

Those who distrust or despise God's complete sufficiency do not live uprightly before him.

**The righteous shall live by faith** in these precious promises even while they await their fulfillment.

Those who are justified by faith will truly live and enjoy God's blessing now and forever.

## Warnings against the greed and oppression of Chaldea (verses 5–14)

The prophet announced judgment against proud, violent powers that oppress God's people.

Human beings become ensnared through **the desires of the flesh, the desires of the eyes and the pride of life**.

The power that had carried Israel into captivity was itself captive to these sinful desires.

We should regard only what was **honestly acquired** as properly ours.

Riches are like **thick clay**.

Gold and silver are no more than differently colored earth.

Traveling through deep mud slows a person down and makes the journey dirty.

So people who surround themselves with riches may find their progress burdened and polluted.

How foolish it is to carry such a heavy load of anxiety, guilt from acquiring and spending it, and accountability before God!

People can become so weighed down by wealth that it drags them toward destruction.

What was taken violently from others may be taken violently from the thief in turn.

Greed brings disturbance to a household.

A person greedy for gain brings trouble upon the family.

Worse, fraud and injustice call down divine judgment upon the household's affairs.

**Honest gain** may support a family with God's blessing.

Dishonest gain instead tends toward poverty, ruin and spiritual danger.

Those who injure their neighbors **do still greater injury to their own souls**.

The possessions they have accumulated will testify against them, however carefully their frauds were planned.

Few people endure a worse slavery than those devoted entirely to worldly pursuits.

They are disappointed when they fail to obtain wealth and disappointed again when they possess it.

They finally discover that the result was not merely emptiness but **distress of spirit**.

By humiliating the world's pretended glory, God displays his own true glory.

The whole earth will be filled with its knowledge **as the waters cover the sea**.

## Drunkenness, exploitation and idolatry judged (verses 15–20)

The Lord pronounced severe judgment against drunkenness everywhere, from a royal palace to the smallest tavern.

Giving a thirsty or exhausted person a drink can be a work of mercy.

But deliberately making another person drunk in order to expose them, discover private matters, trap them into a bad bargain or use them for some other evil purpose is **serious wickedness**.

To enjoy this sin is to contribute to the destruction of both body and soul.

Punishment will correspond to the offense.

The prophet also showed the foolishness of worshiping idols.

The Lord dwells in his holy heavenly temple.

He allows us to approach him in the way he has appointed.

May we welcome his salvation and worship him through **Jesus Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit**.
