# Psalm 123 — Modern English

## Original heading

**Confidence in God under contempt.**

## Looking to our heavenly Master for mercy

Jesus taught us to approach God in prayer as **our Father in heaven**.

A godly person lifts the soul toward him in every prayer, especially when suffering.

We ask for mercy, trust he will grant it, and continue waiting until it comes.

Servants watch their master's hand for several reasons.

**First, for direction**: they expect him to assign their work.

**Second, for provision**: they look for the food they need at the right time.

Likewise, we look to God for daily bread and sufficient grace, receiving both with thanksgiving.

**Third, for protection**: if servants are mistreated while fulfilling their duties, their master should defend them.

**Fourth, for correction**: when we sin, we ought to turn humbly to the very One who has disciplined us, submitting under his mighty hand.

**Finally, for reward**: faithful servants look to their master's kindness.

Hypocrites look to the world's approval and receive the reward of worldly honor.

But genuine Christians regard **God as both their Master and their Rewarder**.

God's people may receive little mercy from other human beings.

Their comfort is that **the Lord is full of mercy**.

Mockery and contempt have always been, and will likely continue to be, part of the suffering of God's people.

It is difficult to endure.

Yet those who serve the Lord should not be surprised to receive treatment like that given to **his beloved Son**.

When suffering nearly overwhelms us, look to **Jesus** and entrust ourselves through faith and prayer to the mercy of God.
