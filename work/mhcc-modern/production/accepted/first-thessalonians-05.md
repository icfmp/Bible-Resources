# 1 Thessalonians 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle exhorts to be always ready for the coming of Christ to judgment, which will be with suddenness and surprise. | 1–11 |
| He directs to several particular duties. | 12–22 |
| And concludes with prayer, greetings, and a blessing. | 23–28 |

## Be prepared for Christ's sudden coming (verses 1–5)

It is neither necessary nor useful to demand to know **the exact date of Jesus Christ's return**.

Even the apostles were not told that time.

We should learn and observe **the times and opportunities for work** God has given us.

But the hour at which we must give an account of our lives is unknown, and **we do not need to know it**.

Christ's coming will take many people by surprise, as our Lord himself taught.

For each individual, **death** has much the same significance that the final judgment has for humanity as a whole.

The same warnings therefore apply to both.

Christ's return will be terrifying for those who live in ungodliness.

Destruction will overtake them while they imagine themselves safe, entertain empty hopes and pursue distracting pleasures.

They will find no way to escape **that day's terror and punishment**.

But for the righteous it will be a happy day.

Believers are not in darkness; they are **children of the light**.

How many people tell themselves that everything is peaceful and safe while destruction hangs over them!

Let us awaken ourselves and one another and remain watchful against our spiritual enemies.

## Stay sober and encourage one another (verses 6–11)

Most people fail to think about eternal matters.

Some are **asleep**, giving them no thought at all.

Others are **dreaming**, imagining them falsely.

Christians should be known for **moderation in earthly things**.

With the light of the gospel shining upon them, how could they neglect their souls or forget the world to come?

We need the spiritual armor of **faith, love and hope**.

Faith teaches us that God always sees us and that we must prepare for another life.

Love for him and his purposes keeps us awake and sober.

Hope of salvation moves us to avoid anything that would weaken **our trust in Christ**.

Our hope is secure because salvation comes **through the Lord Jesus Christ**, who died **to atone for sins and ransom souls**.

We should strengthen one another through **prayer, praise and good example**, helping one another learn to live for the Lord with whom we hope to live forever.

## Respect ministers, help the weak and return good for evil (verses 12–15)

Gospel ministers are known by their work: **serving and honoring the Lord**.

Their responsibility is not only to offer advice but also **to warn their flock about dangers and reprove wrongdoing**.

Church members should honor and love faithful ministers because their work concerns **the wellbeing of souls**.

Believers should maintain peace with one another and do everything reasonable to prevent quarrels.

But **loving peace never excuses tolerating sin**.

Those who are frightened, discouraged or sorrowful need encouragement.

A **kind word** can help greatly.

We must bear with one another, practice patience and restrain anger.

This applies **toward everyone**.

Whatever people do to us, we are still commanded **to do good to others**.

## Rejoice, pray, give thanks and test all teaching (verses 16–22)

We should enjoy earthly comforts as blessings that will not last forever.

But **joy in God** can last always.

A genuinely religious life is marked by continual joy.

We would have more reason to rejoice if we **prayed more faithfully**.

Prayer helps us with legitimate business and every good work.

Those who pray continually will also find continual reasons for **thanksgiving**.

We should thank God for blessings that spare us trouble and those that prevent greater harm; for common and extraordinary mercies; for past and present gifts; and for both earthly and spiritual benefits.

Thanksgiving should not be restricted to pleasant circumstances.

We may thank God even amid **trials, corrections and painful providences**, because he intends them for the good of his people even when we cannot yet understand how.

**Do not extinguish the Holy Spirit's work**.

The Spirit is compared to **fire** because he **illuminates, gives life and purifies** the soul.

Fire can be put out by taking away its fuel or covering it with water or earth.

Likewise, believers resist the Spirit's work when they indulge sinful desires, become absorbed in earthly things or refuse to follow the holy affections he has awakened.

Paul's reference to **prophecies** here concerns **the preaching, explanation and application of Scripture**.

Do not despise preaching merely because it is plain or repeats truths we have heard before.

Search the Scriptures.

**Test everything** and hold firmly to what is good.

Turn away from sin and from **whatever resembles, leads toward or borders upon it**.

People who are careless about tempting circumstances and early approaches to wrongdoing will soon fall into actual sin.

## Pray for complete sanctification and share Scripture widely (verses 23–28)

Paul prayed that believers would become **more completely sanctified**.

Even the best Christians remain only **partly sanctified in this life**.

We should therefore pray for and strive toward **complete holiness**.

Without God's sustaining power we would fall away.

Ask him to finish the work he began so that we may be presented **without fault before his glorious throne**.

Believers should pray for one another, expressing genuine family love in this way.

Paul also directed that this letter **be read to every believer**.

Henry draws an explicit lesson: ordinary Christians are not merely permitted to read Scripture; **they have a duty to read it**.

The Bible should **not be confined to a language people cannot understand**.

It should be **translated and made accessible**, because everyone needs to know God's word.

Scripture should be read aloud in **every public congregation**, especially to help those with little education.

We need nothing more for true happiness than **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ**.

He is a **fountain of grace that never stops flowing** and provides everything necessary for his people.
