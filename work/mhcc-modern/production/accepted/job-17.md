# Job 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job appeals from man to God. | 1–9 |
| His hope is not in life, but in death. | 10–16 |

## Job appeals to God (verses 1–9)

Job reflected on his friends' harsh judgments and, considering himself near death, appealed to God.

Our time on earth is coming to an end.

We should use its remaining days wisely, preparing for eternity.

The righteous should learn from Job's troubles, whether they came through God's permission, hostile enemies or unkind friends.

Instead of becoming discouraged from serving God because Job, his faithful servant, was treated so severely, believers should become more determined to persevere.

Those who keep **heaven as their destination** will keep their feet on the path of faith, whatever hardships or discouragements meet them along the way.

## Job looks beyond earthly recovery (verses 10–16)

Job's friends tried to comfort him by predicting a return to worldly prosperity.

But it is unwise to offer comfort grounded entirely in the possibility that earthly circumstances may improve.

We should encourage ourselves and others through **the promises of God, his love and grace, and a well-founded hope of eternal life**—comforts that cannot fail.

Job learned to make peace with the thought of the grave.

Believers can be willing to die because, in one sense, death is like going to bed after a long day.

They are tired, and it is time to rest.

Why should they refuse when their Father calls them?

Our bodies are already linked to decay, worms and dust.

Let us seek the living hope that will be fulfilled when the wicked person's false hope has disappeared into darkness.

While our bodies rest in the grave, our souls may enjoy **the rest God has reserved for his people**.
