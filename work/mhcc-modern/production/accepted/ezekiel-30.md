# Ezekiel 30 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A prophecy against Egypt. | 1–19 |
| Another. | 20–26 |

## Egypt will be devastated (verses 1–19)

This prophecy gives a full account of **Egypt's coming destruction**.

Those who join themselves to God's enemies will share in their punishment.

The king of Babylon and his army would become **instruments of that destruction**.

God often makes **one wicked person a means of judgment against another**.

No place in Egypt would escape the fury of the Chaldeans.

The Lord makes himself known through the judgments he executes.

Yet all these are only **present effects of divine displeasure**.

They are not worthy of comparison with **the wrath to come**, from which **Jesus delivers his people**.

## Egypt is weakened further (verses 20–26)

Egypt would become weaker and weaker.

If lesser judgments do not humble sinners and lead them to reform, **God may send greater ones**.

He justly breaks power that has been abused **to oppress or cheat others**.

Babylon would grow stronger.

It is useless to try to **bind up the arm God has decided to break**, or to strengthen people he has determined to bring down.

Those who disregard the revelations of God's **truth and mercy** will come to know his **power and justice** through the punishment of their sins.
