# Psalm 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist prays to God to plead his cause, and judge for him. | 1–9 |
| He expresses confidence in God, and will give him the glory of his deliverance. | 10–17 |

## David appeals to the Judge of hearts (verses 1–9)

David fled to God for help and protection.

Yet **Jesus Christ alone** could call heaven to witness that he was completely upright in every respect.

All his works were righteous.

The prince of this world could find nothing in him that deserved accusation.

Nevertheless, for our sake Christ accepted being treated as guilty and suffered every evil.

He remained innocent and triumphed over them all.

David's appeal was directed to **the righteous God who examines hearts and the deepest desires**.

God knows the hidden sins of wicked people and how to bring their wrongdoing to an end.

He also knows the secret sincerity of those who live uprightly and can establish their cause.

Anyone who has first made peace with God concerning personal sins through **the sacrifice of the Mediator**, according to God's mercy and grace, may then appeal to divine justice when falsely accused by enemies.

## The righteous God saves and warns (verses 10–17)

David trusted that God would be a powerful Savior.

The threatened destruction of a sinner may be avoided through **repentance and conversion**.

The warning is therefore an act of mercy.

God gives people time to repent and escape the judgment they otherwise face.

He is patient and slow to punish, not delighting in the destruction of sinners.

In verses **14–16**, sinners are pictured exerting great effort in destroying their own souls.

If those same efforts were turned toward repentance and obedience, they could lead toward salvation.

In this sense, the description applies to everyone who continues in sin.

Throughout our trials we must look to the Savior.

Blessed Lord, grant that we may follow your spotless example as you go before your church in suffering.

When persecution and trouble mark our smaller path, may looking to Jesus strengthen our minds and comfort our hearts.
