# Matthew 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ's resurrection. | 1–8 |
| He appears to the women. | 9, 10 |
| Confession of the soldiers. | 11–15 |
| Christ's commission to his disciples. | 16–20 |

## Jesus rises from the dead (verses 1–8)

Christ rose on **the third day**, just as he had repeatedly predicted.

On the first day of creation's week God commanded light to shine out of darkness.

On the first day of this new week, **the Light of the World shone out of the darkness of the grave**.

Henry observes that the New Testament repeatedly mentions this day as the day on which Christians gathered solemnly to honor Christ.

Jesus could have rolled away the stone through his own power.

But he chose to have **an angel** do it.

His resurrection brought **joy to his friends** and **fear and confusion to his enemies**.

The angel comforted the women.

Those who persist in sin should fear God's judgment.

But followers of Christ may hear, **“Do not be afraid.”**

The resurrection is their comfort.

Our fellowship with the risen Savior is **spiritual**, enjoyed through faith in his word.

When we begin to treat this world as our permanent home, remembering only how pleasant it is to live here, we should recall that **Jesus is no longer in the grave—he has risen**.

Therefore lift your hearts toward heavenly things.

He rose **as he said**.

Never treat as strange what Christ's word has prepared us to expect, whether present suffering or future glory.

Faithfully contemplating the place where our Lord lay can strengthen us.

But the angel told the women to **go quickly**.

It was good to visit the tomb, yet God had other work for them.

When duty calls, **useful service to others must take priority over the pleasure of private spiritual enjoyment**.

They were to tell the disciples so those sorrowing followers could be comforted.

Christ knows where his disciples live and graciously visits them, even when they are far from an abundance of outward opportunities for worship.

**Fear and joy together** made the women hurry.

Christians should willingly share their experiences of fellowship with the Lord, telling one another what God has done for their souls.

## The risen Jesus appears to the women (verses 9 and 10)

God's gracious encouragement often meets us **while we are doing our duty**.

Those who use the help they have received for the benefit of others often receive still more.

The women did not expect to encounter Jesus personally, but he was near them.

He remains near believers today **through his word**.

His greeting revealed his continuing love for humanity, even after entering his exalted state.

Christ desires his people to be **joyful and cheerful**.

His resurrection supplies more than enough reason for joy.

**Do not be afraid.**

The risen Lord has defeated death to free his people from their fears.

The disciples had shamefully abandoned him during his suffering.

Yet he still called them **his brothers**.

This reveals his willingness to forgive and teaches us to forgive.

Despite his majesty and purity and our weakness and unworthiness, he graciously calls believers **brothers and sisters**.

## The guards receive money to lie (verses 11–15)

Love of money can lead people into dreadful wrongdoing.

The soldiers accepted a large payment to promote a story they **knew was false**.

How strange that some spend generously to support falsehood while others begrudge small gifts for spreading truth!

Let us not allow a worthy cause to fail through neglect when wicked causes receive lavish support.

The religious leaders promised to protect the soldiers from Pilate's punishment.

But they could not protect them from **God's judgment**, which confronts those who deliberately love and tell lies.

A person promising that someone can sin deliberately **without danger** promises more than can be delivered.

Their story contradicted itself.

If the guards had all been sleeping, how could they know what happened?

If any were awake, why did they not awaken the others?

If they had actually fallen asleep on duty, why would they freely confess it?

And had the story been true, would the authorities not have prosecuted the apostles for the supposed theft?

Henry regards these contradictions as strong evidence of the account's falsehood.

This was not merely a failure to understand evidence.

It showed **deliberate wrongdoing of the heart**.

God allowed the deceivers to expose the weakness of their own plan.

Christ's resurrection is the great testimony to his identity as **Son of God**.

The soldiers had unusually strong evidence of the truth, yet accepted bribes to discourage belief.

Even the clearest external evidence does not convert a human heart without **the Holy Spirit's work**.

## The risen Jesus commissions his disciples (verses 16–20)

Matthew omits some appearances of the risen Jesus found in Luke and John and moves toward the solemn commission.

Jesus had appointed this meeting before his death and appeared after rising.

Those who see Christ through **the eyes of faith** worship him.

Yet even sincere faith may be weak and wavering.

Jesus supplied convincing evidence of his resurrection so that faith could overcome doubt.

He commissioned the apostles and their successors in gospel ministry **to go among all nations**.

The salvation they proclaimed is a **common salvation**.

Whoever desires may come to Jesus and receive its benefits.

All people are welcome.

Christianity is the faith of a sinner who seeks deliverance from **deserved judgment and sin**.

The sinner approaches the mercy of **the Father** through **the atonement of the incarnate Son** and through **the sanctification of the Holy Spirit**.

The believer gives himself to worshiping and serving **Father, Son and Holy Spirit—three Persons, one God**—in all the ordinances and commands Christ appointed.

**Baptism is an outward sign of the Spirit's inward washing and sanctifying work**, which seals and evidences a believer's justification.

We must therefore examine whether we possess the spiritual reality that baptism signifies: **death to sin and new birth into righteousness**, through which children of wrath become God's children.

Jesus promised his followers **his constant presence**.

He is with his churches and ministers **all the days, every day**.

There is no hour when they are abandoned by their Lord.

Without his presence, even for a moment, their work would fail.

The Savior may at times seem hidden, but he is **never far away**.

Let us join the final **Amen**.

Lord Jesus, remain with us and all your people.

Let your face shine upon us so that your way may be known throughout the earth and **your saving power among all nations**.
