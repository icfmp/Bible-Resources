# Hebrews 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortations to various duties, and to be content with what Providence allots. | 1–6 |
| To respect the instructions of faithful pastors, with cautions against being carried away by strange doctrines. | 7–15 |
| Further exhortations to duties, that relate to God, to our neighbour, and to those set over us in the Lord. | 16–21 |
| This epistle to be seriously considered. | 22–25 |

## Love one another and be content (verses 1–6)

Jesus gave himself to redeem **a people eager for good works**.

Faith in him is the strongest bond of genuine friendship.

Christians are called to many duties, especially **contentment**.

The opposite is greed: an excessive desire for earthly possessions mixed with envy of people who have more.

Because we possess treasures in heaven, we can be content even in humble earthly circumstances.

People who cannot be content now would often be just as discontent if their position improved.

**Adam lived in paradise and was discontent**. Some angels were not content even in heaven.

Yet Paul learned contentment in every circumstance, including poverty and loss.

The great divine promise sums up all the others: **“I will never leave you or forsake you.”**

Henry notes that the original expression contains **five negatives** emphasizing certainty.

Believers have God's gracious presence in life, at death and forever.

Human opposition can accomplish nothing beyond God's rule; he can turn even hostility to his people's good.

## Follow faithful leaders while holding fast to Christ (verses 7–15)

Remember the teaching and example of faithful ministers who completed their service honorably.

Some leaders die and others will soon die, but **Jesus Christ, the church's Head and High Priest, never changes**.

He is the same across both Testaments and forever—equally merciful, strong and sufficient.

He continues feeding hungry souls, strengthening the fearful and welcoming repentant sinners.

He still rejects pride and self-righteousness, despises empty religious profession and teaches all whom he saves to **love righteousness and hate evil**.

Our hearts are established through **free grace applied by the Holy Spirit**, not through deceptive innovations.

Christ is both **our Altar and our Sacrifice**, consecrating what we offer.

**The Lord's Supper is the feast of the gospel Passover**.

Clinging to Levitical sacrifices would exclude people from the Christian altar according to the old law's own structure.

Therefore we must go **outside the camp to Christ**—leaving the ceremonial order, sin, the world and selfish confidence.

Through his blood we are set apart to God.

Our present home cannot endure, because sin and death will not let us remain.

Seek through faith the rest and peace only Jesus provides.

Bring to God through Christ **a continuous sacrifice of praise**, including worship, prayer and thanksgiving.

## Serve others, respect biblical leaders and pray (verses 16–21)

Help meet people's **spiritual and bodily needs** according to your means.

Such offerings please God, who receives them and blesses their givers **through Christ**.

Submit to your living ministers **only so far as their teaching agrees with God's will revealed in Scripture**.

No Christian is too wise or important to learn.

Believers must **search the Scriptures** and receive their ministers' counsel insofar as it agrees with that standard.

It benefits the congregation when its pastors can give a joyful rather than grieving account of its spiritual condition.

Faithful ministers must fulfill their responsibility; stubbornly unfaithful hearers bear responsibility for their own ruin.

Pray earnestly for ministers. The more sincerely a church prays, the more benefit it may expect from their service.

Even ministers with a good conscience need others' prayers.

God is **the God of peace**, reconciled to believers and the source of reconciliation between himself and sinners.

He desires peace among his churches and places spiritual peace in believers' hearts.

His covenant rests securely on **the blood of his Son**.

The great prayer is that Christians may become **fully equipped for every good work** and fitted for the fellowship and happiness of heaven.

Every good thing wrought within us is **God's work, through Christ and by his Spirit**.

## Receive this exhortation and remain in grace (verses 22–25)

Even believers, because sin remains, sometimes reject important and comforting truth when it challenges them.

They must therefore be urged to **receive rather than resent** loving instruction.

Christians should have **the law of love and kindness written in their hearts** toward one another.

True religion teaches gentleness, courtesy and consideration, not harshness or rudeness.

May God's favor remain with you, and may his grace work continually in you, producing **the fruits of holiness as the first taste of heavenly glory**.
