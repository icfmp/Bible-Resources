# 2 Kings 24 — Modern English

## Jehoiakim comes under Nebuchadnezzar's rule (verses 1–7)

If Jehoiakim had served the Lord, he would not have had to serve **Nebuchadnezzar** in this way.

Had he accepted his position as a subject and kept his word, his situation need not have become worse.

But he rebelled against Babylon and brought still more suffering upon himself.

Consider how important it is for nations to mourn and turn from the sins of earlier generations, lest they suffer the consequences.

The Lord's warnings will be fulfilled just as surely as his promises unless sinners turn from their wrongdoing through repentance.

## Jehoiachin is taken captive (verses 8–20)

**Jehoiachin** reigned only three months.

Yet this was enough time to show that he walked in the sins of his predecessors and brought upon himself the consequences of their wrongdoing.

His uncle was appointed to govern.

**Zedekiah** was the final king of Judah.

The judgments that had fallen on the previous three kings should have warned him.

Instead he continued doing evil as they had done.

When people entrusted with the decisions of a nation act foolishly and against the nation's real interests, we should recognize in that conduct the warning of God's displeasure.

Because of a people's sins, the Lord may hide from them the things that would bring public peace.

To fulfill the hidden purposes of his justice, he need only leave people to the blindness of their minds or the desires of their own hearts.

The gradual approach of God's judgments offers sinners time to repent.

It also gives believers time to prepare for coming trouble.

At the same time, it exposes the stubbornness of those who refuse to abandon their sins.
