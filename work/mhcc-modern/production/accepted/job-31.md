# Job 31 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job declares his uprightness. | 1–8 |
| His integrity. | 9–15 |
| Job merciful. | 16–23 |
| Job not guilty of covetousness or idolatry. | 24–32 |
| Job not guilty of hypocrisy and violence. | 33–40 |

## Job declares his integrity (verses 1–8)

Job was not boasting.

He was answering accusations of hypocrisy.

He understood that God's commands reach beyond outward actions into **the thoughts and intentions of the heart**.

Normally our conduct should speak for itself.

But when serious false charges threaten both our reputation and God's cause, there are times when we ought solemnly to protest our innocence.

Two dangers have ruined countless people: **sinful bodily desires** and **love of the world**.

Job had watched carefully against both.

God examines us even more closely than we examine ourselves.

We should therefore live carefully.

Job avoided dishonest ways of gaining wealth.

He feared **forbidden profit** as much as **forbidden pleasure**.

We can use our possessions with comfort, or lose them without disgrace, when they were honestly gained.

Without strict honesty and faithfulness in business and relationships, we have no sound evidence of genuine godliness.

Yet many who profess faith cannot pass this test.

## Job defends his conduct toward his household (verses 9–15)

The sins that pollute a life arise from a heart that has been deceived.

Lust is like a fire burning within.

When people indulge it, it consumes what is good in the soul and leaves the conscience ruined.

It brings God's judgment, which only **Christ's blood** can turn away from eternal destruction.

It may also destroy the body and waste a person's possessions.

Job had managed a large household.

He remembered that **he too had a Master in heaven**.

If God treated us with the strictness we deserve, we would be ruined.

Therefore we should treat everyone entrusted to our care **with gentleness and fairness**.

## Job speaks about mercy toward the poor (verses 16–23)

Job's conscience bore witness to his charitable and just treatment of poor people.

He spoke particularly at length about this because his friends had accused him directly of oppressing them.

He had sought to be tender toward everyone and harmful to no one.

What restrained him from cruelty and lack of compassion?

He feared the Lord and knew that God would oppose him if he harmed the poor.

Concern for worldly reputation may restrain people from committing visible offenses.

But **God's grace alone** can make them truly hate, fear and avoid sinful thoughts and desires.

## Job rejects greed, idolatry and malice (verses 24–32)

Job made **four denials**.

**First**, he had never set his heart upon worldly wealth.

How few prosperous people who profess religion can sincerely say they never rejoiced merely because their wealth increased!

The determination to become rich can ruin souls and pierce lives with sorrow.

**Second**, Job had not practiced idolatry.

Its source lies in the heart, and it corrupts people and brings judgment on communities.

**Third**, he had never wished harm upon an enemy or taken delight in an enemy's misfortune.

Another person's hatred does not justify our hatred.

**Fourth**, he had never been unkind to strangers.

Hospitality is a Christian duty (**1 Peter 4:9**).

## Job rejects hypocrisy and injustice (verses 33–40)

Job denied the charge of hypocrisy.

Human beings are reluctant to confess wrong, eager to make excuses and inclined to blame others.

But people who cover their sins will not prosper (**Proverbs 28:13**).

Job cited his courage in doing right as evidence of sincerity.

People who acquire wealth unjustly may rightly lose every comfort they expected from it.

Their field may be sown with wheat, but the crop will be thistles.

What is not honestly obtained can never bring lasting good.

Here **Job's speeches ended**.

He concluded with a strong appeal to God concerning the accusations against his moral and religious character.

But despite Job's confidence, we will later see that he was mistaken in some of his claims (**Job 40:4–5; 1 John 1:8**).

Let us all examine ourselves.

Where we have sinned, seek pardon through **the blood of Jesus Christ**, which cleanses us from every sin.

May God have mercy on us and write his law upon our hearts.
