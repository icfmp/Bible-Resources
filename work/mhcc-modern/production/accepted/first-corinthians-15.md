# 1 Corinthians 15 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle proves the resurrection of Christ from the dead. | 1–11 |
| Those answered who deny the resurrection of the body. | 12–19 |
| The resurrection of believers to eternal life. | 20–34 |
| Objections against it answered. | 35–50 |
| The mystery of the change that will be made on those living at Christ's second coming. | 51–54 |
| The believer's triumph over death and the grave, An exhortation to diligence. | 55–58 |

## Christ's resurrection is the gospel's foundation (verses 1–11)

The resurrection points toward **life beyond the grave**.

Henry argues that the philosophers' teachings offer nothing comparable to the doctrine Paul proclaims.

**Christ's death and resurrection are the foundation of Christianity**.

If they are removed, every hope of eternal life collapses.

Believers stand firm through trial by holding closely to this gospel.

A person who abandons the gospel's faith has believed in vain.

Christ's resurrection was foretold in the **Old Testament Scriptures**.

Many witnesses saw him after he rose.

Paul had received exceptional privileges, but he kept **a humble opinion of himself**.

God can convert sinners into saints by grace and use the memory of their past sins to make them humble, diligent and faithful.

Paul attributed everything good within him to **the grace of God**.

True Christians can recognize what God has done **for them, in them and through them**, yet also see how far they remain from their duty.

They therefore regard themselves humbly before the Lord.

All the apostles agreed upon the central testimony: **Jesus Christ was crucified and rose from the dead**.

This is the heart of Christian faith.

In this truth they lived and died.

## Denying resurrection makes Christian hope empty (verses 12–19)

Paul had established that Christ rose.

He now answered those denying **the future resurrection of the body**.

If Christ had not risen, there could have been **no effective justification or salvation**.

Faith in someone still held by death would be empty.

The resurrection of Jesus is the proof of our future bodily resurrection.

Even believers who died trusting Christ would have perished in their sins if he had never risen.

If resurrection and future reward were impossible, every Christian hope would be confined to **this life alone**.

Then believers, especially under the persecution experienced in the apostles' day, would be more miserable than other people.

But this is not their actual condition.

Christ's people possess **solid comfort and hope** even amid severe trials and persecution.

## Christ's victory guarantees resurrection to those united to him (verses 20–34)

Everyone joined to Christ through faith has a secure promise of resurrection through **his rising from the dead**.

Through the first Adam's sin, the human race became mortal and inherited fallen nature.

Through Christ's resurrection, everyone brought into the spiritual life of his people by **the Holy Spirit** will rise and live forever.

**There is an order in the resurrection**.

Christ himself is **the firstfruits**.

At his coming, his redeemed people will rise.

Henry says the wicked will also rise later, and then this present order of things will come to its end.

If we wish to triumph on that day, we must now submit to Christ's rule, receive his salvation and live for his glory.

The completion of Christ's saving work will bring all glory to God, and his people will serve and enjoy him forever.

Paul mentions people **“baptized for the dead.”**

The meaning is not certain.

Henry suggests **as one possibility** that baptism here figuratively means **suffering, persecution or martyrdom**, as in **Matthew 20:22–23**.

He asks what would become of people who suffered terrible injuries or lost their lives for faith in resurrection if the dead never rose.

Whatever Paul meant, **the Corinthian readers evidently understood his argument**.

Without resurrection and eternal life, the suffering required by Christian faith would have no promised reward.

But we are not animals who simply live and die without a future.

Failure to believe in resurrection and the life to come reveals ignorance of God.

Recognizing God's existence and Providence, and seeing the many injustices of this life—where good people often suffer most—should lead us to expect a future state in which matters will be put right.

Avoid close fellowship with people who encourage ungodliness.

Warn children and young people especially to avoid corrupting companionship.

**Awaken to righteousness and stop sinning**.

## God can raise bodies transformed for heaven (verses 35–50)

Two questions arise.

**First, how can dead people rise?** By what power and means?

**Second, what kind of bodies will they possess?** Will they have exactly the same shape, size, organs and qualities?

Henry distinguishes the first as **an objection from those opposing resurrection** and the second as **a question from curious but doubtful minds**.

God's almighty power answers the first.

Each year we see a partial analogy in **grain sown into the ground and springing into life**.

It is foolish to deny that God's power can raise the dead while we see him continually bringing life from what appears dead.

As to the second question, **grain changes greatly when it grows**, and resurrected bodies will also be transformed.

We do not fully understand how life arises from a seed.

God's ordinary works of creation and Providence should humble us as well as lead us to admire him.

Different kinds of plants and earthly bodies vary.

So do **the heavenly bodies and their glories**.

Likewise, the raised bodies will be fitted for the heavenly state and may display varied glory.

**Burial resembles placing seed in the ground so that it may spring up later**.

Though a dead body is repellent now, believers will rise with bodies made fit for everlasting union with **spirits perfected in holiness**.

To God all things are possible.

He gives **spiritual life through the Holy Spirit now**, and will later give new life and transformation to the body by the same Spirit.

The dead in Christ will not simply rise; **they will rise gloriously transformed**.

Their new bodies will be appropriate for the world in which they will live forever.

Our bodies in their present weakness and need cannot possess or enjoy heaven in that form.

Therefore, do not sow to sinful flesh and reap corruption.

The body follows the soul's spiritual condition.

Someone neglecting the life of the soul wastes the highest gift of this present life.

## The living will be changed; death's defeat calls for steadfastness (verses 51–58)

Not every believer will die before Christ returns.

But **all believers will be changed**.

The gospel reveals many truths that earlier generations did not clearly know.

In the place to which the risen Lord brings his saints, **death will no longer exist**.

We therefore seek full assurance of faith and hope.

Even during pain or when death approaches, we may think calmly about the grave, confident that **our bodies will sleep there**, while our souls will be **with the Redeemer**.

Sin gives death its harmful power.

**Sin is death's sting**.

But Christ removed that sting through his own death.

He made atonement and obtained forgiveness.

**The law gives sin its condemning strength**.

No human being can satisfy all the law's demands, bear its curse fully or undo personal transgressions through self-righteousness.

This brings terror to people who remain unbelieving and unrepentant.

Death may seize a believer, but **it cannot keep that believer in its power**.

How many reasons for thanksgiving and joy flow from Christ's death, resurrection, sufferings and victories!

Paul therefore concludes by urging believers to be **steadfast**, established in the gospel they received and unshaken in their hope of being raised incorruptible and immortal.

They must **abound in the Lord's work**, continually serving him and obeying his commands.

May Christ grant us faith and strengthen it, so that we may not only be **safe**, but also **joyful and triumphant**.
