## John prepares the Lord's way (verses 1–8)

Both Isaiah and Malachi spoke of **the beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ** through the ministry of John the Baptist.

Their prophecies show that Christ comes among us with both **a treasury of grace and a scepter of government**.

Human corruption puts up great resistance to his progress.

When the Father sent his Son into the world, he prepared a way before him. When he sends Christ into a human heart, he likewise prepares the way.

John considered himself unworthy of the humblest service to Jesus.

The greatest saints have always been the most humble. They recognize their need of **Christ's atoning blood and sanctifying Spirit** more deeply than others.

The gospel promises people who repent and receive forgiveness that they will be **baptized with the Holy Spirit**, purified through his grace and refreshed through his consolations.

Often we gain little profit or comfort from God's ordinances, word and sacraments because we lack **the divine light within us**.

And we lack that light because **we have not asked for it**, though our heavenly Father promises to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him.

## Jesus is baptized and tempted (verses 9–13)

Christ's baptism was his first public appearance after living unnoticed for many years.

How much genuine worth can remain unknown in this world!

Yet what is hidden will, at the appropriate time, be revealed.

Jesus took upon himself the **likeness of sinful human flesh** and consecrated himself for our sake so that we might become holy and be baptized with him (**John 17:19**).

The Father honored his submission to baptism. The Holy Spirit descended upon him like a dove.

Heaven may be said to open to us whenever the Spirit descends and works within us.

The good work God carries out in our hearts is evidence of his favorable purposes toward us.

Mark mentions that Jesus was in the wilderness **among wild animals**.

Even there his Father preserved him, encouraging confidence that the Father would continue providing whatever was necessary.

Special acts of protection give assurance of further help when it is needed.

**The serpent tempted the first Adam in a garden and the second Adam in a wilderness**, with very different outcomes.

He has continued tempting Adam's children in every place and condition.

Company has temptations; so does solitude.

No location, occupation or lawful activity is free from them—not even **fasting and prayer**.

Indeed, some of the fiercest assaults occur during sacred duties, yet through God's grace those duties also provide the sweetest victories.

The ministry of good angels comforts us against the malicious plans of evil angels.

But an even greater consolation is **God the Holy Spirit living in our hearts**.

## Jesus preaches and calls disciples (verses 14–22)

Jesus began preaching in Galilee after John was imprisoned.

When God sets aside one servant, he raises up others to continue his work.

Jesus joined **repentance** and **faith** in his message.

Through repentance we acknowledge the Creator whom we have offended.

Through faith we honor **the Redeemer who came to save us from sin**.

Christ joined these two things; we must not separate them.

He called fishermen, people of modest worldly status who were diligent in their work and treated one another kindly.

Industry and unity are good, and Jesus delights to bless them.

Christ's followers must be willing to leave everything for his sake.

His grace makes them willing.

This does not mean everyone must literally withdraw from society.

Rather, we must **hold worldly possessions and relationships loosely**, giving up anything inconsistent with duty to Jesus or harmful to our souls.

Jesus faithfully observed the Sabbath by doing the spiritual and charitable work for which Sabbath rest was appointed.

His doctrine continually astonished people. The more carefully we hear it, the more reason we have to admire it.

## Jesus casts out an unclean spirit (verses 23–28)

The devil is described as **unclean** because he has lost the purity of his original state, acts in direct opposition to God's Holy Spirit and makes human hearts impure through his suggestions.

Some people quietly attend gatherings where teachers offer only formal religion.

But when Christ comes through **faithful ministers, holy doctrine and the convicting Spirit**, they resist the truth and want no part in Jesus.

The unclean spirit in this story recognized Jesus as **the Holy One of God**.

It feared his holiness and expected destruction from him, rather than salvation.

People who tell God to depart from them speak the language of the same hostility.

The natural mind is opposed to God, particularly his holiness.

When Christ delivers souls from Satan's power, the change may produce turmoil.

The enemy tries to disturb people whom he can no longer destroy.

The witnesses wondered at the power of Jesus' teaching.

Equally astonishing changes occur when people become genuinely converted.

If we did not treat conversion with indifference, the transformation of a notoriously wicked person into **a sober, righteous and godly life** through preaching the crucified Savior would still astonish us.

## Jesus heals many people (verses 29–39)

Wherever Jesus went, **he did good**.

He healed people so that they could serve him and others who belonged to him.

People prevented from attending public worship by illness or another genuine obstacle may still look for his gracious presence.

He can soothe their sorrow and reduce their pain.

The sick came in large numbers.

When we see others receiving help from Christ, it should encourage us to seek him ourselves.

Jesus withdrew to **a solitary place**.

He had no danger of pride or distraction, yet still sought time apart for prayer.

People who do the greatest amount of useful public work must also sometimes be **alone with God**.

## Jesus cleanses a person with leprosy (verses 40–45)

The leper came to Christ with deep humility and full submission to his will.

**“Lord, if you are willing”** was not a question about Christ's compassion or power.

It was an expression of readiness to accept his decision.

We should pray with the same humility and trust.

Christ willingly shows mercy to people who willingly entrust their requests to his will.

He did not want this healing to become a means of attracting human praise for himself.

However, we have **no such reason today to hesitate to proclaim Christ's praises**.
