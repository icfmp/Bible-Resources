# 2 Corinthians 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An exhortation to holiness, and the whole church entreated to bear affection to the apostle. | 1–4 |
| He rejoiced in their sorrowing to repentance. | 5–11 |
| And in the comfort they and Titus had together. | 12–16 |

## Pursuing holiness and receiving faithful ministers (verses 1–4)

God's promises give us strong reasons to pursue holiness.

We must cleanse ourselves from everything that defiles **the body and the spirit**.

If we hope in God as our Father, we should desire to become holy as he is holy and to resemble our heavenly Father.

Only his grace, working by the **Holy Spirit**, can truly cleanse us.

Nevertheless, growth in holiness should be the constant object of **our prayers and efforts**.

It is dangerous when people regard gospel ministers as contemptible, because contempt for the messenger may lead to contempt for the gospel.

Ministers must not flatter anyone, but they should show **gentleness toward everyone**.

They may ask for esteem and goodwill when they can honestly say that they have corrupted no one through false teaching or flattering words, defrauded no one, and never pursued their own advantage at another person's expense.

Paul's great affection for the Corinthians moved him to speak freely to them.

That affection also led him to **speak proudly of their progress** wherever he went.

## Godly sorrow leads to genuine repentance (verses 5–11)

Paul and his companions faced conflict outside and fears within.

They met opposition from both Jews and Gentiles, while also carrying deep anxiety for people who had embraced the Christian faith.

But **God comforts those who are downcast**.

We should look beyond the human means through which comfort arrives and recognize **God as the source** of every true consolation and blessing.

There is a profound difference between **godly sorrow** and the sorrow of this world.

Sorrow that agrees with God's will, seeks his glory and is produced by the Holy Spirit makes the heart **humble, contrite and submissive**.

It moves the believer to **put sin to death and walk in newness of life**.

This repentance belongs with **saving faith in Jesus Christ** and participation in the blessings of his **atoning sacrifice**.

Worldly regret can mourn consequences without turning from sin.

But genuine repentance bears visible fruit.

When **the heart changes, the life and actions change**.

The Corinthians' repentance produced indignation against sin, against their own past conduct, and against the tempter and his agents.

It produced watchfulness and a careful fear of falling into sin again.

It brought a strong desire to be reconciled to God.

It stirred zeal for duty and opposition to wrongdoing.

It also prompted **practical attempts to correct the harm** their sins had caused.

Deep humility before God, hatred of all sin, faith in Christ, a **new heart and a new life** together characterize the repentance that leads to salvation.

May the Lord grant this repentance to each of us.

## Mutual encouragement through Titus and the Corinthians (verses 12–16)

Paul's confidence in the Corinthians had not been disappointed.

He had shared that confidence with **Titus**, and the good news now allowed him to look toward their future with joy.

This passage teaches the mutual responsibilities of **pastors and congregations**.

Believers should ease the difficulties of pastoral service by showing **respect and willing obedience**.

Pastors, in turn, should care faithfully for their people and encourage them by expressing **satisfaction, joy, affection and tenderness** when they respond well.
