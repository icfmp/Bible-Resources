# Philippians 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle offers up thanksgivings and prayers, for the good work of grace in the Philippians. | 1–7 |
| He expresses affection, and prays for them. | 8–11 |
| Fortifies them against being cast down at his sufferings. | 12–20 |
| He stood prepared for glorifying Christ by life, or death. | 21–26 |
| Exhortations to zeal, and constancy in professing the gospel. | 27–30 |

## Thanksgiving for God's work among the Philippians (verses 1–7)

The greatest honor of even the most distinguished minister is **to be a servant of Jesus Christ**.

Those who are not genuinely saints here on earth will never become saints in heaven.

Apart from Christ, even the best believers remain sinners, unable to stand before a holy God.

There is **no genuine peace without grace**.

True inward peace grows from knowing God's favor.

And all grace and peace originate with **God our Father**, the fountain of every blessing.

Paul had been badly treated at Philippi and had seen relatively little immediate fruit from his efforts.

Yet he remembered the believers there **with joy**.

We should thank God when other Christians grow in grace, receive comfort, develop gifts or become useful servants.

We benefit through their growth, and **God receives the glory**.

The work of divine grace will not reach perfection until **the day when Jesus Christ appears**.

Yet we may be confident that God will complete every genuine work of salvation he has begun through **regeneration**.

That confidence must not rest upon **outward appearances**, but upon evidence of **a new creation leading to holiness**.

Believers grow especially dear to the ministers through whose service they have received spiritual help.

Those who suffer together for God's cause should likewise hold one another dear.

## Paul prays for growing love and discernment (verses 8–11)

Should we not love and show compassion to the people whom **Christ loves and pities**?

No one possesses so much of a grace that no further growth is needed.

Paul wanted the believers to **recognize differences** and choose what is most excellent.

Christ's truths and commands really are excellent.

They reveal their excellence to an attentive mind.

**Sincerity** should govern our behavior and is an ornament to all Christian graces.

Believers should not be quick to take offense.

They should be especially careful not to offend **God or one another**.

What brings God the greatest honor also brings us the greatest good.

We should not leave it doubtful whether our lives bear **any spiritual fruit**.

A small amount of Christian love, discernment and fruitful service should never satisfy us.

## Paul's imprisonment advances the gospel (verses 12–20)

Paul wrote while **a prisoner in Rome**.

He wanted the Philippians not to be discouraged or ashamed of his suffering for Christ.

God's wisdom and goodness had turned his imprisonment into a means of advancing the gospel.

People who would otherwise never have known Paul or heard the message now learned about him and began asking questions.

Paul was troubled not only by enemies but also by **supposed friends who preached Christ out of envy and rivalry**, hoping to make his imprisonment harder.

What a miserable attitude toward a faithful servant of God!

Yet Paul remained **composed and joyful**.

If our troubles lead to blessings for other people, we should take comfort and even rejoice.

Every experience that contributes to our salvation does so through **the Spirit of Jesus Christ**.

Prayer is the means God has appointed for seeking that help.

Our highest ambition should not be to win human applause or escape the cross.

We should hope to remain **faithful under temptation, contempt and suffering**.

Let Christ decide how we can best serve his glory.

He may use us through **hard work or suffering**, through **diligence or patience**, by our **living** to honor him or even by our **dying** in his service.

## To live is Christ; to die is gain (verses 21–26)

For a person who lives only for this world, death is **great loss**.

That person loses earthly pleasures, possessions and hopes.

But for a genuine believer, **death is gain**.

It ends remaining weakness and misery, delivers the believer from the troubles of life and brings the soul into possession of the **highest good: Christ himself**.

Paul's difficult choice was not whether earthly existence was better than heaven.

There is no comparison between those two conditions.

His struggle was between **two good things**: **serving Christ here** or **enjoying Christ's presence in heaven**.

Faith and divine grace can make believers willing to die.

On earth we continue struggling against sin.

With Christ, we will be free forever from **sin, temptation, grief and death**.

Nevertheless, those who have the strongest reason to desire heaven should remain **willing to live and serve** as long as God still has work for them.

When God gives mercies we did not expect, his providential hand becomes especially evident in them.

## Live as citizens worthy of the gospel (verses 27–30)

People who profess Christ's gospel should behave like those who **believe its teachings, submit to its commands and rely upon its promises**.

The word translated **conduct or conversation** originally evokes **the life of citizens** who seek their city's honor, peace, safety and prosperity.

The gospel contains truths so valuable that they are worth **struggling to defend and practice**.

Opposition is real, and we must persevere.

People may drift carelessly toward destruction.

Those seeking heaven must remain **watchful and diligent**.

Christians can enjoy **unity of heart and affection** even when they differ in judgment on many lesser matters.

**Faith is God's gift** for Christ's sake.

The ability and willingness to trust him come from God.

If we suffer disgrace or loss for Christ's sake, we should also regard that opportunity as **a gift**.

But the bodily suffering itself does **not earn salvation**.

Persecution is not meritorious.

Our salvation comes **from God alone**, and both **faith and patience** are his gracious gifts.
