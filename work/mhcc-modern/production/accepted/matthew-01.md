## The genealogy of Jesus Christ (verses 1–17)

This genealogy of our Savior has a clear and necessary purpose.

It is not a pointless list of ancestors or a display of family greatness such as worldly leaders sometimes produce.

It establishes that **Jesus belonged to the nation and family from which the Messiah was promised**.

God promised Abraham that a blessing would come through his descendants.

He promised David that the kingdom would be established through his descendants.

The promises concerning **Abraham's offspring** and **David's throne** must therefore be fulfilled in one who belongs to both their families.

Henry points to **2 Samuel 7:12, Psalm 89:3 and Psalm 132:11**.

Unless Jesus truly descended from David and Abraham, he could not be the promised Messiah.

Matthew establishes that descent through recognized records.

The **Son of God** willingly took on our human nature.

He came near to us in our fallen, miserable condition.

Yet he remained **entirely free from sin**.

When we read the names in his genealogy, we should remember how low **the Lord of glory** was willing to stoop in order to save humanity.

## An angel reassures Joseph (verses 18–25)

Consider the humble circumstances in which the Son of God entered this world.

They teach us to value **godliness and holiness** above worldly glory and status.

The mystery of Christ becoming human should be adored rather than treated as an object of idle speculation.

He took our nature while remaining pure from **the original sin that has affected Adam's descendants**.

Joseph was troubled and considered what to do.

God guides thoughtful people who seriously consider their duty, not those who never reflect.

Often his instruction comes when his servants do not know what to do next.

Divine consolation is especially precious when the soul is burdened by uncertainty.

The angel announced that Mary would give birth to **the Savior of the world**.

Joseph was to name the child **Jesus**, meaning Savior, a name corresponding to **Joshua**.

The name describes his work.

**He saves his people from their sins.**

He removes **sin's guilt through the merit of his death** and breaks **sin's power through the Spirit of his grace**.

In saving them from sin, he saves them from divine wrath, the curse and every ultimate misery, both now and hereafter.

Christ came not to make people comfortable **in** their sins, but to rescue them **from** their sins.

He redeems them to belong to himself, the One set apart from sinners.

Joseph promptly and cheerfully obeyed the angel, without arguing or delaying.

In every step of life, especially during major decisions, we should seek God's direction by applying **the general principles of his written word**.

That is the safe and comforting course.
