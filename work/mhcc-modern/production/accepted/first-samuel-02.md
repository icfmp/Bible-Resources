# 1 Samuel 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Hannah praises the Lord (verses 1–10)

Hannah's heart rejoiced not chiefly in her son Samuel, but **in the Lord**.

She looked beyond the gift and praised the One who had given it.

She rejoiced in God's salvation and looked ahead to the coming of the One who is the complete salvation of his people.

God can weaken the strong and strengthen the weak whenever he chooses.

If we are poor, the Lord has allowed our poverty; this should encourage contentment with our circumstances.

If we are rich, he has provided our wealth; this should encourage gratitude, cheerful service and generosity toward others.

God is not impressed by worldly wisdom or the qualities people boast about.

He chooses those whom the world considers foolish, teaches them their guilt and helps them recognize the priceless value of his freely given salvation.

Hannah's prophecy looks forward to **the kingdom of Christ**.

After describing the Lord's rule over his providential world, she speaks of his kingdom of grace.

Here Henry identifies the **first appearance of the title “Messiah,” or “Anointed.”**

Those who belong to Christ's kingdom will be kept safe.

Its enemies will be ruined.

The Anointed One, the Lord Jesus Christ, has power both to save and to judge.

## Eli's sons act wickedly; Samuel serves faithfully (verses 11–26)

Samuel had been set apart for God's service in a special way.

From childhood he performed the sanctuary duties appropriate to his ability.

Because his heart was devoted to God, Scripture describes even those simple tasks as **ministering to the Lord**.

The Lord blessed him.

Young people who serve God as faithfully as they can will be helped to improve and serve him still better.

Eli, however, avoided inconvenience and exertion.

He indulged his children instead of using parental authority to restrain and correct them while they were young.

He overlooked abuses at the sanctuary until they became established habits and led to serious wrongdoing.

His sons should have been teaching worshipers what was good.

Instead, they encouraged them toward evil.

Their wrongdoing happened even in the setting of sacrifices for sin, which pictured the Savior's atonement.

**To sin against the very provision for atonement is especially dangerous**.

It amounts to trampling the blood of the covenant underfoot.

Eli's rebuke was much too weak and gentle.

Children brought up in godly households may become especially corrupt if they break through every restraint.

## A prophecy announces judgment on Eli's household (verses 27–36)

Parents who knowingly allow children to continue in wrongdoing and make no proper effort to restrain them are effectively **honoring their children more than God**.

Eli's example should move parents to oppose the first signs of wickedness and raise children in the Lord's teaching and correction.

Yet amid the judgment pronounced on Eli's house, God promised mercy to Israel.

His work will not fail because there are no servants available to carry it out.

Jesus Christ is the **merciful and faithful High Priest** whom God raised up when the Levitical priesthood gave way.

He perfectly performed his Father's will.

God will build him a lasting house, founded securely on a rock, against which hell itself cannot prevail.
