# 1 Corinthians 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A salutation and thanksgiving. | 1–9 |
| Exhortation to brotherly love, and reproof for divisions. | 10–16 |
| The doctrine of a crucified Saviour, as advancing the glory of God, | 17–25 |
| and humbling the creature before him. | 26–31 |

## Paul's greeting and thanksgiving (verses 1–9)

Through baptism, people who profess Christianity are dedicated to Christ and obligated to holiness.

But the **true church of God** consists of those **sanctified in Christ Jesus**, called to be saints, and who call upon Christ as **God manifested in human flesh** for every blessing of salvation.

They acknowledge and obey him as **their Lord and the Lord of all**.

This is what marks genuine members of his church.

Christians are distinguished from those who disregard God by the fact that they **cannot live without prayer**.

They are distinguished from Jews and pagans in that they **call upon the name of Jesus Christ**.

Notice how frequently Paul names **our Lord Jesus Christ** in these verses.

He was not afraid to speak of Christ often or give him too much honor.

To everyone calling on Christ, Paul offered his familiar greeting of **grace and peace**.

He desired that God's mercy would pardon them, his grace would sanctify them and his peace would comfort them, all **through Jesus Christ**.

Sinners have no peace with God and receive no true peace from him apart from Christ.

Paul thanked God for their conversion and for the grace Christ had given them.

They had been enriched with spiritual gifts, including **speech and knowledge**.

When God gives both, he provides great opportunities to be useful.

These gifts were works of the Holy Spirit, through which God confirmed the apostles' witness.

Those waiting for Jesus' return will be **preserved by him to the end** and declared blameless at his appearing through rich and freely given grace.

What a glorious hope—to be kept by **Christ's power** against inward corruption and Satan's temptations!

## Paul calls the church away from division (verses 10–16)

In the central truths of religion, believers should **agree in understanding**.

Where opinions still differ, they should remain **united in affection**.

Agreement in major matters should outweigh disputes about lesser ones.

There will be perfect unity in heaven.

The closer believers approach that unity here, the closer they come to Christian maturity.

Paul and Apollos were both faithful servants of Christ, helping believers in faith and joy.

But contentious people organized themselves into **rival factions**.

Even the best institutions can be corrupted: the gospel itself may be misused as a weapon for quarrelling.

Satan has always treated **division among Christians** as one of his chief methods of opposing the gospel.

Paul allowed other ministers to perform most baptisms while he focused on preaching, which he regarded as **the more useful task** entrusted to him.

## Christ crucified reveals divine wisdom (verses 17–25)

Paul had received extensive Jewish learning.

Yet the **plain preaching of Jesus crucified** proved more powerful than the combined rhetoric and philosophy of the pagan world.

This is **the heart of the gospel**.

Christ crucified is the foundation of our hope and the source of our joy.

**Through his death we live**.

Preaching that lost sinners are saved through the suffering and death of God's Son seems foolish to those moving toward destruction.

People controlled by sensuality, greed, pride or ambition recognize that the gospel threatens the pursuits they love.

But those who receive the gospel and are enlightened by the Holy Spirit discover more divine wisdom and power in **the crucified Christ** than in any other work of God.

God allowed much of the world to follow the guidance of its proudly asserted human wisdom.

History shows that this wisdom could not discover or preserve a right knowledge of God the Creator.

God chose to save believers through **what the worldly call the foolishness of preaching**.

This does **not** mean preaching that deserves to be called foolish.

Rather, the *message* of Christ appears foolish to people proud of worldly wisdom.

Plain proclamation of Christ continues to distinguish those responding to God's saving grace from those refusing it.

The world despises **salvation by faith in a crucified Savior**, who is God in human nature and purchased the church with **his own blood**.

Yet God has used that message through every generation to save multitudes who believe, freeing them from ignorance, deception and wickedness.

Even the weakest instruments God employs are more effective than humanity's greatest natural strength.

There is **no foolishness or weakness in God**.

What people call foolish or weak defeats their most admired wisdom and power.

## God chooses the lowly to humble human pride (verses 26–31)

God did not chiefly choose the world's philosophers, eloquent speakers, political leaders or wealthy and influential figures to proclaim his gospel.

He knows which people and methods most serve **his glory**.

Not many noble people are called, but some have been called in every age.

They too need forgiveness, and some have gladly confessed Christ despite their status.

A humble believer who possesses little wealth or formal education may understand the gospel better than a scholar who studies Scripture merely **as human writing instead of the word of God**.

Even children have sometimes understood divine truths well enough to answer unbelievers.

The reason is that **God himself taught them**.

The purpose is that **no human being should boast before him**.

Our relationship to Christ does not arise from our own wisdom or effort.

Through God's sovereign choice and regenerating grace we are brought into Jesus Christ by faith.

**Christ becomes for us wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption**.

He gives us everything we need or could rightly desire.

As **Wisdom**, through his word and Spirit, Christ teaches us from the fullness of his knowledge and makes us wise for salvation and service.

As **Righteousness**, he is the atoning sacrifice for guilty sinners deserving judgment.

As **Sanctification**, he remedies our fallen corruption.

And as **Redemption**, he will finally free the soul from all sin and the body from the grave.

All this fulfills the word of **Jeremiah 9:23–24**, that every person should boast only in **the Lord's favor, sufficient grace and precious salvation**.
