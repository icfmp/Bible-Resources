# Deuteronomy 25 — Modern English

## Punishment must respect human dignity (verses 1–3)

Every punishment was to be carried out solemnly.

Those who saw it should be impressed by its seriousness and warned against similar wrongdoing.

The guilty might be shamed and made to suffer, but care had to be taken not to treat them as completely vile or without human dignity.

Happy are those whom the Lord disciplines in this life to humble them, so that they may not share in the world's final condemnation.

## An ox working in the grain (verse 4)

Farmers were commanded to care for the animals working on their behalf.

But the command also teaches a wider duty.

We should be not only fair but kind toward everyone whose work benefits our souls (1 Corinthians 9:9).

## Marriage to a brother's widow (verses 5–12)

The custom regulated here seems to have been established under Jewish law to keep family inheritances distinct.

Henry states that **the same practice is not lawful now**.

## The use of honest weights (verses 13–16)

Dishonestly obtained wealth brings a curse upon a person's possessions, family and soul.

Happy are those who examine their own lives, repent of wrongdoing, turn away from sin and remove evil from their conduct before God brings judgment upon them.

## Remembering Amalek (verses 17–19)

Everyone who persecutes or wrongs God's people should take warning from Amalek's history.

The longer judgment is delayed, the more fearful it may become when it finally arrives.

Amalek can also picture the enemies of our own souls.

May God enable us to put to death every sinful desire and corrupt influence within us or around us, together with the powers of darkness and worldly opposition that hinder us from coming to our blessed Savior.
