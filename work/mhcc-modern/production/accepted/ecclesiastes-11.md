# Ecclesiastes 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortation to liberality. | 1–6 |
| An admonition to prepare for death, and to young persons to be religious. | 7–10 |

## Give generously and continue doing good (verses 1–6)

Solomon calls people who possess wealth to **do good to others**.

Give generously, even when it seems that what you give has been thrown away or lost.

Help many people.

Do not use the good you have already done as an excuse for refusing further opportunities to help.

Such generosity is not wasted but wisely invested.

We should expect that difficult times will sometimes come, since human beings are born to trouble.

That is one reason to do good while we are prosperous.

Riches cannot truly benefit us if we refuse to use them for the good of others.

Every person should labor to become a blessing in the community where **God's Providence has placed them**.

Wherever we live, there is useful work for us if our hearts are willing.

But if we magnify every small obstacle, invent objections and imagine terrible hardships, we will never begin the work, much less finish it.

Winds and clouds of trouble are in God's hands.

He uses them to test us.

His work will fulfill his word, whether or not we can see how.

We may trust the Lord to provide for us without constant anxious and distressing worry.

Do not grow tired of doing good.

**At the proper time, God's time, you will reap** (**Galatians 6:9**).

## Young people should prepare for death and eternity (verses 7–10)

Life is sweet to people who reject God because **the present world is their only portion**.

Life is also sweet to godly people because it allows them to prepare for something far better.

In that sense, life is sweet to all.

Yet even when life seems most pleasant, we should remember death is approaching.

Solomon speaks particularly to **young people**.

They long to pursue many pleasures.

He challenges them: follow those desires if you wish, but remember that **God will bring you into judgment**.

How many young people indulge every appetite and rush toward destructive pleasures!

God records their sinful thoughts and desires, their idle words and their wicked speech.

Those who want to escape remorse and terror, possess comfort and hope near death, and avoid lasting misery must recognize the **vanity of sinful pleasures in youth**.

Henry emphasizes that Solomon is condemning **sinful pleasures**, not legitimate joy.

His goal is to lead the young toward purer and lasting happiness.

Solomon does not speak out of envy because he is too old to share in youthful amusement.

He speaks, in Henry's account, as someone **rescued through a remarkable act of divine mercy** from a dangerous course that few people escape.

He wants young people not to repeat his experience.

If you desire genuine happiness now and lasting happiness afterward, **remember your Creator during your youth**.
