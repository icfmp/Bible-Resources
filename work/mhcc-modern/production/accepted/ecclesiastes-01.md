# Ecclesiastes 1 — Modern English

## Solomon declares the world's vanity (verses 1–3)

We can learn a great deal by comparing one part of Scripture with another.

Here we see Solomon returning **from the world's broken and empty cisterns to the Fountain of living water**.

He records the foolishness and shame of his former choices, the bitterness of disappointment and the lessons he learned.

People who have themselves been warned and have turned away from death should warn others not to continue toward destruction.

Solomon does not merely say that worldly things are *vain*. He says they are **vanity itself**.

**“Vanity of vanities! All is vanity!”**

This is the central theme of the Preacher's message, and he never loses sight of it throughout this book.

If life in this fallen world were all we could look forward to, living for it would be futile.

Even the greatest abundance of wealth and pleasure cannot make a human being happy.

What do we gain from all our work?

We cannot use its profits to meet **the soul's needs**, satisfy its desires, atone for its sins or prevent its destruction.

What will worldly wealth profit the soul **at death, in judgment or in eternity**?

## Human work never finally satisfies (verses 4–8)

Everything changes, and nothing remains at rest.

Despite all human effort, people are no nearer to finding true rest than **the sun, the wind or the flowing river**.

Our souls will find no lasting rest unless it comes **from God**.

Our bodily senses soon grow weary of what they experience, yet they still long for something new and untried.

## Nothing is truly new (verses 9–11)

Human hearts are still marked by the same fallen corruption as in earlier generations.

Their desires, pursuits and complaints have not fundamentally changed.

Recognizing this should lead us to stop expecting happiness from created things and to seek **eternal blessings** instead.

How many people and accomplishments were celebrated as extraordinary in Solomon's time, yet no one remembers them now?

## Even great knowledge leaves the soul unsatisfied (verses 12–18)

Solomon examined every kind of pursuit and found **vanity**.

Even his studies and investigations into knowledge wearied him, not only physically but mentally.

The more deeply he considered what happens under the sun, the more clearly he saw its emptiness, and the more troubled he became.

He could neither gain the satisfaction he hoped for nor accomplish as much good for others as he had imagined.

Pursuing wisdom and knowledge exposed human wickedness and misery.

The more Solomon learned, the more reason he saw for sorrow.

Let us therefore **hate and fear sin**, the cause of the world's emptiness and misery.

Let us value **Christ** and seek our rest in knowing, loving and serving **the Savior**.
