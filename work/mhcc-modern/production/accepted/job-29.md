# Job 29 — Modern English

## Job remembers former comfort (verses 1–6)

Job contrasted his earlier prosperity with his present misery, when he felt that God's comforting presence had been withdrawn.

A soul governed by grace rejoices more in **God's favor** than in the world's approval.

Job had once enjoyed **four particular blessings**.

**First**, confidence in God's protection.

**Second**, the enjoyment of God's favor.

**Third**, fellowship with God's word.

**Fourth**, assurance of God's presence.

God's presence can turn even a small cottage into a fortress and palace.

Job had also enjoyed happiness in his family.

But wealth and flourishing households can be extinguished as quickly as a candle.

When the Holy Spirit enlightens the mind and people live in the light of God's countenance, every outward comfort is doubled and every difficulty becomes easier to bear.

This light guides them cheerfully through life and through death.

Yet the conscious comfort of God's presence may be withdrawn for a time.

Often this follows spiritual neglect or grieving the Holy Spirit.

Sometimes it is instead a test of faith and grace.

We should examine ourselves, seek the cause through earnest prayer and become more watchful.

## Job recalls his public usefulness (verses 7–17)

People of every social rank had honored Job, not merely because of his position but because of his wisdom, honesty, integrity and good management.

Those endowed with such abilities have great opportunities to honor God and serve others.

They must also guard carefully against pride.

A community blessed with good leaders has reason for gratitude.

Job's own measure of the value of his prosperous life was **his usefulness**.

He restrained violent and arrogant people and protected the vulnerable.

Good public officials should similarly hold back evildoers and defend innocent people, using courage and determination.

Such people are blessings to society.

They resemble the Savior, who rescues poor sinners from Satan.

How many who were about to perish now bless him!

Let us trust his mercy and imitate his truth, justice and love.

## Job recalls his expectation of a peaceful old age (verses 18–25)

Because Job was respected and useful, he expected to die in peace and honor at an advanced age.

Such hope is good when it grows from active faith in God's promises and Providence.

But if it rests upon confidence in our own wisdom or in changeable earthly circumstances, the foundation is wrong and becomes sinful.

Not everyone who has wisdom is gifted for leadership.

**Job had both wisdom and the ability to govern**.

He also possessed a tender heart that could console other people.

Those memories were precious to him now that he was mourning.

Jesus Christ is the true **King who hates wickedness** and upon whom the blessing of a world ready to perish rests.

Let us listen to him.
