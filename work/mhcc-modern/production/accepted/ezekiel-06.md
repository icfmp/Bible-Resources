# Ezekiel 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Divine judgments for idolatry. | 1–7 |
| A remnant shall be saved. | 8–10 |
| The calamities are to be lamented. | 11–14 |

## Idolatry brings God's judgments (verses 1–7)

War lays waste to people, places and even things that are regarded as **most sacred**.

God can destroy idolatry through **the hands of idolaters themselves**.

He is right to turn into a desolation anything we have made **an idol**.

Superstitions upon which people rely for safety often become the cause of their destruction.

Henry looks forward to a day when **idols and idolatry will be removed from the professedly Christian church** as thoroughly as they were eventually removed from among the Jews.

## A small remnant will be preserved (verses 8–10)

A remnant of Israel would survive.

In time these people would remember **the Lord**, their obligations to him and their earlier rebellion.

Those who sincerely repent recognize sin as **the hateful thing God abhors**.

They become deeply ashamed of themselves because of the sins they committed.

Their repentance brings **glory to God**.

Whatever awakens us to remember him and to recognize the wrong we have done against him should be received as **a blessing**.

## Lamentation and compassion for sinners (verses 11–14)

Our duty is not only to grieve over our **own sin and suffering**.

We should also have compassion for the misery that other people bring upon themselves through wickedness.

**Sin devastates.**

Therefore stand in awe of God and turn away from it.

If we appreciate the worth of **human souls** and the danger facing those who do not believe, we will consider the salvation of even **one sinner who flees to Jesus** an ample reward for all the contempt or opposition we may encounter in serving him.
