# Genesis 23 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Sarah dies; Abraham seeks a burial place. | 1–13 |
| Abraham purchases the place where Sarah will be buried. | 14–20 |

## Sarah's death and Abraham's request (verses 1–13)

Even the longest human life must come to an end before long.

Blessed be God that there is another world into which sin, death, vanity, and trouble can never enter!

Blessed be his name that not even death can separate believers from their union with Christ.

Those we love most deeply must soon die.

Even our own bodies, which we care for so tenderly, will become lifeless clay and be buried out of sight.

How lightly, then, we ought to hold earthly possessions and outward adornments!

Instead, let us seek to adorn our souls with the graces of heaven.

Abraham showed honor and respect to the leaders of the Hittites, though they belonged to the ungodly Canaanites.

The religion of the Bible requires us to show proper respect to people in authority.

But this does not mean flattering those who hold office or excusing their wrongdoing when their character is unworthy.

And the generous conduct of these Canaanites puts to shame the stinginess, selfishness, and bad temper of many who call themselves God's people.

Abraham did not refuse their gift out of pride or because he thought himself too important to owe Ephron anything.

He refused out of justice and wisdom.

Abraham could afford to pay for the field, so he would not take advantage of Ephron's generosity.

Both honesty and honor forbid us to exploit another person's kindness or impose upon those who freely offer us help.

## Abraham purchases Sarah's burial place (verses 14–20)

Wisdom and justice alike teach us to deal fairly and openly with others.

A dishonest bargain cannot bear close examination.

Abraham paid the price without fraud or delay.

He paid the full amount at once, holding nothing back.

He weighed out the money in the currency accepted by merchants, without deceit.

Money has served trade since ancient times. We should pay honestly what we owe, when payment is due.

God had promised Abraham all the land of Canaan.

But the time when he would possess it had not yet arrived.

So Abraham bought and paid for the land he needed.

**Earthly dominion does not come from grace.**

A believer's title to an eternal inheritance does not give that person a right to the property of others in this world or excuse doing them wrong.

Ephron, for his part, made the transfer honestly and gave Abraham a secure title to the land.

Just as buyers must pay fairly for what they purchase, sellers must deliver and guarantee honestly what they sell.

We should conduct business carefully and punctually so that quarrels can be avoided.

Abraham buried Sarah in the cave or vault on the field he had bought.

This would help make the land dear to his descendants.

It is remarkable that the only piece of land Abraham ever owned in Canaan was a burial place.

Those who possess little of this world will still find a grave in it.

The tomb lay at the end of the field.

Whatever land we own, there is a burial place at the end of it.

The burial also showed Abraham's faith and hope in the resurrection.

He was content to live as a stranger and traveler on earth.

But he secured a place where his body could rest in hope after death.

In the end, our greatest concern must be this: **with whom will we rise?**
