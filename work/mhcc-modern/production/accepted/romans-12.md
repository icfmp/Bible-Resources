# Romans 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Believers are to dedicate themselves to God. | 1, 2 |
| To be humble, and faithfully to use their spiritual gifts, in their respective stations. | 3–8 |
| Exhortations to various duties. | 9–16 |
| And to peaceable conduct towards all men, with forbearance and benevolence. | 17–21 |

## Offer yourselves to God (verses 1 and 2)

Having completed the part of the letter in which he explained Christian doctrine, Paul now urges believers to live out its consequences.

He appeals to them **by the mercies of God** to present their bodies as living sacrifices.

How powerful a reason this is!

Every day we receive fresh evidence of the Lord's mercy.

In response, let us offer him **everything we are, everything we possess and everything we can do**.

Even then, how small is our return compared with the riches we have received!

Such worship is **acceptable and reasonable**.

It is service that we can understand and explain.

Conversion and sanctification involve **the renewing of the mind**.

They do not change the substance of the human soul but transform its qualities and direction.

Growing in holiness means **dying increasingly to sin and living increasingly for righteousness**.

This renewing work continues until it is perfected in glory.

Its great enemy is **conformity to the world**.

Do not plan your happiness as though it depended upon earthly things that will soon vanish.

Do not adopt the customs of those ruled by bodily desires and love of passing things.

The Holy Spirit's work begins by illuminating **the understanding**.

He then renews **the will, affections and conduct** until the whole person increasingly resembles God in knowledge, righteousness and true holiness.

**To be godly is to give ourselves to God**.

## Humility and useful gifts within Christ's body (verses 3–8)

Pride comes naturally to fallen humanity, and we must guard against it.

All believers form **one body in Christ**, who is its Head and the center of their unity.

Different members are fitted and called to different forms of service.

Our aim must be to do all the good we can **for one another and for the common benefit**.

Considering the abilities entrusted to us and how far we fall short of using them properly should humble us.

But we must avoid the opposite mistake.

Under the pretense of humility and self-denial, we must not become **lazy or inactive in serving others**.

We should not say, “I am nothing, so I will sit still and do nothing.”

Rather: **“I am nothing in myself; therefore, through Christ's grace, I will use every ability I have.”**

Whatever our gifts and circumstances, let us serve humbly, diligently, cheerfully and sincerely.

Seek **not personal credit or gain**, but the good of others for this life and the life to come.

## Sincere love and faithful service (verses 9–16)

Christian love must be genuine, free from hypocrisy and empty compliments.

Depending on God's grace, we should hate evil and hold firmly to everything good, kind and useful.

It is not enough merely to perform good actions occasionally.

**We must cling to goodness**.

Love summarizes all our duties toward one another.

Paul calls for the tender affection of parents toward children—natural, sincere, neither forced nor merely ceremonial.

Love for God and others, together with zeal for the gospel, makes wise Christians **diligent even in ordinary business**, improving their skills to serve well.

We must serve God spiritually, under the Holy Spirit's influence.

We honor him by trusting him, especially when we **rejoice in hope**.

God is served not only by work.

When he calls us to endure suffering, **patiently waiting without rebellion** is also true worship.

Those rejoicing in hope are better prepared to be patient in suffering.

We must persevere in prayer without growing cold or weary.

Kindness must extend beyond friends and fellow believers.

We should harbor no hatred toward enemies.

Love expressed only in kind words is false if a brother or sister needs material help and **we can provide it but refuse**.

Welcome people doing good work; show hospitality to strangers as opportunities arise.

**Bless and do not curse**.

Do not bless someone during prayer and curse the same person afterward.

Let goodwill be consistent.

Genuine Christian love shares other people's **joys and sorrows**.

Seek agreement in spiritual truth wherever possible.

Where believers cannot agree about every detail, they should still **agree in affection**.

Do not be impressed by worldly pomp, rank or public reputation.

Accept the place assigned by God's Providence.

**Nothing is beneath us except sin**.

We cannot sincerely serve humbler people while remaining full of our own importance.

That pride must be put to death.

## Peace, forgiveness and overcoming evil (verses 17–21)

Since human beings became enemies of God, they have readily become enemies of one another.

Christians living in a world often unfriendly to Christ should expect opposition.

**Never repay evil with evil**.

Such retaliation belongs to people acting only on instinct, without regard for God or eternity.

Instead, carefully consider how to act **honorably and commendably** toward everyone, giving no needless reason to despise the Christian faith.

Seek peace when it is possible **without offending God or wounding conscience**.

Do not take personal revenge.

This instruction is difficult for fallen nature, so Paul also says to **leave room for anger to pass**.

When someone is intensely angry, allow time for their feelings to settle rather than escalating the confrontation.

Our duty is clear.

If persistent kindness does not soften an enemy, we still must not seek vengeance.

Judgment belongs to God.

In conflict, those who retaliate are **defeated by evil**, while those who forgive become **conquerors**.

Learn to defeat evil designs either by changing the other person's intention or by preserving **your own peace**.

Someone who rules their own spirit is stronger than a mighty warrior.

God's children should recognize what a sweet gift it is to feel and act this way through his Spirit.
