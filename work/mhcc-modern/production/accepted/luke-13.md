# Luke 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ exhorts to repentance from the case of the Galileans and others. | 1–5 |
| Parable of the barren fig-tree. | 6–9 |
| The infirm woman strengthened. | 10–17 |
| The parables of the mustard seed, and leaven. | 18–22 |
| Exhortation to enter at the strait gate. | 23–30 |
| Christ's reproof to Herod, and to the people of Jerusalem. | 31–35 |

## Repent instead of judging victims (verses 1–5)

People told Jesus how some Galileans had been killed.

Henry observes that this tragedy is recorded here briefly but is not described by the historians known to him.

Jesus also mentioned people killed when a tower collapsed.

Buildings constructed for protection can become the occasion of a person's death.

He warned his listeners **not to conclude that unusually great suffering proves unusually great sin**.

No place or occupation can protect someone from sudden death.

Rather than condemning those who suffer, let the death of others awaken us to our own need to repent.

Jesus calls us to repentance because God's kingdom is near, and warns that **unless we repent, we too will perish**.

## The barren fig tree (verses 6–9)

The parable reinforces this warning: a fruitless tree is eventually cut down.

Henry understands it **first as applying to the Jewish people and nation**.

But it also warns everyone who enjoys the privileges of God's visible church and the opportunities offered through his grace.

The Lord has endured our barrenness patiently.

We may hope for further patience, but cannot presume that judgment will be postponed forever.

## The woman healed on the Sabbath (verses 10–17)

Jesus regularly attended public worship on the Sabbath.

Bodily weakness, unless extremely serious, should not ordinarily prevent us from taking part in worship.

The woman came to hear teaching and receive spiritual good, and Jesus then relieved her physical suffering.

Her healing pictures **crooked souls made straight by God's grace**, which then glorify God.

Jesus saw that the synagogue leader's apparent concern for Sabbath observance masked **hostility toward Jesus and his gospel**.

He did not really want the woman healed on any day.

But the Savior's word and power can free those who are in bondage.

Henry observes that spiritual deliverance is often experienced on the Lord's day.

Work that helps someone receive God's blessings fits the purpose of the day.

## The mustard seed and leaven (verses 18–22)

As in **Matthew 13**, these parables show the advance of the gospel.

The Messiah's kingdom is the kingdom of God.

May grace grow in our hearts!

May our faith and love increase so clearly that they give convincing evidence of genuine spiritual life.

May God bless those who live near faithful believers through their example.

May grace spread from heart to heart until something once small grows into a multitude.

## Enter through the narrow gate (verses 23–30)

Our Savior came to direct **consciences**, not satisfy curiosity.

Instead of **How many will be saved?**, ask **Will I be among them?**

Instead of debating another person's end, ask **What must I do, and what will become of me?**

Each of us must personally strive to enter through the narrow gate.

Those who are saved undergo a **change of the whole person**.

The warning is urgent.

But no one should despair about themselves or others.

Some who are first will be last, and some last will be first.

In heaven we may meet people we never thought would be there and miss others whom we confidently expected.

## Jesus speaks of Herod and Jerusalem (verses 31–35)

Jesus accurately called Herod **a fox**.

Even great rulers must answer to God, and Christ had authority to expose this proud ruler's character.

**Henry explicitly says this is not an example for us to imitate** in insulting rulers.

Jesus knew he would die soon.

When his appointed task was completed, he would be perfected in the sense of having finished the work he came to do.

We should regard our remaining time as short and fulfill **today's duty today**.

Jesus is particularly grieved by the wickedness of people and places that profess a special relationship with God.

The final judgment will convince unbelievers.

Let us therefore welcome and profit from those who come **in the Lord's name** to invite us to share Christ's saving grace.
