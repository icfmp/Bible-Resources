# Romans 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The rejection of the Jews is not universal. | 1–10 |
| God overruled their unbelief for making the Gentiles partakers of gospel privileges. | 11–21 |
| The Gentiles cautioned against pride and unbelief, The Jews shall be called as a nation, and brought into God's visible covenant again. | 22–32 |
| A solemn adoring of the wisdom, goodness, and justice of God. | 33–36 |

## God preserves a believing Jewish remnant (verses 1–10)

God preserved **a chosen remnant of Jewish believers**, who received righteousness and life through faith in Jesus Christ.

They were kept according to **the election of grace**.

If their election was by grace, it could not have been based on works, either **already performed or foreseen in advance**.

Every genuinely good disposition in a fallen creature is **the effect of God's grace**, and therefore cannot be its cause.

Salvation from first to last must be either **grace or a debt owed for merit**.

These principles are so opposed that they cannot be mixed.

God glorifies his mercy by changing the hearts and desires of people who were once rebellious.

How deeply should believers praise him!

The nation as a whole was like someone in deep sleep.

They failed to recognize danger or their need for the Savior and did not realize how close they stood to everlasting ruin.

David, moved by the Spirit, foretold Christ's sufferings at the hands of his own people and announced dreadful judgments upon those responsible (**Psalm 69**).

Henry explains David's prayers against enemies as **prophetic warnings of divine judgment**, not merely expressions of personal anger.

God's judgments endure, and worldly-mindedness can make our spiritual eyes dark.

## Gentiles are grafted into God's people (verses 11–21)

The gospel is **a community's greatest treasure**.

The unbelieving Jews' rejection of Christ became the occasion for many Gentiles to be reconciled to God.

Henry expects the future reception of Jews into the church to resemble **a great resurrection from spiritual death into righteous life**.

Abraham was like **the root of God's people**.

Jewish descendants remained branches of the olive tree until the nation rejected the Messiah.

Their inherited place in Abraham's covenant community was, figuratively, cut off.

Gentiles were **grafted into the tree**, becoming members of God's church and receiving Abraham's spiritual privileges.

Every human being is naturally like **a wild branch**.

Conversion resembles grafting such a branch into a fruitful olive tree.

Henry notes that a wild branch was sometimes grafted into a declining fruitful tree and helped it regain vitality.

Gentiles were received by **free grace**.

They should therefore avoid **self-confidence, pride and ambition**.

If they have only dead faith or an empty profession, they can turn away from God and lose their outward religious privileges.

If we stand, it is **through faith**, not personal worth.

Knowing our own guilt and helplessness should make us humble, watchful and alert against self-deception and temptation.

Not only is justification first received through faith.

Believers also remain justified to the end **by faith alone**, yet by a faith **never alone**, for it works through love toward God and other people.

## Gentiles warned; future mercy promised to Israel (verses 22–32)

Among all forms of judgment, **spiritual judgment is the most severe**.

Paul speaks especially of it here.

The future return of Jewish people to the blessings of God's covenant is not nearly as astonishing as the earlier inclusion of Gentiles among Abraham's children.

The fact that Gentiles now possess these blessings does not prevent Jewish people from being welcomed back.

By rejecting the gospel and resenting its proclamation to Gentiles, many Jews had become enemies of God.

Yet God's gracious promises to their faithful ancestors remained important.

When God's appointed time arrives, this enmity will end and his faithfulness to those earlier promises will be displayed.

**True grace never seeks to restrict God's mercy to a favored group**.

People who have received mercy should seek to extend it to others.

Henry makes an important qualification: the Jews' future restoration will **not** mean bringing back the Old Testament priesthood, temple worship or ceremonial system.

Those have come to their appointed end.

Rather, they will be brought to **faith in Jesus Christ** and become **one flock with Gentile believers under Christ the great Shepherd**.

Israel's captivities, dispersion and exclusion from visible covenant privileges picture the discipline believers may receive for wrongdoing.

God's continuing concern and promised future kindness toward this people demonstrate **his patience, love and mercy**.

## Paul worships God's incomprehensible wisdom (verses 33–36)

Paul understood the mysteries of God's kingdom as deeply as almost anyone who has lived.

Yet he confessed that he could not reach the bottom of God's wisdom.

He sat humbly on the edge of that immeasurable depth and worshiped.

Those who know the most in this imperfect life often recognize **their limitations most clearly**.

God's wisdom is not only deep but rich, overflowing with everything truly precious.

His counsel has depth and height, **breadth and length** (**Ephesians 3:18**), beyond human knowledge.

The distance between the infinite Creator and his creatures prevents us from fully comprehending his ways.

What human being is fit to teach God how to govern the world?

Paul adores God's sovereign wisdom.

Everything in heaven and earth, especially everything concerning our peace and salvation, comes **from God in creation**, exists **through him in Providence** and is intended **for his glory**.

God is the source of all.

Through Christ we return to God as our final end.

If all things are from and through him, all should be **to him and for him**.

Whatever we begin, let God's glory be our final purpose.

Above all, when speaking about his hidden counsels, let us respond in worship rather than self-confident argument.

**The saints in heaven do not dispute; they praise**.
