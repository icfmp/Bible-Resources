# 1 Samuel 27 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David retires to Gath. | 1–7 |
| David deceives Achish. | 8–12 |

## David seeks refuge in Gath (verses 1–7)

Even good people easily fall into unbelief when they face outward conflict and inward fear.

It is difficult to overcome both.

**Lord, increase our faith!**

We should be ashamed that a Philistine's word proved more reliable for David than an Israelite's word.

The city of **Gath** became a refuge for him because Israel's cities refused to provide a safe home.

David secured a comfortable place to live.

It was some distance from Gath, yet near enough to Israel that he could remain in contact with his own people.

## David deceives Achish (verses 8–12)

While living in Philistine territory, David attacked surviving peoples of nations that had been marked for ancient judgment.

Henry understands those enemies as having long been condemned.

It can be wise to avoid public notice, but we should never become idle.

In every situation, we should seek an appropriate opportunity to serve God's cause.

David concealed his expeditions from Achish.

But an ambiguous statement intended to mislead is no better in its moral purpose than an outright lie.

**Deceptive evasion is especially dangerous because it can appear respectable while carrying the same intention as falsehood.**

Even genuine believers often show serious imperfections.

But they cannot ultimately be persuaded to abandon God's service, unite their interests with his enemies or become permanent slaves of sin and Satan.

How many evils grow out of unbelief!

When we forget God's former mercies and his promises, anxiety and fear may overwhelm us.

Then we may adopt disgraceful means to escape our difficulties.

Nothing gives firmer steadiness in holy conduct or offers greater protection against needless distress than unwavering trust in **God's promises in Jesus Christ**.
