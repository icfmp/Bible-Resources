# Genesis 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God renews his covenant with Abram. | 1–6 |
| Circumcision is instituted. | 7–14 |
| Sarai receives a new name; Isaac is promised. | 15–22 |
| Abraham and his household are circumcised. | 23–27 |

## God renews the covenant with Abram (verses 1–6)

God's covenant would be fulfilled at the appointed time.

The promised Seed was Christ, and in him the promise extends to Christians.

All who live by faith share in the blessing given to faithful Abram. They receive the blessings of the same covenant.

As a sign of this covenant, God changed Abram's name.

*Abram* means “high father.”

*Abraham* means “father of a multitude.”

Everything the Christian world enjoys through the gospel is owed, under God, to Abraham and his promised Seed.

## Circumcision is appointed as a sign of the covenant (verses 7–14)

The covenant of grace is everlasting.

It is everlasting in God's purposes, which preceded time, and everlasting in the consequences that flow from it.

Circumcision was the sign of that covenant.

Scripture even calls it the covenant that Abraham and his descendants were required to keep.

Those who want the Lord to be their God must resolve to be his people.

Circumcision was not required of Abraham and Isaac alone, or only of the descendants who would come through Isaac.

Ishmael and the servants in Abraham's household were also to receive it.

The sign sealed more than the promise of the land of Canaan to Isaac's descendants.

It also pointed to the promise of heaven through Christ for the whole church of God.

The outward sign belongs to the visible church.

But the inward seal of the Holy Spirit belongs only to those whom God himself knows to be believers. Only God can know them with certainty.

God required this ordinance to be observed and attached a very severe penalty to neglecting it.

It is dangerous to despise God's appointed ordinances or to live as though they do not matter.

The covenant carried great blessings that would reach the whole world through generations yet to come.

Even Abraham's own happiness and the rewards God gave him came for Christ's sake.

Abraham was justified not by his own righteousness but by faith in the promised Messiah.

## Sarah's new name and the promise of Isaac (verses 15–22)

God promised Abraham that Sarah would give birth to a son, through whom the covenant promise would be fulfilled.

To confirm this, God changed Sarai's name to *Sarah*.

*Sarai* means “my princess,” as though her honor belonged to a single household.

*Sarah* simply means “princess,” suggesting a wider honor.

The more favors God gives us, the humbler we should become in our own eyes.

Abraham was filled with joy.

He laughed, but his laughter expressed delight rather than unbelief.

It was now that Abraham rejoiced to see the day of Christ. He saw it and was glad (John 8:56).

Abraham also feared that Ishmael might be abandoned or rejected by God. So he prayed for his son.

God allows us to bring particular requests to him in prayer.

Whatever troubles or frightens us should be laid openly before him.

Parents have a duty to pray for their children.

Above all, they should ask that their children may remain in covenant with God and receive the grace to walk uprightly before him.

God promised ordinary, earthly blessings to Ishmael.

For the sake of godly parents, he often gives outward good things even to children who do not share their parents' spiritual faith.

But the special covenant blessings were reserved for Isaac and assigned to him.

## Abraham and his household obey (verses 23–27)

Abraham and everyone in his household received circumcision.

By accepting the sign of the covenant, they marked themselves off from families that had no part in it.

Abraham's obedience was complete.

He did exactly what God commanded without asking why or demanding an explanation. He obeyed simply because God had spoken.

His obedience was also immediate.

He acted that very day.

Sincere obedience does not delay.

Not only the teachings of God's revelation, but also the signs of his covenant, remind us that we are guilty and defiled sinners.

They show us our need for the blood of atonement.

They point to the promised Savior and call us to place our faith in him.

They teach us that without new birth, holiness through the Spirit, and the putting to death of our corrupt desires, we cannot truly belong to God's covenant people.

Remember, however, that the true circumcision is circumcision of the heart, performed by the Spirit (Romans 2:28–29).

Under both the old covenant and the new, many people have professed faith and received the outward sign without ever being sealed by the Holy Spirit of promise.
