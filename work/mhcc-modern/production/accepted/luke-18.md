## The widow who persisted in seeking justice (verses 1–8)

All God's people are **people of prayer**.

Jesus taught us to persevere, especially in asking for spiritual blessings.

The widow persisted even before **an unjust judge**.

She might have feared that repeated requests would only increase his hostility.

Our situation is different: **our earnest prayers please our gracious God**.

Yet even to the end of the world, believers will need this warning because faith can grow weak.

## The Pharisee and the tax collector (verses 9–14)

Jesus told this parable to challenge those who **trusted their own righteousness and despised other people**.

God sees the motives and attitudes with which we approach him during worship.

The Pharisee trusted in his own supposed righteousness.

He may genuinely have avoided many public and scandalous sins.

Those were commendable things in themselves.

Many people live in ways far worse than this Pharisee, yet **he was not accepted by God**.

Why?

He entered the temple to pray, but his mind was filled with **himself and his own good actions**.

He did not consider God's grace something worth asking for.

Beware of proud religious behavior and contempt for others.

The tax collector's prayer expressed **humility, repentance and a longing for God**.

It was short but reached the point:

**“God, be merciful to me, a sinner.”**

How thankful we should be that this answered prayer has been preserved for us!

The man returned home **justified**.

Everyone who comes to God in the same humble faith through **Jesus Christ** can find that mercy.

The tax collector acknowledged his sin both by fallen nature and by actual practice.

He had nothing to rely on but **God's mercy alone**.

God resists the proud and gives grace to the humble.

**Justification comes from God through Christ**, so those who condemn their own sin rather than proclaim their own righteousness receive it.

## Children are brought to Jesus (verses 15–17)

No child is too small or too young to be brought to Christ.

Jesus knows how to show kindness even to those who are not yet able to serve him.

He desires that children be brought to him.

Henry speaks of the divine promise given to believers and their descendants, encouraging families to welcome children into the Savior's presence.

We must all receive God's kingdom **as children receive a gift**.

It cannot be bought.

We must acknowledge it as a gift from **our heavenly Father**.

## Riches hinder a ruler from following Jesus (verses 18–30)

People may have many admirable qualities yet perish because they lack **one essential thing**.

The ruler could not accept the terms Jesus set because they required surrendering his wealth.

Many people reluctantly leave Christ even when they would rather hold on to him.

Their conscience and sinful desires struggle, but their desires eventually prevail.

They wish they could serve both God and money.

When forced to choose, however, they surrender **God rather than worldly gain**.

Their supposed obedience proves to be only an outward appearance.

Some form of love for the world lies underneath.

Peter spoke too proudly about what the disciples had left and suffered for Jesus.

Instead of boasting about our sacrifices, we should be ashamed that serving the Savior ever caused us reluctance or regret.

## Jesus foretells his death (verses 31–34)

The **Spirit of Christ in the Old Testament prophets** had foretold both the Messiah's sufferings and the glory afterward (**1 Peter 1:11**).

The disciples' assumptions were so strong that they refused to understand Jesus' prediction literally.

They eagerly received prophecies about the Messiah's glory while overlooking those concerning his suffering.

We too misunderstand Scripture when we read **only the parts that sound pleasant to us**.

Our own pride, self-love and worldly desires can close our understanding to the true meaning of Christ's suffering, crucifixion and resurrection.

## Jesus restores a blind man's sight (verses 35–43)

The blind man sat begging beside the road.

He was both **blind and poor**, a fitting picture of the human condition Christ came to heal.

The prayer of faith, guided by Christ's promises and resting on them, is never wasted.

Those who receive grace should acknowledge it thankfully and give God glory.

People whose eyes Jesus opens will follow him.

We should praise God not only for kindness shown to ourselves but also for mercies granted to other people.

To understand spiritual things rightly, let us come to Christ as this man did, earnestly asking him to **open our eyes** to the excellence of his teaching and the value of his salvation.
