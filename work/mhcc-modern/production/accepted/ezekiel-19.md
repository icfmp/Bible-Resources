# Ezekiel 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A parable lamenting the ruin of Jehoahaz and Jehoiakim. | 1–9 |
| Another describing the desolation of the people. | 10–14 |

## Judah's kings are pictured as young lions (verses 1–9)

Ezekiel compares the kingdom of Judah to **a lioness**, and its kings to **her young lions**.

These rulers were cruel and oppressive toward their own people.

We should acknowledge **God's righteousness** when those who terrified and enslaved others are in turn terrified and enslaved.

When people who profess faith form close relationships with the ungodly, their children often grow up following **the standards and fashions of a wicked world**.

Receiving greater authority exposes the **ambition and self-interest** hidden in human hearts.

Those who devote their lives to harmful acts often bring them to an end **through violence**.

## Jerusalem is pictured as a ruined vine (verses 10–14)

Jerusalem had once been like **a fruitful, thriving vine**.

Now the vine had been ruined, though **its roots were not completely pulled up**.

Through wickedness, the people had made themselves like **dry tinder waiting for the sparks of God's judgment**.

Even their own branches became **fuel for their destruction**.

Yet blessed be God!

One **Branch** belonging to the royal line pictured here not only became **a strong rod fit for a ruler's scepter**.

He is **the true and living Vine himself**.

All God's chosen people, through every generation, may rejoice in him.
