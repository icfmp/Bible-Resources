# Hebrews 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

## The nature and power of faith (verses 1–3)

From the world's beginning, **faith has distinguished God's servants**.

The Holy Spirit plants this principle through regeneration, making us receive the truth about **justification through Christ's suffering and merits**.

Faith and hope concern the same promised blessings.

Faith confidently expects that God will accomplish everything promised in Christ.

It gives the soul **a present foretaste and reality of things promised for the future**, and convinces the mind of realities invisible to the eyes.

It also approves all that God has revealed as **holy, just and good**.

Such faith shaped the obedience, service and endurance of the people Scripture commends.

The Bible gives the true account of creation's origin.

We should not distort its account merely to accommodate changing human speculations.

**Everything visible came into being by God's command**.

## Abel, Enoch and Noah (verses 4–7)

Abel presented an atoning sacrifice from the firstborn of his flock, admitting that as a sinner he deserved death and could hope for mercy only through **the great future Sacrifice**.

Cain's jealous hatred of an accepted worshiper prefigured the persecution and even murder of faithful believers in every age.

Though dead, **Abel still speaks** through his example.

Enoch was taken so that he did not experience death, anticipating Christ's receiving into heaven the believers alive at his return.

We cannot approach God unless we believe him to be **as he reveals himself in Scripture** and seek him wholeheartedly.

Noah's faith expressed itself in building the ark as instructed.

His trust and obedience exposed others' unbelief and rebellion.

Godly examples may either **bring sinners to conversion or expose and condemn their unbelief**.

People warned of judgment should flee to **Christ for refuge** and become heirs of righteousness by faith.

## Abraham and Sarah trust God's promises (verses 8–19)

Faith often requires us to leave earthly connections, interests and comforts.

Those sharing Abraham's faith obey God's call even when they cannot know the future.

They remain in the path of duty, expecting God to fulfill his promises.

Abraham's test was to **obey simply and completely**.

Sarah regarded God's promise as dependable because God was both **able and willing** to fulfill it.

Those included in God's promises do not always receive the promised blessings quickly.

But faith can bring distant hopes near, delighting in them and living as **strangers and pilgrims traveling toward heaven**.

Faith even helps believers face death cheerfully.

Those genuinely called out of sin do not want to return to it.

All true believers seek their heavenly inheritance, and stronger faith deepens this longing.

Despite their sinful condition and outward poverty, **God is not ashamed to be called their God**.

They should not be ashamed of him or of his people, nor bring shame upon his name.

Abraham's offering of Isaac was an extraordinary test (**Genesis 22:2**).

We should overcome doubts and fears through trust in God's **almighty power**.

The best way to enjoy earthly comforts is to surrender them to God; he then returns or replaces them as he knows best.

Compare Abraham's obedience with our willingness to accept even lesser acts of self-denial, trusting God to compensate our losses and bring blessing even through suffering.

## Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Israel and Rahab (verses 20–31)

Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning the future. Present possessions are not the highest blessings, and having them or lacking them does not prove divine love or hatred.

Jacob **lived and died by faith**.

Faith is vital throughout life, but especially at death, enabling believers to honor the Lord through **patience, hope and joy**.

Joseph's faith sustained him through temptation, persecution, and later honors and power at Pharaoh's court.

Freedom from wicked laws is a great blessing. When such laws exist, we should use **every lawful means** to protect ourselves.

Henry notes that **Moses' parents displayed faith mixed with some unbelief**, which God graciously overlooked.

Faith strengthens us against enslaving fear of human beings and reveals God's greatness and earthly power's emptiness.

The pleasures of sin are brief. They must end either in **speedy repentance or ruin**.

Worldly pleasures become sinful whenever enjoying them requires turning from God and his people.

**Suffering is better than sin**, because even the smallest sin is more evil than the greatest suffering.

Moses chose to share the reproach of God's people rather than enjoy Egypt's treasures.

Christ considers their reproach **his own**, giving it greater worth than an empire's wealth.

Moses made this choice as an adult capable of understanding its cost.

It is especially important to choose God's service when worldly pleasures are most accessible and appealing.

Believers may rightly seek the **promised reward**.

Trusting God's presence enables perseverance through any difficulty.

We are rescued from judgment **not by our own works but by Christ's blood and credited righteousness**.

True faith finds sin bitter even while receiving forgiveness.

Israel's deliverance and the fall of Jericho show God's power to overcome even mighty opposition through faith.

Faith desires not only a covenant with God but also **fellowship with his people**, willing to share their circumstances.

**Rahab's actions demonstrated her faith**, but they did not merit her justification, because even her act was imperfect and could not satisfy God's perfect justice.

## More faithful witnesses, enduring trials and persecution (verses 32–38)

The more we study Scripture, the more we discover there is still to learn.

Many Old Testament believers possessed strong faith even though God's promises were less fully revealed to them.

How sad if in the brighter light of the gospel **so few believe and faith is so weak**!

Faith enables great achievements without promoting self-importance.

**Gideon** teaches humility amid success. **Barak** sought God's help in danger and gave thanks for deliverance.

**Samson** shows faith triumphing over enemies even in death. **Jephthah** reminds us that God graciously uses deeply undeserving people, whose faith still acknowledges him.

**David** endured severe tests and left the Psalms as a lasting witness of faith.

**Samuel** shows how valuable it is to begin trusting God young.

Faith enables service in whatever work God assigns and can overcome the opposition of kings and kingdoms.

**Doing righteousness is a greater honor than performing miracles**.

Faith receives promises and patiently waits for their fulfillment.

Though believers do not expect dead relatives to return to life in this world, they can endure loss through hope of **a better resurrection**.

The cruelty of persecutors is dreadful; so is the power of grace that enables the faithful to endure it.

**The world is not worthy of those saints** whom it treats as unworthy even to live.

It does not value their company, counsel, example or service, because it rejects Christ and his grace.

## God's better provision for believers (verses 39 and 40)

The world thinks the righteous are **unfit to remain alive**, while God declares the world **unworthy of them**.

Although the world's reasons are wrong, both judgments show that believers' true rest is not in this world.

God receives them from it.

The Hebrew Christians have received **even better privileges** through the gospel.

Therefore they should understand that **God expects greater faithfulness** from them.
