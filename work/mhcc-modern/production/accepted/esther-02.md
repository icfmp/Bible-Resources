# Esther 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Esther chosen queen. | 1–20 |
| Mordecai discovers a plot against the king. | 21–23 |

## Esther becomes queen (verses 1–20)

Without divine revelation, people have often embraced unreasonable social practices.

The arrangement described here shows the need for **the gospel of Jesus Christ**, which cleanses people of sinful desires and calls them back to the original design of marriage.

Esther was chosen as queen.

Henry warns against accusing her of sin merely for entering this situation without understanding the customs of the time and region.

**In Henry's account of those customs**, any woman whom the king took became his wife, though she might hold a lower rank than the queen.

How far human nature has fallen when worldly status and bodily pleasure become people's highest goals!

Disappointment and trouble inevitably accompany such pursuits.

The person best placed to enjoy even this present life is the one who most carefully obeys God's commands.

Yet amid these human practices, we should recognize God's **wise and merciful providence**, carrying forward purposes both hidden and holy.

A change in social position never excuses us from our duties to our parents or to people who have fulfilled a parent's role in our lives.

## Mordecai discovers a conspiracy (verses 21–23)

Good citizens must not conceal a plot they know about against the ruler or the public peace.

Mordecai received no immediate reward for exposing the conspiracy.

But a written record preserved his service.

Similarly, people who serve Christ may not receive their reward until **the resurrection of the righteous**.

Yet God keeps an account of every act of faithful work and loving service.

He does not unjustly forget any of it.

Those who serve God should faithfully discharge every trust and remain watchful for the interests of those who employ them.

Even if they appear to be overlooked now, their service will be remembered later.

**Nothing we do is forgotten.** Even hidden thoughts are recorded in enduring books (**Revelation 20:12**).
