# 1 Peter 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A temper suitable to the Christian character as born again, is recommended. | 1–10 |
| Holy conversation among the Gentiles directed. | 11, 12 |
| Subjects exhorted to pay all proper obedience to their civil governors. | 13–17 |
| Also servants to their masters, and all to be patient, according to the example of the suffering Saviour. | 18–25 |

## Desire the Word and grow upon Christ the Cornerstone (verses 1–10)

**Evil speech** reveals malice and deceit within the heart and keeps us from benefiting from Scripture.

New life requires appropriate nourishment.

Just as a newborn eagerly seeks milk, a Christian should eagerly desire **God's word**.

Our Lord Jesus Christ is merciful to miserable sinners and possesses an abundance of grace.

Yet even the finest of God's servants taste only a small part of his full comfort during earthly life.

Christ is called **a Stone**, teaching that he is his people's protection, security and foundation.

His nature, office and work make him infinitely precious.

All true believers are **a holy priesthood**, consecrated to God and useful to others through spiritual gifts and graces.

But even their best prayers and praises become acceptable **only through Jesus Christ**.

As the **chief Cornerstone**, Jesus unites the entire church into one eternal temple and bears its weight.

To be built upon him means truly to **believe in him**, but many people deceive themselves about what faith involves and whether they share his salvation.

Though the world should fall apart, the person truly resting on this foundation need not fear. The believer runs quickly **toward** Christ but never discovers any reason to flee **from** him.

God's people are **one chosen family**, different from the world in heart, principle and conduct, because they were chosen in Christ and sanctified by the Spirit.

He called them from spiritual darkness into light so that they would show his praise through true confession and holy conduct.

Being without divine mercy is miserable even if a person enjoys every worldly advantage.

Nothing moves people toward genuine repentance as tenderly as the right understanding of **God's mercy and love**.

Do not abuse grace while claiming to be saved by it. Those who receive mercy should live as God's people.

## Abstain from sinful desires and live honorably (verses 11 and 12)

Even believers chosen by God require warnings against serious sin.

Sinful bodily desires wage war against the soul. Being given over to them is a dreadful judgment.

A day of divine visitation may bring others to repentance through God's word and grace.

Then the consistent, holy conduct of Christians will have helped bring about this change and **their observers will glorify God**.

## Respect civil authorities without abusing Christian freedom (verses 13–17)

A Christian life must be honest.

That honesty includes fulfilling genuine duties arising from relationships and responsibilities.

Such conduct obeys God's will and silences foolish, malicious accusations.

**Christian freedom must never become an excuse for wrongdoing or neglecting obligations**.

In every relationship, remember that we are God's servants.

## Bear unjust treatment by following the suffering Savior (verses 18–25)

In Peter's time many servants were **enslaved people**, frequently under pagan masters who treated them harshly.

The apostle called them to fulfill their obligations reverently toward God, even under unreasonable rulers.

One person's sinful conduct never justifies another's wrongdoing.

Nevertheless, Henry also insists **masters must be gentle and kind to servants and people under their authority**.

Patience when punished for an actual fault is no remarkable achievement.

But remaining upright when wrongfully mistreated, without seeking revenge, displays divine grace and will be rewarded by God.

**Christ's death is not only an example of patient suffering.** He **carried our sins and their deserved punishment**, satisfying divine justice and taking guilt away.

The intended fruit of his sufferings is that sinners **die to sin and live for righteousness**.

Through his death and resurrection believers receive not just an example but **power and motivation** to do so.

Christ was wounded and crucified as the sacrifice for sin; by his wounds the diseases of our souls are healed.

Human beings wander from the shepherd through their own sinful choices, leaving themselves exposed to countless dangers.

**Conversion is the grace-given return to Christ**, the Shepherd and Overseer of souls.
