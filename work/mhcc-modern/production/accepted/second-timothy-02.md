# 2 Timothy 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle exhorts Timothy to persevere with diligence, like a soldier, a combatant, and a husbandman. | 1–7 |
| Encouraging him by assurances of a happy end of his faithfulness. | 8–13 |
| Warnings to shun vain babblings and dangerous errors. | 14–21 |
| Charges to flee youthful lusts, and to minister with zeal against error, but with meekness of spirit. | 22–26 |

## Serve faithfully as soldier, athlete and farmer (verses 1–7)

As trials increase, our **faith, resolution and love for God and Christ** should become stronger. That is not the same as relying on our own strength.

All Christians, and ministers especially, must remain loyal to **Christ their Captain**, determined to please him.

We must struggle against our sinful desires and corrupt habits, but we cannot expect a crown unless we compete **according to the rules**.

We must do good **in the right way**, lest our good actions themselves be spoken against. Even zeal for outward ceremonies or doubtful controversies can misdirect our energies.

The farmer must labor before receiving the harvest; the runner must run before receiving the prize. We must perform God's will patiently as we wait for what he promised.

Alongside prayer that God will give others understanding, we must encourage them **to reflect carefully on what they hear and read**.

## Remember Jesus and endure (verses 8–13)

Suffering believers should look to **Jesus, the Author and Perfecter of faith**, who endured the cross and shame for the joy before him and now sits at God's right hand.

We should not be surprised when good people are treated badly.

Even when his servants are imprisoned, **God's word cannot be bound**.

Paul's suffering arose from his work for the gospel.

If we have died to this world's pleasures, profits and honors, we will live with Christ eternally in a better world.

Jesus is faithful both **to his promises and to his warnings**. The former secures believers' salvation; the latter makes unbelievers' condemnation certain.

## Avoid quarrels and become a useful vessel (verses 14–21)

People eager to quarrel commonly dispute minor matters. Arguments over words can tear down God's work.

Some false teachers had **not rejected the resurrection outright but distorted its meaning**. Such error can overturn the temporary belief of some professing Christians.

The solid foundation has two assurances: **God knows and preserves those who truly belong to him**, and **everyone who calls upon Christ must turn away from wrongdoing**.

To enjoy the comfort of God's promises, we must seriously perform the duty that accompanies them. Christ gave himself to **redeem us from every lawless deed** (**Titus 2:14**).

The church is like a house containing vessels made from various materials, some for honorable service and others for common use.

When dishonorable vessels are removed, those God approves are prepared for his service. We must seek to be **holy vessels, useful to the Master**.

## Correct opponents with meekness (verses 22–26)

The more earnestly we pursue what is good, the further we will move from evil.

Fellowship with faithful believers helps keep us separate from fruitless works of darkness.

The repeated warnings against religious quarrels show that true faith depends more on **believing and doing what God commands** than on intellectual cleverness in controversy.

A person who loves fighting and speaks fiercely is poorly equipped to teach.

**Teaching rather than persecution is Scripture's method of addressing people in error.**

Only God, who reveals the truth, can grant the repentance by which a person accepts it.

There is no uncertainty about his forgiving **those who actually repent**. What we cannot know is whether he will **grant repentance** to a particular person who currently opposes him.

Sin traps people in the devil's snare and makes them his captives. They cannot escape except through **repentance as God's gift**, which we should ask for in persistent prayer.
