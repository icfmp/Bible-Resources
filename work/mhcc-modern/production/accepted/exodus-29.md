# Exodus 29 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The sacrifice and ceremony for the consecration of the priests. | 1–37 |
| The continual burnt-offerings, God's promise to dwell among Israel. | 38–46 |

## Aaron and his sons are consecrated (verses 1–37)

Aaron and his sons were to be set apart for the priestly office through solemn ceremonies.

Our Lord Jesus is the great High Priest whom we profess.

God appointed him to this work.

He was anointed with the Spirit, and so bears the title **Messiah**, or **Christ**.

He is clothed with glory and beauty.

He was sanctified through his own blood and made perfect—or fully consecrated to his mission—through sufferings (Hebrews 2:10).

All believers are also spiritual priests.

They offer spiritual sacrifices to God (1 Peter 2:5).

Washed in Christ's blood, they have been made priests to God (Revelation 1:5–6).

They too are clothed with the beauty of holiness and have received an anointing (1 John 2:27).

The Spirit of God is called the **finger of God**.

Compare Luke 11:20 with Matthew 12:28.

Through that Spirit, the merit of Christ is applied to our souls.

The priests' consecration therefore represents the admission of a sinner into the spiritual priesthood.

That person may now offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

## The continual sacrifices and God's presence (verses 38–46)

One lamb was to be offered on the altar each morning, and another each evening.

These sacrifices pointed to Christ's continual intercession for his church.

He offered himself **once for all**.

Yet the benefits of that one sacrifice continue, as he lives forever to intercede.

The morning and evening lambs also teach us to present our own daily spiritual sacrifices of prayer and praise to God.

Morning and evening worship are among the most necessary duties of each day.

They are also among the greatest comforts available to us.

We should observe prayer times as faithfully as meal times.

Those who do not keep coming to God's throne of grace starve their own souls.

Steadiness in serving God brings the comfort of fellowship with him.
