# Deuteronomy 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Monuments of idolatry to be destroyed. | 1–4 |
| The place of God's service to be kept. | 5–32 |

## Israel must reject pagan worship practices (verses 1–4)

Moses began explaining the laws Israel had to observe in Canaan with instructions about worshiping God.

The Israelites were not to introduce the customs and rituals of idolaters into their worship of the true God, even under the pretext of improving it.

We cannot serve both God and wealth.

We cannot worship both the living God and idols.

Nor can we claim to trust in Jesus Christ while also resting our hope on superstition or self-righteousness.

## The place chosen by God and the worship he accepts (verses 5–32)

The command to bring sacrifices to the entrance of the tabernacle was now explained in view of Israel's settlement in Canaan.

Moral duties of worship were different.

People could pray and worship God wherever they lived, as they later did in synagogues.

The place God would choose for sacrifices was described as the place where he would put **his name**.

It would be his dwelling, where he, the King of Israel, could be approached reverently by his people.

Under the gospel there is no temple or altar that makes offerings acceptable except **Christ himself**.

The prophets foretold that spiritual incense would be offered to God everywhere (Malachi 1:11).

Jesus declared that true worshipers are accepted when they worship God **in spirit and truth**, not because they come to one mountain or to Jerusalem (John 4:21).

Even an Israelite who could not bring a sacrifice to the altar could honor God, maintain fellowship with him and receive his mercy.

The work of God should be done with holy joy and cheerfulness.

Children and servants were to rejoice before him as well.

Worship should be a pleasure and delight, not a dreary task.

People should be kind to ministers who instruct them faithfully and set them a good example.

We will continue needing their ministry until we reach a world where religious ordinances are no longer necessary.

Whether we eat or drink, or do anything else, everything should be done for God's glory.

Whatever we do should be done in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, thanking the Father through him.

Israel was not even to investigate the methods used in idol worship.

What spiritual advantage could come from exploring such depths of Satan?

We will find increasing contentment as we grow in love and good works springing from faith and the indwelling Spirit of Christ.
