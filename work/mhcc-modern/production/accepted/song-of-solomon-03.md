# Song of Solomon 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The trials of the church by the withdrawing of Christ. | 1–5 |
| The excellences of the church, The care of Christ for her. | 6–11 |

## Seeking Christ when his presence seems withdrawn (verses 1–5)

For the Old Testament church, discerning Christ through the ceremonial law was difficult. Its watchmen often gave little assistance to those seeking him.

Night can picture spiritual coldness, darkness, drowsiness and uncertainty.

When believers first become troubled by a loss of felt fellowship with Christ, they may make only weak attempts to recover that comfort. When these efforts fail, they are stirred to greater diligence.

The streets and squares suggest **the appointed means of grace** in which we should seek the Lord. The soul asks those responsible for watching over others, but still does not immediately find satisfaction.

We must not stop at the outward means. By faith, turn directly to Christ.

Taking hold of him and refusing to let go pictures **persistent, humble prayer joined with active faith in his promises**.

The Lord is not offended by earnest requests from believers who cling to him in faith. He welcomes such pleading.

A person who has found the Savior wants to help others know him too.

Wherever Christ is found, welcome him into our **homes and especially our hearts**.

Let us remind ourselves and one another not to grieve the Holy Spirit, our Comforter, or provoke the withdrawal of the Beloved's comforting presence.

## Christ safely carries his people through the wilderness (verses 6–11)

The wilderness pictures the present world. Believers come out of it when they turn away from its sinful pleasures, goals and patterns and seek happiness in fellowship with the Savior.

Through the Comforter's guidance, even a poor and weary soul will rise like **incense from the altar**, with affections directed toward heaven.

The Holy Spirit makes the believer's devotion lively and fills the heart with heavenly graces.

Christ is **the Peace of his people and the King of heavenly Zion**. He has prepared a safe way to carry his redeemed through the wilderness.

The decorated bed or portable litter represents a place of rest and secure travel, made splendid in a manner befitting its owner.

The church is strongly guarded: **more are with her than against her**. Even when believers fear the dangers of night, they are safe when resting in Christ.

Henry understands the chariot as a picture of **the covenant of redemption and the way of salvation**, designed both for Christ's glory and for the comfort of his people, securely ordered in every part.

The purple covering represents **the blood of the covenant**, which shelters believers from divine wrath and the storms of this life.

At its center is **Christ's love surpassing knowledge**, on which believers rest.

The crown points to the honor, power and kingship belonging to Christ as he reveals himself through the gospel.
