# Proverbs 25 — Modern English

## Verses 1–3 — God knows all; rulers must investigate

God never needs to investigate: **nothing can be hidden from him**. Rulers, however, are honored when they carefully search out matters and bring hidden wrongdoing into the light.

## Verses 4 and 5 — Reforming government

A ruler best establishes a government by removing evil and correcting vice among the people.

## Verses 6 and 7 — Choose humility before honor

True religion teaches humility and self-denial. Anyone who has seen **God's glory in Jesus Christ** recognizes personal unworthiness.

## Verses 8–10 — Prevent unnecessary disputes

Rushing into conflict produces needless difficulty. Every war eventually ends; often it would be better **not to have begun**. The same is true of private quarrels: do everything possible to settle them.

## Verses 11 and 12 — Words that suit their moment

Counsel and correction offered at the proper time are beautiful, like fine fruit enhanced by a vessel of silver.

## Verse 13 — Be a faithful messenger

The aim of anyone entrusted with a task is to **carry it out faithfully**. We should especially welcome a faithful minister who serves as **Christ's messenger**.

## Verse 14 — Do not claim gifts you have not given

Those who pretend to possess or have given gifts they never had are like clouds promising rain but leaving the fields dry.

## Verse 15 — Persuasion needs patience

Bear a temporary injury patiently and speak without anger. Gentle persuasion is often more effective than force against a stubborn mind.

## Verse 16 — Good things require moderation

God permits us to enjoy pleasant things, but warns against excess.

## Verse 17 — Be a considerate neighbor

Even sincere neighbors need discretion to live peacefully together. **God is a better Friend than any human friend**: the more often we come to him, the more welcome we are.

## Verse 18 — False testimony injures

A lying witness brings danger into every matter in which testimony is needed.

## Verse 19 — An unreliable helper increases pain

Trusting an unfaithful person during hardship brings not merely disappointment but additional suffering.

## Verse 20 — Do not trivialize sorrow

Trying to comfort grieving people by forcing merriment upon them is the wrong approach.

## Verses 21 and 22 — Love even enemies

Loving enemies is already commanded in the Old Testament. **Jesus Christ** provided the supreme example, loving us **while we were his enemies**.

## Verse 23 — Do not reward slander with attention

Slander would be spoken less readily if people did not eagerly listen to it. Sin loses courage when it meets firm resistance.

## Verse 24 — Choose peace over miserable companionship

Solitude is better than sharing life with someone who continually destroys its peace.

## Verse 25 — Good news from the far country

Heaven is a distant homeland. How refreshing is its news in **the everlasting gospel**, and in **the Spirit's witness that we are God's children**!

## Verse 26 — When the righteous yield to sin

A righteous person led into wrongdoing harms others like a **public spring whose waters have become poisoned**.

## Verse 27 — Resist excessive appetite and praise

By grace we must learn to deny not only bodily indulgence but also craving for **other people's admiration**.

## Verse 28 — Ask the Spirit for self-control

A person unable to govern anger can easily lose peace. May we surrender ourselves to the Lord, ask him to put **his Spirit within us**, and follow his commands.
