# Psalm 110 — Modern English

## Original heading

**Christ's kingdom.**

## The eternal King and Priest

This psalm speaks glorious truths concerning **Jesus Christ**.

He is not only greater than all the rulers of the earth.

He already existed in glory as **the eternal Son of God**.

Christ **sits at God's right hand**.

Sitting is a posture of rest after labor and suffering, and one from which the King exercises judgment and authority.

It also signifies permanence: **he reigns forever**.

All his enemies are already **in chains**, though they have not yet been made **his footstool**.

His kingdom has been established and will continue despite every power of darkness.

**Christ's people are willing people**.

The Holy Spirit's power makes them willing to serve him.

They worship him clothed with **the beauty of holiness**, which belongs to his house forever.

Many will dedicate themselves to him.

We should consecrate even the earliest days of our youth to the Lord Jesus, like fresh dew appearing at dawn.

Christ is not only **King** but also **Priest**.

He serves on God's behalf toward us and pleads our cause before the Father.

He is therefore **the Mediator between God and humanity**.

His priesthood follows **the order of Melchizedek**, earlier than Aaron's priesthood and superior to it in several ways.

It represents the priesthood of Christ more fully.

His place at God's right hand brings joy to his people and terror to his enemies.

Ultimately his victory will bring the final overthrow of those who oppose him.

The Redeemer **saves and comforts his friends**.

He was humbled, drinking from **the stream along his path**.

Henry understands this as a possible picture of the curse of the law and the wrath associated with our redemption, which Christ endured.

He drank the waters of suffering on the road toward his throne of glory.

**He was humbled, but now he is exalted**.

What about us?

Has Christ's gospel become **God's saving power in our lives**?

Does he reign in our hearts?

Are we willing subjects?

We once did not recognize our need for his salvation and resisted his rule.

Are we now willing to abandon every sin, turn away from an evil and deceptive world, and rest **only upon his merit and mercy**?

Do we welcome him as **Prophet, Priest and King**, and desire holiness?

Those transformed in this way receive the blessings of **his sacrifice, intercession and grace**.

**Full original-English secondary witness directly read:** [CCEL, Psalm 110](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xx.cx.html). Published original `MHCC 0.1.1` remains authority, requiring full native SWORD/independent acceptance.

One continuous prose commentary headed **“Christ's kingdom.”** Preserved: Christ's **preexistent divine glory and permanent enthronement**, enemies restrained but not fully defeated, **willing people through Spirit**, youth devoted to Christ, **Melchizedek's priesthood** and mediation, suffering as **the stream**, final enemies' defeat, full concluding **self-examination as willing subjects**.
