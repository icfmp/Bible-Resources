# 3 John 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle commends Gaius for piety and hospitality. | 1–8 |
| Cautions him against siding with Diotrephes, who was a turbulent spirit; but recommends Demetrius as a man of excellent character. | 9–12 |
| He hopes soon to see Gaius. | 13, 14 |

## Gaius shows faith through generous hospitality (verses 1–8)

Those loved by Christ should love their fellow believers **for his sake**.

**Prosperity of the soul** is the greatest blessing available on this side of heaven.

Grace and physical health make fine companions. Grace makes good use of health.

Yet a rich and gracious soul may inhabit a weak body. In that situation, grace is exercised through patient submission.

It is right to pray that people whose souls prosper might also enjoy health, providing further opportunities for service.

For many outwardly successful religious people, one could wish the reverse: **that their souls might prosper as much as their health and circumstances**.

Living faith works through love.

Those who receive genuine spiritual benefit should testify truthfully to the church, giving a good report of the people who helped them.

Godly people rejoice when they hear of other believers' growth in grace.

Faithful parents and ministers delight to see those they have taught living in a way that honors their profession.

Gaius overlooked **minor disagreements among sincere believers** and freely helped everyone who bore Christ's likeness and performed his work.

He was upright in doing so.

A faithful Christian can hear deserved praise without becoming proud, because any good found in that person leads back **to the foot of Christ's cross**.

We should consider not only **what we are required to do**, but also **what good we have an opportunity to do**.

Even ordinary acts of kindness can serve God's glory.

Those who have resources should support faithful preachers who freely proclaim Christ.

People unable to preach themselves can still **welcome, assist and encourage** those who do.

## Do not imitate Diotrephes; follow what is good (verses 9–12)

Guard both **heart and speech**.

**Diotrephes** was controlled by ambition and pride.

It is wrong to neglect doing good personally. It is even worse to **hinder others who want to do good**.

Counsel offered in a loving spirit is more likely to be accepted.

Imitate what is good, because someone who does good from a genuine love for it shows evidence of being born of God.

People performing evil while boasting of knowing God deceive themselves.

Do not follow proud, selfish or harmful examples merely because they come from people of rank or influence.

Instead imitate **God's love and the example of Jesus**.

## Demetrius's good reputation and the joy of personal fellowship (verses 13 and 14)

**Demetrius** had a good testimony.

A favorable reputation in the gospel and the churches is worth more than worldly status.

Few people are praised by everyone; sometimes universal applause is even a bad sign.

Blessed are those whose **character and conduct commend them to God and to other people**.

We should be willing to testify when someone lives faithfully, especially when our testimony can stand examination by people who know that person's life closely.

Speaking face to face can avoid the delay, confusion and misunderstandings that written letters sometimes create.

It is right for Christians to enjoy meeting one another.

John closes with the blessing **peace**—asking that genuine happiness be granted.

People who hope to dwell together in heaven should gladly greet one another on earth.

Keeping company with faithful Christians and following their example helps cultivate inner peace, peace with fellow believers and hope of everlasting fellowship in glory.
