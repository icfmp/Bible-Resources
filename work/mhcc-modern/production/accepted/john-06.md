# John 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Five thousand miraculously fed. | 1–14 |
| Jesus walks on the sea. | 15–21 |
| He directs to spiritual food. | 22–27 |
| His discourse with the multitude. | 28–65 |
| Many of disciples go back. | 66–71 |

## Jesus feeds the five thousand (verses 1–14)

John describes the miracle of feeding the crowd because it is closely connected with Jesus' teaching that follows.

Notice what the miracle produced among the people.

Even many ordinary Jews expected a Messiah who would come as a **great prophet**.

The Pharisees despised these people for not knowing the law.

Yet the ordinary people in some respects knew more of **the One who is the law's true goal** than their religious leaders did.

Still, a person can acknowledge Jesus as the promised prophet and **refuse to listen to him**.

## Jesus walks on the sea (verses 15–21)

The disciples were following the path of duty, and Jesus was praying for them.

Nevertheless, they faced danger.

Having a share in Christ does not remove every trial or trouble of this world.

Clouds and darkness can surround **children of light**.

When the disciples saw Jesus walking on the water, the very approach of their deliverance **frightened them**.

People often mistake the coming of comfort for another threat.

Few things confront a sinner's conscience more powerfully than the Savior declaring, **“I am Jesus whom you are persecuting.”**

Few words comfort a believer more than his assurance, **“I am Jesus whom you love.”**

If we have received Jesus Christ as Lord, we may be confident that, however dark the night and strong the wind, **we will soon reach the shore**.

## Jesus calls the crowd to seek lasting food (verses 22–27)

The people wanted to know how Jesus had arrived.

Instead of answering their curiosity, he confronted their motives for following him.

We should seek salvation with the greatest earnestness, using all the means God has appointed.

Yet salvation must be received **as a gift from the Son of Man**, not something we earn.

God the Father has **set his seal upon Jesus**, proving his divine authority.

The Son of Man is also **the Son of God in power**.

## Jesus teaches about the bread of life (verses 28–65)

### Believe in the true bread from heaven (verses 28–35)

Continually exercising faith in Christ is the most important—and often the most difficult—part of the obedience required of sinners seeking salvation.

When his grace enables us to **live by faith in the Son of God**, holy dispositions and acceptable service follow.

The Father once gave Israel food from heaven to sustain physical life.

Now he gives **the true Bread** to save souls.

**Coming to Jesus and believing in him mean the same thing.**

Christ nourishes and sustains spiritual life as ordinary bread nourishes the body.

But unlike ordinary bread, which nourishes only because a body is already alive, **Jesus is himself living Bread**.

He gives and sustains life by his own power.

The teaching of Christ crucified remains just as strengthening and comforting to a believer now as it always was.

Jesus is **the Bread that came down from heaven**.

This declares the divine nature of his person, his authority and the heavenly origin of every saving benefit received through him.

May we ask sincerely, **Lord, always give us this Bread**.

### The Father draws people to the Son (verses 36–46)

When the Holy Spirit reveals to people **their guilt, danger and remedy**, they become willing—even eager—to come to Jesus and surrender everything that hinders receiving his salvation.

It is the Father's will that **not one of those he gave to the Son should be rejected or lost by him**.

No one comes until divine grace has subdued and partly changed the heart.

Consequently, **no one who truly comes to Jesus will ever be turned away**.

The gospel does not find fallen sinners naturally eager to accept its humbling, holy way of salvation.

God draws people through **his word and the Holy Spirit**.

The hearer's duty is to **hear and learn**, receiving the offered grace and consenting to God's promise.

Only the beloved Son has seen the Father.

Jesus taught that his listeners needed **his inward influence on their minds**, as well as his spoken word and the ministers he sent.

### The living Bread is greater than manna (verses 47–51)

Manna gave Israel a blessing limited to **this earthly life**.

The living Bread is far greater: those who feed on him receive **eternal life**.

This Bread refers to **Christ's human nature**, which he took so he might offer himself to the Father **as a sacrifice for the world's sins**.

Through this offering he purchased everything necessary for life and godliness for sinners **of every nation who repent and believe in him**.

### Feed upon Christ through faith (verses 52–59)

The flesh and blood of the Son of Man refer to the Redeemer **in human nature**, especially Christ crucified and the redemption secured by his sacrifice.

That redemption includes **forgiveness, acceptance with God, access to the throne of grace, the covenant promises and eternal life**.

These blessings are called his flesh and blood because they were purchased **through his broken body and shed blood**, and because they nourish souls as food and drink nourish bodies.

**Eating Christ's flesh and drinking his blood mean believing in him.**

We receive Christ and his benefits **by faith**.

The person who recognizes personal spiritual need finds in the Redeemer—God manifested in flesh—everything needed to quiet the conscience and increase holiness.

Meditating on the cross awakens **repentance, love and gratitude**.

We live spiritually through Christ as our bodies live through food.

We also live through him as limbs receive life from the head and branches receive life from the root.

**Because he lives, we shall live also.**

### Spiritual life comes through the Spirit (verses 60–65)

Christ's human nature had not previously existed in heaven.

Yet because he is **God and man in one Person**, it was right to say that he came down from heaven.

The Messiah's kingdom was **not an earthly kingdom**.

His listeners should understand by faith his teaching about living spiritually through him and receiving his fullness.

Just as a body without a soul has no living power, **religious forms without God's life-giving Spirit are dead and useless**.

Only the One who provided salvation can teach us to understand it and draw us to receive it by faith.

We should gladly apply to Jesus, thankful that everyone willing to come to him is assured of a welcome.

## Many disciples turn away (verses 66–71)

When we entertain suspicious and harsh thoughts about Jesus' words and works, we enter into temptation.

Unless the Lord mercifully prevents it, such distrust can lead to **turning back**.

A corrupt heart finds an occasion for offense even in teachings that should bring the deepest comfort.

Jesus had promised eternal life to his true followers.

Some listeners focused upon his difficult sayings and left him.

The faithful disciples held to **his clear promise of eternal life** and remained with him.

Christ's teaching is **the word of eternal life**.

We must live and die by it.

To abandon Jesus is to abandon **our own mercy and salvation**.

His disciples believed that he was the Messiah promised to Israel, **the Son of the living God**.

Whenever tempted to withdraw from Christ, return to these first principles and stand firm.

Remember his searching question:

**Will you also go away?**

Where else could we go?

Only Jesus grants salvation through **the forgiveness of sins**, true assurance, peace and joy.

He alone gives solid happiness now and opens the way toward eternal happiness.
