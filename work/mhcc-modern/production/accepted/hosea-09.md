# Hosea 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The distress to come upon Israel. | 1–6 |
| The approach of the day of trouble. | 7–10 |
| Judgments on Israel. | 11–17 |

## Israel's misplaced joy will end (verses 1–6)

Israel offered gifts to its idols.

People who are **stingy about true worship** may spend lavishly on **their own sinful desires**.

Anyone who values **the harvest floor's reward** more highly than **God's favor and eternal life** is, in that sense, an idolater.

The people rejoiced over their harvest while having **no desire to grieve over sin**.

When we make the world and its possessions **our idol and our portion**, God is just in exposing our folly and correcting us.

Those who refuse God's laws and his love cannot expect to enjoy the Lord's land as though nothing were wrong.

When we possess **the means of grace**, we should ask how we would live if those gifts were removed.

The joy of fellowship with God remains secure amid earthly change.

But pleasant homes and properties bought or protected with money may be **destroyed**.

No famine is more dreadful than **a famine of the soul**.

## The appointed watchmen become traps (verses 7–10)

There had been a time when Israel's **spiritual watchmen** walked with God.

Now they acted like **a birdcatcher's snare**, trapping people and bringing them toward ruin.

The people's corruption resembled that of **Gibeah** (**Judges 19**).

Their crimes would bring comparable judgment.

At first, God had found Israel delightful, like **grapes discovered by a traveler in the wilderness**, or **the first ripe figs of the season**.

These pictures show how much pleasure God had taken in his people.

Nevertheless, they abandoned him and followed idols.

## Judgment reaches Israel's comforts and descendants (verses 11–17)

God departs from a person or people when he withdraws **his goodness and mercy**.

When the Lord has withdrawn, what can created things accomplish?

Outward comforts may remain for a time, but **the true blessing is gone when God is gone**.

Children as well as parents would suffer.

God's wrath **dries the roots and withers the fruit** of worldly comforts.

Henry points to **the continued scattering of the Jewish people in his own era** as a warning not to neglect or abuse gospel privileges.

Yet **not every painful blow means that God is drying up the root**.

Sometimes he strikes a plant so that **its sap goes deeper into the root**, producing greater **humility, patience, faith and self-denial**.

God is just when he judges people who despise the mercy he offers.
