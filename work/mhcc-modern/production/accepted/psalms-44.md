# Psalm 44 — Modern English

## Original heading

**A petition for succour and relief.**

## Original source divisions

| Source commentary | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| First section | 1–8 |
| Second section | 9–16 |
| Third section | 17–26 |

## Remembering previous deliverances (verses 1–8)

Past experiences of God's power and goodness strengthen faith and provide compelling reasons for prayer during present distress.

Israel did not win its victories through human strength or merit. Success came from **God's favor and free grace**.

The less praise this permits us to claim, the more comfort it offers: the achievement rests on his goodness.

God fought for Israel; otherwise its people would have fought in vain.

The same applies to the establishment of the Christian church. It did not arise through human strategy or power.

**Christ, through his Spirit, went out conquering and to conquer.**

The One who planted the church will sustain it by the same power and love.

God's people trust and triumph in him, so let anyone who boasts boast in the Lord.

If they find comfort in his name, they should also give him **all its due glory**.

## The church suffers and fears abandonment (verses 9–16)

Believers experience times of temptation, suffering and discouragement. The church also passes through seasons of persecution.

In such circumstances God's people may fear he has cast them off and that his truth and name are being dishonored.

But they should look beyond the human instruments of their distress and recognize that even their worst enemies can do **no more than God permits**.

## Faithfulness under persecution (verses 17–26)

Affliction must never drive us to obtain relief through **sinful compromise**.

We should continue reflecting upon the truth, purity and complete knowledge of God, who searches the heart.

Hidden sin and inward motives are known to him and will be brought into account. He understands the secret thoughts beneath words and deeds.

As long as suffering does not drive us away from duty to God, we should not permit it to drive us from **comfort in God** either.

Be careful that prosperity and ease do not make the heart complacent and spiritually lukewarm.

The true church cannot be forced by persecution to forget God, nor will the believer's heart finally turn from him.

Henry sees the Spirit's prophecy as pointing toward people who suffered **to death for their testimony to Christ**.

The appeal in **verses 25–26** is not based upon personal merit or righteousness but upon the plea of a needy sinner.

No one belonging to Christ will finally be rejected. Every one of them will be saved forever.

**God's mercy**, purchased, promised and continually supplied to believers, overcomes the doubts arising from their sins.

Let us therefore pray in faith: **Redeem us for your mercy's sake**.
