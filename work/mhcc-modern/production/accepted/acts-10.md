# Acts 10 — Modern English

## Cornelius is told to summon Peter (verses 1–8)

Until this point, those admitted to the Christian church through baptism had been Jews, Samaritans or converts who had accepted circumcision and Moses' ceremonial law.

Now **Gentiles would receive every privilege of God's people without first becoming Jews**.

Genuine religion is sometimes found where we least expect it.

Where reverence for God rules a heart, it produces **both acts of charity and acts of devotion**.

Neither excuses neglect of the other.

Henry considers it certain that Cornelius truly trusted God's word **as far as he understood it**, although he did not yet have clear knowledge of Jesus Christ.

This early faith, Henry explains, was already the Holy Spirit's work through **Christ's mediation**, even before Cornelius consciously knew the Mediator.

The same is true when anyone formerly dead in sin receives spiritual life.

Through Christ, Cornelius's prayers and charitable gifts were accepted. Apart from that mediation they could not merit God's favor.

When heaven directed Cornelius to send for Peter, he obeyed **without arguing or delaying**.

We should waste no time in matters concerning our souls.

## Peter's vision (verses 9–18)

Peter's prejudices against Gentiles would have prevented him from entering Cornelius's home if God had not prepared him.

For a Jew, hearing that formerly unclean animals must now be treated as clean amounted to learning that the old **ceremonial restrictions of Moses' law had ended**.

Peter soon discovered how this vision applied to his mission.

God knows in advance what work he will give us and prepares us for it.

Often we understand what he has taught **only when we encounter the occasion to use it**.

## Peter goes to Cornelius (verses 19–33)

Once a call to service becomes clear, we should not be paralyzed by needless doubts arising from prejudice or inherited opinions.

Cornelius had invited his relatives and friends to hear Peter.

We should not want to enjoy every spiritual blessing **alone**.

It is an act of friendship and kindness to invite family and friends to worship and learn with us.

Cornelius explained the divine message that had prompted him to summon Peter.

Our aims are right when we attend gospel preaching **because God has appointed this means of grace**.

What a privilege for a minister to speak to people who have truly gathered **in God's presence to hear everything he has commanded**!

That was the readiness of Cornelius's household.

## Peter preaches Jesus to the Gentiles (verses 34–43)

No one is accepted by God except through **the covenant of mercy and the atoning work of Jesus Christ**.

But wherever genuine religion is found, God accepts it **without regard to names, nations or religious factions**.

Reverence for God and works of righteousness are the practical substance of true religion.

Yet these works are **effects of special grace and signs of acceptance**, **not the cause** of a person's acceptance before God.

What is still lacking in true knowledge or maturity will be supplied in due time by the Lord who began the work.

Cornelius and his hearers already knew the broad gospel message proclaimed in Israel, beginning with John's baptism of repentance.

That message was the good news of **peace with God through Jesus Christ**.

They needed to understand that Jesus, through whom God and sinners are reconciled, is **Lord of all**.

He is Lord not only as **God over all, blessed forever**, but also in his work as Mediator.

Every authority in heaven and on earth belongs to him, and all judgment has been entrusted to him.

God accompanies those he has anointed by his Spirit.

Peter told them about Jesus' resurrection and its proofs.

Christian faith responds to testimony.

It rests upon the foundation supplied by **the apostles and prophets**, through the witness they gave.

Everyone will answer to **Jesus as Judge** and must therefore seek his favor.

Those who trust him receive **justification through his righteousness**.

The forgiveness of sins is the foundation of every other saving blessing, removing the guilt that prevents sinners from receiving them.

**If sins are pardoned, all will finally be well**.

## The Holy Spirit comes before water baptism (verses 44–48)

On other occasions, the Spirit had visibly come upon people after baptism, strengthening their faith.

Here he came upon the Gentiles **before water baptism**.

God thereby showed that he is **not bound to outward signs or ceremonies**.

These people were neither circumcised nor yet baptized when they received the Spirit.

It is the **Spirit who gives life**; human flesh cannot do so.

The new believers magnified God and spoke about Christ and the benefits of redemption.

Whatever gifts we receive, we must use them to honor God.

Jewish Christians were astonished to see the Holy Spirit given to Gentiles.

Our mistaken ideas about God's work often make his ways appear harder to understand than they really are.

Because the Gentiles had unmistakably received **the baptism and gifts of the Spirit**, Peter concluded they must not be refused **the outward ordinance of water baptism**.

The reasoning was sound: **how can we deny the sign to those who have already received the reality signified?**

Those who have begun knowing Jesus should continually desire to know him more.

Even people filled with the Holy Spirit need to keep learning God's truth every day.
