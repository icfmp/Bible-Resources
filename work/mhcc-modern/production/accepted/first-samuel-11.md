# 1 Samuel 11 — Modern English

## Saul rescues Jabesh-gilead (verses 1–11)

Saul's first great act as king was to rescue **Jabesh-gilead** from the Ammonites.

People may be willing to surrender their freedom, or even lose their eyes, to save their earthly lives.

Would it not be wiser to surrender a cherished sin, however dear to us—like our right eye—rather than be cast into the fire of judgment?

Notice Saul's faith and confidence.

From that faith came his courage and determination.

Notice also how actively he carried out the work.

When the Lord's Spirit comes upon people, he can make them skillful even when they lack experience.

When zeal for God's glory and love for brothers and sisters move us to energetic service, and the Lord is pleased to help, great results can follow very quickly.

## Saul's kingship is confirmed (verses 12–15)

The people who had despised Saul now honored him.

Turning an enemy into a friend benefits us more than killing that enemy.

One day everyone will acknowledge **the Savior they once despised** as the Lord's Anointed King.

For now, Christ receives repentant rebels at his mercy seat.

He even intercedes for them.

But the day is approaching when he will sit upon the throne of righteous judgment and condemn those who persist in opposing him.
