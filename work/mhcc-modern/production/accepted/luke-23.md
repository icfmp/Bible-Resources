## Jesus stands before Pilate (verses 1–5)

Pilate clearly understood the difference between Jesus' followers and an armed uprising.

Yet when Pilate said he found Jesus innocent, the accusers were not moved to consider whether they were becoming guilty of **innocent blood**.

They became even angrier.

God nevertheless brought his holy purposes to completion through the actions of people pursuing their own sinful plans.

The different parties' efforts to accuse Jesus combined instead to establish his innocence.

He was therefore shown to be the fitting **atoning sacrifice for our sins**.

## Herod questions Jesus (verses 6–12)

Herod had heard much about Jesus' works in Galilee and longed to see him out of curiosity.

Jesus never refused the humblest beggar who sought a miracle to relieve genuine need.

But he did not grant a miracle merely to satisfy this **proud ruler's curiosity**.

Herod could previously have seen Christ's works in Galilee, but had chosen not to.

When he later wished to see a sign, he did not receive one.

Herod sent Jesus back to Pilate.

Wicked people sometimes become friends through their common participation in wrongdoing.

They may agree on little except hostility toward God and contempt for Christ.

## Barabbas is released and Jesus condemned (verses 13–25)

Fear of people's opinions can tempt someone to act unjustly **against conscience** rather than risk trouble.

Pilate acknowledged Jesus' innocence and wanted to release him.

Yet to please the crowd, he proposed **punishing an innocent person as though guilty**.

If no fault had been found in Jesus, why should he be punished?

Pilate eventually surrendered.

He lacked courage to stand against public pressure and delivered Jesus to be crucified.

## Jesus speaks to the women of Jerusalem (verses 26–31)

Our blessed Jesus, **the Lamb of God**, was led toward the sacrifice like a lamb brought to slaughter.

Many insulted him, but some pitied him.

His death, however, was **a victory over his enemies**, our deliverance and the purchase of eternal life.

Therefore, rather than merely weep over him, we should mourn over **our own sins and those of our children**, which brought such suffering upon him.

We should also fear what we bring upon ourselves by rejecting his grace.

If God allowed such suffering to fall upon his own Son when he was made a sacrifice for sin, what will happen to unrepentant sinners who make themselves like a **dry, fruitless tree**?

Even the holiest believers are like dry trees compared with the perfectly holy Christ.

If he suffered so severely, they should not assume they will suffer nothing.

How dreadful, then, is the final judgment of hardened sinners!

The Savior's suffering solemnly reveals **God's justice against sin**.

## Jesus is crucified; the repentant criminal (verses 32–43)

Once Jesus was fastened to the cross, **he prayed for those who crucified him**.

The chief benefit he died to purchase for sinners is **forgiveness**; that is what he requested for them.

Two criminals were crucified beside him.

Their different responses picture the varied effects of Christ's cross when the gospel is preached.

One remained hardened until death.

Suffering alone cannot change a sinful heart.

The other was softened at the end of life, rescued by divine mercy from the very edge of destruction.

This **must not encourage anyone to postpone repentance**.

Genuine repentance is never too late, but **late repentance is often not genuine**.

No one can be certain of having the opportunity or capacity to repent on a deathbed.

This man's experience was exceptional.

He rebuked the other criminal's insults.

He acknowledged that **he deserved his sentence**.

He recognized that Jesus had suffered unjustly.

He confessed faith while Jesus was still suffering deep disgrace, before the signs that would later move the centurion.

He believed in life to come and sought happiness there—not merely rescue from the cross.

He prayed humbly, asking only **“Lord, remember me.”**

He left to Jesus the way that promise should be fulfilled.

Although his remaining time was brief, he showed all the **fruits of repentance** his circumstances allowed.

Jesus was as gracious **upon the cross as upon his throne**.

Amid dreadful suffering he had compassion on this repentant sinner.

Christ died to open heaven to repentant believers.

This one striking example teaches that **we should despair of no sinner**.

Yet it stands beside the warning of the second criminal, who died in unbelief while the crucified Savior was so near.

Usually, people die in the spiritual condition in which they have lived.

## The death of Jesus (verses 44–49)

Extraordinary signs accompanied Christ's death and testified to its importance.

Jesus' final words showed that **he freely offered his life**.

Let us honor God through true repentance and conversion.

Let us oppose the sinful attitudes of those who crucified Christ, live soberly and righteously, and use our abilities in the service of the Savior who died and rose for us.

## Jesus is buried (verses 50–56)

Some people make little outward display of religion but, like **Joseph of Arimathea**, are more willing to perform genuine service when needed than those who make a great show of religious devotion.

Jesus was buried quickly because the Sabbath was approaching.

The mourners' sorrow was real, but **weeping must not prevent the sowing of duty**.

Even while grieving Jesus' death, they needed to prepare for the holy Sabbath.

Henry urges believers to arrange ordinary business so that it does not obstruct the responsibilities of sacred rest.

He applies the principle to **the Lord's day**, on which the heart should be prepared for worship whatever the burdens of the preceding week.
