# 2 Kings 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elijah divides Jordan. | 1–8 |
| Elijah is taken up into heaven. | 9–12 |
| Elisha is manifested to be Elijah's successor. | 13–18 |
| Elisha heals the waters of Jericho, Those that mocked Elisha destroyed. | 19–25 |

## Elijah parts the Jordan (verses 1–8)

The Lord had made it known to Elijah that his departure was near.

He therefore visited the different schools of the prophets to give his last encouragements and blessing.

Elijah's departure pictured **Christ's ascension** and the opening of heaven's kingdom to all believers.

Elisha had followed Elijah for years.

He was unwilling to leave him now, when he hoped to receive a final blessing.

Let those who follow Christ beware of growing tired and giving up just before the end.

Long before, the Jordan had parted for the ark.

Now it parted before Elijah's mantle as a sign of God's presence.

For God's faithful people, death is like the Jordan they must cross to reach heaven.

Christ's death has opened a path through those waters for the redeemed.

O death, where are your sting, injury and terror?

## Elijah is taken to heaven (verses 9–12)

The source of divine fullness from which prophets and apostles received their gifts still exists.

We are encouraged to ask God freely for abundant grace.

By faithfully attending Elijah, especially in his final hours, Elisha put himself in the position most suited to receive a greater share of his spirit.

What departing believers say about their experience of God can strengthen our resolve and brighten our own hope.

Elijah was carried to heaven in a fiery chariot.

Many questions about the event cannot be answered.

It is enough to notice what his Master found him doing when the hour arrived.

He was having a serious conversation with Elisha, encouraging and instructing him about God's kingdom among people.

We are wrong to imagine that preparing for heaven consists only of contemplation and acts of worship.

The chariot and horses appeared like fire, **not burning but radiant and glorious**.

Through the way Elijah and Enoch left the world, God gave a glimpse of the eternal life revealed in the gospel and the glory awaiting the bodies of believers.

It also represented Christ's ascension.

Yet even though Elijah entered heaven triumphantly, Israel could hardly spare him.

People who feel no sorrow when faithful and useful servants of God die have hard hearts.

Elijah's counsel, rebukes and prayers had protected the nation more effectively than its greatest military force.

Christ left the gospel with his disciples as Elijah left his mantle.

It is a sign of divine power working to overthrow Satan's rule and establish God's kingdom.

That same gospel remains with us, **although the miraculous powers have been withdrawn**.

It still has divine power to bring sinners to conversion and salvation.

## Elisha receives Elijah's mantle (verses 13–18)

Elijah left his mantle to Elisha.

It represented the Spirit's gift and was worth far more than a fortune of gold or silver.

Elisha did not treat it as a sacred relic to worship.

He took it as a meaningful garment for use.

Elisha's question after Elijah's departure sought two things.

**First**, he sought God, the One to whom we may always turn when the human comforts we love are removed.

**Second**, he sought **the God whom Elijah had served, honored and trusted**.

The Lord God of the holy prophets remains the same yesterday, today and forever.

What use are the mantles, positions or books left by faithful people if we do not share their Spirit and their God?

Elisha parted the Jordan.

God's people need not fear the final crossing of death, because the Lord opens the way.

The sons of the prophets insisted on searching for Elijah, although the search was unnecessary.

Wise people may sometimes consent to a harmless but pointless request for the sake of peace and goodwill.

Yet searching hills and valleys will never bring us to Elijah.

Following his faithful and zealous example may, in God's appointed time, bring us to where he has gone.

## Jericho's water healed; mockers judged (verses 19–25)

Elisha healed the water of Jericho.

Those who serve as prophets should make the places they visit better through their ministry, softening bitter hearts and making fruitless souls productive through God's word.

That word is like the salt Elisha put into the water.

The miracle pictured God's grace changing a sinful human heart.

Through the gospel, entire families, towns and cities have been transformed from wrongdoing to fruitful works of righteousness, to God's glory through Christ.

The young people of Bethel mocked Elisha as the Lord's prophet.

Their curse was not without cause.

They mocked him by saying **“Go up,”** referring contemptuously to Elijah's ascent to heaven.

Henry describes Elisha's curse as acting under an **exceptional divine prompting**.

He reasons that the ensuing judgment would not have followed unless the Holy Spirit directed that solemn action.

God is righteous, hates sin and will call it to account.

Young people should beware of wicked words.

God notices what they say.

They must not mock anyone's physical or intellectual limitations.

Mocking another person for doing good is especially dangerous.

Parents who desire true comfort from their children should teach them well from their earliest years and work to restrain the foolishness growing within them.

How terrible it will be for parents to discover at judgment that their bad example, carelessness or corrupt instruction contributed to their children's destruction!
