# Habakkuk 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The prophet beseeches God for his people. | 1, 2 |
| He calls to mind former deliverances. | 3–15 |
| His firm trust in the Divine mercy. | 16–19 |

## Habakkuk prays for God's mercy (verses 1 and 2)

Here the word **prayer** appears to describe an act of devoted worship.

The prophet asks the Lord to renew his work among the people in the midst of years of trouble.

This prayer can be applied whenever the church, or individual believers, suffer afflictions and trials.

We must flee to **God's mercy** as our refuge.

It is the only ground on which we may plead for his help.

We must not say, “Remember what we deserve.”

We should say, **“Lord, remember your own mercy.”**

## Remembering God's earlier deliverances (verses 3–15)

When God's people experience trouble and feel near despair, they should recall what he did in earlier generations.

They can bring those acts before him in prayer.

The resemblance between the **captivity in Babylon** and the earlier **slavery in Egypt** encourages hope that the Lord can deliver again.

God revealed himself in glory.

The powers of nature trembled and their usual course was altered.

All this served the salvation of his people.

Even events that appear least likely to help will be made to serve their deliverance.

These past victories were also pictures of the world's redemption through **Jesus Christ**.

The prophet spoke of salvation with God's anointed.

**Joshua**, who led Israel in battle, was a figure of the Savior who bears the corresponding name—**Jesus, our true Joshua**.

Henry understands the ancient acts of salvation as accomplished by God with reference to **Christ, the Anointed One**.

All the earlier wonders performed for Israel were surpassed by what happened when the Son of God **suffered on the cross for his people's sins**.

How glorious his resurrection and ascension were!

How much more glorious his return will be, when he puts an end to everything that opposes him and brings suffering to his people!

## Joy in the Lord despite every outward loss (verses 16–19)

When trouble approaches, we need to prepare.

A sound hope through grace is accompanied by a holy fear.

Habakkuk recalled what God had done in former times.

As he considered those deliverances, his confidence returned and his heart filled with **holy joy**.

He decided to delight and triumph in the Lord.

Even if he lost everything else, **he would not lose his God**.

If the vines and fig trees fail, all the pleasures of a merely worldly heart disappear.

But people who enjoyed God when everything was plentiful can continue to find **everything they need in him** after possessions vanish.

They can sit among the ruins of their earthly comforts and still praise the Lord as **the God of their salvation**.

They rejoice in the salvation of their souls, even through the deepest distress.

Joy in the Lord is especially appropriate when losses and troubles surround us.

Even when food is lacking, showing that human beings do not live by bread alone, the Spirit can supply **grace and consolation**.

God's gifts make us strong for spiritual work and warfare.

With hearts enlarged by grace, we can run in the way of his commandments and rise above our troubles.

The prophet began his prayer **in fear and trembling** and ended **in joy and triumph**.

Faith in Christ prepares believers for every circumstance.

When we can speak of Jesus as **our Savior**, his name becomes medicine for every wound and refreshment in every care.

It is like fragrant perfume filling the soul.

Hoping for the crown of heaven, let us hold earthly possessions lightly and bear suffering with cheerful patience.

In a little while, **the One who is coming will come**, and we will be where he is.
