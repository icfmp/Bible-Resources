# Mark 3 — Modern English

## Jesus heals a withered hand (verses 1–5)

The man's condition was pitiful: a withered hand prevented him from earning his living.

Those unable to help themselves are particularly fitting recipients of **practical charity**. We should help them.

Stubborn unbelievers may have nothing truthful to say against Christ's teaching, yet refuse to surrender to it.

People notice outward misconduct, but Jesus sees **the root of bitterness in the heart**, including hardness and blindness.

He was grieved by that inward evil.

Those who refuse repentance should fear the judgment of the One who sees their hearts.

The Sabbath is particularly suitable for acts of mercy. A house of prayer is a suitable place for healing, but **the power belongs to Christ**, not to either the day or the location.

The gospel's call resembles Jesus' command to stretch out the withered hand.

If people refuse to respond, they remain responsible for refusing the gracious summons.

But if they are healed, **all glory belongs to Christ's power and grace**.

## The crowds come to Jesus (verses 6–12)

Human sickness and suffering ultimately arise from God's just displeasure against sin.

Through **Christ's atoning blood**, the removal of such troubles—or their transformation into spiritual blessings—has been purchased for believers.

But we should fear the **diseases of the soul** even more than those of the body.

Christ can heal those spiritual troubles with a single word.

May ever more people press toward him for cleansing and deliverance from their spiritual enemies.

## Jesus appoints his apostles (verses 13–21)

Christ calls the people he wills, because grace belongs to him to give.

He summoned the apostles to separate themselves from the crowd, and they came.

He gave them power to heal diseases and cast out demons.

May the Lord continue to send people who have **been with him and learned from him** to proclaim his gospel as servants of his gracious work.

People whose hearts are fully engaged in God's service can easily endure inconvenience to themselves.

They would rather miss a meal than miss an opportunity to do good.

Those who serve God zealously should expect difficulties caused both by **the hatred of enemies** and by **the mistaken affection of friends**.

They must be alert to both.

## The scribes accuse Jesus of serving Satan (verses 22–30)

Jesus' doctrine plainly weakened the devil's kingdom.

His expulsion of demons from suffering people demonstrated the same power.

It would make no sense for Satan to support a mission directed against his own dominion.

Christ gave a serious warning about the scribes' blasphemous accusation.

The gospel offers forgiveness through Christ's saving work to even the **greatest sinners**.

But by this particular sin these opponents would oppose the gifts of the **Holy Spirit** to be poured out after Christ's ascension.

Such is the hostility of fallen hearts that they may accuse God's servants of doing Satan's work **precisely when sinners are being converted and begin living holy lives**.

## Jesus identifies his true family (verses 31–35)

All genuine Christians have a remarkable comfort.

They are dearer to Christ as his disciples than even merely human ties to a mother, brother or sister could make people, **even if those relatives were themselves holy**.

The privilege remains available today.

We cannot enjoy Christ's physical presence as his first disciples did, but **his spiritual presence is not withheld from us**.
