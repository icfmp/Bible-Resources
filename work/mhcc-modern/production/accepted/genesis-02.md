## The first Sabbath (verses 1–3)

After six days, God ceased his work of creation. In miracles, he has overridden nature, but he has never changed its established course or added to it.

God did not rest because he was tired. He rested because he was pleased with what he had made.

Here we see the beginning of the kingdom of grace, in God's setting apart the Sabbath as holy.

Everyone to whom God has made his holy Sabbaths known has a duty to observe one day in seven solemnly, as a day of holy rest and holy work for his honor.

At that time, no human beings existed except our first parents. The Sabbath was appointed for them, and clearly for all the generations that would follow.

The Christian Sabbath we observe is one day in seven. On it we celebrate the rest of God the Son and the completion of the work of our redemption.

## The Creator, the earth, and the human soul (verses 4–7)

Here the Creator is given the name “Jehovah.” Wherever our English Bibles print “LORD” in capital letters, the original uses “Jehovah.”

This name declares that God alone exists of himself, and that he gives existence to every creature and everything else.

Plants and herbs receive special attention because God made them and appointed them as food for humanity.

The earth did not produce its fruit by itself. It did so through almighty power.

In the same way, grace does not grow by itself in the natural soil of the human soul. It is God's work.

Rain, too, is God's gift. It did not come until the Lord God caused it to fall.

God ordinarily works through means, yet he can do his work without them whenever he chooses.

We must not put God to the test by neglecting the means he has given us. But we must trust him both when those means are available and when they are not.

In one way or another, God will water what he himself has planted.

Divine grace falls like dew and waters the church quietly.

God formed the human body from the fine dust on the surface of the earth.

But the soul was not made from the earth as the body was. How sad, then, that it should cling to the earth and devote itself to earthly things!

Before long, we must give God an account of how we have used our souls.

If we lose our souls, even though we gain the whole world, we are ruined forever.

Fools despise their own souls by caring more for their bodies.

## The garden of Eden and its two trees (verses 8–14)

God did not give Adam a palace to live in, but a garden.

The more content we are with simple things, and the less we seek what feeds pride and luxury, the closer we come to innocence.

Nature is content with a little and with what is most natural. Grace is content with even less. But sinful desire craves everything and is never satisfied.

Nothing can truly satisfy the soul except the delights God himself has provided and appointed for it.

*Eden* means delight and pleasure.

Wherever the garden was, it had every desirable convenience without a single disadvantage. No other house or garden on earth has ever been like it.

It was beautiful with trees pleasing to the eye and rich with trees whose fruit was delicious and good for food.

Like a tender Father, God cared not only for Adam's benefit but also for his pleasure. Innocence brings pleasure—indeed, only in innocence is there true pleasure.

When Providence places us amid abundance and delight, we ought to serve God joyfully with the good things he has given us.

Two trees were unique to Eden.

First, the tree of life stood in the middle of the garden. Adam could eat from it and live.

Christ is now the Tree of Life to us (Revelation 2:7; 22:2), and the Bread of Life (John 6:48, 51).

Second, there was the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

It bore this name because God had given a definite revelation of his will concerning it. Through that command, Adam could know moral good and evil.

What was good? Not eating from that tree. What was evil? Eating from it.

Through these two trees, God placed before Adam good and evil, blessing and curse.

## Adam is placed in the garden to work (verse 15)

After making Adam, God placed him in the garden. This left no room for boasting.

Only the One who made us can make us happy.

He formed our bodies and is the Father of our spirits. No one else can fully provide for the happiness of both.

Even in Paradise, human beings had work to do. None of us was sent into the world to be idle.

The God who gave us bodies and souls has also given us abilities with which to work. And the God who gave us the earth as our home has given us tasks to carry out upon it.

Even the children and heirs of heaven must spend some of their time and thought on earthly responsibilities while they live here.

When they do this work with their eyes on God, they serve him just as truly as when they kneel in prayer.

Farming is an ancient and honorable calling. It was needed even in Paradise.

There is also real pleasure in doing the work God has called us to do.

Adam could not have been happy in idleness. God's law still stands: anyone unwilling to work has no right to eat (2 Thessalonians 3:10).

## God's command and the trial of obedience (verses 16–17)

Let us never set our own will against God's holy will.

Adam was free to eat the fruits of Paradise. More than that, everlasting life was assured to him on condition of obedience.

God appointed a test of his obedience.

By transgressing the command, Adam would forfeit his Maker's favor and deserve his displeasure, with all its dreadful consequences: pain, disease, and death.

Worse still, he would lose God's holy image and the comfort of his favor.

He would suffer the torment of sinful passions and the terror of his Maker's vengeance, enduring forever with his undying soul.

The prohibition against eating the fruit of one particular tree was wisely suited to the condition of our first parents.

While innocent and alone in the world, what opportunity or temptation did they have to break any of the Ten Commandments?

What happened afterward shows that the whole human race was involved in the trial and fall of our first parents.

To argue against this is to struggle both against stubborn facts and against divine revelation.

Human beings are sinful. From their earliest actions, and throughout their lives, they show how ready they are to do evil.

They remain under God's displeasure, exposed to suffering and death.

Scripture consistently describes humanity as sinful and miserable in this way. These truths apply to people of every age and every nation.

## The animals, the woman, and marriage (verses 18–25)

God gave Adam authority over the creatures. As proof of that authority, Adam named them all.

Giving them names also demonstrated his understanding of God's works.

Yet although he was lord over the creatures, nothing in the created world was a suitable helper for him.

Every helper we have comes from God. If we rest in him, he will work all things for good.

God caused Adam to fall into a deep sleep. While Adam knew no sin, God ensured that he would feel no pain.

Then God, like a father bringing his daughter to her husband, brought the woman to Adam as his second self and a helper suited to him.

A wife whom God has especially prepared by his grace and brought by his providence is likely to become a fitting helper to her husband.

How much wisdom and prayer we need when choosing a relationship so close and lasting!

Something that is meant to last a lifetime must be entered into carefully.

Our first parents needed no clothes to protect them from cold or heat, because neither could harm them. Nor did they need clothing for decoration.

How easy and happy life was for humanity in its innocence!

How good God had been to Adam! How many favors he had given him! How simple were the laws he had commanded him to keep!

Yet humanity, though placed in such honor, failed to understand what was truly in its own interest, and soon became like the beasts that perish.
