# Song of Solomon 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Inquiry where Christ must be sought. | 1 |
| Where Christ may be found. | 2,3 |
| Christ's commendations of the church. | 4–10 |
| The work of grace in the believer. | 11–13 |

## Those who hear of Christ seek him (verse 1)

People who learn something of **Christ's excellence** and of the comfort of belonging to him naturally want to know where they may find him.

Anyone wishing to find Christ must seek him **early and diligently**.

## Christ among his people (verses 2 and 3)

Christ's church is like **a sheltered garden**, set apart from the world. He cares for it, visits it and delights in it.

Those wishing to find him should attend the means through which he makes himself known: **his word, the sacraments and prayer**.

When Christ comes among his people, he comes to welcome his friends and also to take believers to himself.

Henry pictures him gathering his lilies one by one. At the final day he will send angels to gather them all, so that he may be glorified in them.

The death of a believer is like a gardener picking a treasured flower. **Christ preserves that flower from fading and causes it to flourish forever**, with increasing beauty.

If our conscience can testify that we truly belong to Christ, we need not doubt his commitment to us: **the covenant will not fail on his side**.

The church takes comfort in knowing that Christ finds delight among the lilies—his own redeemed people.

## Christ's praise of the church (verses 4–10)

Every genuine form of holiness on earth belongs to the life of Christ's church.

Christ continues conquering his enemies, while believers fight and gain victories over **the world, sinful desires and the devil**.

He shows tenderness toward the people he redeemed and delights in the work of his own grace within them.

Only true believers possess the **beauty of holiness**, and when their real character becomes evident, that beauty will be recognized.

At conversion, believers resemble **the morning dawn**: their light is still small but growing.

In sanctification, the church is **fair as the moon**, receiving every ray of holiness and grace from Christ.

In justification, believers are **clear as the sun**, clothed in the righteousness of Christ, **the Sun of Righteousness**.

Under his banner they persevere in the spiritual fight of faith.

## The Spirit's work and the Shulammite (verses 11–13)

Christian character grows through **private reflection and meditation**, but not through lazy, self-indulgent withdrawal from responsibility.

When ordinary duties allow some quiet, the believer no longer finds the world's entertainments attractive.

Instead, the heart prays that the things belonging to God's Spirit may grow within it and around it.

The world may misunderstand this life and imagine such a person has abandoned true interests. In fact the believer is occupied with matters of lasting worth.

A humble Christian may prefer to avoid public notice, yet the Lord delights to honor that person.

Henry also sees a possible reference here to **angels sent to receive a believer's soul at death**. Their arrival may startle the departing Christian, but the Lord will be that soul's strength and eternal portion.

Henry understands the title **Shulammite** to signify *perfection and peace*. The church does not possess these in herself but **in Christ**—complete through his righteousness, reconciled by his blood and given peace by his Spirit.
