# 2 Samuel 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Ziba falsely accuses Mephibosheth (verses 1–4)

Ziba slandered **Mephibosheth**.

People in positions of great authority should always be cautious about flatterers.

Before making a judgment, they should take care to hear **both sides**.

## Shimei curses David (verses 5–14)

David endured Shimei's curses better than he had endured Ziba's flattery.

Flattery had led him into making an unjust judgment about someone else.

The curses led him to judge **himself** rightly.

For this reason, the world's smiles can be more dangerous than its frowns.

David had spared Saul's life more than once while Saul tried to kill him.

Yet he was now accused of bloodguilt.

Innocence does not always protect us from malice and lies.

We should not be surprised to be accused of the very wrongdoing we have been especially careful to avoid.

It is a comfort that human beings are not our final judges.

Our Judge is the Lord, who judges according to truth.

Notice how patiently David bore this insult.

His conduct reminds us of **Christ**, who prayed for those who mocked and crucified him.

A humble person can turn insults into useful rebukes and learn from them rather than responding in anger.

David recognized God's hand in what had happened and trusted that the Lord would bring good out of his suffering.

We may depend upon him to reward not only our good service but also the suffering we endure in faithfulness.

## Absalom follows wicked counsel (verses 15–23)

**Ahithophel and Hushai** were regarded as the wisest advisers of their day.

Absalom imagined that having both meant his success was certain.

He relied upon their counsel instead of seeking God, even though the ark was with him.

But neither could truly guide him to good.

Hushai had no intention of giving Absalom wise advice.

Ahithophel intentionally advised him to do something wicked.

That was just as harmful, because anyone who advises sin is advising a person toward destruction.

In the end, **honesty is the best policy**.

Ahithophel's counsel was designed to make reconciliation between David and Absalom impossible.

Such a scheme came from the devil.

How desperately wicked the human heart can be!
