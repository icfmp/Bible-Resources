# Daniel 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The conclusion of the vision of the Scriptures of truth. | 1–4 |
| The times of the continuance of these events. | 5–13 |

## The resurrection and final deliverance (verses 1–4)

The name **Michael** means **“Who is like God?”**

Henry understands this name and the title **“the great Prince”** as pointing toward **the divine Savior, Christ**.

Christ stood in place of his people as **their sacrifice**.

He bore the curse that they deserved to **remove it from them**.

He now stands for them **as their Intercessor at the throne of grace**.

After the destruction of antichrist, Henry expects **the Lord Jesus** to appear upon earth at the last day and bring about **the complete redemption of his people**.

When God delivers his people from persecution, the change is like **life from the dead**.

As the gospel spreads, people who seem asleep in dust are awakened to spiritual life.

Henry speaks of this awakening among **Jews and Gentiles**.

At the end, **the multitude sleeping in the dust will literally awaken**.

Some will rise **to life**, others **to shame**.

Future glory is reserved for all God's saints, those who are wise concerning **their souls and eternity**.

Those who help turn many people toward righteousness—bringing sinners back from error and helping save their souls from death (**James 5:20**)—will share the joy and glory of those they helped reach heaven.

That joy will increase their own happiness.

## The remaining prophetic times and Daniel's hope (verses 5–13)

One angel asked how long these extraordinary events would last.

The reply was **“a time, times, and half a time,”** the period also mentioned in **Daniel 7:25** and **Revelation**.

Henry interprets this as **1,260 prophetic days, or years**, beginning when the power of God's people was scattered.

He suggests that what he calls **“the imposture of Mohammed”**, referring polemically to Islam, and **papal domination** began at roughly the same period and represented a **twofold attack upon the church**.

These are **Henry's polemical chronology and historical identification**, not a firm date supplied by the biblical passage.

Despite the long conflict, **everything will end well**.

Every power opposing God's purpose will be overthrown.

Holiness and love will triumph and be honored forever.

This prophecy encompasses many different events over successive ages, reaching even to **the general resurrection**.

Daniel was encouraged by the prospect of his own happiness **at death, at judgment and through eternity**.

It is good for all of us to consider **our departure from this world**.

We must leave it, but not until God calls us and has completed the work he assigned to us here.

It is as though God then says: **Go your way. Your work is finished. Others may take your place.**

The comfort Daniel received belongs to every faithful believer.

Whatever their circumstances during life, **they will receive a good portion at the end**.

This should be everyone's chief concern.

With such hope we can be content with our present circumstances and welcome God's will.

Believers can know happiness already: **they rest in God by faith now**, and **a fuller rest is reserved for them in heaven**.
