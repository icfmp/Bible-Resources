# Isaiah 23 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The overthrow of Tyre. | 1–14 |
| It is established again. | 15–18 |

## Tyre's commercial greatness is brought low (verses 1–14)

Tyre had become **a great marketplace for the nations**. Its inhabitants were especially known for festivity and entertainment, and those pleasures made them reluctant to consider the warnings God sent through his servants.

Its merchants were as wealthy and honored as princes, and they lived with all the luxury of rulers.

Yet its splendor would not keep the city from destruction. When Tyre lay waste, the merchants would flee from it and seek shelter elsewhere.

Those who move away to escape a trouble that has unsettled them may discover that a different location cannot give them inward rest. **When God's judgments pursue sinners, they will overtake them wherever they go.**

How could such a wealthy and powerful city fall? Its destruction was **from the Almighty**.

God intended its fall to reveal the emptiness and uncertainty of earthly fame, magnificence and prosperity.

The ruin of Tyre warns every city and every person to beware of pride. **Whoever exalts himself will be humbled.**

The Lord had the power to bring this judgment himself; he chose to use **the Chaldeans** as his instruments.

## Tyre's recovery and the right use of wealth (verses 15–18)

Tyre's ruin would **not last forever**. The Lord would eventually visit the city with mercy.

Yet when Tyre returned to commercial freedom, its people would once more practice the old arts through which they tempted others.

Love of worldly wealth is **spiritual idolatry**. Covetousness is also a form of idolatry.

The prophecy therefore teaches those who possess wealth to employ it in **God's service**.

When we remain in fellowship with God while pursuing our ordinary occupations, and do everything within our ability to advance the gospel, our goods and earnings become **holy to the Lord**, provided our true purpose is his glory.

Christians should undertake commercial work as **God's servants**, treating riches as possessions entrusted to them for which they must answer as his stewards.
