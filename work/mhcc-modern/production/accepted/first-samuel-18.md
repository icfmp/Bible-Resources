# 1 Samuel 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jonathan's friendship for David. | 1–5 |
| Saul seeks to kill David. | 6–11 |
| Saul's fear of David. | 12–30 |

## Jonathan and David become friends (verses 1–5)

The friendship between David and Jonathan was a work of divine grace.

Grace brings genuine believers into fellowship, giving them **one heart and one soul** and causing them to love one another.

This union comes from sharing the Spirit of Christ.

When God has united hearts, worldly differences are too weak to tear them apart.

Those who love Jesus Christ as they love their own souls will gladly enter an everlasting covenant with him.

It was a remarkable sign of God's grace in David that he received so much praise and honor without becoming proud or imagining himself greater than he was.

## Saul tries to kill David (verses 6–11)

David's troubles followed immediately after his triumph.

Indeed, they arose **because** of that triumph.

This shows how empty even the greatest worldly success can be.

A person who has become irritable, envious, suspicious and malicious like Saul displays signs that God's Spirit no longer governs the heart.

Compare **David holding a harp**, seeking to serve Saul, with **Saul holding a spear**, seeking to kill David.

Notice both the gentleness and usefulness of God's persecuted servant and the cruelty of the persecutor.

David's safety, however, belonged to God's providence.

## Saul fears David and seeks his destruction (verses 12–30)

David lived for a long time under the constant threat of being killed by Saul.

Yet he continued behaving with gentleness, respect, wisdom and restraint toward the very person persecuting him.

How rare such sound judgment and self-control are amid insult and provocation!

We should ask whether we follow David's example.

Do we behave wisely in every part of our lives?

Are there avoidable failures of duty, rash words or sinful reactions in us?

Other people's stubbornness or unfair treatment never justifies sinful anger in ourselves.

It should instead make us more careful to fulfill the responsibilities belonging to our own position.

Consider Christ, who endured sinners' hostility against himself, so that you do not become weary and lose heart (**Hebrews 12:3**).

David considered it an honor to become the king's son-in-law.

How much more should we value the privilege of being **children of the King of kings**!
