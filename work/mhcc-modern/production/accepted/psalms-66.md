# Psalm 66 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Praise for God's sovereign power in the creation. | 1–7 |
| For his favour to his church. | 8–12 |
| And the psalmist's praise for his experience of God's goodness. | 13–20 |

## Praise for God's sovereign power (verses 1–7)

The holy church throughout the world should raise its voice to praise the name above every name.

By **both words and deeds**, believers should make the praise of Jesus glorious, so that others will be moved to glorify him.

No one can praise God rightly unless **his effective grace recreates the heart in holiness**.

The redemption accomplished through Christ's death and the wonderful deliverances that flow from it are **greater works than Israel's rescue from Egyptian slavery**.

We should remember these mighty acts when inviting all the nations to worship God.

## God's kindness toward his church (verses 8–12)

The Lord not only preserves our bodily lives; he sustains the **spiritual life** he has given believers.

Through suffering, his people are tested like **silver in the furnace**.

The trials of the church will certainly end well.

Through conflicts and troubles, people enslaved by Satan are delivered from his rule and brought to **joy and peace in believing**.

Believers must pass through many tribulations before entering God's kingdom.

## A testimony to answered prayer (verses 13–20)

Tell others who fear God what he has done for your soul.

Describe how he heard and answered prayer, and invite them to join in thanksgiving. This encourages everyone and brings glory to the Lord.

But the comforts of fellowship with God cannot be enjoyed while we **cherish the love of sin in the heart**, even if we avoid openly committing it.

Deliberately protecting a cherished sin destroys the comfort and effectiveness of prayer. God finds the sacrifice of the wicked detestable.

There is, however, an important difference between **welcoming sinful desires** and **grieving over their presence while firmly resisting them**.

If we feel sin within but sincerely long to be freed from it and refuse its demands, that is evidence of genuine repentance and sincerity.

Prayers offered with simplicity and godly sincerity will be answered according to God's goodness.

Then our gratitude rises toward the One who has not turned away either our prayer or his mercy.

**Prayer did not obtain deliverance by its own power; God's mercy sent the deliverance.**

That mercy is the foundation of hope, the fountain of comfort and the proper object of praise.
