# Isaiah 33 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's judgments against the enemies of his church. | 1–14 |
| The happiness of his people. | 15–24 |

## God defeats the proud destroyer (verses 1–14)

The deceitful and proud destroyer will answer for violence and fraud.

The righteous Lord sometimes repays wicked people **in the same manner in which they treated others**.

Those who wait humbly upon God through faith will find him gracious.

**As each day comes, so may the strength for that day come.**

If God abandoned us to ourselves for even one morning, we would be lost.

We should therefore place ourselves in his hands **every morning**, and undertake the day's work through his strength.

When he rises to defend his people, his enemies scatter.

True knowledge and wisdom lead to **the strength of salvation**, making us steadfast in God's ways.

True devotion is the only treasure that cannot be robbed or consumed.

Jerusalem faced terrible suffering.

But God's time to help his people often comes precisely when **every other helper has failed**.

Everyone who hears what God has accomplished should recognize that **he is able to do all things**.

Sinners within Zion must answer for privileges they knowingly abused, an accountability greater than that of others.

Those who reject the commands of God's word cannot claim its comforts when distress arrives.

God's wrath against unrepentant sin is pictured as **unquenchable fire**: the justice of the eternally living God confronting the guilt of a soul that does not cease to exist.

## The security and joy of the upright (verses 15–24)

True believers remain watchful against **opportunities to sin**.

God's power keeps them safe; faith in that power enables them to remain at peace.

They will not lack anything truly necessary for their ultimate good.

The blessings of salvation are freely given to all who seek God in **humble, believing prayer**.

The upright receive bread and water, but they also see **the King in his beauty—the beauty of holiness**.

Remembering earlier terrors deepens the joy of deliverance.

It is good to enjoy peace in one's own home, but better still to enjoy peace **in God's house**.

Christ will always preserve people who serve him.

Jerusalem had no large river flowing past it.

Yet **God's presence and power were sufficient to supply every need**.

Through faith we receive Christ as **Prince and Savior**, reigning over his redeemed people.

Those who refuse his reign place their souls in peril.

Illness is removed mercifully when its spiritual result is that **sin is taken away**.

If guilt has been pardoned, believers have far less reason to complain about bodily suffering.

The final verse points beyond earthly blessing toward **heaven**, where sickness and trouble cannot enter.

The One who blots out our transgressions will also **heal our souls**.
