# 1 Samuel 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Samuel sent to Bethlehem to Jesse. | 1–5 |
| David is anointed. | 6–13 |
| Saul troubled with an evil spirit, is quieted by David. | 14–23 |

## Samuel is sent to Bethlehem (verses 1–5)

Saul had become very wicked. If he could even contemplate killing Samuel, what evil might he not commit?

The elders of Bethlehem trembled when Samuel arrived. We should stand in awe of God's messengers and tremble at his word.

Samuel answered that he had come in peace, to offer a sacrifice.

When the Lord Jesus came into the world, people might have feared that he had come to condemn it. Instead, he assured them that his mission was peace: he came to sacrifice, bringing the offering himself. God had prepared a body for him.

Let us therefore consecrate ourselves to the Lord and rely upon Christ's sacrifice.

## David is anointed (verses 6–13)

Samuel had been disappointed by Saul, whose impressive appearance and height had recommended him. Yet Samuel almost made the same mistake again.

We see how people look. God sees what they truly are.

He examines the heart, valuing the faith, reverence and love planted there—qualities beyond ordinary human sight.

We often judge other people's characters wrongly.

Nor does God distribute his favor to our children according to our partial preferences. He frequently blesses those whom others esteem least.

At last David was chosen. He was Jesse's youngest son, apparently the least regarded. His name means **“beloved,”** and Henry sees him as a picture of God's beloved Son.

The Lord's Spirit came upon David from that day onward.

His anointing was not an empty ceremony. Divine power accompanied the appointed sign.

Although David's outward situation did not immediately change, he received growing wisdom, courage and the qualities needed for kingship. This confirmed that God had chosen him.

In the same way, the best evidence of being appointed to the kingdom of glory is the **Spirit of promise sealing us**, and the work of grace experienced within our hearts.

## David's music relieves Saul (verses 14–23)

Saul had become a terror to himself. The Spirit of the Lord departed from him.

If we do not let God and his grace rule us, sin and Satan will take possession.

With God's permission, the devil troubled Saul through his disordered bodily condition and the passions of his mind. He became irritable, dissatisfied and at times frenzied.

Music can improve a person's state of mind. It is therefore regrettable when it is misused to support vanity, luxury or distraction from God and serious thought. Such misuse drives away the **good Spirit**, not the evil spirit.

Music, recreation, company and business may temporarily quiet an injured conscience.

But only **Christ's blood, received by faith**, and the **sanctifying Spirit**, assuring the soul of pardon through holy comfort, can accomplish genuine healing.

Every other attempt to silence spiritual conviction without dealing with sin will eventually deepen the distress, whether in this life or the next.
