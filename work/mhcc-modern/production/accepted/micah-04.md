# Micah 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The peace of the kingdom of Christ. | 1–8 |
| The judgments to come upon Jerusalem, but the final triumph of Israel. | 9–13 |

## Peace in Christ's kingdom (verses 1–8)

The nations have not yet submitted to **the Prince of Peace** so fully that swords have become plowshares and warfare has ceased.

Yet these promises concerning **the gospel church** are precious.

They will increasingly be fulfilled because **the One who promised is faithful**.

In the latter days, during the reign of **the Messiah**, a glorious church will be established.

**Christ himself will build it upon a rock**.

The Gentiles once worshiped false gods.

But the people described here will hold firmly to the Lord, love him wholeheartedly and delight in obeying his will.

Those who **limp** in their spiritual walk are people who do not follow God's word faithfully.

The return of the captives from Babylon was **a pledge of future healing, cleansing and prosperity for the church**.

Christ's reign will continue until it reaches the everlasting kingdom of heaven.

We should encourage one another to attend **God's appointed worship**, learn his ways and obey them.

God's law must be **written upon our hearts by his Spirit**.

That transformation gives evidence of our share in **the Redeemer's righteousness**.

## Jerusalem's distress and final triumph (verses 9–13)

Many nations would gather against Zion, intending to celebrate her defeat.

But they would not know that the Lord had brought them together **like bundles of grain prepared for threshing**.

Zion would receive strength to overcome them.

Henry says nothing in Jewish history up to his time fully corresponded to this prophecy.

He expected its fulfillment **in a future event**.

Whenever God assigns his people work to overcome opposition, he also provides **the strength and ability they need**.

Believers facing suffering should cry out **in the prayer of faith**, not in hopelessness.
