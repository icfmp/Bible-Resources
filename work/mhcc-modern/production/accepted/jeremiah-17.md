# Jeremiah 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The fatal consequences of the idolatry of the Jews. | 1–4 |
| The happiness of the man that trusts in God; the end of the opposite character. | 5–11 |
| The malice of the prophet's enemies. | 12–18 |
| The observance of the sabbath. | 19–27 |

## Sin written on the heart (verses 1–4)

People often scarcely notice the sins they commit.

Yet **every sin is recorded before God**, and all are so engraved upon the human heart that conscience will eventually recall them.

What is engraved in the heart becomes visible in a person's conduct.

**Our actions reveal the desires and purposes within us**.

How much reason we have to humble ourselves before the Lord when we consider our sinfulness in his sight!

Let us depend upon his mercy and grace.

Ask him to **search and test our hearts**, keep us from being deceived by them and create in us, through his Spirit, **a new, clean and holy nature**.

## The desert shrub and the fruitful tree (verses 5–11)

Someone who trusts in human beings is like **a lonely shrub in a desert**—the poor and useless product of barren land.

People who depend on **their own righteousness and strength**, imagining they can do without Christ, have made **human flesh their support**.

They cannot prosper in spiritual grace or comfort.

But those who make God their hope flourish like **an evergreen tree**.

They remain settled in peace and contentment, with no need for panic in a year of drought.

God supplies every comfort they truly need, even when created comforts are absent.

They do not stop bearing **the fruit of holiness and good works**.

The heart and conscience of fallen humanity are **more deceitful than anything else**.

They call evil good and good evil.

They pronounce **peace upon people who have no true peace**.

This is how desperate its corruption is.

Conscience should correct the errors of our other faculties.

But when conscience itself becomes the leader in self-deception, our situation is especially serious.

We cannot fully know our hearts or how they will behave in a sudden temptation.

**Who can understand their own errors?**

Still less can we fully know or safely depend on **other people's hearts**.

Anyone accepting God's testimony about this will learn to **watch the heart** and will recognize many useful lessons for conduct.

Much about our own motives and those of others will remain unknown to us.

But **nothing within us is hidden from God**.

People may be deceived.

**He cannot be deceived**.

Those who acquire wealth dishonestly will not truly enjoy it.

At death the worldly person must leave possessions behind.

**The wealth cannot follow into eternity, but its guilt can**.

Someone may spend a whole life acquiring wealth, brood over it anxiously and never receive satisfaction.

Sinful gain may disappear altogether.

Let us become wise in time: **earn honestly and give generously**, so that we may be prepared for eternity.

## The Lord is a fountain of comfort (verses 12–18)

Jeremiah acknowledged God's goodness in establishing true worship.

The Lord is an overflowing **fountain of comfort**, forever fresh and clear like springwater.

The sinful world's pleasures resemble **muddy puddles**.

The prophet asked for the Lord's healing and saving mercy.

He appealed to God as the One who knew how faithfully he had fulfilled his prophetic calling.

He humbly asked the Lord to acknowledge and defend him in the service to which he had called him.

Whatever wounds or diseases afflict our **hearts and consciences**, let us seek healing from God.

His hands can bind up troubled consciences and heal broken hearts.

He can cure **the deepest corruption of our nature**, giving us reason to praise his name.

## Keep the Sabbath holy (verses 19–27)

Jeremiah had to set before both rulers and ordinary people the command to keep **the Sabbath day holy**.

They were to obey the **fourth commandment**.

God promised renewed prosperity if they obeyed.

The Sabbath is appointed for rest, not ordinary labor, except when **necessity** requires work.

We should watch carefully against treating the day as common.

Even our souls should not be weighed down with **worldly business and anxieties** on the Sabbath.

Henry compares religious life to a river: the stream runs deep or shallow according to whether **the banks of Sabbath observance** are maintained or neglected.

The care with which a people observe this ordinance, or the indifference they show toward it, reveals much about their spiritual condition.

Everyone should work through **personal example and attention to family life** to prevent its neglect.

National good matters, but even more important is **the salvation of souls**.
