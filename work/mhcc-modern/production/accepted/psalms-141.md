# Psalm 141 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David prays for God's acceptance and assistance. | 1–4 |
| That God would appear for his rescue. | 5–10 |

## Prayer, self-control and protection from sin (verses 1–4)

The more we value God's gracious presence, the more earnestly we will seek him in prayer.

David asked God to come quickly.

Our prayers become acceptable to him **through the sacrifice and intercession of Jesus Christ**, just as the daily sacrifices and burning of incense were acceptable under the former covenant.

Prayer is **a spiritual sacrifice** in which we offer our souls and best affections to God.

Faithful people know the danger of **sins committed with the tongue**.

When enemies provoke us, we may speak rashly and do wrong.

Because we live in a fallen world and have sinful hearts, we need grace to keep us from being **enticed or driven into evil**.

Sinners imagine that sin offers them delicious food.

But its pleasures quickly turn bitter.

Those who understand this pray that the Lord will remove such enticements and turn their hearts against them.

**God's people pray against the pleasures of sin**.

## Welcoming correction and seeking rescue (verses 5–10)

We should receive our heavenly Father's correction and welcome **loving rebuke from fellow believers**.

It will not break my head if it helps to **break my hard heart**.

We should respond to such correction gratefully.

Those who formerly ignored God's word may welcome it eagerly once suffering has opened their ears to instruction.

**When the world tastes bitter, God's word tastes sweet**.

We should pray for deliverance from Satan's traps and from the schemes of people devoted to wrongdoing.

Lord, let our inadequate prayers express the fact that **you alone are our hope and confidence**.

Prepare us for faithful prayer through your grace.

Clothe us in **Christ's righteousness** and plant the **Holy Spirit's gifts** within our hearts.
