# 2 Chronicles 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Joash, of Judah, The temple repaired. | 1–14 |
| Joash falls into idolatry, He is slain by his servants. | 15–27 |

## Joash repairs the temple (verses 1–14)

Joash showed even greater zeal for repairing the temple than **Jehoiada** did.

Yet it is easier to build or repair a temple than **to become a temple of God oneself**.

Repairing places used for public worship is still a good work that everyone should help advance.

Many worthy projects remain unfinished simply because no energetic person takes responsibility for moving them forward.

## Joash abandons God and is killed (verses 15–27)

The death of a faithful, zealous and useful servant of God can be a great judgment on a ruler or people.

This shows why our faith must spring from an **inward principle**, not depend entirely on the presence of another human being.

Otherwise, when a parent, minister or friend dies, we may lose our religion along with that person.

Rulers and ordinary people alike have often been **flattered into ruin**.

Only genuine grace enables a person to keep bearing spiritual fruit until the end.

**Zechariah**, Jehoiada's son, stood up in the Spirit of prophecy and exposed the people's sin.

That is the task of ministers: using God's word like a lamp, they reveal wrongdoing and explain the meaning of divine Providence.

The people stoned Zechariah to death **in the temple court**.

As he died, he called upon the Lord to see and require judgment for the crime.

Henry says this was **not a cry of private revenge but a prophetic declaration**.

God struck Joash with severe illness, whether in body, mind or both, before the Syrians left.

When judgment pursues sinners, the end of one trouble may simply introduce another.

Joash's own servants killed him.

The judgments were described as burdens because God's wrath is a load too heavy for any person to bear.

May God help us learn from the warning, remain upright in heart and persevere in his ways until the end.
