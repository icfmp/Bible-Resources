# Deuteronomy 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Intercourse with the Canaanites forbidden. | 1–11 |
| Promises if they were obedient. | 12–26 |

## Israel must reject idolatry (verses 1–11)

Moses warned Israel strictly against friendship and fellowship with idols and those devoted to them.

Those who live in fellowship with God must not share in the fruitless works of darkness.

The commands to destroy these nations were restricted **to the nations specifically named**.

Henry explicitly says this restriction shows that **later generations must not use these commands as a precedent**.

An adequate understanding of sin's evil and of the mystery of the crucified Savior helps us recognize God's justice in both present and eternal judgments.

The Christian's own warfare, however, is with the sinful desires that attack the soul.

We should deal with those desires decisively, putting them to death instead of treating them gently.

Many people have been ruined by marriages that draw them away from God.

Henry warns that in a religiously unequal marriage it is more likely that the faithful person will be led astray than that the unfaithful person will be converted.

Those who choose a life partner outside shared faith cannot assume that partner will help them live for the Lord.

## God's promises and the gradual defeat of sin (verses 12–26)

We risk sharing in the works of darkness when we take delight in the company of those who practice them.

Anything that becomes a trap to us exposes us to judgment.

If we remain constant in our duties, we may trust God's constant mercy.

Diseases, Henry says, are under God's providence: they go where he sends them and fulfill his purposes.

Putting our souls' sins to death is therefore good even for our bodily welfare.

Sin will never be completely destroyed within us during this life.

Indeed, it would exercise far less power over us if we were more alert and diligent.

God acts according to his own hidden purpose.

But that secret purpose gives **no excuse whatsoever for laziness or neglect**.

We should not imagine that because the church's deliverance or the defeat of our sinful desires does not happen immediately, it will never happen.

God carries out his own work in the way and at the time he chooses.

His method and timing are always best.

Corruption is gradually driven from believers' hearts.

Sanctification advances little by little and will finally end in complete victory.

But pride, carelessness and other sins encouraged by prosperity can be more dangerous than wild animals.

They are especially liable to increase in people who think themselves secure.
