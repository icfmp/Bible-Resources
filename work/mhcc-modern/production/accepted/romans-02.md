# Romans 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews could not be justified by the law of Moses, any more than the Gentiles by the law of nature. | 1–16 |
| The sins of the Jews confuted all their vain confidence in their outward privileges. | 17–29 |

## Judgment is based upon our true conduct (verses 1–16)

The Jews thought they were a holy people who deserved their privileges, even while they were unthankful, rebellious and unrighteous.

But everyone who behaves this way, of every nationality, generation and social position, must remember that **God judges according to a person's true character**.

The matter is so plain that we may appeal to the sinner's own conscience.

Every deliberate sin expresses contempt for God's goodness.

Although disobedience appears in many different forms, it grows from the same underlying root.

True repentance requires **hatred of former sinfulness**.

It comes from **a change in the inner person**, leading us to choose good and refuse evil.

Repentance also involves recognizing our own inward misery.

This great transformation is **conversion**, and every human being needs it.

Sinners' ruin comes from a heart that remains **hard and unrepentant**.

Such people are described as **storing up wrath** for themselves.

In Paul's account of righteous conduct, notice how demanding God's law is.

It requires **pure motives**, rejecting actions performed for merely selfish ambitions or earthly ends.

His account of unrighteousness shows **contention against God** as a principle of evil.

The fallen human will stands in enmity toward him.

Even Gentiles without the written law possess an inward moral awareness through **the light of nature**.

**Conscience bears witness**.

Sooner or later it will testify, approving or condemning people according to their response to what they knew.

Nothing should bring greater fear to sinners or greater comfort to saints than the fact that **Jesus Christ will be the Judge**.

At his judgment, hidden acts of devotion will receive their reward, and secret sins will be exposed and punished.

## Mere profession cannot excuse Jewish sin (verses 17–24)

Paul now addressed Jewish people who professed to know God but continued in sinful behavior.

There is a right way to glory in God: **humbly, thankfully and through faith**.

This attitude lies at the heart of true religion.

But there is also **proud boasting in God and in possessing outward religious privileges**.

This lies at the heart of hypocrisy.

Spiritual pride is the most dangerous kind of pride.

A grave evil committed by people who profess the faith is that **their conduct brings dishonor upon God and religion**.

Some look down on neighbors who possess only a lifeless outward form of godliness.

Yet these critics may themselves trust in a **lifeless form of religious knowledge**.

Others boast of the gospel while living such unholy lives that they cause **God's name to be blasphemed**.

## True belonging to God requires inward renewal (verses 25–29)

No outward form, ordinance or religious theory benefits a person without **regenerating grace**.

Such grace leads a sinner to seek **God's righteousness through faith**.

A Christian who is merely one outwardly is no more a genuine Christian than an Israelite who was only outwardly a Jew was spiritually faithful.

Neither is true baptism merely something outward in the flesh.

The real Christian is someone who inwardly believes and shows **obedient faith**.

The true baptism of the heart is **the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit**.

This brings a spiritual frame of mind and a willingness to follow God's truth in holy living.

Let us pray that we may become **real Christians inwardly**, in heart and spirit rather than only in outward letter.

May we be baptized **not merely with water but with the Holy Spirit**.

And may our praise come **from God, not from human beings**.
