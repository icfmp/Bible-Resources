# Deuteronomy 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Enticers to idolatry to be put to death. | 1–5 |
| Relations who entice to idolatry not to be spared. | 6–11 |
| Idolatrous cities not to be spared. | 12–18 |

## Resisting false religious teaching (verses 1–5)

Moses had already warned about the danger posed by the Canaanites. Now he warned against idolatry arising **within Israel itself**.

We must become familiar with the truths and commands of Scripture, because temptation often comes disguised as something good and error may appear to be truth.

Only the clear and explicit testimony of God's word can reliably expose such deception.

Sincere love for God is shown by refusing to abandon him and serve false gods, even when the temptation is presented attractively.

## Resisting temptation from loved ones (verses 6–11)

Satan often seeks to lead us into evil through people we love.

We are least likely to suspect them of intending harm, are eager to please them and find it natural to follow their example.

The person tempting us might be a brother, child, wife or close friend—someone bound to us by family or affection.

Even so, we must put God and faithfulness to him before our dearest earthly relationships.

We must not break God's law merely to please those we love.

We should refuse the invitation, even if the reason for considering it is curiosity, companionship or a desire to gain the person's affection.

“If sinners entice you, do not consent” (Proverbs 1:10).

Henry also reminds his readers that people must not obstruct God's justice.

## The case of an idolatrous Israelite city (verses 12–18)

The law describes an Israelite city turning away from its God to worship idols.

Henry observes that even when ancient Israel was commanded to **preserve** its faith through force, it was **not permitted to compel outsiders into that faith through fire and sword**.

He distinguishes those ancient civil judgments from the gospel era.

Under the Christian dispensation, the spiritual judgments at stake are more dreadful than the execution of criminals.

Christians still have every reason to fear God's anger against sin.

We should especially beware the spiritual idolatry of greed and devotion to worldly pleasure.

Parents must not encourage such idolatry by their example or in the way they raise their children.

May the Lord write his law and truth upon our hearts, reign there and pour his love into us!
