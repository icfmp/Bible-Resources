## The idolatry within the temple (verses 1–6)

In a vision, the glorious figure Ezekiel saw seemed to take hold of him and carry him **in the Spirit to Jerusalem**.

There he saw a place prepared for **a degrading idol within the inner court of the temple**.

The entire scene was presented to the prophet as a vision.

If God gave any of us a clear view of **his glory and majesty** alongside the many abominations practiced in a city, we would see the justice of even his severest judgments against that city.

## The elders' secret worship (verses 7–12)

A hidden room was opened to Ezekiel.

The walls were covered with pictures of creatures, and **elders of Israel** worshiped before them.

High position in the world does not preserve people from sinful desires or idolatry if they are abandoned to the deceit of their own hearts.

Some who quickly tire of God's service are willing to spend **great time, labor and money on superstition**.

Hypocrites try to conceal themselves behind a wall of outward profession.

Yet some opening remains through which careful observers may discover what they are really doing.

Much wickedness is practiced in secret.

These people imagined God could not see them.

Those who try to make **the Lord responsible for their sins** are especially ready for judgment.

## Tammuz, sun worship and the depth of corruption (verses 13–18)

The annual mourning for **Tammuz** was associated, in Henry's account, with shameful practices.

He supposes that those worshiping **the sun** were priests.

The Lord called Ezekiel to recognize the gravity of these sins.

The reference to **putting a branch to the nose** is, in Henry's explanation, a custom performed in honor of an idol.

The more closely we examine **human nature and our own hearts**, the more wrongdoing we discover.

The more earnestly a believer searches the heart, the more humbly that person should stand before God and the more highly value **the fountain opened for cleansing from sin**.

Let us seek to wash there.
