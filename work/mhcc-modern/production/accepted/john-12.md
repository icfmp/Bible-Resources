# John 12 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Mary anoints Jesus (verses 1–11)

Jesus had once corrected Martha for being distracted by too much service. She did not respond by abandoning service altogether, as people sometimes run from one extreme into another when corrected.

She continued serving, now **within hearing of Christ's gracious words**.

Mary expressed affection toward Jesus, who had already shown profound love to her household.

God had anointed Christ with the oil of gladness above his companions. We ought to offer him the **best fragrance of our hearts' affections**.

Judas concealed an ugly sin beneath a plausible concern for the poor.

We should not suppose that someone fails to serve God acceptably merely because they do not serve **in our preferred way**.

When the love of money rules the heart, it becomes a kind of **theft within the heart**.

The grace of Christ interprets godly words and deeds charitably, making the best of their imperfections and most of their goodness.

Opportunities for service must be used, especially those least likely to return.

The authorities proposed killing Lazarus, the very person Jesus had raised. Their hatred was so unreasonable that only the heart's hostility against God explains it.

The gospel's success can enrage wicked people until they behave as if they could defeat **the Almighty himself**.

## Jesus enters Jerusalem (verses 12–19)

All four evangelists recount Jesus' triumphal entry.

His disciples did not immediately understand all that Scripture and God's providence were accomplishing.

We, too, often understand the spiritual meaning of divine events only afterward.

Understanding that **Christ's kingdom is spiritual** keeps us from misusing prophecies about it.

## Greeks seek Jesus (verses 20–26)

Whenever we attend sacred worship, especially the gospel Passover, our deepest desire should be **to see Jesus**—to know him as ours, enjoy fellowship with him and receive grace from him.

The coming of Gentiles to seek him displayed the Redeemer's glory.

A grain of wheat bears no harvest unless it falls into the ground.

Christ could have remained in heavenly glory without becoming human. Even after taking human nature, he might have entered heaven by his own perfect righteousness without suffering or death.

But then **no human sinner could have been saved**.

Every soul saved before Christ's crucifixion, and every soul saved afterward, depends upon **the death of this grain of wheat**.

Let us ask whether Christ lives within us as our hope of glory. May he loosen our attachment to trivial concerns, enabling us to serve willingly and follow his holy example.

## A heavenly voice testifies to Jesus (verses 27–33)

Our sins brought distress to Christ's soul when he undertook to redeem us and **offer his soul for our sins**.

He was willing to suffer, yet still prayed to be delivered from suffering.

**Praying against trouble can coexist with patient submission** to God's will.

Christ humbled himself to satisfy the honor of God offended through human sin.

The Father had already declared Jesus his beloved Son at his baptism and transfiguration. Now a voice again confirmed that God's name had been glorified and would be glorified.

By the merit of his death Christ reconciles sinners to God, breaks death's dominion and casts Satan down as a destroyer.

Through the teaching of his cross, he breaks sin's power and drives Satan out as a deceiver.

Souls once far from Christ come to love and trust him. Ascending to heaven, Jesus draws their hearts toward himself.

There is **power in Christ's death to draw sinners to him**.

The gospel exalts free grace **and** commands duty. We must embrace both sincerely rather than separate them.

## Jesus urges the people to walk in the light (verses 34–36)

The listeners misunderstood Scripture because they overlooked the prophecies about the Messiah's suffering and death.

Jesus told them the light would remain among them only a little longer. They should believe it and walk in it before darkness overtook them.

Without faith, people fail to recognize the saving significance of Jesus lifted upon the cross and cannot experience its power as revealed by the Holy Spirit.

They invent countless objections to justify unbelief.

## Many refuse to believe (verses 37–43)

Conversion begins as sinners see the reality of divine things.

They learn something of God's truth and turn from sin to Christ as **their happiness and their portion**.

God heals them: **justifying and sanctifying** them, forgiving sins like open wounds and mortifying corrupt desires like hidden diseases.

But worldly approval can smother even serious convictions.

A desire for human praise can make people **hypocrites** when religion is fashionable and earns respect.

The same desire can make them **apostates** when religion is despised and threatens their reputation.

## Jesus gives his final public appeal (verses 44–50)

Jesus publicly taught that faith in him is also faith in the Father who sent him.

In Christ we see the Father's glory and learn to obey, love and trust the Father.

By continually looking to Jesus, the light of the world, we are increasingly freed from the darkness of ignorance, error, sin and misery.

God our Savior's command is **everlasting life**.

Yet the same divine word will confirm the condemnation of those who reject or neglect it.

## Editorial review — John 12

**Whole secondary original-English witness directly read:** [CCEL, John 12](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xxxv.xii.html). Original `MHCC 0.1.1` remains authoritative; native SWORD/markup/notes matching and independent meaning acceptance pending.

- **1–11:** Martha **continues serving after correction**, Mary's anointing and best affections, Judas's **false piety masking greed**, charity concerning differing kinds of service, threatened **second killing of Lazarus**.
- **12–19:** four-Gospel entry, disciples' delayed understanding, Christ's spiritual kingdom.
- **20–26:** Gentiles seek Savior; **grain of wheat's death necessary for saving sinners before and after**, personal communion and holy service.
- **27–33:** Christ's voluntary suffering and prayer for relief, divine voice, Satan **destroyer and deceiver** overcome by death/cross, **free grace and duty together**.
- **34–36:** overlooked prophecies about suffering, urgent faith in the light.
- **37–43:** conversion as justification **and** sanctification; people's applause producing **hypocrisy or apostasy**.
- **44–50:** believing Christ means believing Father, progressive liberation from darkness, gospel word and final judgment.

**Secondary transcription defect:** CCEL reads “The sin of our souls was the **troubled** of Christ's soul” in 27–33. The intended sense is rendered in Modern English, but only direct published MHCC 0.1.1 collation can establish the authoritative reading; no source correction is claimed.
