# Hebrews 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Hebrews are urged to go forward in the doctrine of Christ, and the consequences of apostacy, or turning back, are described. | 1–8 |
| The apostle expresses satisfaction, as to the most of them. | 9, 10 |
| And encourages them to persevere in faith and holiness. | 11–20 |

## Progress toward maturity and beware apostasy (verses 1–8)

Every part of God's truth and will must be explained to those professing the gospel and pressed upon their hearts and consciences.

Outward matters have their proper place, but they can consume attention that should be directed to more important things.

**A repentant sinner, confessing guilt and crying for mercy, must not take this warning as proof that forgiveness is impossible.**

Nor does this text prove that a person genuinely **made new in Christ** can become a final apostate.

According to Henry, it is not describing people who had **only an empty religious name** and no experience of gospel truth, because such people have little from which to fall.

It does not refer to **temporary spiritual decline**, ordinary backsliding or even grave sins into which believers may fall under powerful temptation.

Instead, the sin warned against is **an open and deliberate repudiation of Christ**, arising from hatred toward him and his people, approving the actions of his murderers **after knowing gospel truth and tasting some of its comfort**.

Of such persons it says that renewal to repentance is impossible.

This is **not because Christ's blood would be insufficient to pardon such a sin**.

Rather, the sin itself is a settled opposition to **repentance and everything leading to it**.

Those frightened by misunderstanding their own condition should recognize the difference between **deliberate, hostile renunciation** and the grief of a repentant conscience.

Warn seriously against apostasy, but **do not unnecessarily terrorize weak believers or discourage those who have fallen and now repent**.

True believers do more than taste God's word: they drink it in and bear fruit, like well-watered soil.

A merely nominal Christian who remains fruitless or produces deceit and self-interest is in serious danger. We must watch ourselves with humility and prayer.

## Works of love accompany salvation (verses 9 and 10)

Certain qualities belong to genuine salvation and lead to eternal salvation.

They are better evidence than outward enlightenment or temporary religious experiences.

**Acts of love done for Christ's glory and for his people** are signs of saving grace.

Genuine love is **active love**; and works are genuinely good only when they spring from **love for Christ**.

## The sure promise and anchor of hope (verses 11–20)

Christian hope means confidently anticipating what God has promised, desiring those blessings and valuing them.

Hope, like faith, may vary in strength.

God's promise comes from his **eternal purpose**, settled among **Father, Son and Holy Spirit**.

His counsel and oath are two unchangeable things. **God cannot lie**, because lying contradicts his will and nature.

His promises therefore ensure believers' salvation just as his warnings confirm judgment upon unbelief.

Believers inherit those promises.

God's comforts are strong enough for the heaviest trials.

Every sinner fleeing to divine mercy through **Christ's redemption under the covenant of grace**, leaving behind other confidences, finds refuge.

We are like ships at sea, threatened by storms and shipwreck.

**Gospel hope is the soul's secure anchor.** Its strength rests upon God's free grace, Christ's merits and intercession, and the Spirit's powerful work.

Christ himself is the **object and basis of our hope**.

Set your affection on things above and wait patiently for his appearance, when believers will share his glory.
