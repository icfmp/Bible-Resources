# 2 Kings 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Athaliah usurps the government of Judah, Jehoash made king. | 1–12 |
| Athaliah put to death. | 13–16 |
| The worship of the Lord restored. | 17–21 |

## Athaliah seizes power; Jehoash becomes king (verses 1–12)

Athaliah tried to destroy everyone she knew to have a claim to the throne.

But one of the king's sons, **Jehoash**, was hidden.

God's promise to David now seemed to depend upon a single life, yet it did not fail.

Similarly, according to his promise, the Lord preserves a spiritual family for the Son of David.

His people may sometimes be hidden and unseen, but they are safe under God's protection.

Athaliah ruled tyrannically for **six years** before the rightful king was brought before the people.

He was only a child, but he had a good guardian—and, better still, the good God to whom he could turn.

We should welcome the reign of Christ within our own hearts with such joy and satisfaction.

Let Satan the usurper be cast out and the true King established upon his throne.

May Jesus live and reign forever in my soul and throughout the world.

## Athaliah is put to death (verses 13–16)

Athaliah hurried toward her own ruin.

She was herself the greatest traitor, yet she was the first and loudest to cry out, **“Treason! Treason!”**

Those most deeply guilty are often the quickest to accuse others.

## The worship of the Lord is restored (verses 17–21)

The king and people would be most faithfully united to one another when both were united to the Lord.

It is good for a nation when political changes help revive and strengthen true religion.

Solemn covenants remind us of duties that already bind us and strengthen our commitment to fulfill them.

The people immediately put an end to idolatry.

In accordance with their covenant, they also expressed their willingness to help one another.

The people rejoiced, and Jerusalem was peaceful.

The path to lasting joy and peace is wholehearted service to God.

Joy and thanksgiving are heard in the homes of the righteous, but there is no peace for the wicked.
