# Acts 20 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| Paul's journeys. | 1–6 |

## Paul travels and serves (verses 1–6)

Disorder or opposition may force a Christian to leave a particular place or change a planned course.

But wherever believers go, their **work for God and delight in serving him** should remain the same.

Paul considered it worthwhile to spend **five days traveling to Troas**, though he would stay there only seven.

He knew, as we should, how to **redeem the time spent traveling** and turn it toward useful purposes.

## Eutychus is restored to life (verses 7–12)

The disciples read Scripture, reflected, prayed and sang in private, maintaining their fellowship with God.

But they also **came together for public worship** and maintained fellowship with one another.

They gathered on **the first day of the week, the Lord's day**.

Henry understands that day as one that all disciples should keep holy.

In the breaking of bread, believers remember both **Christ's body broken for us as a sacrifice for our sins** and **the benefits of that body given to us as spiritual nourishment and a feast for our souls**.

In the earliest church, the Lord's Supper was ordinarily received **every Lord's day** in remembrance of Christ's death.

Paul preached during this assembly.

Preaching God's word should accompany the sacraments.

His listeners were willing to hear, and seeing that, Paul spoke until **midnight**.

Sleeping while listening to Scripture can reveal a low esteem of God's word.

We should guard against needless sleepiness and seek hearts attentive enough to the message that sleep is driven away.

Yet we must distinguish **physical weakness, which deserves tenderness**, from **contempt, which deserves firm correction**.

Eutychus's fall interrupted Paul's message.

But the miracle of restoring him to life **confirmed that same message**.

Because the congregation did not know when they would see Paul again, they made full use of his presence.

They considered a night's lost sleep well spent.

How rarely do we give up hours of rest for worship!

How frequently do people lose them through amusement or sinful revelry!

Spiritual life does not grow easily within fallen human hearts, whereas worldly habits grow readily.

## Paul continues toward Jerusalem (verses 13–16)

Paul was eager to reach Jerusalem, yet continued seeking ways to do good while traveling.

Every godly person should act similarly.

In God's work, **our own preferences and the wishes of our friends** sometimes must give way.

We should not spend more time with loved ones when duty calls us elsewhere.

## Paul speaks to the elders at Ephesus (verses 17–27)

The elders knew Paul was not motivated by scheming or selfish ambition.

Anyone serving God acceptably and helping other people must act **with humility**.

Paul's preaching was **plain**: he explained his message so others could understand.

It was **powerful**: the gospel testified for those receiving it and against those rejecting it.

It was **useful**: he sought to inform their understanding and reform their hearts and conduct.

It was **hardworking**: he labored diligently.

It was **faithful**: he did not withhold necessary rebuke or the message of the cross.

It was **Christian and evangelical**: he did not concentrate on speculative ideas, doubtful matters, politics or questions of civil government.

He proclaimed **repentance toward God and faith in the Lord Jesus Christ**.

These two, with their genuine fruits and effects, provide the essential summary of salvation.

Without them no sinner escapes judgment; with them no one misses everlasting life.

The elders should not imagine Paul left Asia because he feared persecution.

He fully expected hardship, yet continued because he was confident God directed him.

We should thank God that we do not know everything awaiting us in the next year, week or even day.

It is enough for God's child to know that **strength will be provided for each day's need**.

We do not know what tomorrow brings, and ought not wish to know.

The powerful work of the Holy Spirit binds a believer to duty.

Even when persecution and trouble are expected, **Christ's love compels the Christian to continue**.

None of these dangers turned Paul from his work or deprived him of his true comfort.

Our life's business is to **prepare for a joyful death**.

Believing he would never see the elders again, Paul appealed to the integrity of his teaching.

He had proclaimed **the whole counsel of God**.

As he had preached a **pure gospel**, he had preached it **completely**, regardless of whether people would accept or reject it.

## Paul warns, prays and says farewell (verses 28–38)

If the Holy Spirit has made ministers **overseers and shepherds**, they must remain faithful to their trust.

They should recognize the care their Master has for his flock.

This church belongs to the One who **purchased it with his own blood**.

The blood belonged to Jesus as **a man**.

Yet his divine and human natures are so closely united that Scripture calls this **the blood of God**, because it is the blood of the One who **is God**.

This gives Christ's sacrifice its infinite worth, sufficient to redeem believers from every evil and purchase every good.

Paul cared affectionately for the elders' souls and their future welfare.

He commended them to **God and the word of his grace**.

That word is not only **the foundation of hope and source of joy**, but also **the rule for holy conduct**.

Even the most mature believers can grow further.

God's word helps them.

Those who are not sanctified could not be welcome guests with the holy God, nor would heaven truly be heaven to them.

But heaven is certain for those born again, in whom **God's likeness has been renewed**, as certain as his power and truth.

Paul's own example showed freedom from excessive attachment to worldly gain.

That example would help the elders pass through earthly life with more comfort.

He quoted a saying of the Lord Jesus: **“It is more blessed to give than to receive.”**

Henry regards this as something Jesus often told his disciples.

Worldly-minded people think differently.

They fear giving unless they can receive something in return.

To them **profit through taking** seems the happiest thing possible.

But Christ teaches that **giving is greater and more blessed**.

Giving makes us more like God, who gives to everyone and needs nothing from anyone, and like Jesus, who went about doing good.

May the mind of Christ be in us.

When friends must part, prayer is a fitting farewell.

Those who counsel and pray for one another may experience many tears and painful separations.

But they will meet again before God's throne, **never to part again**.

Christ's presence would accompany Paul on his journey, and that same presence would remain with the elders he left behind.
