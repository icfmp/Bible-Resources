# 2 Corinthians 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle, with others, proved themselves faithful ministers of Christ, by their unblamable life and behaviour. | 1–10 |
| By affection for them, And by earnest concern, that they might have no fellowship with unbelievers and idolaters. | 11–18 |

## Faithful servants during the day of salvation (verses 1–10)

The gospel is a word of grace sounding in our ears.

The present time is **the day of salvation**: the gospel is offered, the means of grace are provided, and God's saving invitation can be received **now**.

Tomorrow is not ours. We do not know what will happen to us or even where we will be.

We have a day of grace today. Let us not neglect it.

Ministers should remember that they are **God's servants** and behave in every circumstance as people entrusted with his work.

Paul did this through **patience under suffering**, sincere motives and a manner of life appropriate to his calling.

Believers also need God's grace when people speak well of them or speak against them.

They need grace to receive approval **without becoming proud**, and to bear criticism **with patience**.

Christians have nothing to boast of in themselves, yet **in Christ they possess everything**.

Such contrasts form the fabric of the Christian life.

Our path to heaven passes through changing circumstances and conflicting opinions.

In all of them, we should strive to be approved by God.

When the gospel is preached faithfully and fully received, it can improve even the poor person's earthly condition.

Money once wasted recklessly is saved. Time once spent in harmful pursuits is put to constructive work.

In comparison with former sinful living, such people become richer even in this world, while also receiving **the far greater riches of the world to come**.

## Love and separation from sinful partnerships (verses 11–18)

Paul's affection for the Corinthians led him to warn them against binding themselves in fellowship with people who reject God.

Here **unbelievers** means those who do not have genuine saving faith.

Faithful pastors will warn the people they love not to become **unequally yoked**.

The harmful consequences of ignoring Scripture's warnings concerning marriage can be very serious.

A marriage that was meant to provide a suitable helper can instead become a trap.

Henry makes an important qualification: **people already bound in such a marriage without having deliberately chosen to disregard God's command may look to him for consolation under that cross**.

But believers who willingly enter those unions despite Scripture's warnings should expect considerable distress.

The warning also extends to ordinary social relationships.

Henry counsels believers not to **choose wicked people as their closest companions**, while explicitly recognizing that it is **impossible to avoid every encounter, conversation or shared situation** with them.

The point is to resist being drawn into others' sinful practices.

Come away from those who devote themselves to wrongdoing.

Reject the futile pleasures, pursuits and moral corruption of this present world.

If it is considered a wonderful privilege to be the son or daughter of an earthly ruler, how much greater is the dignity and happiness of being **a son or daughter of Almighty God**!
