# Ezekiel 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Ezekiel's vision of God, and of the angelic host. | 1–14 |
| The conduct of Divine Providence. | 15–25 |
| A revelation of the Son of man upon his heavenly throne. | 26–28 |

## Ezekiel sees the living creatures (verses 1–14)

It is a mercy when God's word reaches us in the midst of affliction, and our duty to listen to it diligently.

God's voice came through **the Holy Spirit**, in abundant light and power.

The visions seem intended to fill Ezekiel's mind with **great and exalted thoughts of God**.

They would awaken fear in sinners while comforting those who feared God and humbled themselves.

The first part, in **verses 4–14**, pictures God attended by a vast company of **angels**—his messengers and servants, who do his will.

The scene inspires solemn awe of his holiness and displeasure against sin, yet also hope for blessings.

The fire is surrounded by glory.

Although we cannot discover everything about God by searching, we can see the **brightness surrounding his glory**.

The living creatures came from the fire, reminding us that angels receive their being and strength from God.

Their appearances combine outstanding qualities.

**A human being** has understanding, and the angels understand far more.

**A lion** represents strength and boldness.

**An ox** represents diligence, patience and tireless work.

**An eagle** represents speed, sharp vision and soaring height.

The angels surpass human beings in all these qualities.

They have **wings**, showing the speed with which they carry out God's commands.

They stand upright, firm and steady.

But they have **hands as well as wings**.

Some people rush from place to place but never accomplish useful work. They have wings without hands.

God's servants, however, are ready **both to go and to act**.

They move straight ahead, not turning aside to waste time on trivial matters.

Their work needs no correction because they follow **the Spirit's direction**.

Their fiery appearance suggests **seraphim, or burning ones**, representing their ardent love for God and zeal in serving him.

Even visions we cannot fully understand can teach useful lessons.

We should attend especially to whatever concerns our **peace and duty**, leaving secret matters to the Lord.

## The wheels picture God's Providence (verses 15–25)

The wheels represent **the changing events of Providence**.

A wheel turns: first one spoke is above, then another. Yet the rotation remains steady and orderly.

We need not despair during adversity: the wheel may turn in God's time.

Nor should prosperous people become proud, because their circumstances may quickly change.

The wheels stand beside the living creatures because **angels serve in God's providential government**.

The spirit of the living creatures is in the wheels.

The same divine **wisdom, power and holiness** that govern the angels direct events in the earthly world.

The wheel presents **four faces**, picturing Providence extending in every direction.

Wherever you look, there is a face of Providence turned toward you.

A **wheel within a wheel** expresses how complex and difficult to understand God's dealings can be.

Yet everything is wisely ordered.

The wheels move constantly and follow the Spirit without turning aside.

If we likewise followed the Spirit's direction, we would not repeatedly have to **undo through repentance** what we had done wrongly.

Their immense rims cause awe.

Thinking about the height and depth of God's counsel should humble us.

Their rims are **full of eyes**.

Every movement of Providence is directed by **infinite wisdom**.

The Lord's eyes see both good and evil everywhere.

**There is no blind chance or fortune governing the world**.

Above the creatures is a glorious yet fearsome firmament like **crystal**.

What seems to us a dark cloud is clear to God; he sees through it and knows all who live on earth.

When the angels have awakened a careless world, they lower their wings so that **God's voice may be heard clearly**.

The events of Providence should open our ears to God's **revealed word**.

The sounds of earth should lead us to listen to the voice from heaven.

How can we escape if we reject the One who speaks there?

## The throne and the likeness of the Son of Man (verses 26–28)

Henry understands the figure on the throne as representing **the eternal Son, the second Person of the Trinity**, who would later assume human nature.

He first notices the **throne**.

It is a throne of **glory, grace, triumph, government and judgment**.

It is good news for humanity that the throne above the firmament is occupied by One who appears **in the likeness of a human being**.

A **rainbow** surrounds the throne.

This familiar sign of the covenant represents God's mercy and covenant love toward his people.

The fire of divine anger was about to break out against Jerusalem, yet **limits would be set to the judgment**.

God would remember his covenant.

Everything Ezekiel saw prepared him to hear what God would say.

When the prophet fell on his face, he heard **the voice of One speaking**.

God delights to teach the humble.

Therefore, let sinners humble themselves before him.

And let believers contemplate his glory so that **the Spirit of the Lord may gradually transform them into his likeness**.
