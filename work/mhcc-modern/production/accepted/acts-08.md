# Acts 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Saul persecutes the church. | 1–4 |
| Philip's success at Samaria. Simon the sorcerer baptized. | 5–13 |
| The hypocrisy of Simon detected. | 14–25 |
| Philip and the Ethiopian. | 26–40 |

## Saul persecutes the church (verses 1–4)

Persecution must not make us abandon God's work, though it may send us **to serve him in another place**.

Wherever mature believers are driven, they carry knowledge of the gospel and can tell others of Christ's worth.

When a sincere desire to do good governs the heart, opponents cannot close **every possible opportunity for useful service**.

## Philip preaches in Samaria; Simon professes faith (verses 5–13)

Wherever the gospel advances, evil spirits are driven out—especially the unclean influences that entice people toward fleshly desires and war against their souls.

The illnesses healed in Samaria were among the hardest to cure by natural means. They vividly pictured **sin's deep disease**.

Pride, ambition and the desire for greatness have brought immense damage upon the world and the church.

People had treated Simon the magician as though he were God's great power.

See how easily people lacking discernment misunderstand spiritual things!

But divine grace was powerful enough to turn them toward Christ, who is **Truth itself**.

They attended carefully to Philip's preaching, recognized it as coming from God rather than human invention and submitted to it.

Nevertheless, people whose hearts remain devoted to greed can assemble with genuine believers and participate in religious practices for some time.

Some are impressed by signs proving divine truth without ever experiencing **the truth's sanctifying power**.

The gospel can affect the mind outwardly while producing **no inward holiness**.

**Not everyone who professes belief has experienced saving conversion**.

## Peter exposes Simon's hypocrisy (verses 14–25)

The converts in Samaria had not yet received the Spirit in the form of the **extraordinary miraculous gifts** first bestowed at Pentecost.

We should pray that everyone whose spiritual welfare concerns us receives **the renewing grace of the Holy Spirit**, the gift containing every spiritual blessing.

No person can bestow the Spirit merely by laying hands on another.

Yet we must diligently teach the people for whom we pray.

Simon wanted the **honor and power of an apostle**, not the heart or character of a Christian.

He wanted glory for himself rather than good for others.

Peter exposed his error.

Simon treated worldly money as though it could purchase **forgiveness, the Holy Spirit and eternal life**.

Such an attitude is incompatible with saving grace.

God knows the heart exactly as it is.

A proud and greedy heart cannot be right before him.

A person may remain under sin's control while displaying an outward appearance of religion.

When offered money as an inducement to wrongdoing, remember how quickly wealth perishes and reject it.

**Christianity must not become a business for earning worldly advantage**.

Even thoughts and desires may be terribly sinful.

False beliefs, corrupt affections and evil schemes need repentance.

Yet forgiveness is promised when repentance is genuine.

Henry is careful: the doubt concerning Simon is **whether his repentance was sincere**, not whether God would forgive if he truly repented.

Lord, give us a faith unlike Simon's—one that transforms the heart instead of merely admiring miracles.

Keep us from using religion to satisfy pride or ambition, and especially from the subtle spiritual pride that seeks admiration even for appearing humble.

Teach us to seek honor **from God alone**.

## Philip explains Scripture to the Ethiopian official (verses 26–40)

God directed Philip to a desert road.

He sometimes opens opportunities for ministry **where they seem least likely to appear**.

When traveling, we should consider how we might do good to the people we meet.

We need not be excessively distant toward strangers.

Even when we know nothing else about them, **we know they have souls**.

People with demanding responsibilities should redeem the time for worship and Scripture study, using brief available moments wisely.

When reading Scripture, pause to ask whom and what its authors were describing.

Above all, direct your attention toward **the Redeemer**.

The Ethiopian understood through the Spirit's teaching how Scripture was fulfilled in Christ.

He came to understand the Messiah's kingdom and salvation and desired to belong among Jesus' disciples.

Anyone sincerely searching Scripture for truth will benefit.

Henry understands the Ethiopian's confession as expressing **simple reliance upon Christ for salvation and wholehearted devotion to him**.

Let us seek this same faith through diligent Scripture study and the Spirit's instruction, until it governs our hearts.

After his baptism, Philip was taken away by the Spirit.

This helped confirm the official's faith.

Once sinners know Jesus and his gospel, they can continue **rejoicing on their way**, carrying out their ordinary responsibilities with new motives and in a new spirit.

But **water baptism** in the name of Father, Son and Holy Spirit is not enough without **the inward baptism and renewing work of the Holy Spirit**.

Lord, grant that renewing grace to every one of us, that we may go on our way rejoicing.
