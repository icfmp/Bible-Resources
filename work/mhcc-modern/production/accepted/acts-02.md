# Acts 2 — Modern English

## The Holy Spirit is poured out at Pentecost (verses 1–4)

While Jesus was with them, the disciples had repeatedly argued about which would be greatest.

Now those quarrels had ended, and they had lately prayed together more earnestly.

If we desire the Spirit's blessing, let us be **of one accord**.

Believers may disagree in opinion and personal interests, but should **agree to love one another**.

Where brothers and sisters live in unity, the Lord commands his blessing.

A mighty rushing wind announced the Spirit's arrival.

It pictured the Spirit's powerful work in human minds and through them in the world.

His **convicting work prepares the way for his comfort**.

The strong gusts of that holy wind prepare hearts for its gentle breezes.

Something resembling flames of fire rested on each disciple, fulfilling John the Baptist's announcement that Christ would **baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire**.

The Spirit works like fire: softening hard hearts, burning away impurity and kindling reverent, loving devotion.

Believers then offer spiritual sacrifices, as sacrifices once burned upon the altar.

The disciples were **filled with the Holy Spirit more fully than before**.

They received greater sanctifying grace, became less attached to this world and more acquainted with the world to come.

They enjoyed stronger comfort, more assurance of Christ's love and more hope of heaven; grief and fear were overwhelmed.

They also received **miraculous gifts** for advancing the gospel.

They spoke, not by recalling prepared thoughts, but as the Spirit supplied their words.

## The apostles speak in different languages (verses 5–13)

The division of languages begun at **Babel** had long hindered the spread of knowledge and religion.

The apostles could not have advanced the gospel throughout many nations without a gift that enabled them to speak those languages.

This gift also confirmed that their authority came **from God**.

## Peter preaches to the people (verses 14–36)

### Peter explains the promise of the Spirit (verses 14–21)

Peter's sermon showed how completely he had recovered from his earlier fall and how fully he had been restored to God's favor.

The man who had denied Christ now **confessed him boldly**.

Peter explained that the miraculous pouring out of the Spirit should move his hearers to embrace faith in Jesus and join his church.

The event fulfilled Scripture and followed **Christ's resurrection and ascension**, confirming both.

Although filled with the Spirit and able to speak miraculously, Peter **did not set aside the Scriptures**.

Disciples of Christ never outgrow their Bibles.

The Holy Spirit was not given to abolish Scripture, but to help believers **understand, receive and obey it**.

No one will escape final condemnation except those who call upon the Lord through his Son Jesus Christ, **Savior of sinners and Judge of humanity**.

### Peter proclaims the crucified and risen Jesus (verses 22–36)

Peter used the Holy Spirit's gift to preach **Jesus himself**.

He recalled Christ's suffering and death, which his listeners had witnessed only a few weeks before.

Christ's death must be considered in **two distinct ways**.

As **God's purpose**, it revealed extraordinary grace and wisdom: divine justice would be satisfied, God and sinners reconciled and Christ glorified according to an eternal counsel that could not fail.

As **the people's act**, it was terrible wickedness and folly.

Jesus' resurrection removed the disgrace attached to his execution.

Peter explained it at length.

Jesus was **God's Holy One**, set apart for the work of redemption.

His death and suffering would open the way to everlasting blessedness for himself and all who belong to him.

The event took place as Scripture foretold, and the apostles were eyewitnesses.

The resurrection was also confirmed by the Spirit whom Jesus poured out, giving visible miraculous gifts and divine power to his disciples.

Through Christ the way of life is revealed, and believers may hope to enjoy God's presence and favor forever.

All these blessings arise from faith that **Jesus is Lord and the anointed Savior**.

## Three thousand people respond to the gospel (verses 37–41)

From the beginning, God accompanied the gospel message with divine power, bringing thousands to **the obedience of faith**.

Neither Peter's words nor the miracle the crowd had seen could have brought about this conversion apart from **the Holy Spirit**.

When the eyes of sinners are opened, they feel **conviction of sin** and sorrow of heart.

Peter called them to repent and publicly confess Jesus as Messiah through **baptism in his name**.

They were promised forgiveness and participation in the Spirit's gifts and graces.

True repentance also involves separating from wickedness rather than continuing to identify with it.

Repentant believers show sincerity by breaking with sinful ways.

By God's grace, **three thousand** people accepted the gospel invitation.

All genuine believers receive the Spirit's indispensable **adopting, converting, guiding and sanctifying grace**.

This must be distinguished from the apostles' special miraculous gifts.

Repentance and forgiveness are still proclaimed in Christ's name even to the worst sinners.

The Holy Spirit still confirms God's promise in believers' hearts.

The promise remains for **us, our children and those far away**.

## The first believers share their lives (verses 42–47)

These verses describe the church's earliest days.

It was young and, like the innocence of infancy, marked by unusual sincerity.

The believers faithfully continued in God's appointed means of worship and lived in prayer and devotion.

When Christianity rules the heart, believers seek fellowship with God through the ordinances in which he promises to meet his people.

What they had experienced lifted their hearts above worldly possessions.

The Spirit filled them with such love that they treated one another's needs almost as their own.

Thus they had things in common **not by abolishing private property, but by overcoming selfishness and practicing generosity**.

God knew these believers would soon face persecution and displacement from their possessions in Judea.

Every day the Lord inclined more people to receive the gospel.

They were not merely outward professors but people truly received by God through **regenerating grace**.

Those whom God has appointed for everlasting salvation will be brought to Christ, until knowledge of his glory fills the earth.
