# Psalm 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist complains of the wickedness of the wicked. | 1–11 |
| He prays to God to appear for the relief of his people. | 12–18 |

## The psalmist laments the wicked (verses 1–11)

When God seems to withdraw his presence, his people find it deeply painful, especially in trouble.

Often it is **our unbelief that keeps us distant from God**, even as we complain that he is distant from us.

Angry words against wicked people often accomplish more harm than good.

If we must speak of their sins, bring the matter before **God in prayer**.

He can transform them.

The sinner proudly celebrates worldly strength and success.

Wicked people do not seek God or call upon him.

**To live without prayer is to live without God**.

They think about countless projects, schemes and possessions, yet give no thought to God in any of them.

They do not submit to his will or seek his glory.

The cause is **pride**.

They imagine genuine religion is beneath them.

People would not so readily disregard justice and kindness toward other humans unless they had first rejected reverence for God.

## A prayer for the oppressed and tempted (verses 12–18)

The psalmist was amazed both at the bold wickedness of human beings and at God's patience in allowing it to continue.

God prepares hearts for prayer.

He awakens holy desires, strengthens faith, directs attention and raises affections toward himself.

Then he graciously accepts the prayer.

**Preparing the heart is itself God's work**, so we should ask him to prepare our hearts.

Poor, afflicted, persecuted or tempted believers should remember that Satan is the prince of this present world and the father of the ungodly.

We cannot expect justice or mercy from people of the same spirit as those who crucified the Lord of glory.

But **Jesus, who once suffered, now rules as King over all the earth**.

His reign will never end.

Entrust yourselves to him, humbly relying on his mercy.

He will rescue his followers from every temptation, break the power of oppressors and soon **crush Satan beneath their feet**.

In this present life believers already taste something of the promised deliverance.

But **only in heaven** will sin and temptation finally be excluded altogether.
