# Jeremiah 31 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The restoration of Israel. | 1–9 |

| God's care over the church. | 27–34 |

## The Lord draws his people with everlasting love (verses 1–9)

God assured his people he would restore them to **covenant fellowship with himself**.

When we reach an unusually low condition and difficulties seem overwhelming, it helps to remember that the church has suffered greatly before.

Yet when we experience present displeasure, we find it hard to take comfort in **past signs of God's favor**.

For those who, through grace, share in the Lord's love, there is great happiness in knowing that it is **everlasting love**.

It has existed from eternity in **God's purposes** and continues **forever in its effects**.

Those whom he loves with this love he draws to himself **through the work of the Holy Spirit within their souls**.

When we praise the Lord for what he has already done, let us also ask him to grant what his church **still needs and expects**.

When God calls us, we should not claim that we cannot come.

**The One who calls also helps and strengthens us**.

His goodness leads people **to repentance**.

His redeemed people will weep over sin with **deeper bitterness and tenderness after deliverance** than they did when they were suffering under captivity.

If we receive God as our Father and join the **church of the firstborn**, we will lack nothing truly good for us.

Henry sees these promises as looking to **a future gathering of Israel from every part of the world**.

He also sees in their language a figurative description of **sinners converted to Christ** and led along a **clear and safe path**.

## The scattered are gathered, and sorrow gives way to joy (verses 10–17)

The God who scattered Israel knows **where every one of his people may be found**.

His providential gifts give us abundant reason for gratitude.

But the soul is not a fruitful garden unless watered by **the dew of his Spirit and grace**.

The prophecy then makes a precious promise which, Henry says, will not find its **full accomplishment apart from the heavenly Zion**.

Those who know God's loving-kindness and are satisfied with it need no other blessing to make them truly happy.

**Rachel** is pictured as rising from her grave and refusing comfort because she believes her descendants have been destroyed.

The killing of Bethlehem's children by **Herod** (**Matthew 2:16–18**) fulfilled this prophecy **to some degree**, but Henry explicitly says it **could not be its entire meaning**.

If we have a secure hope of **eternal inheritance**, both for ourselves and those we love, we can endure temporal suffering—and God can make those troubles serve our good.

## Ephraim repents and asks God to turn him (verses 18–20)

**Ephraim**, representing Israel's ten tribes, mourns sin.

He is angry with himself over **wrongdoing, foolishness and stubborn rebellion**.

He recognizes he cannot, by his own power, remain close to God—much less restore himself when he has wandered away.

Therefore he prays:

**“Turn me, and I will be turned.”**

His own will is brought into submission to God's.

When **the Holy Spirit's teaching** accompanied God's providential correction, the change was accomplished.

This is a comfort amid our suffering: **the Lord has not forgotten us**.

He has mercy reserved for everyone who seeks him sincerely—**rich mercy, certain mercy and mercy fitted to their need**.

## The Savior prepares the road to freedom (verses 21–26)

The way from slavery to sin into **the liberty of God's children** is a highway.

It is both **clear and safe**.

Yet no one is likely to walk along it without deliberately **setting the heart toward it**.

The people receive encouragement from the promise of something **new, unheard of and extraordinary**—a creative work of almighty power.

Henry identifies it with **the human nature of Jesus Christ, formed and prepared by the Holy Spirit's power**.

He sees this as encouragement to the Jews to return to their land.

They were also promised a happy settlement there.

God has joined **godliness with honesty**.

No one may separate them or imagine that one can compensate for the absence of the other.

In the Lord's love and favor, the **weary soul finds rest** and the sorrowful find joy.

What could be more satisfying to see than **Jerusalem's welfare and peace upon Israel**?

## The new covenant writes God's law upon the heart (verses 27–34)

God's people would become numerous and enjoy his blessing.

This passage is quoted in **Hebrews 8:8–9** as the substance of **the covenant of grace made with believers in Jesus Christ**.

The promise is not that God will simply give people **a different law**.

Christ came **not to destroy the law but to fulfill it**.

Rather, the same law that had been written on stone tablets will be **written upon human hearts by the Holy Spirit**.

Through grace the Lord will make his people **willing to obey him**.

Everyone will have the opportunity to know the Lord and receive the means of that knowledge.

When the gospel is proclaimed, **the Holy Spirit will be poured out**.

No person is finally destroyed except because of **that person's own sins**.

No one willing to receive **Christ's salvation** will be rejected.

## God's covenant secures the future of his people (verses 35–40)

The sun, moon and other heavenly bodies will continue moving according to the order established by their Creator until time ends.

The roaring sea also remains under his authority.

Just as certainly, the Jews will continue to exist as **a distinct people**.

Henry says that scarcely any words could declare **Israel's restoration** more forcefully.

The rebuilding, enlargement and establishment of Jerusalem would be a pledge of the greater blessings God intended for **the gospel church**.

The future restoration of Israel, and **every true believer's personal happiness**, rest upon **God's promise, covenant and oath**.

His love surpasses knowledge.

Anyone who takes hold of that love receives every present mercy as **a foretaste and pledge of salvation**.
