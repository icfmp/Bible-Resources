# Hebrews 2 — Modern English

## Do not drift away from the gospel (verses 1–4)

Because Christ is greater than the angels, we must pay careful attention to his message.

Our minds and memories resemble **leaking vessels**; without attention, what is poured into them escapes.

Our fallen nature, temptations, worldly concerns and pleasures all contribute to this forgetfulness.

The great sin against the gospel is **neglecting its wonderful salvation**—treating God's saving grace in Christ as unimportant, ignoring both its value and our desperate need of it.

Judgments under the gospel are chiefly **spiritual** and are therefore more terrifying, not less.

Even partial negligence may bring reproof and **spiritual darkness** without ultimately destroying a believer.

Christ's teaching was confirmed by those who heard him—the evangelists and apostles who witnessed his words and works—and by gifts of **the Holy Spirit** distributed according to God's will.

God provided reliable grounds for faith and a firm foundation for hope.

Let us therefore attend to **the inspired Scriptures**, which preserve the witness of those who heard the Lord, and choose the good portion that cannot be taken away.

## Christ's suffering and exaltation (verses 5–9)

God has not placed either the church's present condition or its final restoration under the rule of angels.

**Christ reigns** and will bring every earthly kingdom under his dominion.

Why was Christ given to humanity? **Because of God's grace.**

His unlimited authority followed his humiliation and death. Ancient Scripture finds fulfillment in him.

God has shown wonderful kindness in creation and Providence, yet human sin has repaid those gifts shamefully.

## Christ leads many children to glory (verses 10–13)

Carnal people may despise Christ's cross, but believers recognize its particular glory.

It was fitting for the Author of salvation to reach the appointed completeness of his mediatorial work **through suffering**, as he leads many sons and daughters to glory.

His path to the crown ran through the cross; the path of his followers must also pass through trials.

**Jesus sanctifies his people**. He purchased and sent **the Holy Spirit**, who makes believers holy as **the Spirit of Christ**.

Believers are set apart and equipped for sacred purposes. They belong to God's family, through Christ.

When Jesus calls them brothers and sisters, that does **not** make him merely their equal. His grace in calling them family reveals his dignity and their dependence.

The writer illustrates this relationship from **Psalm 22:22, Psalm 18:2 and Isaiah 8:18**, as cited in the source commentary.

## He took our nature to save us (verses 14–18)

Fallen angels were left without a redeemer. Christ did not come to save them and therefore did not take **an angelic nature**.

An angel could not provide an appropriate sacrifice for **human sins**.

The Son took **our human nature**, despite fully knowing the suffering and death involved. His sacrifice was sufficient and suited to those it redeemed.

Through this atonement, his people are delivered from Satan's slavery and receive forgiveness through faith.

Those afraid of death should not try to silence their fears through bravado, despair, worldliness or human tricks.

Instead, seek **pardon, peace, grace and a living hope of heaven** through the One who died and rose again.

Because Christ himself suffered and experienced temptation **without ever sinning**, he understands and readily helps his tempted people.

Those facing temptation should not conclude that temptation itself makes them too unworthy to pray.

Henry reassures believers that no soul sincerely crying to the Lord in faith from the danger of temptation will be abandoned to perish.

When temptation first surprises us, turn immediately to him. **Prompt prayer is a wise way to stop its progress**.
