# 2 Kings 21 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Wicked reign of Manasseh. | 1–9 |
| The prophetic denunciations against Judah. | 10–18 |
| Wicked reign and death of Amon. | 19–26 |

## Manasseh's evil reign (verses 1–9)

Young people often long to be independent and to possess wealth and power before they have acquired good judgment.

But early independence can destroy their future comfort and harm others.

It is often far better for young people to remain under the care of parents or guardians until growing experience and wisdom prepare them to make decisions.

Even if they receive fewer immediate indulgences, they may later be thankful for the protection.

Manasseh committed enormous wickedness before the Lord, almost as though he wanted to provoke divine anger.

He became more wicked than the nations the Lord had previously driven out.

He progressed from evil to worse until he was carried captive to **Babylon**.

The people readily complied with Manasseh's desires.

Some wanted his favor; others found his commands suited their own corrupt inclinations.

When a great community undergoes reform, many people are merely following the advantage of the moment.

Such people fall away quickly under temptation.

## God's judgment is announced against Judah (verses 10–18)

This passage announces what will happen to Judah and Jerusalem.

The image describes the city being emptied and left desolate, yet not finally destroyed.

It would be cleaned out and preserved for the Jews to inhabit again later.

Judah would be abandoned for a time, but **not forever**.

Its outward privileges would be lost, yet individual believers would be preserved even during judgment.

The Lord may reject a community that professes his name while dishonoring him through wrongdoing.

He will **never abandon his cause on earth**.

The book of **Chronicles** records Manasseh's repentance and God's acceptance of him.

That should keep us from despairing even over the recovery of very great sinners.

But no one should continue deliberately in sin, assuming repentance and reform can be postponed until a convenient time.

Scripture gives some striking examples of the conversion of notorious sinners **so no one will despair**.

Yet they are few **so no one will presume**.

## Amon's evil reign and death (verses 19–26)

Amon polluted God's house with idols.

God then permitted his own house to be stained with his blood.

Those who killed him acted wickedly, yet God was righteous in allowing it.

Judah now experienced a remarkable change: from one of its worst kings to one of its best.

Once more the nation was given an opportunity for reform.

Whether the Lord patiently delays judgment on stubborn sinners or ends their lives quickly, all who continue refusing his ways face destruction.
