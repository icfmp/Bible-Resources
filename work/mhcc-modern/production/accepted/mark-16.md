# Mark 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ's resurrection made known the women. | 1–8 |
| Christ appears to Mary Magdalene and other disciples. | 9–13 |
| His commission to the apostles. | 14–18 |
| Christ's ascension. | 19, 20 |

## The women learn that Jesus has risen (verses 1–8)

Nicodemus had brought many spices for Jesus' burial, yet the faithful women wanted to bring their own. Another person's demonstration of love for Christ should never prevent us from showing our own love.

Those whose devotion moves them to seek Jesus diligently often find that seemingly insurmountable obstacles are removed.

When we accept trouble and expense out of love for Christ, he accepts the devotion even if our plans do not succeed.

The sight of the angel ought to have encouraged the women, but instead they were frightened. We often turn what should comfort us into a cause for fear through our own misunderstanding.

Jesus **was crucified, but now he is glorified**. He is risen; he is no longer in the tomb, but alive again.

They would see him again. For now, they could look at the place where he had lain.

Those mourning for Jesus will receive encouragement at the proper time.

The angel named **Peter** particularly. This news would be precious to someone deeply grieved by his own sin.

Seeing Christ is a welcome comfort for a true penitent; and a truly repentant person is welcome before Christ.

The women went quickly to tell the disciples. Yet troubling fear may hinder us from serving Christ and other people as we would if our faith and joy were stronger.

## Jesus appears to Mary Magdalene and other disciples (verses 9–13)

There can be no better news for grieving disciples than the announcement that **Christ has risen**.

We should seek to comfort sorrowing believers by telling them what we have learned of Jesus.

In God's wise Providence, proofs of the resurrection were given **gradually** and received **cautiously**. This made the apostles' later confident testimony more convincing.

Yet how slowly we accept the comfort offered by God's word!

While Jesus consoles his people, he may also need to rebuke their **hardness of heart**, both for doubting his promises and for failing to obey his commands.

## Jesus commissions the apostles (verses 14–18)

The evidence confirming the gospel is abundant. People who reject it may be rightly rebuked for unbelief.

Jesus confirmed the calling of **the eleven apostles** and instructed them to take the gospel into all the world, preaching it to every creature.

Only those who are truly Christians are saved through Christ.

**Simon Magus** professed faith and received baptism, yet was later described as remaining bound in wickedness (**Acts 8:13–25**).

The faith that saves receives Christ in **all his offices and characters**, trusting him for every purpose of salvation.

It produces corresponding effects in the heart and life. **Mere intellectual assent is dead faith and cannot save**.

The commission reaches every creature throughout the world.

The gospel's message includes not only truths, invitations, encouragements and commands, but also solemn warnings.

The apostles received powers to perform miracles that **confirmed the teaching they preached** and assisted its spread among nations that had not heard it.

## Christ ascends into heaven (verses 19 and 20)

After speaking to the apostles, Jesus ascended into heaven.

His **sitting** signifies both rest and royal authority.

He has finished his work and taken possession of his kingdom.

To sit at **God's right hand** declares his sovereign dignity and universal power.

Everything God does for us, gives us or accepts from us is through **his Son**, who is now glorified with the glory he possessed before the world existed.

The apostles went out preaching everywhere, near and far.

Their message was heavenly and spiritual, opposed to the world's ordinary desires, and met with great resistance.

They possessed none of the usual worldly supports for such a mission. Nevertheless, within a few years the gospel was announced across the known world.

Ministers today, Henry says, **do not need to perform miracles** to prove their message. Scripture's divine origin has already been confirmed, leaving people responsible for accepting or rejecting its testimony.

The changes the gospel continually produces when faithfully preached and genuinely believed—transforming human character and conduct—remain a constant and, in Henry's language, **miraculous proof that the gospel is God's power for salvation to believers**.

- **1–8:** Nicodemus' spices and the women's independent devotion, **God accepting well-meant efforts even if unsuccessful**, terror misreading comfort, risen Lord, **Peter specially comforted as penitent**, fear hindering service — retained.
- **9–13:** evidence appearing **gradually and cautiously accepted**, resurrection news comforting mourners, rebuke of hard-hearted unbelief — retained.
- **14–18:** **the Eleven**, world mission, **Simon Magus despite baptism (Acts 8:13–25)**, saving faith beyond **bare assent**, warnings alongside promises, apostolic signs confirming the gospel — retained.
- **19 and 20:** ascension as **rest and royal rule**, power through Christ, gospel spreading without worldly supports, **Henry's assertion that continuing miracles are not required**, changed lives as continuing evidence — retained.
