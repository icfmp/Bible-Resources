# 1 Peter 1 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

## A living hope through Christ's resurrection (verses 1–9)

This letter addresses believers scattered among nations who remain **strangers** in whichever country or city they inhabit.

They should credit salvation to **the Father's electing love, the Son's redemption and the Holy Spirit's sanctification**, giving glory to one God in three persons, into whose name they were baptized.

Worldly hope is often an uncertain expectation, resting on sand. Many people's ideas about heaven are no more than blind guesses.

But **God's children possess a living hope**, both in its object and in its active effects: it encourages and comforts them in trouble and gives strength to overcome obstacles.

This springs entirely from God's **abundant mercy**.

The heavenly inheritance is incorruptible, undefiled and unfading. It cannot run out, become polluted or lose its glory.

Every worldly possession comes with imperfection. Even a beautiful home may hide anguish; luxurious beds and full tables cannot prevent bodily illness or an uneasy heart.

Possessions are frequently acquired or used sinfully, and they soon pass away like flowers.

God gives his people grace **and preserves them for glory**.

The hope of this inheritance ought to show in the believer's demeanor and conduct.

God does not enjoy afflicting people, yet his wise love can appoint sharp trials to reveal and strengthen their faith.

**Gold loses material in the refining fire; faith may gain firmness and strength through testing.**

Gold eventually perishes and purchases only perishable things, while proven faith yields praise, honor and glory.

Believing Christ's excellence and love awakens an affectionate response of love toward him.

God's glory and our happiness belong together. When we seek the former sincerely now, we will possess the latter fully where evil can no longer reach us.

## The prophets foretold Christ's suffering and glory (verses 10–12)

**Jesus Christ was the great subject of the prophets' study**.

They examined the sufferings appointed for him and the glory afterward, discovering the central gospel message: **he was handed over for our sins and raised for our justification**.

God often answers our genuine needs rather than our exact requests.

The teaching of the prophets and of the apostles agrees because **the same Holy Spirit** gave their message.

The gospel's success depends on the Spirit's working and blessing. Therefore study carefully the Scriptures that explain salvation.

## Be holy in every part of life (verses 13–16)

Travelers, runners, workers and soldiers gathered their flowing garments so they could act freely.

Christians should likewise prepare **their minds and desires** for active service.

Be sober, alert against spiritual dangers, moderate in behavior, humble in judging yourselves and restrained in your opinions as well as actions.

Complete trust in God's grace goes together with earnest effort in duty.

**Holiness is both the desire and the duty of every Christian**.

It belongs in every occupation, condition and relationship.

Pay special attention to temptations toward which your own nature inclines you.

**God's written word is the surest guide to Christian conduct**.

God makes those whom he saves holy.

## Live in reverent gratitude for redemption (verses 17–25)

Holy confidence in God as **Father** and reverent fear of him as **Judge** belong together.

When believers act sinfully, the Lord may correct them as a Father.

They should neither doubt his promises nor surrender to enslaving terror of his wrath, but honor his holiness.

A person with no reverent fear becomes careless and exposed to Satan. But someone overcome by despair loses the courage to use the spiritual privileges already given.

The price of redemption is **Christ's precious blood**.

Not only open wickedness but also unprofitable, empty living is dangerous, even when defended as family tradition.

It is foolish to say that a practice must be right merely because our ancestors followed it.

God planned special mercy for his people long before he revealed it.

Now that Christ has come, **the light of the gospel, strength of faith and effectiveness of God's appointed ordinances** are more abundant than before.

Through union with Christ by faith, his present glory assures believers that they will be with him (**John 14:3**).

The soul must be purified before it can abandon self-centered desires.

God's word, planted in the heart by the Holy Spirit, creates spiritual life and changes thoughts, desires and conduct until they lead toward eternal life.

Human nature apart from grace resembles **grass and its flower**: vigorous for a moment and soon gone.

We should therefore receive and love **the living, enduring word**, valuing it above everything else.

Keep it in your heart as the greatest treasure available now and the certain pledge of the inheritance stored in heaven.
