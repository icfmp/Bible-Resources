# Matthew 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| The wise men's search after Christ. | 1–8 |
| The wise men worship Jesus. | 9–12 |

| Death of Herod, Jesus brought to Nazareth. | 19–23 |

## The wise men seek the newborn King (verses 1–8)

People who live far away from the usual means of grace may sometimes pursue Christ more diligently and come to know more of his salvation than those living nearby.

But no secret arts or merely human learning can bring anyone to a true knowledge of Christ.

We must learn of him through **God's written word**, which shines like a lamp in a dark place, and through **the Holy Spirit's instruction**.

When the morning star has risen in someone's heart, bringing genuine knowledge of Christ, that person desires to **worship him**.

Herod was old.

He had never been especially affectionate toward his own family and was unlikely to live long enough to see a newborn child grow into a political rival.

Nevertheless, he was terrified at the announcement of a new king.

He did not understand that **the Messiah's kingdom was spiritual**.

We must beware of **lifeless faith**.

A person may be convinced of many religious truths and still hate them because they challenge ambition or sinful desires.

Such a belief only increases resistance to God and his cause.

A sinner may be foolish enough to imagine that resistance will succeed.

## The wise men find and worship Jesus (verses 9–12)

The wise men rejoiced when they saw the star again.

Their joy resembles that of people who have passed through a long season of temptation, spiritual darkness and fear and then receive **the Spirit of adoption**.

That Spirit witnesses with their spirits that they are children of God.

They may have been surprised when they found **a cottage instead of a palace**, and a poor mother instead of royal attendants.

But they did not turn away.

They had found the King they sought and gladly gave him their gifts.

Sincere seekers of Christ will not be disappointed because they find him and his followers in obscure places rather than among wealth and power.

Perhaps someone wants to worship Christ but thinks, **“I am poor and foolish, and I have nothing to offer him.”**

You still have your heart, though it is unworthy, hard and unclean.

**Give it to Christ as it is.**

Be willing for him to use and change it as he pleases.

He will receive it, make it better, shape it in his likeness and give you himself forever.

The wise men offered **gold, frankincense and myrrh**.

Providence had sent these gifts as timely help for **Joseph and Mary**, who were poor.

Our heavenly Father knows what his children need.

He can appoint one person to provide for another and bring support even from the ends of the earth.

## Joseph takes the family to Egypt (verses 13–15)

Egypt had once been the place of Israel's slavery and had been particularly cruel to Israelite infants.

Now God made it a refuge for the holy child Jesus.

The Lord can use even the worst places for the best purposes.

This journey tested the faith of Joseph and Mary.

Their faith was tested and proved firm.

Whenever we or our young children experience danger and hardship, let us remember the difficulties Christ himself faced as an infant.

## Herod kills the children of Bethlehem (verses 16–18)

Herod ordered the murder of the young boys in Bethlehem and its surrounding villages.

Uncontrolled anger, backed by illegitimate power, can lead to almost unimaginable cruelty.

Henry writes that God was not unjust in permitting these events, because fallen human beings' lives are subject to divine judgment from the beginning.

He relates the deaths and diseases of young children to **original sin**.

These are Henry's theological explanations of divine permission; they do **not** excuse Herod's murder.

Henry calls the children's deaths **their martyrdom**.

How early persecution against **Christ and his kingdom** began!

Herod may have believed he had frustrated the Old Testament prophecies and defeated the wise men's search.

But whatever cunning and cruelty human beings devise, **the Lord's purpose will stand**.

## Jesus returns and lives in Nazareth (verses 19–23)

Egypt could serve as a temporary place of refuge, but not as the family's permanent home.

Christ was sent to **the lost sheep of the house of Israel**, so he was to return to them.

If we regarded this present world as our **Egypt**, a place of temporary exile and bondage, and heaven as our **Canaan**, our true home and rest, we would depart just as readily when the Lord calls us.

Joseph brought the family to **Galilee**.

They settled in **Nazareth**, a town people regarded with contempt.

The name **Nazarene** was later used as an accusation against Christ.

Wherever Providence assigns us to live, we should be prepared to share the disgrace Christ experienced.

Yet we may gladly bear his name, knowing that **if we suffer with him, we will also be glorified with him**.
