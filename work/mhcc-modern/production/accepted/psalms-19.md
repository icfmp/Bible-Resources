# Psalm 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The glory of God's works. | 1–6 |
| His holiness and grace as shown in his word. | 7–10 |
| Prayer for the benefit of them. | 11–14 |

## Creation declares God's glory (verses 1–6)

The heavens proclaim God's glory, wisdom, power and goodness so clearly that those who ignore him are without excuse.

Their very existence shows that they must have been made by a Creator who is eternal, infinitely wise, powerful and good.

The continuous alternation of **day and night** displays divine power.

It also reminds us that in Providence, as in nature, God makes light and creates darkness (**Isaiah 45:7**), placing them alongside each other.

The sun is a picture of **Christ, the Sun of Righteousness**, the church's Bridegroom and **the Light of the World**.

Through his gospel he brings divine light and salvation to nations everywhere.

He delights to bless the church he has chosen as his bride.

His course is as tireless as the sun's until the earth is filled with his light and salvation.

We should pray for the day when every nation enjoys the life-giving light of the Savior.

The heavens have no ordinary words or language, yet their **voice is heard**.

People everywhere can understand this testimony in their own tongue.

Give God the glory for the comfort and benefit received from heavenly lights.

Yet always look beyond them to **the Sun of Righteousness**.

## God's word reveals grace and holiness (verses 7–10)

The Holy Scriptures offer something far more important than day, night, the air we breathe or sunlight.

Fallen human beings need **God's word** to be restored.

The word translated **“law”** can also be understood as **teaching**, embracing everything by which God instructs us in true religion.

The whole is perfect.

It turns the soul away from sin and worldly dependence toward God and holiness.

It shows our sinfulness and misery when we depart from God and the necessity of returning to him.

God's testimony is certain and reliable.

People without education who believe what he says can become **wise for salvation**.

His word is a trustworthy guide for duty, a living source of comfort and the foundation of lasting hope.

The Lord's statutes are right and bring gladness to the heart.

His commands are **pure, holy, just and good**.

They reveal our need of the Savior and teach us how to honor his gospel.

The Holy Spirit uses the commandments to **enlighten our eyes**, showing us sin and misery and guiding us toward duty.

**Fear of the Lord**, that is, genuine godliness, cleanses our ways and lasts forever.

The ceremonial law has long since ended, but **the obligation to fear God has not changed**.

His judgments and commands are entirely true and righteous.

Gold serves bodily and temporary interests.

Grace serves **the soul and eternity**.

God's word received by faith is more precious than gold and sweeter to the soul than honey.

Physical pleasures soon become excessive but never truly satisfy.

The joys of true religion are **substantial, lasting and satisfying**, without danger of excess.

## The word warns and leads us to pray (verses 11–14)

God's word warns the wicked not to continue in evil and warns the righteous not to abandon the good path.

There is a reward **not only after keeping God's commands, but in keeping them**.

True religion makes our ordinary comforts sweeter and our crosses easier to bear.

It makes life valuable and even death something a believer can welcome.

David wanted forgiveness and cleansing not only for sins he had already discovered and confessed, but also for **sins he had forgotten or overlooked**.

Everything Scripture teaches us about our guilt should drive us to **the throne of grace**.

There we should pray.

Our dependence is the same as that of believers who confess that in **Jesus Christ alone** they have righteousness and strength.

No prayer is acceptable apart from **our Redeemer's strength and mediation**.

He became our **divine Kinsman** by taking human nature, so that he could redeem us to God and restore the inheritance we had lost.

May the excellence of God's word deeply affect our hearts, along with the wickedness of sin and the danger sin brings.
