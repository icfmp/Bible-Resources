# Philemon 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle's joy and praise for Philemon's steady faith in the Lord Jesus, and love to all the saints. | 1–7 |
| He recommends Onesimus as one who would make rich amends for the misconduct of which he had been guilty; and on behalf of whom the apostle promises to make up any loss Philemon had sustained. | 8–22 |
| Salutations and a blessing. | 23–25 |

## Paul's joy in Philemon's love (verses 1–7)

Faith in Christ and love for him should unite believers **more closely than outward family or social ties**.

Paul remembered his friends individually in private prayer. We should also bring Christian friends before God regularly, thinking about what they need and carrying them in our hearts.

Differences of opinion or practice in **nonessential matters** should not destroy affection between believers faithful to the truth.

Paul wanted to know whether his friends' faith and love were **real, growing and fruitful**.

Philemon's good deeds comforted and encouraged him and others. They therefore desired that he would continue increasing in these fruits for **God's glory**.

## Paul's loving appeal for Onesimus (verses 8–14)

It does not diminish our dignity to ask humbly, even when we could demand something as a matter of authority.

**Paul chose to appeal through love rather than command** on behalf of the man converted through his ministry, Onesimus.

The name **Onesimus** means **“useful.”** Formerly he had been unprofitable to Philemon, but conversion had made him useful.

Those living without holiness do not fulfill the highest purpose of their existence.

What a wonderful change conversion brings: turning evil into good and the formerly unprofitable into useful people!

Henry describes religious servants as a treasure to a household because they conscientiously use their time and fulfill their trusts.

Yet opportunities for useful service elsewhere should not lead anyone to ignore genuine obligations. A key sign of repentance is a desire to fulfill duties formerly neglected.

In Henry's reading of the events, Onesimus acknowledged his wrongdoing in leaving and now wished to make amends.

We often do not know what providential purposes God will accomplish through changes of place or plans originally undertaken for wrong reasons.

Without God's overruling mercy, some of our own sinful plans would have led us to ruin.

## Receive a repentant brother and forgive the debt (verses 15–22)

We must never **minimize the evil of sin itself**. But when dealing with a repentant sinner, we should follow God's merciful example and cover the offense rather than keep holding it against the person.

People changed by grace can become blessings to those around them.

Christianity does not abolish genuine obligations toward others; it guides us in fulfilling them rightly.

Repentant people should openly acknowledge harm done to others. Henry understands Onesimus to have admitted his fault to Paul.

Fellowship between Christians does not erase distinctions of property.

Paul's proposal that another person's debt should be **charged to him voluntarily** illustrates one person accepting another's obligation to free that person from a deserved penalty.

Henry sees this as a figure of **Christ willingly bearing the punishment of our sins, so that we may receive his righteousness**.

Although Philemon was Paul's spiritual son, Paul appealed to him as **a brother**.

And although Onesimus was a poor enslaved man, Paul pleaded for him **as earnestly as if asking a great personal favor**.

Christians should bring one another joy and encouragement. People can expect trouble from the world, but should find consolation among other believers.

When comforts disappear, trust the Lord, use the available means and **persevere in prayer**. Prayer is powerful, but it does **not earn** what God grants.

Believers who never meet again on earth will soon meet before God's throne to praise his redeeming love.

Onesimus's conversion should encourage the most deeply guilty sinners to seek God. It must **never become an excuse for postponing repentance**: many die in their sins, and hardening the heart against present conviction is dangerous.

## Paul's greetings and blessing (verses 23–25)

Believers may find especially great enjoyment of God's presence when **suffering together for Christ**.

**Grace** is the finest blessing we can wish for ourselves or others. Paul began and ended his letter with it.

All grace comes from Jesus Christ, who **purchased and gives it**.

If his grace is with our spirits, what more do we need for true happiness?

Let us learn now to value what people often recognize only when dying: even the smallest portion of genuine grace and faith is worth more than **an earthly kingdom**.
