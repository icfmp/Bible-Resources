# Jeremiah 44 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews in Egypt persist in idolatry. | 1–14 |
| They refuse to reform. | 15–19 |
| Jeremiah then denounces destruction upon them. | 20–30 |

## The Jews in Egypt persist in idolatry (verses 1–14)

God reminded the Jews that Judah's earlier sins had brought about the country's destruction.

We ought to warn others earnestly about the danger of sin:

**Do not do it!**

If you love God, do not sin, because it provokes him.

If you love your own soul, do not sin, because it destroys you.

Let conscience speak to us in this way whenever we are tempted.

The Jews whom God had sent into **the land of the Chaldeans** were, through his grace, being drawn away from idolatry.

But those who had deliberately gone into **Egypt against his command** became even more attached to idols.

When we enter situations of temptation without reason or divine calling, it is just for God to leave us to the consequences of our own choices.

If we walk against him, he will walk against us.

The greatest and most dreadful misery people experience arises from **neglecting the salvation offered to them**.

## The people refuse to reform (verses 15–19)

These bold sinners did not even attempt to excuse themselves.

They openly declared that they intended to **continue doing what God forbade**.

People who knowingly disobey God often become steadily worse; sin deceives and hardens their hearts.

Their words reveal the true attitude of a rebellious heart.

Even afflictions that ought to have separated them from idolatry were interpreted by them as **reasons to keep practicing it**.

It is terrible when people who ought to encourage one another toward God and heaven instead encourage one another in sin and thereby prepare one another for judgment.

Combining **idolatry with the worship of God** and rejecting **the mediation of Christ** provoke God and bring ruin upon people.

Henry extends this warning against idolatry to **the worship of images** and what he regards as religious honors wrongly paid to **saints, angels and the queen of heaven**. This is his own theological application of the passage, preserved as such rather than presented as a new statement of the biblical narrative.

He urges readers to remember what happened because of the Jews' idolatrous practices.

## Jeremiah announces judgment (verses 20–30)

When evil comes upon us, we should consider how we have sinned against the Lord.

We should stand in awe of him and **turn from sin**.

Since these people were determined to remain in idolatry, God would continue judging them.

Even the little true religion still present among them would disappear.

The earthly comforts and sources of security in which we place our greatest confidence may fail just as quickly as those in which we place little trust.

They are what **God makes them**, not what we imagine them to be.

A well-founded hope of sharing God's mercy is always joined with **repentance and obedience**.
