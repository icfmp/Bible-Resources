# Job 38 — Modern English

## The Lord calls Job to answer (verses 1–3)

Job had silenced his friends but had not persuaded them.

Elihu had silenced Job without bringing him to acknowledge his fault before God.

Now **the Lord himself intervened**.

God humbled Job and brought him to repent of the passionate words he had spoken about divine Providence.

The Lord did so by setting a series of contrasts before him:

**God lives forever**, while Job's life is brief.

**God knows everything**, while Job's knowledge is limited.

**God possesses almighty strength**, while Job is weak.

It seriously offends God when human folly obscures the counsel of his wisdom.

**Humble faith and sincere obedience** give us the clearest understanding of his will.

## God questions Job about earth and sea (verses 4–11)

To humble Job, God showed that he knew so little even about **the earth and the sea**.

We are not qualified to find fault with God's work.

Nor need we fear that his work will fail.

The works of his Providence and creation are secure.

So is the work of **redemption**, whose foundation and cornerstone is **Jesus Christ**.

God's church rests on a foundation as firm as the earth.

## Light, darkness and our ignorance (verses 12–24)

The Lord challenged Job to recognize the extent of what he did not know.

When we examine ourselves in this way, we discover how little our knowledge is compared with everything unknown to us.

Through God's tender mercy, **the Dayspring from on high** has visited us, bringing light to people sitting in darkness.

God enlightens hearts so they receive his truth like clay receiving the impress of a seal (**2 Corinthians 4:6**).

God's way in governing the world is described as being **in the sea**—hidden from us.

If we are assured that the gates of heaven will open to us after death, we need not fear the gates of death.

Someone unable to measure even the breadth of the earth should not presume to search the full depths of God's counsel.

At the brightest noon, do not assume it will always be day.

At the darkest midnight, do not despair of morning's return.

This applies to our **inward spiritual condition** as well as outward circumstances.

How foolish to fight against God, when our greatest interest lies in **making peace with him and remaining in his love**!

## Other mighty works and divine compassion (verses 25–41)

Earlier, God's questions exposed Job's **ignorance**.

Now they also exposed his **weakness**.

Knowing so little, Job should not put God's plans on trial.

Being able to accomplish so little, he should not oppose Providence.

The Lord's care is enough to satisfy the needs of every living creature.

He even feeds **young ravens**.

Surely he will not neglect his own people.

That example of compassion is only one among countless gifts God provides every day without our awareness.

Every glimpse of divine perfection should remind us that God deserves our love, that sin against him is evil, and that **we need his mercy and salvation**.
