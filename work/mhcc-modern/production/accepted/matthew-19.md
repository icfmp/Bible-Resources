# Matthew 19 — Modern English

## Jesus enters Judea and heals the crowds (verses 1 and 2)

Large crowds followed Jesus.

Whenever Christ moves on, the best response is to follow him.

They discovered that he was just as willing and able to help them there as he had been in Galilee.

Wherever **the Sun of Righteousness** rises, he brings healing.

## Jesus responds to questions about marriage and divorce (verses 3–12)

The Pharisees hoped to draw Jesus into saying something that could be portrayed as contrary to Moses' law.

Marriage disputes are numerous and can be confusing.

They become difficult not because God's law is defective but because human beings bring **sinful desire and foolishness** into them.

People often decide what they want to do before they even seek advice.

Jesus referred his listeners to **the creation account and the first marriage**.

He thereby showed that departing from God's original design is wrong.

In deciding about marriage or singleness, we should seek the condition that best serves our souls and helps prepare and preserve us for **God's kingdom**.

True acceptance of the gospel produces kindness within families and faithfulness in friendships.

It teaches people to carry one another's burdens and patiently bear one another's weaknesses.

It directs them to consider the peace and happiness of relatives and friends above their own convenience.

People who do not fear God still need lawful civil restraints so that society can live in peace.

Marriage should be undertaken with **great seriousness and earnest prayer**.

## Jesus welcomes children (verses 13–15)

It is good for us to come to Christ, and also to **bring our children to him**.

Young children need his blessing and are capable of receiving good from him and sharing in his intercession.

We can only ask that blessing.

**Christ alone can command and bestow it**.

How fortunate that Jesus possesses even greater kindness and tenderness than his finest disciples!

Let us follow his example and never discourage anyone sincerely seeking him, even when that person is weak.

Those given to Christ as part of his redeemed people will never be cast away by him.

He is displeased when others try to exclude people whom he receives.

All Christians should bring their children to the Savior, asking for **spiritual blessings** for them.

## The rich young man asks about eternal life (verses 16–22)

Jesus knew that **love of riches** was the particular sin most likely to rule the young man.

His possessions may have been acquired honestly.

Yet he could not willingly give them up when Christ called him to do so.

That exposed the weakness of his professed sincerity.

Christ's promises make his commands possible to bear and his yoke pleasant.

The promise to the young man tested his **faith** as much as the command tested his **charity and willingness to leave the world**.

Those following Christ must attend to his appointed ordinances, imitate his example and submit willingly to his direction.

This must be done from **love for him and dependence upon him**.

Simply selling everything and giving to poor people **would not itself be enough**.

What matters is **following Jesus**.

The gospel is the only remedy for lost sinners.

Many people abstain from openly scandalous behavior while neglecting their duty toward God.

Thousands of wrong thoughts, words and deeds remain recorded against them.

Many leave Christ because they love this present world.

They may experience conviction, sincere-sounding desires and deep sorrow, yet still turn away.

We must examine ourselves because the Lord will examine us.

## Riches and the reward of discipleship (verses 23–30)

Few wealthy people are free from trusting their wealth.

Few poor people escape every temptation to envy.

Human striving for earthly gain can resemble **building a high wall that shuts oneself and one's children out of heaven**.

People of limited means can be thankful for not facing all the temptations of great power and prosperity.

Their circumstances may be harder here, but if they enter the better world more readily, they have no reason to complain.

Jesus taught that wealth makes the Christian path especially difficult.

The gate is narrow for everyone, but in particular for the rich.

More is expected from those with greater opportunities.

They face many temptations to sin and find a prosperous world very attractive.

They must answer to God for the use of their advantages.

A person whose heart belongs completely to wealth **cannot enter heaven while remaining enslaved to it**.

Jesus described something impossible by merely human power.

Only **the almighty grace of God** can overcome this obstacle.

Who, then, can be saved?

Poverty has its own dangers. Pride and sinful desires also threaten people without riches.

**No one can save himself through created power.**

The beginning, growth and completion of salvation depend entirely upon God's almighty grace.

Rich people are saved **from their worldliness**, not **while remaining worldly**.

Peter said, **“We have left everything.”**

What they left amounted outwardly to a few boats and fishing nets.

We tend to exaggerate our sacrifices and services for Christ.

Yet Jesus did not rebuke them.

Their possessions were few, but they were **all they had**, precious to them as greater wealth would be to someone else.

He graciously accepted their willingness to leave everything and follow.

Jesus promised the apostles a special dignity in the coming renewal, when the Son of Man sits upon his throne of glory.

They would share in judgment concerning people judged by the **doctrine they faithfully declared**.

This displays their office's honor and authority.

Everyone who gives up possessions or earthly comforts for Christ and the gospel will also receive a final reward.

May God give us faith to rely on his promise, making us willing for every service or sacrifice.

Jesus' final warning corrects another mistake.

The heavenly inheritance is **not distributed like earthly inheritances**, according to outward position or expectation.

It follows God's pleasure.

Do not trust outward profession or an appearance of future greatness.

Others who seem unlikely now may yet become distinguished in faith and holiness.
