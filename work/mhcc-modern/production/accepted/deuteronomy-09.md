# Deuteronomy 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Israelites not to think their success came by their own worthiness. | 1–6 |
| Moses reminds the Israelites of their rebellions. | 7–29 |

## Victory comes from God, not Israel's merit (verses 1–6)

Moses described the strength of the nations Israel was about to confront.

This was meant to drive the people toward God and teach them to place their hope in him.

He assured them of victory because **the Lord would be with them**.

But they must never imagine that their own righteousness had earned God's favor.

In Jesus Christ we receive both righteousness and strength.

We must boast in him alone, never in ourselves or in any ability of our own.

The nations of Canaan were driven out because of their own wickedness.

All those whom God rejects are rejected because of their wrongdoing.

But none whom he accepts is accepted because of personal righteousness.

In this way all human boasting is excluded (Ephesians 2:9, 11–12).

## The wilderness rebellions show Israel's need of mercy (verses 7–29)

Moses recalled the people's repeated rebellions.

If they believed their righteousness had brought them to Canaan, they should remember what a miracle of mercy it was that they had survived the wilderness at all.

We should often recall our former sins with sorrow and shame.

Doing so helps us understand our debt to free grace.

It reminds us that we have never deserved anything from God except righteous judgment.

Human pride is persistent.

It finds many different ways to return.

We readily imagine that our own goodness has gained us God's special favor, though our wickedness is often more obvious than our weakness.

On the day of judgment, when the hidden history of every human life is revealed, the whole world will stand guilty before God.

Yet we have an Advocate before the mercy seat.

He did not merely fast for us.

**He died upon the cross for our sins.**

Through him, even sinners who condemn themselves may approach God and ask for undeserved mercy and eternal life as his free gift.

Let every victory, all glory and every expression of praise belong to him who alone brings salvation.
