# 2 Peter 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortations to add the exercise of various other graces to faith. | 1–11 |
| The apostle looks forward to his approaching decease. | 12–15 |
| And confirms the truth of the gospel, relating to Christ's appearing to judgment. | 16–21 |

## Grow in the graces of living faith (verses 1–11)

**Faith unites both weak and strong believers to Jesus Christ** and truly purifies their hearts. Every sincere believer is justified before God **through faith**.

Faith produces genuine godliness, doing things no other grace can do in the soul.

Every fullness is found in Christ. Through the Holy Spirit he gives **forgiveness, peace, grace, spiritual knowledge and new principles of life**.

God's promises invite believers to ask whether they really have been made new inwardly and share in **the divine nature**.

Turn these promises into **prayers for the Spirit's transforming and cleansing work**.

Add knowledge to virtue by growing in understanding of God's complete truth and will.

Add **self-control** to knowledge: moderation in relation to this world's goods and pleasures.

Add **patience** to self-control: a cheerful submission to God's will amid troubles.

Suffering can produce patience, enabling us to carry crosses and losses with quiet submission.

Add **godliness** to patience: holy desires and affections that mark true worshipers.

Love your Christian brothers and sisters tenderly. They have **the same Father and Master**, belong to the same household, travel toward the same country and inherit the same kingdom.

Work to gain **assurance of your calling and election** through believing and doing what is right.

Such perseverance is evidence that God's mercy and grace sustain us so that we will not finally fall away.

Those diligent in living faith will enter the everlasting kingdom triumphantly, reigning with Christ forever.

It is **in carrying out good works**, not in pretending that works purchase acceptance, that believers should expect to approach this promised entrance.

## Peter prepares for his death (verses 12–15)

We must become firmly established in the truth so that every changing opinion will not overturn us.

Pay special attention to those aspects of Christian truth most needed in **our own day** and to errors currently opposed to it.

The body is the soul's **tent**, a temporary dwelling that can be moved and taken down.

As death approaches, Peter grows more diligent in his appointed work.

Nothing makes death more peaceful than knowing we have **sincerely followed Jesus and sought his glory**.

Those who fear the Lord naturally speak of his loving kindness.

The enduring written word enables God's people to pass on the knowledge of him after they are gone.

## Scripture is the Spirit-inspired word of God (verses 16–21)

The gospel is not weak speculation. It comes **with divine power** (**Romans 1:16**).

The law shows humanity's miserable condition under sin, but it does not provide the saving cure.

**Jesus crucified, revealed in the gospel, heals the soul**.

Rational arguments alone often fail to change a person ruled by greed, anger or lust. One piece of gold outweighs every argument for the greedy person; someone furious will not listen; a single tempting smile can defeat mere reasoning.

But the gospel announces **Christ's precious blood**, shed to save sinners from deserved judgment and atone for their sins.

That message has the power to make believing hearts burn and to move even opponents, like **Agrippa**, toward recognizing its truth (**Acts 26:28**).

God is pleased with Christ and accepts people **in him**.

Old Testament prophets foretold both the promised Messiah and the gospel's salvation, speaking and writing through **God's Spirit**.

Their words provide a firm foundation for faith.

When the Spirit brings Scripture's light into a dark understanding, it is like **daybreak spreading toward full daylight**.

Everyone should study Scripture to understand its real meaning.

True Christians recognize God's word through the sweetness, spiritual power and divine glory they experience in it.

Fulfilled prophecies concerning Christ and his church testify to Christianity's truth.

**The Holy Spirit inspired the prophets' actual words**, guiding their communication so that what they wrote faithfully expressed what God had revealed.

The Scriptures must therefore be received and honored as **the words of the Holy Spirit**, with all their clarity, simplicity and power from God.

Receive the word in faith and cherish the Bible as a book written by holy people **instructed by the Holy Spirit**.
