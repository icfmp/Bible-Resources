# 1 Kings 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David's dying charge to Solomon. | 1–4 |
| David's charge as to Joab and others. | 5–11 |

## David's final instruction to Solomon (verses 1–4)

David told Solomon to **keep the Lord's command**.

A dying father's authority deserves serious respect, but it is far below that of the **living God**.

The Lord had promised that the **Messiah** would come from David's descendants, and that promise was unconditional.

But the promise that David's family would never lack someone on Israel's throne was **conditional**.

The descendants must walk before God sincerely, with zeal and determination.

To do this, Solomon would need to watch over his own conduct carefully.

## David's instructions about Joab and others (verses 5–11)

David's dying counsel concerning **Joab and Shimei** did not arise from private anger.

It was intended to preserve Solomon's throne and prevent future wrongdoing.

Joab had already committed murders, and David believed he would repeat them if doing so furthered his plans.

Although punishment had been delayed, Joab would finally be called to account.

**The passage of time does not erase guilt**, especially the guilt of murder.

David warned Solomon not to regard Shimei as innocent or a genuine supporter.

He believed the same malice still remained in Shimei's heart.

David's final words are recorded as spoken under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit (**2 Samuel 23:1–7**).

In them the Lord revealed to David the offices and salvation of the coming Messiah.

All David's hope and comfort rested upon that Savior.

The passage gives convincing evidence that David died with genuine **faith and hope**, under the Spirit's influence.

## Adonijah's request leads to his death (verses 12–25)

Solomon treated his mother Bathsheba with all the honor due to her.

But we should never ask a person to do something that person has no right to grant.

A godly person should neither make an improper request nor support an unjust cause.

In the royal customs of the time, Adonijah's request to marry **Abishag** clearly expressed another attempt to seize the throne.

Solomon could not be secure while Adonijah continued that ambition.

Restless, ambitious people often prepare their own destruction.

Many have lost their lives trying to seize a crown.

## Abiathar loses office; Joab is executed (verses 26–34)

Solomon's words to **Abiathar**, and Abiathar's silence, suggest that recent conspiracies had been taking place.

Kindness shown toward God's people will be remembered favorably.

Solomon spared Abiathar's life because of his earlier service, but removed him from his offices.

The altar provided refuge in matters where the appointed sacrifice could atone.

But Joab's murders did not fall within that category.

Solomon looked to **God as the Author of peace** and toward eternity for peace's perfect fulfillment.

The Lord of peace himself will give us everlasting peace.

## Shimei is punished (verses 35–46)

Old hostility remains within a heart that has not been changed.

We should be cautious about people who have demonstrated enmity but shown no evidence of repentance.

Worldly people can become so determined to pursue their own plans that neither solemn commitments nor danger will restrain them.

They continue even when doing so forfeits their lives and souls.

God will not bend his judgment to suit our preferences.

He sees everything we do.

We should live as though continually in his presence, because we are.

Every act, word and thought should be governed by the knowledge that a time is approaching when **the smallest details of our lives will be revealed** and our eternal condition will be fixed by an entirely righteous Judge.

Solomon's throne was established in peace.

It pictured the **Redeemer's kingdom of peace and righteousness**.

Although Christ's enemies rage, they plot in vain.

His throne stands firm, and no one can overthrow it.
