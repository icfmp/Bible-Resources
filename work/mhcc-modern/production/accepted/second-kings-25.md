# 2 Kings 25 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jerusalem besieged, Zedekiah taken. | 1–7 |
| The temple burnt, The people carried into captivity. | 8–21 |
| The rest of the Jews flee into Egypt, Evil-merodach relieves the captivity of Jehoiachin. | 22–30 |

## Jerusalem falls and Zedekiah is captured (verses 1–7)

Jerusalem was so strongly defended that its attackers could not capture it until famine weakened the people inside and made resistance impossible.

Jeremiah's prophecy and Lamentations describe these events more fully.

Here it is enough to note how terrible the people's wickedness and suffering had become.

Eventually the city was stormed.

The king, his household and leading officials escaped at night through secret passages.

But those who think they can escape God's judgments deceive themselves just as surely as those who imagine they can resist them.

Zedekiah's fate fulfilled **two prophecies that initially appeared contradictory**.

Jeremiah said he would be taken to Babylon (**Jeremiah 32:5; 34:3**).

Ezekiel said he would not see Babylon (**Ezekiel 12:13**).

Both statements came true.

Zedekiah was taken to Babylon, but his eyes had been put out, so he **never saw it**.

## The temple is burned and the people exiled (verses 8–21)

Jerusalem and its temple were burned.

Henry considers it **probable** that the ark was destroyed in the fire.

This showed how little God values the outward splendor of worship when its living power and reality are neglected.

Jerusalem's walls were demolished.

Its people were carried into captivity in Babylon, and the temple's vessels were taken away.

When the people had rejected the realities the sacred symbols represented, why should the symbols remain?

It was just for God to remove the blessings of true worship from people who had preferred false forms of worship.

Those who had insisted on having many altars would now have **none**.

God did not spare angels who sinned.

He has appointed death for fallen humanity and judgment for those who refuse to believe.

Nor did he spare **his own Son**, but gave him up for us all.

Therefore we should not be astonished that he brings serious judgments upon guilty nations, churches or individuals.

## Gedaliah is killed; Jehoiachin receives relief (verses 22–30)

The king of Babylon appointed **Gedaliah** as governor and protector of the Jews who remained in the country.

But the people did not recognize the opportunity for peace given to them.

**Ishmael** treacherously killed Gedaliah and his associates.

The surviving Jews then went to Egypt against **Jeremiah's counsel**.

Through their folly and disobedience they brought further ruin upon themselves (Jeremiah 40–45).

After **thirty-seven years** in prison, Jehoiachin was released.

No one should say that good can never return merely because suffering has continued for a long time.

Even people in extreme distress cannot know what change God's providence may yet bring to their circumstances, or what comfort may await them after years of affliction.

Even in this life, the Savior gives freedom from bondage to the distressed sinner who seeks him, granting a foretaste of the pleasures at his right hand forever.

**Sin alone can truly harm us; Jesus alone can truly do good to sinners.**
