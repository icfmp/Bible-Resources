# Genesis 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| One language in the world; the building of Babel. | 1–4 |
| Languages are confused; Babel's builders are scattered. | 5–9 |

| Terah, Abram's father and Lot's grandfather; the journey toward Haran. | 27–32 |

## One language and the building of Babel (verses 1–4)

How quickly human beings forget even the most terrible judgments and return to their old sins!

The destruction caused by the flood was still fresh in their minds. They were descended from righteous Noah, and Noah himself was still alive.

Yet wickedness was already growing rapidly.

Nothing except the sanctifying grace of the Holy Spirit can remove the sinful desires of the human will and the corruption of the human heart.

God intended humanity to become many nations and populate the whole earth.

But most people came together in defiance of his will and against Noah's counsel.

They resolved to build a city and a tower so they would not have to separate.

Idolatry had begun, and Babel became one of its principal centers.

The builders encouraged one another, making each other bolder and more determined in evil.

Let us learn from this to encourage one another toward love and good works, as sinners encourage one another toward wickedness.

## God confuses their language and scatters them (verses 5–9)

Scripture says the Lord came down to look at the city. This is language adapted to human understanding.

God is just and fair in everything he does against sin and sinners. He condemns no one without hearing the case.

The godly Eber was not among this rebellious company. He and his household belonged among God's children, and they did not join their souls to the gathering of those who lived without God.

The Lord allowed the builders to make considerable progress.

He intended the work from which they expected lasting honor to become an enduring disgrace.

God has wise and holy reasons for allowing enemies of his glory to succeed for a long time in their wicked schemes.

Consider both his wisdom and his mercy in the way he defeated their project.

His mercy appeared in making the punishment less severe than the offense deserved. God does not deal with us according to our sins.

His wisdom appeared in the sure means he chose to stop their work.

Once they could no longer understand one another, they could no longer help one another.

That would bring the building to an end.

God has many effective ways to defeat the schemes of proud people who oppose him. One of them is to divide them against one another.

However united and stubborn the builders had been, God was above them all. Who has ever hardened his heart against God and prospered?

Their language was confused.

We still suffer from that confusion. The trouble we take to learn other languages is part of the legacy of our ancestors' rebellion at Babel.

Perhaps even the unhappy disputes that arise from misunderstandings of words and arguments have their roots in that event.

The builders stopped working on the city.

The confusion of languages had made cooperation impossible. It also showed them that God's hand was against them.

It is wise to stop doing what we see God himself is opposing.

God can bring every scheme of Babel's builders to nothing. No wisdom or plan can prevail against the Lord.

The families separated according to their languages and went to the lands appointed for them.

Since that day, humanity has never gathered into one assembly as it did at Babel.

And it will not do so again until the great day when the Son of Man sits upon his glorious throne and all the nations gather before him.

## The descendants of Shem (verses 10–26)

Here is a genealogy ending with Abram, the friend of God.

It leads us toward Christ, the promised Seed, who would descend from Abram.

Only names and ages are recorded. The Holy Spirit seems to hurry through these generations to reach Abram's story.

How little we know about people who lived before us, even in the very places where we now live!

We know just as little about many people living far away from us today.

We have enough to do in attending faithfully to the responsibilities God has given us.

As the earth became more populated, human lifespans began to shorten. This was the wise ordering of God's providence.

## Terah's family leaves Ur and settles in Haran (verses 27–32)

Here begins the story of Abram, whose name is celebrated in both Testaments.

Even the descendants of Eber had become worshippers of false gods.

Those who, through grace, are heirs of the promised land should remember the land from which they came: their corrupt and sinful natural condition.

Abram had two brothers.

One was Nahor, from whose family both Isaac and Jacob would later take wives.

The other was Haran, Lot's father, who died before his own father.

Children cannot assume that they will outlive their parents.

Haran died in Ur, before the family had the blessing of leaving that idolatrous country.

We should therefore hurry to leave our natural condition of sin before death overtakes us in it.

Abram left Ur of the Chaldeans with his father Terah, his nephew Lot, and the rest of his family, in obedience to God's call.

This chapter leaves them partway between Ur and Canaan.

They settled there until Terah died.

Many people come as far as Haran but never reach Canaan.

They are not far from God's kingdom, yet they never enter it.

## Chapter-level editorial audit

The modern prose was compared in a second pass against [Genesis 11 in the original Concise reading witness](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc/mhcc.ii.xi.html). The primary derivative source remains published `MHCC 0.1.1` and its controlled correction history.

- **1–4:** rapid return to evil despite the flood and Noah's presence, Spirit's sanctifying necessity, God's purpose to populate earth, rebellion **against Noah's advice**, early idolatry, mutual encouragement to evil versus love/good works—all retained.
- **5–9:** anthropomorphic descent of God, impartial judgment, Eber's separation, delayed divine judgment and its mercy, language confusion as the chosen remedy, learning-language difficulties and *possible* misunderstandings as consequences, the future final assembly before the Son of Man—all retained without turning conjecture into certainty.
- **10–26:** genealogy leading to Christ through Abram, bare names and ages, Spirit's editorial direction, little knowledge of other generations, responsibility for own work and shortening lives in providence—all retained.
- **27–32:** descendants of Eber and false worship, natural condition of grace's heirs, Nahor, Haran and Lot, child dying before parent, Ur to Haran with Terah and Lot, *not yet* reaching Canaan, spiritual warning against stopping short—all retained.
