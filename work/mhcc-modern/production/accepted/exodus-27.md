# Exodus 27 — Modern English

## The altar for burnt offerings (verses 1–8)

The altar stood in the court in front of the tabernacle.

The people brought their sacrifices there, and the priests offered them to God.

It was made of wood covered in bronze.

A bronze grate was fitted inside the hollow altar, near its middle.

The fire burned upon this grate, and the sacrifices were consumed there.

The grate was an open network, like a sieve, so that ashes could fall through it.

The bronze altar was a picture of Christ, who died to atone for our sins.

The wood would have been consumed by the fire from heaven if the bronze had not protected it.

In the same way, Henry says, Christ's human nature could not have endured God's wrath unless divine power had sustained it.

## The court around the tabernacle (verses 9–19)

A court enclosed the tabernacle.

Henry describes it as about sixty yards long and thirty yards wide.

Curtains hung upon bronze pillars fixed in bronze bases formed its boundary.

Inside this enclosure, priests and Levites offered the sacrifices.

Other Israelites were also admitted into the court.

Henry understands these distinctions as pointing to the difference between the **visible church of those who merely profess religion** and the **true spiritual church**.

Only the latter truly has access to God and enjoys communion with him.

## Oil and light for the lamps (verses 20–21)

The pure oil represented the gifts and graces of the Holy Spirit.

Believers receive these from Christ, the good Olive Tree.

Without them, our light cannot shine before other people.

The priests were charged with lighting the lamps and tending them.

Likewise, ministers are charged with enlightening God's church on earth through preaching and explaining the Scriptures.

God's word is a lamp.

Blessed be God: this light is no longer confined to the Jewish tabernacle.

It has become a light for the Gentiles and a message of salvation to the ends of the earth.
