# Isaiah 44 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Here are promises of the influences of the Holy Spirit. | 1–8 |
| An exposure of the folly of idolatry. | 9–20 |
| Also the deliverance of God's people. | 21–28 |

## God's Spirit promised to his people (verses 1–8)

Israel is called **Jeshurun**, meaning **“the upright one.”**

Those who truly belong to Israel in the spiritual sense are people **without deceit**.

God acknowledges those who serve him.

He helps them through their difficulties and supports them in their service.

Water represents **the Holy Spirit**.

Just as water refreshes, cleanses and makes the earth fruitful, the Spirit brings spiritual renewal, purification and fruitfulness to the soul.

The gift of the Holy Spirit is the great blessing whose abundant outpouring God reserved for the latter days.

Where God gives **his Spirit**, he also gives **every other blessing needed**.

Through this gift, the church will grow greatly and spread into distant regions.

Is there any other **Rock** or **Protector** who can defend God's people?

No other being could announce in advance the things God had revealed through his prophets.

Divine predictions were ordered just as surely as God's purposes.

Who can compare with **Israel's Redeemer and King**?

## The emptiness of handmade gods (verses 9–20)

The description of image-making exposes the foolishness of idolatry.

A person takes a piece of wood and uses part of it as fuel.

From what remains, the same person makes an image, bows before it and begs it for deliverance.

Human beings dishonor God when they attempt to represent him as a human image.

Satan blinds unbelievers' eyes, leading them into absurd reasoning about religion.

People who seek lasting happiness in worldly possessions, unbelief, superstition or any other false system are **feeding on ashes**.

Pride, love of sin and departure from God deceive the heart and lead it away from his truth and worship.

While our desires remain corrupt, we cling to a lie as though it were our most valuable treasure.

Are our hearts absorbed in the world's wealth and pleasures?

Those things will ultimately betray our hope.

If we depend on an outward religious profession or on our own actions to save us, we deceive ourselves as surely as idolaters do.

**Learning to suspect our own self-deception is the first step toward deliverance from it.**

Anyone concerned for the soul should examine the conscience and ask:

**“Is there not a lie in my right hand?”**

## The Lord calls his redeemed people home (verses 21–28)

**Return to me.**

People who have wandered away from God, like the ancient Jews, should hurry to return to him.

The redemption Christ accomplished for us gives us reason to expect every blessing we need from him.

Our sins resemble a thick cloud hanging between heaven and earth.

Sin separates us from God and threatens a storm of divine judgment.

When the Lord pardons sin, he **wipes away and disperses that cloud**.

The way to heaven is opened again.

The **Sun of Righteousness** scatters the cloud completely.

Comfort after pardon resembles sunshine following darkness and rain.

Israel need not despair.

Nothing is too difficult for the God who made everything and can direct every creature to accomplish his purpose.

Once people know Christ, every other kind of knowledge seems insignificant in comparison with knowing him.

God turns the schemes of Christ's enemies into foolishness and catches them in their own cleverness.

The accurate fulfillment of Scripture's prophecies confirms its truth and its divine origin.

God announced particular favors for the people who would later be exiled **long before their captivity took place**.

Their return would face enormous obstacles, but divine power would remove them.

God knew **the person who would deliver them** and revealed his name so that, when his people heard of him, they would recognize that deliverance was drawing near.

For a great person, the highest honor is to serve as an instrument of divine kindness toward God's people.

Even people who seek only their own interests fulfill God's secret purposes.

And there is **a greater Shepherd than Cyrus**, who carries out his Father's will until his entire work is accomplished.
