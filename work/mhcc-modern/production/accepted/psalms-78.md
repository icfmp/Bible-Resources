# Psalm 78 — Modern English

## Scripture and the duty to teach children (verses 1–8)

The teachings in this psalm are called **deep or dark sayings** because they deserve careful examination.

God's law was given with an express command that parents should **teach it diligently to their children**, so that God's people would continue serving him through future generations.

The record of his providential dealings—both mercy and judgment—should help children recognize God's will and obey it.

Remembering his mighty works strengthens our resolve to keep his commandments.

**Hypocrisy opens a broad road toward apostasy**.

People whose hearts are not rightly devoted to God will not remain faithful.

Negligent or wicked parents may be responsible for the spiritual destruction of their children.

Young people must honor their parents and obey everything that is lawful.

But **they must not obey commands requiring sin or imitate evil parental examples**.

## Israel's repeated unbelief (verses 9–39)

Sin drains courage and weakens the heart.

Forgetting God's works causes disobedience to his commands.

The history of Israel displays a long conflict between **divine goodness and human wickedness**.

God hears people's complaining and distrust and is greatly displeased with it.

Those who will not trust his merciful power may come to experience his righteous judgment.

We cannot claim to trust God for **eternal salvation** while consistently refusing to trust his Providence along the way.

He opens heaven's door to those who ask, seek and knock through faith and prayer.

Our distrust, therefore, aggravates our sins.

God sometimes punished Israel not by refusing their sinful cravings but by **granting what they demanded**.

Unrestrained desires never become satisfied.

Those who indulge a lust do not thereby escape it.

Hard indeed are hearts that neither divine kindness melts nor divine judgment breaks.

People who persist in sin should expect continuing trouble.

Our own lives are often joyless and unproductive because **we do not live by faith**.

Under correction, Israel sometimes professed repentance.

But their conduct soon showed their insincerity and lack of perseverance.

Israel's history reflects the condition of **our own hearts and lives**.

Its record also resembles the history of nations and churches, which often ignore both divine mercy and judgment until wrongdoing reaches its full measure.

Great spiritual privileges have not always kept even churches faithful to God's word.

True believers also acknowledge years in which they abused Providence's kindness.

When they enter heaven, they will marvel at God's patience and mercy in bringing them there.

## Wilderness guidance and settlement (verses 40–55)

Receiving divine mercy should never make us feel free to sin.

That misuse of mercy may bring judgment closer.

But people corrected for sin should not become discouraged from repentance.

The Holy One of Israel acts for **his own glory** and for the ultimate good of his people.

When Israel forgot his previous favors, it began to **limit God in its expectations** for the future.

God led his people out **like a flock of sheep** and guided them through the wilderness with the tenderness and care of a shepherd.

So **Jesus, the true Joshua**, leads his church through the spiritual wilderness.

No earthly Canaan and no worldly advantage should make believers forget that **the church remains in a wilderness during this life**.

A far more glorious rest is still reserved for God's people.

## God's mercy despite continued ingratitude (verses 56–72)

Once Israel settled in Canaan, the children repeated their parents' sins.

Though God gave them his testimony, they turned away.

Deliberate sin among people bearing God's name insults his holiness and exposes them to his justice.

Those whom the Lord abandons become easy prey to destructive enemies.

Yet sooner or later he also brings disgrace upon the enemies of his people.

He gave Israel good government through a king after his own heart.

Henry sees this final act of divine favor as the **climax of the psalm**.

**David pictured Christ, the great and good Shepherd**.

Christ was first humbled and afterward exalted.

He is filled with the Spirit of **wisdom and understanding**, and his subjects may trust both the uprightness of his heart and the skill of his hands.

His government and peace will have **no end**.

Every test of human nature confirms the Bible's warning that the heart is deeply deceitful and corrupt.

Only **being recreated by the Holy Spirit** can cure human ungodliness.
