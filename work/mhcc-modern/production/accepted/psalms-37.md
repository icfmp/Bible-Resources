# Psalm 37 — Modern English

## Original heading

**David persuades to patience and confidence in God, by the state of the godly and of the wicked.**

## Do not envy the wicked (verses 1–6)

The world contains many wicked people who prosper and live in comfort. That was true in David's day and should not surprise us now.

Their success tempts us to be resentful, to mistake them for the happiest people and eventually to imitate them.

But outward prosperity fades. Those who look forward with faith see no reason to envy wicked people, whose judgment is everlasting.

True religion is **trust in the Lord expressed through diligent obedience**. To claim trust while neglecting duty is to **test God**, not trust him.

Life does not consist in possessions. Enough food and provision for our needs is more than we deserve and sufficient for someone traveling toward heaven.

Delighting in God is both a **duty and a privilege**.

He has not promised to gratify every bodily appetite or whim, but to satisfy the desires of a soul renewed and made holy.

The true desire of a godly heart is to know, love and serve God.

To **commit our way to the Lord** is to roll its burden upon him: bring our concerns before him in prayer and avoid tormenting ourselves about future events.

We must do our duty and leave what happens afterward in his hands.

## Wait patiently for the Lord (verses 7–20)

Trust that God will make everything work for our ultimate good. Do not become agitated by what you see in this world, because a discontented spirit invites many temptations.

The little given to a righteous person is better and more useful than abundant wealth acquired or used wickedly. It comes from the hand of special divine love.

God provides for his servants **both when they work and when they wait**.

They possess something better than worldly wealth: **peace of conscience, peace with God and peace in God**—peace the world cannot give.

God knows all the days of his people, and not one day's faithful work will go unrewarded.

Earthly days are quickly numbered, but heavenly happiness endures forever.

Those resting upon **the Rock of Ages** need not envy wicked people leaning upon broken reeds.

## Righteousness, generosity and divine guidance (verses 21–33)

God requires us to act justly and give others what is owed.

It is a serious sin to **refuse payment of a just debt when able to pay**; it is a serious hardship to be **unable** to pay.

Truly merciful people continue showing mercy. We must abandon sin, learn to do good and persist in doing so.

God's blessing is the source, sweetness and security of every earthly comfort. Those assured of his favor will lack nothing truly good for them.

By grace and the Holy Spirit, he directs the thoughts, affections and plans of his people; by Providence he orders events to guide them.

He does not always show the whole road. He often leads **one step at a time**, as a parent guides a child.

A believer may fall into sin or trouble, and the sin may bring real pain. But God will keep such a person from ultimate ruin.

Few, if any, have seen a consistent believer and all that believer's children left in complete, friendless destitution.

God does not abandon his saints in affliction. Their permanent home, however, is **heaven**.

Even when Satan buffets a faithful person severely, God does not leave that person in the enemy's hands.

## Trust the Lord for the outcome (verses 34–40)

**Duty belongs to us; outcomes belong to God.**

Verses **35 and 36** picture the apparently flourishing enemy of God whose prosperity soon disappears.

The Lord brings proud plans to nothing, especially plans of persecutors.

No one is perfect in personal righteousness; believers are made complete **in Christ Jesus**.

Even if a believer's whole life seems dark and cloudy, the final days may be peaceful. And if death itself comes under a cloud, **eternal peace still awaits**.

Salvation belongs to the Lord. He helps his people fulfill their duties, bear their burdens, endure difficulties profitably and, in his time, escape them.

Let sinners therefore turn from evil and do good. Let them repent, abandon sin and trust **God's mercy through Jesus Christ**.

Let them take Christ's yoke and learn from him, so that they may dwell in heaven forever.

Consider the different endings of human lives, and always depend on God's mercy.
