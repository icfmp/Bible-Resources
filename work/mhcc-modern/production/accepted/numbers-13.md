# Numbers 13 — Modern English

## Twelve spies are sent to Canaan (verses 1–20)

This chapter begins the sad account, continued in the next chapter, of Israel turning back at the borders of Canaan.

Because of unbelief and complaining, that generation would be sentenced to wander and die in the wilderness.

According to Deuteronomy 1:22, the proposal to send scouts came from the people.

They trusted their own strategy more than God's wisdom.

This is how we harm ourselves when we trust what our senses report rather than what God has revealed.

We walk by sight rather than by faith.

Moses told the twelve men to be courageous.

Their task was difficult and required good judgment and determination.

But it was also a great responsibility requiring faithfulness.

True courage in such circumstances grows from strong faith.

Only Caleb and Joshua possessed that faith.

## The spies explore the land (verses 21–25)

The scouts brought back a large cluster of grapes and other fruit.

These gave evidence of the land's goodness.

They were a sample and a promise of the harvest Israel could later enjoy in Canaan.

Likewise, the comfort we now receive through fellowship with God gives us a foretaste of the complete joy awaiting us in the heavenly Canaan.

From those present blessings, we gain some idea of what heaven will be like.

## The spies give their report (verses 26–33)

It is astonishing that Israel waited forty days for the scouts' return when the people were already at the border, prepared to enter the land.

They had received every assurance that God's power and promise would bring success.

His earlier miracles should have confirmed that assurance.

Yet they distrusted both him and his word.

How much harm we bring upon ourselves through unbelief!

When the scouts returned, most of them discouraged the people.

Israel had depended upon human judgments when God's own promise was sufficient.

It was therefore just that the people's misplaced confidence brought this severe temptation upon them.

The land was as good as God had said.

But the scouts refused to believe that possessing it was as certain as he had promised.

They looked at military difficulties and despaired of receiving what the God of truth had guaranteed.

Caleb, supported only by Joshua, urged Israel to go forward.

He did not say, “Let us go conquer it,” but **“Let us go possess it.”**

Faith took hold of God's promise instead of relying on human power.

The difficulties in salvation become small when we exercise living, active faith in God's strength and promises.

Whatever God has truly promised is possible for those who believe.

We cannot trust merely worldly reasoning or people who profess faith while depending on sight.

Unbelief forgets God's power, magnifies dangers and fills the heart with fear.

May the Lord help us believe him and discover that his promise is certain.
