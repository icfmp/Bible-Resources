# Romans 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle's commission. | 1–7 |
| Prays for the saints at Rome, and expresses his desire to see them. | 8–15 |
| The gospel way of justification by faith, for Jews and Gentiles. | 16, 17 |
| The sins of the Gentiles set forth. | 18–32 |

## Paul's commission (verses 1–7)

The gospel Paul proclaimed fulfilled the promises previously given through the prophets.

It concerns **Jesus Christ, the Son of God and promised Savior**, descended from David in his human nature and declared to be the Son of God through divine power by his resurrection from the dead.

Professing Christianity does not consist simply in understanding religious ideas or agreeing that certain propositions are true.

Still less does it consist in obstinate disputes.

It consists in **the obedience of faith**.

Only those effectively called by Jesus Christ are brought to this obedience.

Christians have **a privilege**: they are loved by God and belong to the people he loves.

They also have **a duty**: they are called to holiness, to be saints.

Paul greeted them by asking God to give them **grace to sanctify their souls and peace to comfort their hearts**.

Both come from God's free mercy, as the Father reconciled to believers through **the Lord Jesus Christ**.

## Paul's prayers and desire to visit Rome (verses 8–15)

We show love toward fellow believers not only by **praying for them**, but also by **giving thanks to God for them**.

When considering our plans and desires, we should remember to say, **“If the Lord wills” (James 4:15)**.

A journey becomes successful or otherwise according to God's will.

We should gladly share the gifts and opportunities entrusted to us.

We should find joy in making others joyful, particularly in fellowship with people united with us in Christian faith.

If Christ has redeemed us through his blood and converted us by his grace, **we belong entirely to him**.

For his sake, we owe everyone whatever good we are able to do.

This service is not optional generosity beyond duty; **it is our duty**.

## The gospel reveals justification through faith (verses 16 and 17)

These verses introduce the theme of the whole letter.

Paul charges **all humanity with sin**.

He announces **the only way to escape condemnation**: faith in God's mercy through Jesus Christ.

He then builds on that salvation the need for **purity of heart, grateful obedience and continuing growth in Christian grace**, which living faith in Christ alone can produce.

God is holy and just; we are guilty sinners.

To stand before him, we need a righteousness that meets his justice.

Such a righteousness has been brought by **the Messiah** and revealed in the gospel.

Here is a gracious way to be accepted despite our guilt: **the righteousness of Christ, who is God**, arising from a satisfaction of **infinite value**.

Faith is essential not only **at the beginning**, but **throughout** the Christian life.

Paul does **not** say that faith first brings us into a justified state and afterward our works keep us there.

It is **from faith to faith throughout**: faith presses forward and overcomes unbelief.

## All people need the gospel because of sin (verses 18–25)

Paul begins showing that every person needs the gospel's salvation.

No one can obtain God's favor or escape his wrath by personal works.

No human being has perfectly fulfilled every duty toward God and neighbor.

Nor has anyone always lived according to the moral understanding already given.

Sin includes **ungodliness**, offenses against duties owed to God, and **unrighteousness**, offenses against duties owed to others.

Its cause includes **holding back or suppressing the truth through wrongdoing**.

Every person sometimes does what they know is wrong and neglects what they know is right.

Therefore, no one can claim perfect innocence through ignorance.

The Creator's invisible power and deity are displayed through his creation so clearly that idolaters are left **without excuse**.

Yet rational creatures exchanged worship of the glorious Creator for worship of animals, creeping creatures and lifeless images.

They wandered from God so far that true religion would have disappeared without the gospel's revelation.

Whatever claims are made for the sufficiency of unaided human reason to discover God's truth, understand moral duty and direct life properly, the historical facts contradict them.

Human beings have dishonored God with absurd idolatries and superstitions, and degraded themselves through shameful desires and acts.

## Human beings abandon God's truth (verses 26–32)

Human depravity demonstrates Jesus' words: **light came into the world, but people loved darkness more than light because their deeds were evil**.

Divine truth did not please them.

Human beings can reason themselves out of believing what they dislike, even against strong evidence.

But **no bondage is worse than being given over to one's own sinful desires**.

Because the Gentiles rejected the knowledge of God, they committed deeds opposed to reason and to their own welfare.

Human nature remains fallen, whether a person is from a pagan background or claims the Christian name.

Paul's charges apply, in greater or lesser measure, to people in every age until they submit to Christ through faith and are **renewed by divine power**.

Everyone has reason to grieve over inward corruption and secret resistance to God's will.

Therefore, this chapter calls us all to **examine ourselves**, recognize our guilt deeply and understand the necessity of rescue from condemnation.
