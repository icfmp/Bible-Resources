# 2 Samuel 9 — Modern English

## David remembers Jonathan's family (verses 1–8)

Amid many responsibilities, we easily forget the gratitude we owe and the promises we have made—not only to our friends, but to God himself.

Yet people whose faith is genuine cannot remain content until they fulfill these duties.

The people who most deserve practical kindness and charity do not always come to our attention without some effort to find them.

Jonathan had been David's friend, bound by solemn promise.

David therefore sought out Jonathan's son **Mephibosheth** to show him kindness.

God remains faithful to us.

We must not act unfaithfully toward one another.

If Providence lifts us into a better position while our friends or their families suffer poverty or disgrace, we should regard this change as an especially good opportunity to help them.

## David provides for Mephibosheth (verses 9–13)

David pictured Jesus Christ, who is both David's Lord and Son—his **Root and Offspring**.

David's kindness to Mephibosheth therefore reminds us of God's astonishing love toward fallen humanity.

David owed something to Jonathan because of their covenant.

**God, however, was under no such obligation to save sinners.**

The Son of God came looking for a lost and ruined human race.

Human beings were not seeking him.

He came **to seek and to save them**.
