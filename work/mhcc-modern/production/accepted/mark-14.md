## Christ is anointed at Bethany (verses 1–11)

Christ poured out his soul to death for us. Can we regard anything as too precious to give him?

Do we offer him the precious ointment of our finest affections? We should love him with all our hearts, even when others misunderstand or criticize genuine devotion.

Charity toward poor people, though required, does not excuse neglecting particular acts of devotion to Jesus. Christ praised the woman's action and made it known to believers in every generation. Those who honor Christ will be honored by him.

**Covetousness ruled Judas.** The love of money that he had cherished became the occasion for his betrayal of his Master. The devil adapted his temptation to Judas's ruling desire and overcame him through it.

Sinful people often construct elaborate schemes to achieve their purposes. Yet the very circumstances they regard as helping them succeed will become curses to them in the end.

## The Passover and the announcement of betrayal (verses 12–21)

The events surrounding this Passover were not the result of merely human foresight. The Lord knew them before they happened, as he knows everything concerning us.

If we receive him, he will dwell in our hearts.

The Son of Man went toward suffering and death as Scripture had foretold, like a lamb led to slaughter. Nevertheless, the betrayer remained guilty and faced a dreadful judgment.

**God's permission of human sin does not compel anyone to sin.** Nor does God's ability to bring glory out of wicked actions excuse those who freely commit them or reduce their punishment.

## Jesus institutes the Lord's Supper (verses 22–31)

The Lord's Supper is nourishment for the soul. Therefore, only a small quantity of bread and wine is needed for the bodily sign.

Our Master instituted it by his own example and practice. It is to continue until his second coming.

He established it with blessing and thanksgiving, as a remembrance of his death.

The blood of Christ is repeatedly described as **the price of our redemption**. What comfort for repentant sinners that this blood is shed **for many**! If it is for many, why should I not seek its benefits myself?

The Supper signifies the communication of the blessings purchased by Christ's death. We should apply the truth of Christ crucified to ourselves, so that it becomes food and drink for the soul, strengthening and refreshing spiritual life.

It also gives an earnest and a foretaste of the happiness of heaven. Enjoying spiritual blessings should weaken our attachment to pleasures of the senses.

Whoever has tasted spiritual delights is led to desire eternal ones.

Although the great Shepherd endured every suffering without a single false step, his followers were scattered by a much smaller measure of suffering.

We are quick to think highly of our own courage and trust our own hearts. Peter should not have answered his Master with such self-confidence, but with fear and trembling.

Lord, give me grace so that I do not deny you.

## Christ's agony in Gethsemane (verses 32–42)

Christ's suffering began with what was most painful: **the suffering of his soul**.

He became deeply amazed and distressed. Mark's words here express his anguish even more forcefully than Matthew's account.

The terrors of God stood before him, and he allowed himself to contemplate them. No sorrow had ever been like his.

He was now **made a curse for us**. The curses of the law were laid upon him as our Surety. He tasted death in all its bitterness.

His fear was the natural human fear of pain and death, from which human nature recoils.

Can we think lightly of sin when we see what our sins—even though they were only reckoned to him—brought upon Jesus? How can sin weigh so little upon us when it weighed so heavily upon him?

As we look upon the One we pierced, we should mourn over our sin rather than treat it as a small matter.

As **man**, Christ prayed that, if possible, the suffering might pass from him. As **Mediator**, he submitted himself completely to the Father's will: not what I will, but what you will.

The disciples' weakness returned and overcame them. Our bodies can weigh heavily upon our souls.

Yet when trouble approaches, we should prepare for it. Even believers can look upon the Redeemer's suffering drowsily: instead of being ready to die with him, they are sometimes unwilling to watch with him for one hour.

## Jesus is betrayed and arrested (verses 43–52)

The religious rulers sought to destroy Jesus because he did not appear as an earthly prince. Instead, he preached repentance, reform and holy living and directed people's thoughts, desires and hopes toward the world to come.

Peter wounded one of those who came to arrest Jesus.

**It is easier to fight for Christ than to die for him.**

Yet there is an important distinction between disciples who fail through weakness and hypocrites. Hypocrites may rashly call Jesus their Master and profess great affection, while delivering him into the hands of his enemies.

By their betrayal, they hurry toward their own destruction.

## Jesus stands before the high priest (verses 53–65)

Jesus was condemned before the great Jewish council.

Peter followed, but the fire beside the high priest and the company of the high priest's servants were dangerous places for him. He had entered a temptation.

The council sought false witnesses diligently. Yet even by stretching their law as far as possible, their testimony was insufficient to establish a capital charge.

Jesus was asked whether he was **the Son of the Blessed**, meaning the Son of God.

He pointed to **his second coming** as proof of his identity.

The insults and violence directed against him reveal both humanity's hostility toward God and God's freely given, unspeakable love toward humanity.

## Peter denies Jesus (verses 66–72)

Peter's denial began when he kept his distance from Christ.

Those ashamed of godliness are already on the path toward denying him.

People who fear spending time among Christ's followers because they might be drawn into suffering with them take a worse risk by spending time with his enemies: there they may be drawn into sin against him.

When crowds admired Jesus, Peter readily acknowledged him. Now, when Jesus was deserted and despised, Peter refused any association with him.

Yet Peter repented **very quickly**.

Let anyone who thinks he stands be careful not to fall.

And let anyone who has fallen remember Peter, consider their own sins, and return to the Lord with tears and earnest prayer, seeking forgiveness and renewal through the Holy Spirit.
