# Jeremiah 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The disobedient Jews reproved. | 1–10 |
| Their utter ruin. | 11–17 |
| The people would be destroyed who sought the prophet's life. | 18–23 |

## God requires obedience to his covenant (verses 1–10)

God has never promised to bless rational creatures **while they deliberately continue in disobedience**.

Pardon and acceptance are freely promised to everyone who believes.

But no one can be saved while refusing God's command **to repent, believe in Christ, separate from sin and the world, deny self and walk in newness of life**.

People readily listen when speakers describe **doctrines, promises and privileges**.

Yet when the subject turns to **duty**, they often refuse to hear.

## Idolatry cannot protect a nation from judgment (verses 11–17)

Evil pursues sinners and entangles them in **traps from which they cannot free themselves**.

In their distress, their many gods and altars provided no help.

People whose own prayers will not be accepted cannot presume to benefit from **someone else's prayers**.

A mere profession of religion does no good.

When trouble came, the people had relied on that profession, but God rejected it.

His altar could give them no comfort.

Remembering God's earlier favors toward them would not console them amid judgment.

Nor could those favors be used as a reason why he must rescue them now.

Every sin committed against the Lord is also **a sin against ourselves**.

Sooner or later its consequences will prove that true.

## Jeremiah is threatened by people from his own town (verses 18–23)

Jeremiah's book reveals much about his personal suffering.

He lived in deeply troubled times.

Men from **his own town** secretly planned his death.

They hoped to shorten his life.

But Jeremiah outlived **most of his enemies**.

They also wanted to destroy his reputation.

Yet his memory has survived until today, and it will continue to be honored as long as time endures.

God knows **every secret plan** of those who oppose his servants and his people.

When he chooses, he can disclose those plans.

God's justice is a source of terror to wicked people but a comfort to those who trust him.

When others wrong us, we have **a God to whom we can entrust our cause**.

We should commit the matter to him.

And we must watch our own attitudes, lest evil overcome us.

Through **patient prayer for our enemies and kindness toward them**, we can overcome evil with good.
