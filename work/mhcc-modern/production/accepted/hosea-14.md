# Hosea 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An exhortation to repentance. | 1–3 |
| Blessings promised, showing the rich comforts of the gospel. | 4–8 |
| The just and the wicked. | 9 |

## Return to the Lord with words of repentance (verses 1–3)

Israel was urged to turn from sins and idols and return to **Jehovah**.

The people were to come through **faith in God's mercy and grace through the promised Redeemer** and through careful attention to worship and service.

**Take away our wrongdoing**, they prayed.

Remove it as **a heavy burden** under which we are sinking, or as **a stumbling block** over which we repeatedly fall.

Take **all of it** away through a free and complete pardon, because **we cannot remove it ourselves**.

**Receive our prayer graciously**.

The people did not prescribe exactly which earthly benefit they desired.

They entrusted the outcome to God.

What they needed was not **the good the world could show**, but **the good God could give**.

They had to consider their sins, their needs and the remedy.

Instead of bringing sacrifices as the substance of repentance, they were to bring **words that expressed their hearts' true desires**.

This clearly portrays **a sinner's conversion to God through Jesus Christ**.

When approaching God in prayer and faith, we should first ask him to **teach us what to pray for**.

We must earnestly plead that he **remove all our sins**.

## God promises healing, love and fruitfulness (verses 4–8)

Israel was invited to seek God's favor and would not seek it in vain.

He would turn away his anger.

When God loves, **he loves freely**.

His love does not arise from people's merit but **from his own gracious pleasure**.

God will be for his people **everything they need**.

The gifts of his Spirit are like **hidden manna** or **dew**.

Grace freely given will not be wasted.

His people will **grow upward**, becoming beautiful like **a lily** (**Matthew 6:28–29**).

They will also **grow downward**, becoming deeply rooted like **the cedars of Lebanon**.

Much of genuine spiritual growth occurs **in the hidden roots**, where it is unseen.

They will also spread **like a vine**, with branches reaching outward.

When believers abound in **good works**, their branches extend and help others.

They will be pleasing both to God and to other people.

**Holiness is the soul's beauty**.

The church resembles **a vine and an olive tree** that bear useful fruit.

God's promises belong to people who **remain under the care of his appointed ordinances**, not those who flee briefly into their shade merely to escape a passing hardship.

When a person comes to God, others dwelling under that person's influence **benefit too**.

The fruits of holiness will become visible in life.

Thus believers grow in the experience and fruitfulness of the gospel.

Ephraim will say, **“What have I to do with idols anymore?”**

God himself puts that resolve into the person's heart.

**God's promises are a stronger foundation for putting sin to death than our promises to him**.

Divine grace changes the heart so deeply that former idols become **as hateful as they once were beloved**.

Even suffering can be sanctified for that purpose.

Ephraim suffered because of idolatry, and the intended fruit was **the removal of sin** (**Isaiah 27:9**).

True repentance means **a firm determination to have nothing more to do with sin**.

The Lord receives repentant people mercifully, as **the father welcomed his returning prodigal son**.

God becomes **their delight and defense**.

They can rest happily in his shade.

The Lord himself supplies **the root from which all good fruit grows**.

From him come the grace and strength needed for duty.

## The righteous walk in God's ways (verse 9)

Who benefits from the prophet's teaching?

Those who earnestly try **to understand and know** these truths.

God's providential ways are right: **he does everything well**.

Christ is **a foundation stone** to some.

To others, he becomes **a stumbling stone and rock of offense**.

Something appointed to bring life can, when abused through unbelief, lead instead to death.

**The same sun softens wax and hardens clay**.

Those who turn away even while walking among God's ordinances are in great danger.

They stumble over **the Rock of Ages** and draw poison from **the Balm of Gilead**.

Let sinners in Zion take warning.

May we learn to walk in the Lord's **right ways** as faithful servants, and may none of us stumble at his word through **disobedience and unbelief**.
