## Trust the Lord rather than your own understanding (verses 1–6)

The life of believing obedience generally brings peace and health.

Even if it does not lengthen our earthly days, **we will live forever in heaven**.

Hold tightly to **mercy and truth**: God's mercy in making promises and his faithfulness in keeping them.

Walk consistently with both, treasure them and receive their comfort.

**Trust in the Lord with your whole heart**, believing that he is willing, wise and able to do what is best.

Those who know themselves find their own understanding **a broken reed** that cannot bear their weight.

Do not make plans that violate God's law.

Ask him to direct you even when a decision seems obvious.

In pleasant and successful circumstances, **acknowledge him with thanks**.

When a road is difficult and hedged in by thorns, **acknowledge him with submission**.

God will direct your paths, making them safe and ultimately good.

## Honoring God and learning from fatherly correction (verses 7–12)

Few things are more destructive to reverence for God than **conceit about our own wisdom**.

The prudence and moderation religion teaches benefit both **soul and body**.

Earthly wealth is poor and perishable, but even that should be used to **honor God**.

Those who do good with what they have can be entrusted with more opportunities to do good.

When the Lord sends sickness and hardship, remember that he addresses us **as his children**.

Do not lose courage under a prolonged trial, surrender to despair or use sinful methods to seek relief.

A loving father corrects a child **because he loves the child** and wants that child to become wise and good.

Through grace, affliction may actually **increase the holiness** of God's people.

## Wisdom more valuable than treasure (verses 13–20)

No jewel or earthly treasure compares with true wisdom, whether we consider this life or eternity.

Make obtaining wisdom your serious pursuit, willing to give up everything for it.

**This Wisdom is Jesus Christ and the salvation he gives**, received through faith and prayer.

Our unbelief, remaining sin and carelessness often lead us away from his pleasant paths, causing our own sorrow.

Christ is the Wisdom **through whom the worlds were created and by whom they continue to exist**.

Happy are those to whom God has made him their wisdom.

He possesses everything needed to fulfill his promises.

## Wisdom guards our steps (verses 21–26)

Do not let Christ's words slip away from your attention.

Keep sound wisdom and discretion.

When we follow the Lord, **his providence** guards our natural life and **his grace** preserves our spiritual life.

He can keep us from falling into sin or being crushed by trouble.

## Acting justly and avoiding unnecessary conflict (verses 27–35)

Our duty is to obey Christ's commands and follow his example.

**Act justly, love mercy and beware of greed**.

Be ready for every good work.

Avoid quarrels that serve no purpose.

When possible, it is better to bear a wrong patiently than to engage in pointless legal disputes.

Little lasting good comes from needless fighting.

Do not envy oppressive people who enjoy prosperity.

A disciple of Christ should not imitate their methods.

Greedy and self-indulgent people may mock such teaching, but their final reward is dishonor.

**God favors the humble believer**.
