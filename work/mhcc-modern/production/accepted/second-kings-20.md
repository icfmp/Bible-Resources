# 2 Kings 20 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Hezekiah's sickness, His recovery in answer to prayer. | 1–11 |
| Hezekiah shows his treasures to the ambassadors from Babylon, His death. | 12–21 |

## Hezekiah becomes ill and recovers (verses 1–11)

Hezekiah became gravely ill, close to death, in the same year that the king of Assyria besieged Jerusalem.

Isaiah brought him a warning to prepare for death.

Prayer is one of the best ways to prepare for death.

Through prayer we receive grace and strength from God to finish life well.

Hezekiah wept bitterly.

Some understand this as an unwillingness to die, and it is natural for human beings to dread the separation of body and soul.

But Hezekiah's circumstances were special.

He was in the midst of useful service.

His prayer, recorded also in **Isaiah 38**, helps us understand his tears.

Nothing in it suggests that he was tormented by the enslaving fear of death.

His devotion to God made his sickbed easier to bear.

He prayed, **“Lord, remember me.”**

He did not imply that God needed reminding, or that he could demand a reward as something owed to him.

Only **Christ's righteousness** has purchased mercy and grace.

Hezekiah did not simply pray, **“Spare my life.”**

He prayed that God would remember him.

Whether he lived or died, he wanted to belong to the Lord.

God always hears the prayers of the brokenhearted.

He grants health, longer life and earthly deliverance for as long as those gifts are truly good for his people.

Hezekiah was instructed to use means of recovery.

Yet his disease was so advanced, and the change so sudden, that his healing was miraculous.

When ill, we should use appropriate treatments that support the body's natural healing.

Refusing such help does not display faith in God; it **tests** him.

To confirm Hezekiah's faith, God caused the sun's shadow to move backward, miraculously lengthening the period of light.

The sign showed the Lord's power in heaven as well as on earth, his attention to prayer and his kindness toward those he has chosen.

## Hezekiah displays his wealth (verses 12–21)

At that time the king of Babylon was independent of Assyria, though he would soon be conquered by it.

Hezekiah proudly showed the visitors his treasures, weapons and other evidence of wealth and power.

This revealed pride, love of display and a departure from simple trust in God.

It also seems he missed an opportunity to tell the Babylonian visitors about the Lord who had performed the miracles that interested them and to explain why idolatry was foolish and wrong.

Showing visitors our homes and possessions is not wrong in itself.

But when we display them because we want praise for ourselves while withholding glory from God, it becomes sin, as it did for Hezekiah.

We may expect trouble from anything in which we take excessive pride.

Isaiah had often comforted Hezekiah.

Now he came to correct him.

The blessed Spirit is likewise **both Comforter and Reprover** (John 16:7–8).

Ministers must fulfill both roles when needed.

Hezekiah acknowledged the justice of God's sentence and his goodness in delaying it.

Yet the announcement of what would happen to his family and nation must have been painful.

Hezekiah had truly been humbled for his pride.

Blessed are those who die in the Lord: they rest from their work, and their deeds follow them.
