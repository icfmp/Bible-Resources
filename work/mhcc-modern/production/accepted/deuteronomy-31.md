# Deuteronomy 31 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Moses encourages the people, and Joshua. | 1–8 |
| The law to be read every seventh year. | 9–13 |
| The Israelites' apostacy foretold, A song given to be witness against them. | 14–22 |
| The law delivered to the Levites. | 22–30 |

## Moses encourages Israel and Joshua (verses 1–8)

Moses assured Israel that God would continue to be present with the people.

The apostle applies this promise to God's spiritual Israel, giving every believer strong reason for faith and hope.

The gospel is preached to us as well: the Lord will never fail or abandon his people (Hebrews 13:5).

Moses presented Joshua as Israel's next leader.

The people already knew his wisdom, courage and affection.

God himself had appointed Joshua and would acknowledge and bless his leadership.

Joshua gladly received Moses' instruction to be strong and courageous.

Anyone who has God on their side should be bold.

Through him we can act bravely and overcome.

When we resist the devil, he will flee from us.

## Reading the law aloud every seventh year (verses 9–13)

We should read God's word privately, but we must not think it unnecessary to hear it read publicly.

The law was to be read solemnly during the year of release.

That year pictured the grace of the gospel, the acceptable year of the Lord.

Forgiveness and freedom through Christ give us powerful reasons to obey his commands.

The law was to be read to **all** Israel: men, women, children and foreigners.

Everyone should become familiar with God's word, because its commands apply to everyone.

Those who remember how hard people once worked to obtain even fragments of Scripture should feel grateful that whole copies are so widely available today.

For many centuries, Israelites had lived under very different conditions.

Yet human hearts are naturally so careless that even abundant access to Scripture may not be enough to preserve the knowledge of God's truths, commands and worship.

## Israel's future rebellion is foretold (verses 14–22)

Moses and Joshua stood before the divine Majesty at the entrance of the tabernacle.

The Lord reminded Moses that he would soon die.

Even people who are prepared and willing to die may benefit from being reminded of its approach.

God also foretold that Israel would break the covenant Moses had worked so hard to establish.

The people would abandon the Lord.

Then, in righteous judgment, he would abandon them.

Moses was instructed to give the people a song that would remain as a permanent witness.

It would testify **for God**, showing he had faithfully warned them, and **against Israel**, showing the people had neglected the warning.

God's word exposes the thoughts and intentions of the human heart.

Ministers who preach it cannot know all their listeners' thoughts.

But the God whose word they proclaim understands those thoughts perfectly.

## The book of the law is entrusted to the Levites (verses 23–30)

Moses solemnly handed over the book of the law to the Levites.

It was to be placed in—or, more precisely, **beside**—the ark.

The song recorded in the next chapter was given to Moses and then to Israel.

He first wrote it as the Holy Spirit instructed him, then spoke it aloud before the people.

Moses warned that he knew they would become corrupt after his death.

That prospect must have caused him deep sorrow.

Yet he could take comfort in knowing that he had performed his duty.

God's glory would not fail, whether revealed through Israel's settlement in the promised land or through its dispersion.

The foundation of God's work remains secure.
