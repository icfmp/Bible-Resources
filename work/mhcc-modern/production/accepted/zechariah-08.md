# Zechariah 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The restoration of Jerusalem. | 1–8 |
| The people encouraged by promises of God's favour, and exhorted to holiness. | 9–17 |
| The Jews in the latter days. | 18–23 |

## Jerusalem restored as a city of truth (verses 1–8)

Zion's worst enemies were **her own sins**.

God would remove those sins; afterward, no other enemy could finally harm her.

Those professing religion must honor that profession through **honesty and godliness**.

A city of truth and a mountain of holiness will become a place of peace and flourishing.

The vision of **elderly people and children filling the streets together** beautifully describes outward peace accompanied by abundance, moderation and contentment.

Scattered Israelites would be gathered from every direction.

God would not forsake them in the way of mercy, according to his promise.

They would not forsake him in duty, according to theirs.

Henry considers the promises **partly fulfilled** among the Jews between the return from captivity and Christ's coming.

He sees a fuller accomplishment in the **gospel church**, while saying the prophecy's complete meaning concerns either **future periods of the Christian church or the future restoration of the Jews**.

These are **alternative possibilities in Henry's interpretation**, not one verified sequence.

What seems impossible to humanity is possible with God, whose thoughts and ways are far above ours.

When true godliness is weak, we struggle to imagine so profound a spiritual transformation.

Yet the almighty **recreating Spirit** could accomplish it in even less time than God used to create the world.

Let everyone working for the gospel take courage and serve in genuine holiness.

Their work will not be wasted.

## Promises strengthen people who obey (verses 9–17)

Those who put their hands to the plow of duty receive strength from God's promises of mercy.

Those who turn away from their fathers' sins may find that a curse has become a blessing.

Believers should show that they trust God's promises **through their actions**, then patiently await fulfillment.

When God is displeased, commerce may decline and people may turn against their neighbors.

When he returns in mercy, happiness and prosperity can return.

People who believe in Christ must take seriously the commands to **stop lying**, **speak truth and peace to neighbors**, **hate what God hates** and **love what pleases him**.

## Fasts become joy; the nations seek God (verses 18–23)

When God comes toward us in mercy, we should meet him with thankfulness and joy.

Be faithful and honest in all your dealings.

**Enjoy doing what is right**, even if dishonesty would bring greater financial gain.

As far as it lies in your power, live peaceably with everyone.

Let God's **truth govern your mind** and his **peace govern your heart**.

In this way the conduct of God's earlier servants attracted the notice of people in other nations and softened their prejudices.

The prophecy announced great growth for the church.

The Jews had often adopted the idols of surrounding nations.

How astonishing that they should instead **teach genuine religion even to their conquerors and to the principal nations of the world**!

Yet this was foretold and, in Henry's judgment, already wonderfully fulfilled in part.

He expected future events to explain it still more fully.
