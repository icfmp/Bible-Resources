# Psalm 45 — Modern English

## Original heading

**This psalm is a prophecy of Messiah the Prince, and points to him as a Bridegroom espousing the church to himself, and as a King ruling in it, and for it.**

## The Messiah's beauty and victory (verses 1–5)

God's Spirit guided the psalmist's tongue as a skilled writer's hand guides a pen.

This psalm concerns **King Jesus, his kingdom and government**.

It is a shame we speak so little about him when there is more in Christ to engage our love than in any created thing.

This world's attractions continually draw our hearts away from him. We should therefore learn how far he exceeds everything else in worthiness.

Through his word, promises and gospel, God's goodwill becomes known and his gracious work begins and grows within us.

In **verses 3–5**, the psalmist joyfully foretells the Messiah's advancing kingdom.

The arrows of conviction trouble sinners until they are humbled and reconciled; the arrows of vengeance bring still more terrible judgment upon enemies who refuse to submit.

Those who have seen Christ's glory and tasted his grace rejoice when his **word and Spirit** bring former enemies and strangers under his rule.

## The eternal King and his bride (verses 6–9)

The throne of this almighty King stands **forever**.

The Holy Spirit leads Christ's people to his cross, where they see both **the evil of sin** and **the beauty of holiness**.

None can rightly use Christ's work as encouragement to remain in sin.

**The Mediator is God**. Otherwise he would neither have been able to accomplish his work nor fit to wear the Mediator's crown.

The Father, as Christ's God with respect to his **human nature and mediatorial office**, gave him the Holy Spirit **without measure**.

Anointed as **Prophet, Priest and King**, Christ possesses the Spirit's gifts and graces in fullness and shares them with his human brothers and sisters.

The Spirit is called the **oil of gladness** because Christ rejoiced in accomplishing salvation.

The angels rejoice when sinners are saved; how much more does the Son himself!

As believers are conformed to Christ's holy image, they experience the Comforter's joyful influence.

The fragrance of Christ's garments pictures his excellences, the suitability of his offices and the sufficiency of his grace.

His church, composed of true believers, is compared to a queen, **betrothed to Jesus by an everlasting covenant**.

She is **the Lamb's bride**.

Her purity resembles fine linen and her costly honor resembles gold. Both her redemption and her beauty depend on **the Son of God's precious blood**.

## The bride's holiness and the nations' hope (verses 10–17)

To share these blessings, we must listen to Christ's word and abandon sinful attachments and worldly pursuits.

He must be **our Lord as well as our Savior**.

Every idol must be cast away so that we can give him our whole heart.

This renunciation has a rich reward: Christ values the beauty of holiness in his church and every believer.

Grace is the Spirit's own workmanship, forming **Christ's image in the soul** and giving a share in divine holiness.

There is no sin in that work of grace itself; the old corrupt nature has no true glory.

The Father's gift of **Christ's righteousness** clothes his church.

No one comes to Christ unless brought by the Father.

The clothing and entrance of the bride picture **conversion** and the garments of salvation Christ gives his people.

Those who love him sincerely and remain close to him share the bride's grace, privileges and salvation.

They will **all be brought to the King; not one will be forgotten**.

The New Testament church, including Gentile believers, will succeed the Old Testament church in this prophetic picture.

As we look forward to eternal happiness, let us remember **Christ as the only way** and teach his name to generations still to come, so that his praise endures forever.
