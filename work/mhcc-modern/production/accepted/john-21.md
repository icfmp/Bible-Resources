# John 21 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Jesus appears beside the sea (verses 1–14)

Christ usually reveals himself to his people through his appointed ordinances.

But he may also visit them by his Spirit **while they are engaged in ordinary work**.

Disciples benefit from being together in common conversation and everyday labor.

The time for their new public work had not yet arrived. Meanwhile they could earn their own support and avoid becoming a burden to others.

Christ often reveals himself **when his followers are most at a loss**.

He knows their material needs, promising not only sufficient grace but also what is necessary for daily provision.

God's Providence extends to the smallest details. Happy are those who acknowledge him in everything.

Humble, diligent and patient workers may encounter failure after failure, yet their efforts are not wasted. Sometimes they live to see a happy change after many struggles.

Obeying Christ's instructions is never a loss. It is like **casting the net on the right side of the boat**.

Jesus demonstrates his care by providing what no one else could supply, often in unexpected ways.

He would see to it that those who had abandoned everything to follow him lacked no truly good thing.

Later mercies should also remind us of earlier ones, so that we do not forget the bread already received from his hand.

The disciple whom Jesus especially loved was the first to recognize him: **“It is the Lord!”**

John had remained near Jesus during the sufferings and now recognized him first.

Peter, known for zeal, was the first to reach Jesus.

God gives different gifts and different ways of honoring Christ. Yet all faithful servants can be acceptable to him.

The others stayed in the boat, pulled the net and brought the catch to shore.

They should not be called worldly: **their diligent work served Christ just as truly**.

Jesus had a meal prepared for the disciples.

We need not speculate about precisely where it came from; the important lesson is his loving care.

Although the fish were numerous and large, none was lost and the net remained intact.

The **net of the gospel** has gathered multitudes, yet it remains strong enough to bring more souls to God.

## Jesus restores Peter and commands him to shepherd (verses 15–19)

Jesus addressed Peter by his former name, as if his denials had deprived him of the name Peter.

When Peter replied, he appealed to Jesus' knowledge of his love, but **did not claim to love him more than the other disciples**.

If our conduct has made our sincerity doubtful, we should not be surprised when others question it.

Remembering former sins—even forgiven sins—renews the sorrow of a genuinely repentant person.

Yet Peter, conscious of his sincere love, appealed solemnly to Christ, who knows every secret of the heart.

Our failures ought to make us more **humble and watchful**.

The sincerity of our love for God must be tested.

We should repeatedly ask the Lord, who searches hearts, to examine whether our love is genuine.

No one is qualified to feed **Christ's lambs and sheep** who does not love the Good Shepherd more than worldly advantage or any earthly object.

Whatever manner of death God appoints, a godly person's concern should be **to glorify him in dying**, just as in living.

Our final purpose is to belong to the Lord and die at his command.

## Jesus speaks about John's future (verses 20–24)

Pain, suffering and death can frighten even an experienced believer.

But the hope of glorifying God, leaving a sinful world and being present with Christ can make us willing to follow him through death into glory.

Jesus calls each disciple to **attend to personal duty**, not become preoccupied with the hidden future of oneself or others.

Many things we worry about are really **not our business**.

Let us work quietly and fulfill our own calling instead of meddling in other people's affairs.

Some curious questions concern God's hidden plans or the unseen world. We need not pretend to answer them.

If we concentrate on following Jesus, we will have little desire or time to intrude into what does not belong to us.

This passage also shows how unreliable **unwritten traditions** can be.

Let **Scripture explain Scripture**, for it is its own light and, to a large extent, its own evidence.

Christ's exact words easily corrected the mistaken rumor about John.

The language of Scripture is the safest expression of biblical truth—the words taught by the Holy Spirit (**1 Corinthians 2:13**).

Believers who disagree about technical theological language and its uses may still unite in Scripture's own words **and love one another**.

## The Gospel's final sentence (verse 25)

Only a small part of everything Jesus did has been recorded.

We should thank God for what he has given in Scripture: **so much truth in comparatively little space**.

Enough has been written to guide our faith and govern our conduct.

More was not needed for that purpose.

Even as it is, much of the Bible is neglected, forgotten or made the subject of fruitless disputes.

In heaven we may look forward to discovering more completely all that Christ did and said, and the full story of his providence and grace in our own lives.

May that blessed knowledge be ours.

These things are written so that we may **believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in his name** (**John 20:31**).
