# Psalm 21 — Modern English

## Giving thanks for victory (verses 1–6)

Happy are the people whose ruler places confidence in **God's strength** and finds joy in **God's salvation**.

Such a king takes pleasure in the advance of God's kingdom and trusts the Lord to sustain every effort made in its service.

All our blessings are gifts of **God's goodness**, not wages earned by our own worthiness.

Sometimes those blessings arrive **earlier than expected**, are **greater than we imagined**, or come **before we even asked**.

Sometimes God gives precisely what we had feared we would not receive.

In these circumstances we can say that he **went ahead of us with his blessings**.

Nothing can come before Christ himself.

But for humanity, **no blessing has ever more wonderfully come before our request than redemption through Christ**.

The Father has made the Son a universal and everlasting blessing to the world.

Through him the families of the earth have been and will be blessed.

Christ finds joy in the Father's favor toward him and toward the work he has accomplished.

Henry sees the Spirit of prophecy moving here beyond what can be said of an earthly ruler into what belongs to **Jesus Christ alone**.

No merely human king is **blessed forever**, much less a blessing for the whole world forever.

## Confidence in the Messiah's ultimate victory (verses 7–13)

The psalmist teaches us to look forward through **faith, hope and prayer**, considering what God will yet accomplish.

David's victories foreshadow the complete defeat of Christ's enemies.

Some people could have received Christ as the One who would **rule and save them**.

But they rejected him and fought against him.

If they continue in rebellion, the memory of that refusal will become a torment that does not end.

God works in sinners through his grace, making them **willing to come**, receiving them into his favor and rescuing them from coming judgment.

May he exalt himself in our hearts by **almighty grace**, destroying every stronghold of **sin and Satan**.

How great should our joy be when we see **our Brother and Friend upon his throne** and think of every blessing we may receive from him!

Yet Christ himself delights in his exaltation because he can now bestow **happiness and glory upon sinners** who have learned to love and trust him.
