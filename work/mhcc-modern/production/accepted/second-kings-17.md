# 2 Kings 17 — Modern English

## Hoshea and Israel's exile (verses 1–6)

When sin reaches its full measure, the Lord no longer delays judgment.

Samaria's inhabitants must have suffered terribly.

Some poor Israelites remained in the land.

Most of those carried into distant exile were absorbed among the other nations.

## Why Israel was taken captive (verses 7–23)

The destruction of Israel's northern kingdom is reported briefly, but these verses explain its causes in detail.

The ruin came from the Almighty.

**Assyria was only the rod of his anger** (**Isaiah 10:5**).

Those who bring sin into a country or household bring disaster with it and must answer for the suffering that follows.

The world's outward acts of wrongdoing are enormous.

Yet even greater are the hidden sins of the human heart—evil thoughts, desires and intentions.

Some public sins carry obvious disgrace.

But ingratitude toward God, neglect of him, hostility to him, idolatry and the ungodliness arising from them are even more deeply malignant.

No one possesses genuine godliness without turning from every evil path and keeping God's commands.

But such obedience must grow out of **faith in God's testimony** concerning his anger against all unrighteousness and his mercy in Jesus Christ.

## The peoples settled in Israel (verses 24–41)

The terror of divine judgment can produce forced or feigned submission in people whose hearts remain unconverted.

This happened among the foreigners brought to settle in Israel.

Such people hold unworthy views of God.

They expect outward religious forms to satisfy him.

They also attempt the impossible task of combining his service with love of the world and indulgence of sinful desires.

May the fear of the Lord, which begins true wisdom, take hold of our hearts and direct our conduct.

Then we will be prepared for whatever change life brings.

Earthly homes and settlements are uncertain.

We do not know where we may be driven before death, and in any case we must soon leave this world.

But the righteous have chosen the good portion that will never be taken away.
