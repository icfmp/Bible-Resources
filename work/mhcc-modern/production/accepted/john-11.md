# John 11 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The sickness of Lazarus. | 1–6 |
| Christ returns to Judea. | 7–10 |
| The death of Lazarus. | 11–16 |
| Christ arrives at Bethany. | 17–32 |
| He raises Lazarus. | 33–46 |
| The Pharisees consult against Jesus. | 47–53 |
| The Jews seek for him. | 54–57 |

## Lazarus becomes ill (verses 1–6)

Those whom Christ loves may still become ill. Bodily afflictions can correct corruption and test the graces of believers.

Jesus did not come to protect his people from every sickness or trouble. He came to save them from **sin and the wrath to come**.

Nevertheless, we ought to bring sick or afflicted relatives and friends to him in prayer.

God's darkest dealings serve his glory, even when they involve sickness, loss or disappointment. When God is glorified, we may rest in his wisdom.

Jesus loved Martha, Mary and Lazarus. A family enjoying affection and peace is blessed; the happiest of all are families whose members **love Jesus and are loved by him**.

Even in small households, how sadly often that is not true of everyone.

God's purposes remain gracious even when help appears delayed. Deliverance for an individual, household, church or nation comes **at the right time**.

## Jesus prepares to return to Judea (verses 7–10)

Christ never brings his people into danger without going with them.

We sometimes imagine we are defending God's cause when we are mainly defending **our reputation, comfort, property or safety**. We must examine our motives.

God preserves our earthly day until the work appointed to us has been finished and our testimony completed.

Someone walking in the way of duty, guided by **God's word and providence**, may enjoy peace and confidence.

Jesus always walked in the light of his Father's will.

Those who follow their own desires, worldly customs and merely fleshly calculations rather than God's honor stumble into temptation.

Divine light guides **moral action** as daylight guides our physical steps.

## Jesus speaks about Lazarus's death (verses 11–16)

The promised resurrection to eternal life can make the believer's death seem like putting aside one's clothes and going to sleep.

For the Christian, death is rest after a day's labor. In one respect it is better than ordinary sleep, because it ends **all earthly cares and work**.

The disciples hoped Jesus would not need to return to danger.

We often wish someone else could perform the good work we have been called to do when the duty exposes us to risk.

But Lazarus's resurrection would strengthen existing believers and bring many others to faith.

Let us go to Christ. **Death cannot separate us from his love or remove us beyond the reach of his call**.

Like Thomas, we should encourage one another in difficult times. Our Savior's willingness to die should make us willing to die when God calls us.

## Jesus reaches the grieving family (verses 17–32)

A household may fear God and enjoy his blessing and still become a house of mourning. Grace does not prevent every sorrow from visiting our homes.

When God approaches us in mercy, we should go out to meet him through **faith, hope and prayer**, as Martha did.

Mary remained seated in the house. Her reflective disposition had previously drawn her to Jesus' feet to hear his teaching, but in this sorrow the same inclination contributed to sadness.

We should make wise use of our natural dispositions while guarding against their particular temptations.

When we do not know precisely what to request, leave the matter in God's hands.

Jesus declared himself **the Resurrection and the Life**. He is resurrection's source, substance, firstfruits and cause.

The redeemed soul lives happily after death. After the resurrection, **both body and soul** are delivered from evil forever.

Having heard Christ's word about eternity, each of us should ask: **Do I really believe this?**

If we believed eternal realities as we ought, today's suffering and comfort would not dominate our feelings so completely.

Christ calls us through his word and ordinances. Those who sat at his feet to be taught in peaceful days may fall at those same feet confidently for mercy during trouble.

## Jesus raises Lazarus (verses 33–46)

Jesus' distress, compassionate questions and tears revealed the tenderness of his heart.

He truly shared the grief of his friends. The Savior was **a man of sorrows, acquainted with grief**.

His tears give us an example of genuine compassion. But he did not praise sentimental people who weep over stories yet remain hard toward real suffering.

Instead of seeking only noisy amusements, let us make time to comfort the afflicted.

We have a High Priest who **feels our weaknesses**.

Before a soul receives spiritual life, prejudices and barriers often need to be removed, just as the stone was taken from Lazarus's tomb to make room for Christ's word.

If we trust Christ's promise and power, we will see God's glory.

Jesus taught us to address God as **Father** with both reverence and bold confidence. His public prayer showed the crowd that the Father had sent him as his beloved Son.

Jesus could have raised Lazarus silently by his own will and the Spirit's unseen life-giving power. Instead, he **called aloud**.

That call pictured both the gospel bringing spiritually dead sinners out of their graves and the final trumpet summoning the dead to judgment.

The grave of sin and the world is no fitting place for those Christ has made alive. **They must come out**.

Lazarus returned not merely to life but to **health**.

A sinner cannot give life to the soul, yet must use God's appointed means of grace. A believer cannot sanctify the self apart from God, yet must put aside every hindrance.

We cannot convert our relatives and friends, but we **can instruct, warn and invite them**.

## The leaders plot against Jesus (verses 47–53)

This plot shows the desperate hostility toward God that can exist in the human heart.

Speaking prophetic words does **not** prove that a person has saving grace within.

The disaster people seek to escape through sin may be the very disaster their sin brings upon them.

So it is with people who oppose Christ's kingdom to protect their own interests.

Converting sinners means gathering them to **Christ as their Ruler and Refuge**.

He died to accomplish this, purchasing his people for himself and securing the **gift of the Holy Spirit** for them.

His love in dying for believers ought to unite them closely to one another.

## Jesus withdraws before the Passover (verses 54–57)

Before celebrating the gospel Passover we should renew repentance and prepare seriously to meet God.

Among the Jews, some particularly devout worshipers went to Jerusalem early for purification before the feast.

Human schemes cannot change God's purposes.

While hypocrites become absorbed in forms and arguments, and worldly people pursue their plans, **Jesus continues directing all things toward his glory and his people's salvation**.

## Editorial review — John 11

**Complete secondary original-English witness directly read:** [CCEL, John 11](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xxxv.xi.html). Published `MHCC 0.1.1` is authoritative for original-native SWORD/annotation alignment, independent review and compiled readback.

- **1–6:** loved believers still sick, prayer for sick friends, gracious **delays** and Christ-loving households.
- **7–10:** Christ goes with disciples into danger, false zeal for **safety/comfort**, light of word and providence.
- **11–16:** resurrection gives rest, disciples avoiding danger, Thomas' courage, death does not separate from Christ.
- **17–32:** godly homes still mourn, Martha and Mary temperament contrast, **Christ both Resurrection and Life**, soul after death and body at resurrection.
- **33–46:** **real vs sentimental compassion**, barriers removed, Christ's **public filial prayer**, shouted call as **regeneration and final resurrection**, no human power to convert though humans must warn and instruct.
- **47–53:** prophecy **without necessarily saving grace**, self-defeating sin, Christ's death gathering people and securing **Spirit**.
- **54–57:** repentance before Passover, divine purposes undefeated.

All seven original authorial sections and verse ranges preserved. Same-assistant comparison with secondary CCEL witness, **not** independent editorial or native-SWORD acceptance.
