# Psalm 55 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Prayer to God to manifest his favour. | 1–8 |
| The great wickedness and treachery of his enemies. | 9–15 |
| He is sure that God would in due time appear for him. | 16–23 |

## David prays under fear and grief (verses 1–8)

These verses show David **praying and weeping**.

Prayer is healing medicine for every wound and a relief for spirits burdened by sorrow.

Tears can lighten grief, while sorrows never expressed may grow heavier.

David was deeply alarmed.

Henry regards it as likely that the revolt of **Absalom**, with many people deserting David, lay behind these words.

The memory of David's sin concerning **Uriah** may also have intensified his fear.

When conscience is guilty, we may mourn deeply; even strong believers can be temporarily overwhelmed by dread.

Yet no one suffered deeper anguish than **the holy Jesus**, when the Lord laid our sins upon him and made his soul an offering.

In his agony he prayed earnestly and was heard and delivered.

Trusting and following him, we too will be sustained through every trial.

David was weary of human ingratitude and betrayal, and of the cares and disappointments attached to his high position.

He longed to hide in a wilderness away from the changeable anger of his people.

He was seeking **rest, not victory**.

Even a barren wilderness seemed desirable if it offered quiet.

The wisest and best people often value peace most deeply, especially after constant noise and distress.

One reason death can become desirable to God's child is that it brings final escape from this world's storms into **perfect and everlasting rest**.

## The pain of betrayal (verses 9–15)

Nothing grieves a believer more than discovering serious wickedness among people who profess to belong to God's church.

We should not be astonished by corruption within the earthly church, but should long for **the New Jerusalem**.

David described an especially active enemy.

God sometimes brings down hostile groups by dividing them; a cause divided against itself cannot stand.

Christians must expect suffering even from people who once claimed close friendship.

That is a painful trial, but looking to Jesus enables us to endure it.

Christ was betrayed by a companion, disciple and apostle, whose wrongdoing and end resembled those of **Ahithophel**.

Both were soon overtaken by divine judgment.

Henry understands the psalm's prayer against treachery as **a prophecy of the final ruin of those who persist in opposing the Messiah**.

## Cast every burden on the Lord (verses 16–23)

In every trial, call upon the Lord.

He will hear us, and will not rebuke us for coming too often; **the more often we pray, the more welcome we are**.

David had imagined everyone was against him.

Now he recognized that many people were still on his side—more than he had thought.

He gave the credit to God, who raises up friends and makes them faithful.

There are more genuine believers, and God's people have more friends, than they sometimes imagine during their darkest hours.

His enemies would face judgment.

They could not relieve their fears through trust in God as David could.

No matter how powerful human beings appear, they are easily humbled before the eternal Lord.

People who refuse to be corrected through suffering face destruction.

Affliction is a heavy burden, especially when Satan's temptations accompany it.

There is also the burden of **sin and corruption**.

The only sure relief is to look to **Christ, who carried that burden**.

Whatever you desire God to grant, leave him to give it **in his own time and way**.

Worry makes the heart stoop.

To cast our burdens upon the Lord is to rest upon **his providence and promises**, letting him do what he knows to be best.

He will carry us in his mighty arms as a nurse carries a child, and strengthen our spirits through his Spirit.

He will never allow the righteous to be so shaken by trouble that they finally abandon duty or comfort in him.

He will not let them be utterly cast down.

The Savior who carried the burden of our sorrows invites us to give him the burden of our cares.

**Why should we refuse to trust Christ to govern the world he has redeemed?**
