# Hebrews 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Humble, cautious fear is urged, lest any should come short of the promised rest, through unbelief. | 1–10 |
| Arguments and motives to faith and hope in our approaches to God. | 11–16 |

## Strive to enter God's promised rest (verses 1–10)

Gospel privileges are greater than those available through Moses' law, although **the gospel was preached in substance under both Testaments**.

In every generation, many hear God's word without benefiting. **Unbelief** is the root of unfruitful hearing; **faith gives life to the word received**.

Partial neglect and careless, wavering profession can make people fail to reach the promised rest.

Therefore pursue a sure entrance into God's kingdom.

Just as God finished his creating work and then rested, he will bring believers to the completion of their appointed labor and give them rest.

A **greater and more spiritual Sabbath** remains than the seventh-day rest or the land into which Joshua led Israel.

There is **rest in grace, comfort and holiness now**, through Christ and his gospel, and **rest in glory afterward**.

Henry identifies the rest chiefly in view as **the heavenly rest** promised to God's people after the labor and distress of earthly life, when Christ appears.

Unbelief excludes people from both true spiritual rest here and eternal rest thereafter.

God has always revealed that the human soul finds rest **in him**, and that its only way into that rest is faith in **his promises through his Son**.

## God's word searches us; our High Priest welcomes us (verses 11–16)

The goal is spiritual and eternal rest: grace here and glory afterward, **in Christ on earth and with Christ in heaven**.

Let us work diligently and encourage others to do the same. Labor for God makes final rest more satisfying.

The **Holy Scriptures are God's word**. When he brings Scripture to the heart through his Spirit, it powerfully convicts, converts and comforts.

It humbles pride, softens rebellious hearts, exposes thoughts, motives and selfish ends, and cuts away sinful habits deeply rooted in the soul.

The word shows sinners what is inside them.

Hold fast **Christian doctrine in the mind, its living principles in the heart, its public confession on the lips and practical obedience in the life**.

Christ fulfilled one part of his priestly service **on earth, dying for us**. He fulfills another part **in heaven**, interceding and presenting his people's offerings.

To save humans, he needed fellow-feeling born of genuine human experience. He encountered the effects of sin that can be experienced **without sharing its guilt**.

God sent his Son in **the likeness of sinful flesh** (**Romans 8:3**), yet Jesus himself was entirely holy.

Because he knew sin's evil perfectly, he is all the more eager to deliver his people from its guilt and power.

This excellent High Priest invites us to come **boldly to the throne of grace**.

We need both **mercy to pardon sin** and **grace to purify the soul**.

Besides asking for our daily needs, we should prepare through prayer for special seasons of temptation—through **adversity or prosperity**, and especially for the hour of death.

Approach reverently, but not as people dragged before a criminal court: **we are welcomed to a mercy seat**.

We enter the holiest place through **Jesus' blood**. He is our Advocate, who has purchased everything the soul needs.
