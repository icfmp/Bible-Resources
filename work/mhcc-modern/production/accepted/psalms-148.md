# Psalm 148 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The creatures placed in the upper world called on to praise the Lord. | 1–6 |
| Also the creatures of this lower world, especially his own people. | 7–14 |

## The heavens praise their Creator (verses 1–6)

We know little of the heavenly world of light while living in this dark and sinful world. Yet we know that **holy angels dwell there and continually praise God**.

The psalmist calls them to praise him in the finest possible way. By joining them we acknowledge our fellowship with the spirits above.

The heavens and everything within them declare the Lord's glory. They call us to honor **the Creator and Redeemer of the universe** in both words and deeds.

## Every creature and every person called to praise (verses 7–14)

God is praised even in a fallen world. Nature's strongest and stormiest forces do only what he directs. People who rebel against his word may seem more violent than storm winds, but they cannot thwart his purpose.

Mountains and hills, barren and fertile alike, give reasons for praise. **Rational creatures should praise him especially**, whatever their social position.

His saints should praise him continually. He is not only our Creator but **our Redeemer**, who has brought his people near.

Henry says that **“the horn of his people” may mean Christ**, whom God has exalted as **Prince and Savior**, the eternal defense and praise of his saints. Redemption displays extraordinary glory and supplies the foundation for our hope and joy.

May the Lord forgive us and teach us to **love him more and praise him better**.
