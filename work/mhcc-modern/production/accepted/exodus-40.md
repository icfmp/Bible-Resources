# Exodus 40 — Modern English

## The tabernacle is to be raised and the priests consecrated (verses 1–15)

Whenever a new year begins, we should seek to serve God better than we did in the previous year.

The tabernacle was completed in only half a year.

When many people are deeply committed to a good cause, much can be accomplished in a short time.

And when God's commands are continually followed as the rule for the work, everything will be done well.

The high priesthood remained within Aaron's family until Christ came.

In him—the reality to which all these earlier shadows pointed—the priesthood continues forever.

## Moses carries out everything God commanded (verses 16–33)

When the tabernacle and its furnishings were ready, Israel did not postpone erecting it until the people reached Canaan.

They obeyed God's will and set it up in the middle of their camp.

People whose lives are unsettled must never use their circumstances as an excuse to neglect worship and faith.

We cannot say that it is enough to begin serving God after our lives have become stable.

No.

We need a tabernacle for God even in the wilderness.

Indeed, we may enter the next world before we ever find a settled home in this one.

We must also be careful not to deceive ourselves with **an outward appearance of godliness**.

The fact that so few of those who left Egypt eventually entered Canaan should warn young people in particular against putting off the care of their souls.

## God's glory fills and guides the tabernacle (verses 34–38)

A cloud covered the tabernacle even on the clearest days.

It was not an ordinary cloud that sunlight could scatter.

All Israel could see it day and night as a sign of God's presence.

They should never again need to ask whether the Lord was truly among them.

The cloud guided the entire camp through the wilderness.

When it rested over the sanctuary, the people stayed where they were.

When it moved, they followed.

The Lord's glory filled the tabernacle.

In light and fire, the **Shekinah**—the visible sign of God's presence—made itself known.

God is light.

Our God is also a consuming fire.

The light was so dazzling and the fire so fearsome that Moses could not enter the tent of meeting until its splendor diminished.

But what Moses could not do, our Lord Jesus has accomplished.

God caused him to draw near.

And Jesus now invites us to come boldly even to the mercy seat.

The Holy Spirit teaches us to follow Christ's example as well as depend upon him.

If we attend to his appointed worship and obey his commands, we will not lose our way.

He will guide us along right paths until we reach heaven, where his holiness dwells.

**Blessed be God for Jesus Christ!**
