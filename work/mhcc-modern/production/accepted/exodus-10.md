# Exodus 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

| The plague of locusts. | 12–20 |
| The plague of thick darkness. | 21–29 |

## Pharaoh is warned of locusts (verses 1–11)

Egypt's plagues show how sinful sin really is.

They warn everyone not to fight against their Maker.

Pharaoh had pretended to humble himself.

But God did not accept that display, because it was not sincere.

Moses warned him that a plague of locusts was coming, worse than any Egypt had ever known.

Pharaoh's servants urged him to reach an agreement.

He then offered to let the men go, falsely claiming that the men were all Moses had asked to take.

He refused to allow their children to leave.

Satan works hard to prevent people who worship God from bringing their children to worship him too.

He is a determined enemy of faith developed early in life.

Whatever seeks to keep us from bringing our children into God's service should make us suspicious of Satan's influence.

Young people themselves should remember that the Lord calls them to remember their Creator in the days of their youth.

Satan, by contrast, wants them to remain slaves to sin and the world.

Our great enemy tries to hold us through the ties of family affection.

Pharaoh meant to keep the women and children as hostages so that the men would return.

Satan is willing to share our worship and obedience with the Savior.

But the Savior will not accept such divided service.

## The locusts devour Egypt (verses 12–20)

At God's command, Moses stretched out his hand, and locusts came.

An army might have been easier to resist than this enormous swarm of insects.

Who can stand before the great God?

The locusts covered the land and ate its crops.

Plants grow to serve human needs.

Yet God can allow insects to strip them away and take the bread from our mouths.

Let us labor not merely for homes and food that can be lost in this way, but for what endures to eternal life.

Pharaoh asked Moses and Aaron to pray for him.

Some people in trouble seek the prayers of others while having no desire to pray themselves.

This shows that they do not truly love God or delight in fellowship with him.

Pharaoh wanted the deadly plague removed, but not the sin of his hardened heart.

He wanted the locusts to go, but not the much more dangerous plague within him.

An east wind brought the locusts, and a west wind carried them away.

Whichever direction the wind blows, it fulfills God's word and follows his counsel.

From our viewpoint the wind blows wherever it wishes, but it is never independent of God.

Its change should have encouraged the Egyptians to repent.

God was showing that he was ready to forgive and quick to show mercy.

If he answered even outward signs of humility in this way, how much more would he do for those who were sincere!

If only God's kindness would lead us to repentance!

But Pharaoh returned again to his refusal to release Israel.

Those who repeatedly suppress their convictions may justly be left to their heart's sinful desires.

## The plague of darkness (verses 21–29)

The darkness that covered Egypt was terrifying.

It was so thick that it could almost be felt.

People were amazed and frightened.

It lasted three days—like six nights in a row.

Even the brightest palaces became prisons of darkness.

Pharaoh now had time to think, if only he had used it well.

Spiritual darkness is a form of spiritual slavery.

Satan blinds people's eyes so that they cannot see.

He also binds their hands and feet, so that they do not serve God or move toward heaven.

They remain seated in darkness.

It was right for God to judge Egypt in this way.

The darkness of the air mirrored the blindness of their minds.

No mind was more blinded than Pharaoh's; no sky was darker than Egypt's.

Let us fear where sin leads.

If three days of darkness were so dreadful, what will everlasting darkness be?

Meanwhile, Israel's children had light in their homes.

We should not take ordinary blessings for granted, as though we owed God no thanks for them.

The difference also showed God's special favor toward his people.

Wherever a true Israelite is found in this dark world, there is light: a child of light.

Who would not prefer the poor cottage of an Israelite with its light to an Egyptian palace in darkness?

There is a genuine difference between the home of the wicked, under a curse, and the blessed dwelling of the righteous.

Pharaoh again negotiated with Moses and Aaron.

Now he agreed that the children could go, but demanded that the livestock remain behind.

Sinners often try to bargain with the Almighty.

They think they can mock him, but they only deceive themselves.

God's terms for reconciliation cannot be altered, however long people argue about them.

We must submit to his will; we cannot expect him to accept the compromises our sinful desires propose.

We must devote our own lives, our children, and all our possessions to God's service.

We do not know what use he may have for anything we own.

Pharaoh abruptly ended the discussion and refused to see Moses again.

Had he forgotten how often he had sent for Moses to seek relief from the plagues?

What empty hatred it was to threaten with death a man whom God had armed with such power!

See what hardness of heart and contempt for God's word can lead people to do.

After that, Moses did not return until Pharaoh sent for him.

When people drive God's word away from them, he may justly leave them to their own delusions.
