# Psalm 23 — Modern English

## Original heading

**Confidence in God's grace and care.**

## The Lord is my Shepherd

The words **“The Lord is my shepherd”** express a believer's confidence and satisfaction in the care of the great Shepherd of all, the Redeemer and Preserver of humanity.

It is a wonderful thing to have a shepherd—and to know that this shepherd is **the Lord himself**.

A peaceful flock of sheep in green fields, tended by a wise, watchful and loving shepherd, pictures believers brought back to the Shepherd of their souls.

The wealthiest person who lives only for sensual pleasure finds even abundant possessions a **dry pasture**.

A godly person with little money has **green pasture** when faith enables that person to recognize God's goodness in everything received.

God gives the heart quietness and contentment regardless of its circumstances.

When his appointed ordinances provide green pasture, we should not merely pass through them but **remain and feed upon them**.

The Holy Spirit's consolations are the **still waters** beside which God's saints are led. They flow from the Fountain of living water.

People who walk in the paths of righteousness enjoy this comfort. The path of duty is genuinely pleasant, because righteousness produces peace.

Yet no one can walk that path unless God leads them into it and continues leading them onward.

Discontent and distrust spring from unbelief and lead to unsteady conduct. Trust the Shepherd's care and listen to his voice.

The **valley of the shadow of death** may refer to the deepest suffering or the darkest work of Providence.

It also describes the death separating the flock still on earth from the flock already in heaven.

But the psalm's expression reduces our fear: it is **only death's shadow**. A serpent's shadow cannot sting; a sword's shadow cannot kill.

It is a deep, dark valley, but valleys may be fruitful, and even death can bring spiritual comfort to God's people.

We **walk through** the valley. Christ's sheep will not be lost there: they reach safety on the other side.

Death is a king of terrors, but not to those who belong to Christ.

As they die, God restrains their enemy, guides them with his rod and upholds them with his staff.

The gospel contains enough comfort for believers in death, and **everlasting arms** support them.

At the Lord's table, his people feed upon the provisions of his love.

Satan and wicked people cannot destroy their true comfort while they are anointed by the Holy Spirit and drink from the full cup of salvation.

Past experience teaches believers to expect divine goodness and mercy to follow them throughout life.

They resolve to seek happiness in serving God now and hope to enjoy his love forever in heaven.

The Lord can make any condition pleasant through his Spirit's anointing and the joy of his salvation.

But those who hope to enjoy the **blessings of God's house** must also remain attentive to **its duties**.
