## David and the people give willingly (verses 1–9)

Works of devotion and charity should be carried out **willingly, not under compulsion**, for God loves a cheerful giver.

David led by his example.

He gave not because he had to or because he wanted to impress people.

He gave because his heart was devoted to God's house, and he believed he could never do too much to advance the good work.

Those who want to encourage others toward good must take the lead themselves.

## David's prayer of thanksgiving (verses 10–19)

We can scarcely imagine the grandeur of the temple and its surrounding buildings, made with so much gold and silver.

Yet Christ's unsearchable riches surpass the temple's splendor infinitely more than the temple itself surpassed the humblest cottage.

Instead of boasting about their generous gifts, David gave solemn thanks to the Lord.

Everything they offered for God's temple **already belonged to him**.

Had they tried to keep it all, death would soon have taken it from them.

The only way to use it for their true advantage was to dedicate it to the service of the One who had given it.

## Solomon is enthroned (verses 20–25)

The entire assembly joined David in worshiping God.

Whoever leads the congregation in prayer, only those who join sincerely benefit.

It is not primarily a matter of bowing the head but **lifting up the soul**.

Solomon sat upon **the Lord's throne**.

His kingdom was a type of the kingdom of the Messiah, whose throne is truly the Lord's.

## David's reign and death (verses 26–30)

After reading **2 Samuel**, we might hardly have expected David to appear so honorable at the end of his life.

Yet his repentance had been as remarkable as his sin.

His patient conduct through suffering and during his final years seems to have had a good effect on the people around him.

Blessed be God: even the greatest sinners may hope for a glorious departure when they repent and flee to the **atoning blood of the Savior**.

Notice the difference between David, the man after God's own heart, as he lived and died, and false professors who imitate **only his sins** and use his failures as excuses for their own crimes.

Let us watch and pray, lest temptation overcome us and sin dishonor God and wound our consciences.

When we recognize that we have offended him, may we imitate David's **repentance and patience**, looking forward to a glorious resurrection through our Lord Jesus Christ.
