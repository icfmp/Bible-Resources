# Joshua 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

| Preparation to pass over Jordan. | 10–15 |
| The people promise to obey Joshua. | 16–18 |

## The Lord appoints Joshua (verses 1–4)

Joshua had served Moses faithfully for years.

The person now called to honor had first spent a long time working and learning.

Our Lord Jesus took the form of a servant.

Joshua had learned to obey before receiving authority.

Those who have learned to obey are best fitted to lead.

When useful servants of God are taken away, those who remain should become more diligent in doing good.

God ordered Joshua to rise and cross the Jordan.

At that time the river had overflowed its banks.

Joshua had no boats or bridge.

Yet, because God commanded the crossing, he had to trust that God would open the way.

## The promise of God's presence (verses 5–9)

Joshua was to make God's law his rule.

He was commanded to meditate on it day and night so that he would understand it.

No amount of worldly business should cause us to neglect the one thing that is truly necessary.

Everything Joshua commanded the people and every judgment he gave had to agree with God's law.

He remained under that law himself.

No position of power or honor places anyone above God's commands.

Joshua was to draw courage from the promise of God's presence.

His personal weaknesses were no reason to despair.

The all-sufficient God had called, appointed and commissioned him and would uphold him in his duty.

When we are acting according to God's command, we have good reason to be strong and courageous.

Jesus Christ, whom Joshua pictured, was also strengthened through his suffering by his dedication to his Father's will and command.

## Israel prepares to cross (verses 10–15)

Joshua told Israel that the people would cross the Jordan and possess Canaan because God had promised it.

We honor God's truth when we do not waver in unbelief concerning his promises.

The two and a half tribes who had settled east of the Jordan had to cross with their brothers.

When God gives us rest, we should consider how we can help others still struggling to obtain it.

## The people promise obedience (verses 16–18)

Israel promised Joshua willing obedience.

The people would follow his commands without grumbling or arguing and go wherever he sent them.

The best thing we can ask God to give our civil leaders is his own presence.

That will make them a blessing to the people they serve.

When we pray for them in this way, we also seek our own good.

May we willingly follow the Captain of our salvation, obey his commands and fight the good fight of faith alongside everyone who trusts and loves his name.

Christ's authority will finally prevail.
