# Psalm 75 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist declares his resolution of executing judgment. | 1–5 |
| He rebukes the wicked, and concludes with resolutions to praise God. | 6–10 |

## Gratitude, justice and the rule of Christ (verses 1–5)

We often pray repeatedly when we are seeking mercy.

After receiving mercy, should we thank God only once or twice?

The Lord shows that he is near us through the blessings we seek from him.

Everyone entrusted with public responsibility must administer it **with integrity and justice**.

Henry understands these words as especially applicable to **Christ and his government**.

Human sin threatened the ruin of all creation, but **Christ saved the world from complete destruction**.

The One whom God has made our wisdom commands us to be wise.

He warns proud and defiant sinners not to boast of their power or persist in contempt for God.

Every hope humanity has for the present and every prospect of future happiness flows ultimately from **the Son of God**.

## All judgment and promotion belong to God (verses 6–10)

No human cause, considered by itself, can bring people to positions of honor without **the First Cause—God**.

Advancement comes neither from the east, west nor south.

Henry notes the absence of the north: in the language of the source, the word for north can also refer to **a hidden place**, which leads him to relate promotion to **the secret counsel of God**.

From the Lord alone every person receives the final judgment appointed to them.

In the cup of affliction offered to God's people, **mercy and grace** are mixed with suffering.

For unrepentant wicked people, the same image points to a mixture of **divine judgment**.

God's people may experience the same public disasters as other people, but **the dregs of the cup** are reserved for those who remain in sin.

The exaltation of **David's Son, Christ**, will be the subject of everlasting praise among the saints.

Let sinners submit to the **King of righteousness**.

Let believers rejoice in him and obey his commands.
