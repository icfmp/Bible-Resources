# Obadiah 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Destruction to come upon Edom. Their offences against Jacob. | 1–16 |
| The restoration of the Jews, and their flourishing state in the latter times. | 17–21 |

## Edom's pride and cruelty bring judgment (verses 1–16)

The prophecy is directed against **Edom**.

Henry regards its destruction as a **figure** of the fate of those who oppose the gospel church, just as **Esau's rejection** pointed beyond itself.

The prophet foretells that Edom will be plundered and brought low.

Every enemy of God's church will eventually be disappointed in whatever provides their sense of security.

God can humble those who make themselves great, and he will do so.

**False security** prepares people for ruin and makes that ruin more severe when it arrives.

Earthly treasures cannot be hidden so safely that thieves will never discover them.

It is wiser to lay up **treasure in heaven**.

Those who place their confidence in human strength may find that strength turned **against them**.

**God, who keeps his covenant, will never deceive his people**.

But people we trust and join ourselves to may cause great injury and humiliation.

God rightly deprives people of wisdom to escape danger when they refuse to use their wisdom to avoid sin.

Every act of violence and injustice is sinful.

But such acts are especially grievous when directed against **God's people**.

Edom's cruel conduct toward **Judah and Jerusalem** was remembered.

When examining ourselves, we should ask what we ought to have done and compare what we actually did with **the rule of Scripture**.

Sin seen in the light of God's command appears exceedingly sinful.

Those who remain idle spectators of a neighbor's suffering when they could have helped bear serious responsibility.

People imagining they will grow wealthy through the ruin of God's people are actually impoverishing themselves.

They deceive themselves when they claim as their own everything they can seize during another person's disaster.

**Judgment may begin at God's own house, but it will not end there**.

Let suffering believers and arrogant oppressors remember that the righteous person's trials will end, while the wicked person's final misery will not.

## The restoration of Israel and the church's future (verses 17–21)

The Lord promised **deliverance and holiness in Jerusalem**.

The house of Jacob would recover its possessions.

Much of this prediction was fulfilled when **the Jews returned to their own land**.

Henry also believes it points toward **the salvation and holiness of the gospel**, its spread and **the conversion of the Gentiles**.

He expects it to encompass **Israel's future restoration**, **the defeat of antichrist** and **the church's flourishing**, themes he also sees in other prophets.

Only when **Christ comes** will **the kingdom be the Lord's** in its fullest sense.

Those who lift themselves against the Lord will finally be brought down.

But those who wait for him and trust him need never be overcome by fear.

Blessed be **the divine Savior and Judge upon Mount Zion**!

His word brings life to many, while judging and condemning people who persist stubbornly in unbelief.
