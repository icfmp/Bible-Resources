# Daniel 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Nebuchadnezzar's golden image. | 1–7 |
| Shadrach and his companions refuse to worship it. | 8–18 |
| They are cast into a furnace, but are miraculously preserved. | 19–27 |
| Nebuchadnezzar gives glory to Jehovah. | 28–30 |

## Nebuchadnezzar's golden image (verses 1–7)

Henry supposes that the image's height—about **thirty yards**—probably included a pedestal.

He also considers it likely that the image was **covered with gold plates**, not cast from a solid mass of gold.

**Pride and religious intolerance** lead rulers to require everyone to follow their religion, whether it is true or false.

When worldly advantage attracts and harsh punishment frightens, few people resist.

Those who are careless, devoted to bodily pleasures or without faith find it especially easy to follow the crowd.

Almost any evil can be made attractive through **music and celebration**, or enforced through **the threat of fire**.

False worship has often been established and maintained through such methods.

## The three men refuse to worship (verses 8–18)

True devotion tends to make a person **calm, gentle and peaceful**.

Superstition and devotion to false gods, by contrast, can inflame passion.

The king's demand was simple: **bow down or burn**.

Proud people still challenge God's authority, asking why they should fear him.

Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego did not hesitate about their duty.

They did not allow concern for **life or death** to override it.

When an invitation or threat plainly asks us to commit sin, we must not bargain with the temptation.

We should reject it at once, as Christ said, **“Get behind me, Satan.”**

The men gave a direct answer rather than inventing an evasive one.

Those who put obedience first can entrust the consequences to God.

The Lord can control and overrule all the powers arrayed against his people.

**Lord, if you will, you can deliver us.**

If God is for us, we need not fear human opposition.

He may deliver us **from death or through death**.

We must obey **God rather than people**, suffer rather than sin, and never do wrong in the hope of producing something good.

God's preserving them **from sinful compromise** was as remarkable a miracle in the sphere of grace as his preserving them **from the furnace** was in the natural world.

Fear of people, love of the world and especially **lack of faith** cause surrender to temptation.

Strong confidence in the truth helps us avoid denying Christ or being ashamed of him.

Our answers should be **gentle but firm**.

## God preserves them in the furnace (verses 19–27)

Nebuchadnezzar could heat the furnace as much as he wished.

Its fire could torment those cast into it only for a short time.

Henry contrasts that earthly fire with **the unending torment of final judgment**, referring to **Revelation 14:10–11**.

The event fulfilled the promise of **Isaiah 43:2**: those passing through the fire would not be consumed.

The three men left it to the God who preserved them within the fire to bring them out.

They walked in the midst of the flames, sustained and encouraged by **the presence of the Son of God**, as Henry identifies their companion.

Those who suffer for Christ likewise have **his presence amid suffering**, whether in a fiery furnace or in **the valley of the shadow of death**.

Nebuchadnezzar acknowledged them as servants of **the Most High God**, who could save them from his power.

God himself is **the consuming fire** (**Hebrews 12:29**).

If we could see into the eternal world, we would see persecuted believers safe from their enemies while the impenitent persecutors faced God's judgment.

## Nebuchadnezzar praises God (verses 28–30)

This miracle helped the Jewish captives remain faithful to their religion and reject idolatry.

It deeply impressed Nebuchadnezzar with God's power.

But at this time **it produced no lasting transformation of his conduct**.

The God who preserved these faithful Jews in the furnace can also **sustain us through temptation and keep us from falling into sin**.
