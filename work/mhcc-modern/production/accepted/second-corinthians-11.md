# 2 Corinthians 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle gives the reasons for speaking in his own commendation. | 1–14 |
| Shows that he had freely preached the gospel. | 5–15 |
| Explains what he was going to add in defence of his own character. | 16–21 |
| He gives an account of his labours, cares, sufferings, dangers, and deliverances. | 22–33 |

## Paul warns against another gospel (verses 1–4)

Paul wanted to protect the Corinthians from the corrupting influence of false apostles.

There is **one Jesus, one Spirit and one gospel** to be proclaimed and received.

Why should hostile schemers turn the Corinthians against the apostle who had first taught them the faith?

They should not listen to people who, without just reason, tried to draw them away from those God had used to bring them to conversion.

## Paul served without using ministry for personal gain (verses 5–15)

It is far better to speak plainly while living openly and consistently with the gospel than to be admired by thousands and lifted up in pride.

A person admired as an impressive speaker may still disgrace the gospel through sinful attitudes and an unholy life.

Paul deliberately avoided giving his opponents any basis for accusing him of **self-interested motives in preaching**.

He would not let the false teachers in Corinth gain that advantage over him.

**Hypocrisy** is a real danger, particularly because Satan exerts considerable influence over people who continue living in disobedience.

But we must beware of an opposite temptation too.

Satan can advance his purposes by setting **good works against Christ's atonement**, as though salvation could be earned rather than received by **faith and grace**.

False workers will eventually be exposed, and their efforts will end in ruin.

Henry argues that Satan's servants can sometimes present **the law alone** or **the gospel alone** in misleading ways.

The test of a sound system is whether it teaches the law as rightly understood and upheld **through faith in Christ's righteousness and atoning work, together with participation in his Spirit**.

## Paul explains why he must defend his integrity (verses 16–21)

Christians ought to humble themselves in obedience to the Lord's command and example.

Nevertheless, wisdom is needed to decide when it is appropriate to speak of things we have lawfully done.

That includes acknowledging what **God has done for us, within us and through us**.

Paul's defense was not merely boasting.

He referred to real behavior that exposed the character of the false apostles.

How astonishing that such people could bring their followers into **bondage**, take advantage of them and even insult them—and still retain their allegiance!

## Paul's hardships as a minister of Christ (verses 22–33)

Paul recounted his work and suffering not from pride or love of applause, but **to honor God**, who had strengthened him for such service.

His experiences also revealed the difference between his ministry and the claims of opponents who tried to destroy his reputation and usefulness.

Consider the long record of dangers, deprivation, trouble and suffering!

And notice his **patience, perseverance, diligence, cheerfulness and usefulness** amid it all.

How little reason do we have to love this world's luxury and splendor when this blessed apostle experienced so much hardship while serving Christ.

Our hardest work and greatest sacrifices often seem small compared with Paul's.

Even our troubles may be scarcely noticeable beside his.

His example should lead us to examine whether we truly follow Christ.

Here we may learn **patience, courage and steadfast trust in God**.

Let us think less highly of ourselves.

Above all, let us speak **strictly and consistently truthfully before God's presence**, attributing everything to his glory as **the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, blessed forever**.
