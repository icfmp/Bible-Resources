# Ezekiel 2 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Ezekiel is called to speak God's word (verses 1–5)

Ezekiel had received astonishing revelations.

To keep him from pride, God reminded him that he was still **a son of man**, a weak and mortal human being.

Yet the title could also be honorable, since **Christ often called himself the Son of Man**.

Ezekiel's prostrate posture showed reverence.

Standing upright would show greater **readiness and fitness for his work**.

God speaks to those who are prepared to do what he commands.

But Ezekiel lacked strength in himself.

**The Spirit entered him** and gave him the ability to stand.

God graciously works in us **what he requires from us**.

The Holy Spirit raises us to our feet by making our wills inclined toward duty.

When the Lord commands a sinner to awaken and attend to the soul's needs, **the Spirit of life and grace accompanies that call**.

Ezekiel was sent with God's message to the Israelites.

Many would despise it.

Yet the events would prove that **a prophet had indeed been sent to them**.

God's glory and the honor of his word will be upheld, whether the message becomes an aroma **from life to life** or **from death to death**.

## Ezekiel must preach without fear (verses 6–10)

Anyone who wants to serve God effectively must not be ruled by **fear of people**.

The wicked are compared with **briers and thorns**, near to condemnation and burning.

The prophet must be faithful to the souls of those to whom he is sent.

Everyone who speaks God's message to others must first **obey God's voice**.

Discovering sin and hearing warnings of God's judgment should move us to grief and lamentation.

Those who understand God's word see that it contains terrible warnings for **unrepentant sinners**.

They also see that its precious gospel promises belong to **repentant and believing servants of the Lord**.
