# Isaiah 66 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God looks at the heart, and vengeance is threatened for guilt. | 1–4 |
| The increase of the church, when Jew and Gentile shall be gathered to the Redeemer. | 5–14 |
| Every enemy of the church shall be destroyed, and the final ruin of ungodly men shall be seen. | 15–24 |

## God favors the contrite heart, not empty ceremony (verses 1–4)

The Jews took great pride in their temple.

But what lasting satisfaction could **the eternal God** find in a house built by human hands?

He has already made **heaven and earth**, besides the temples constructed by people.

He looks past outward buildings to show favor to people who are **poor in spirit**, earnest, humble and self-denying.

Their hearts are deeply grieved by sin.

Such a heart is **a living temple for God**.

The sacrifice offered by a wicked person is not merely unacceptable.

It is **a serious offense to God**.

Henry argues that offering an animal sacrifice as though the Mosaic sacrificial law still provided atonement **after Christ's completed sacrifice** amounts to setting Christ's offering aside.

Likewise, treating incense as a necessary substitute for **Christ's intercession** dishonors his mediation, like honoring an idol.

Human beings will be deceived by the false confidences they choose for themselves.

An unbelieving heart and an unpurified conscience require no further punishment than to have their own fears become reality.

Whatever people place **instead of Christ's priesthood, sacrifice and intercession** will be hateful to God.

## The church rejoices as the Lord gathers his people (verses 5–14)

The prophet now addresses those who **tremble at God's word**, giving them comfort and encouragement.

The Lord will appear to bring joy to humble believers and shame to hypocrites and persecutors.

When the Holy Spirit was poured out and the gospel was preached from Zion, **great numbers were converted quickly**.

God's word, especially his promises, and his appointed ordinances provide comfort to the church.

Every sinner brought to Jesus Christ increases the joy of genuine believers.

Where the gospel is received in its power, it brings **a river of peace** that carries us toward an ocean of boundless and everlasting happiness.

Divine consolation reaches **the inward person**.

The joy of the Lord becomes the believer's strength.

Both **God's mercy and God's justice** will be displayed and glorified forever.

## Judgment on enemies and final separation (verses 15–24)

This is a prophetic announcement of the Lord's judgment upon those who oppose his church.

Henry applies it especially to what he calls **antichristian opponents of the gospel in the last days**.

Verses **19 and 20** describe abundant means through which sinners will be brought to faith.

These expressions are **figurative**, pointing to the many gracious helps God provides for bringing his chosen people home to Christ.

Everyone who comes will be welcomed.

Nothing needed for their encouragement and support will be withheld.

A gospel ministry will be established among the gathered people, with solemn worship before the Lord.

The final verse portrays the punishment of sinners in the world to come.

Then **the righteous and the wicked will be separated**.

Jesus himself applied this imagery to the everlasting misery of those who continue without repentance.

Let the Lord's redeemed people sing songs of victory in honor of the **free grace** that distinguishes them—yet do so **humbly, with reverent awe**.

Isaiah ends his prophecies with this striking portrayal of the opposite conditions of righteous and wicked people, a distinction that embraces the whole human race.

May God grant, **for Christ's sake**, that our portion be among those who fear and love his name, cling to his truth and persevere in every good work.

May we look forward to hearing **Jesus Christ** graciously invite his people to inherit the kingdom prepared for them from the foundation of the world.
