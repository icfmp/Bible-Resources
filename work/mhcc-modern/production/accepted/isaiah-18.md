# Isaiah 18 — Modern English

## Original heading

**God's care for his people; and the increase of the church.**

## God's care for his church amid uncertain events

Henry describes this chapter as **one of the most difficult passages in Scripture**.

The people who first received it probably understood more of its details than readers do today.

Swift messengers travel by water toward a people singled out by Providence, measured and trampled down.

God's own people may also be **trampled underfoot**.

Yet those who imagine they can swallow up his people discover that, although the church may be brought low, **she is not abandoned or finally destroyed**.

All nations should watch the movements of God's Providence and follow the directions of his will.

The Lord gives Isaiah an assurance to pass on to his people.

**Zion is his resting place forever**.

He watches over it and provides comfort suited to the need of each moment.

That comfort is precious precisely because **it comes at the proper time**.

God will judge both his enemies and the enemies of his people.

His children enjoy his care in **every season**, while their enemies remain exposed to his judgment in every season.

This should bring us to offer **a tribute of praise**.

Whatever we bring to God must be offered **in the way he has appointed**.

We may expect to meet him where he has chosen to make his name known.

Through these events, the nations will recognize that **the Lord is God and Israel belongs to him**.

They too will unite in offering spiritual sacrifices for his glory.

Happy are those who take warning from God's judgments upon others and promptly join his people.

**Whatever exact land or nation Isaiah has in view**, the passage teaches us not to think God has forgotten his church or ceased to govern human affairs merely because he allows evil people to prevail for a while.

He has wise reasons that we cannot presently understand.

They will become clear at **the great day of his appearing**, when every human work is brought into judgment and everyone receives what is due.
