# John 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Pharisees and the adulteress. | 1–11 |
| Christ's discourse with the Pharisees. | 12–59 |

## The Pharisees accuse a woman of adultery (verses 1–11)

Jesus did not find fault with the law.

Neither did he excuse the woman's wrongdoing.

Nor did he approve the Pharisees' false display of zeal for righteousness.

People who condemn others while committing the same sins **condemn themselves**.

Anyone called to correct wrongdoing should take special care to examine personal conduct and remain pure.

Jesus remained focused on the great work for which he came: **bringing sinners to repentance**, not destroying them but saving them.

He intended to lead the woman toward repentance through mercy.

He also intended to lead her accusers toward repentance by exposing **their own sins**.

They were seeking a way to trap him.

He sought to convict and convert them.

He did not take over the role of a civil judge.

Some offenses rightly deserve severe legal penalties, but we must not abandon our own duties to assume offices God has not assigned to us.

Jesus sent her away with a solemn warning:

**“Go, and sin no more.”**

Those who help save someone's physical life should also desire to help save the person's soul.

Truly happy are those whom Christ does not condemn.

The forgiveness of former sins ought to lead us to abandon future ones.

## Jesus speaks with the Pharisees (verses 12–59)

### Jesus is the light of the world (verses 12–16)

**God is light**, and Christ is the image of the invisible God.

One sun lights the whole world.

Likewise, **one Christ gives the saving light the world needs**, and no other is necessary.

Without sunlight, the world would be a dark prison.

Without Jesus, the world would be spiritually dark.

Those who follow him will not walk in darkness.

He provides the truths needed to guard us against destructive errors and the guidance needed to keep us from sins that lead to condemnation.

### Jesus reveals the Father (verses 17–20)

The better we know Christ, **the better we know the Father**.

Those who refuse to learn from Jesus inevitably form empty and misleading opinions about God.

A person who does not know Christ's glory and grace does not truly know the Father who sent him.

The time of our departure from earth is in God's hands.

Our enemies cannot make it come earlier, and our friends cannot delay it longer than he has appointed.

Every true believer can take comfort in saying:

**“My times are in your hand.”**

They are safer there than in my own.

### Jesus warns against dying in unbelief (verses 21–29)

Those who live in unbelief and **die in that condition** are eternally lost.

Jesus' opponents belonged to this fallen world.

But he was of a heavenly and divine nature.

His teaching, kingdom and blessings therefore contradicted their interests.

Yet everyone who accepts the grace of the gospel is delivered from **the curse of the law**.

Only Christ's gracious teaching supplies an argument powerful enough to turn a sinner to God.

Only **the Holy Spirit** supplies power to accomplish that inward change.

Some people recognize Jesus as a prophet or excellent teacher, perhaps even as greater than an ordinary creature.

But they refuse to confess him as **God over all, blessed forever**.

That is not sufficient honor for one who is **the light and life of humanity and one with the Father**.

People will finally recognize his true character either through conversion or in condemnation.

Every claim Jesus made—even his claim to the highest honor—was consistent with **his Father's will**.

### Christ frees sinners from spiritual slavery (verses 30–36)

Many listeners were convinced by Jesus' teaching and professed to believe.

Jesus urged them to remain in his word, trust his promises and obey his instructions despite temptation.

Only then would they be shown to be **his disciples indeed**.

Through his word and Spirit they would discover where to find their hope and strength.

Christ spoke about **spiritual freedom**.

But worldly-minded people recognize only troubles affecting the body and their earthly affairs.

Tell them about legal liberty, damaged property or losses to land, and they understand immediately.

Tell them about sin's slavery, bondage to Satan, danger to their souls and **freedom through Christ**, and the ideas sound foreign.

Jesus taught that anyone who **habitually practices sin is a slave to sin**.

Through the gospel Christ offers genuine liberation, and he possesses the power to accomplish it.

**Those whom the Son frees are truly free**.

It is tragic that people argue passionately for other forms of liberty while remaining enslaved to sinful desires.

### Abraham's children should resemble Abraham (verses 37–40)

Jesus challenged his hearers' proud confidence in their ancestry.

Being descendants of Abraham cannot benefit people whose character opposes Abraham's faith.

When **God's word has no place in the heart**, there is room for every kind of wickedness.

A sick person who refuses the physician's treatment and food is beyond help as long as that refusal continues.

God's truth both heals and nourishes those who receive it.

Human philosophical learning alone does not have that saving power.

Those who claim Abraham's privileges should **do Abraham's works**.

They should live as pilgrims in the world, maintain God's worship in their households and walk before the Lord.

### The devil's lies oppose God's saving truth (verses 41–47)

Satan leads people into self-destruction and harms others through their passions.

He also destroys souls through falsehood.

He is the great promoter of lies.

Every temptation claims that **evil is good or good is evil**, and promises freedom through sinful behavior.

People who imitate Satan's falsehoods become his servants and share his condemnation unless they repent.

The devil's characteristic desires are especially **spiritual evils**: pride, envy, anger, malice, hostility to goodness, deceptive reasoning and tempting others toward wrongdoing.

Here **truth** means God's revealed will concerning human salvation through Jesus Christ.

That is the truth Jesus preached and his opponents resisted.

### Believers will not see eternal death (verses 48–53)

Jesus gave no importance to human applause.

People who are no longer enslaved by the desire for praise can endure contempt more easily.

God will honor those who do not spend their lives seeking their own honor.

Jesus taught the everlasting happiness of those who believe.

A believer is someone who **keeps the words of the Lord Jesus**.

Such a person will never experience death as eternal destruction.

Believers still encounter bodily death.

Yet they are going to a place where death will exist no more (**Exodus 14:13**, the source's associated citation).

### Jesus is the eternal “I AM” (verses 54–59)

Christ and his people depend upon God for true honor.

Human beings may know how to argue about God without truly knowing him.

Scripture associates those who **do not know God** with those who **do not obey Christ's gospel** (**2 Thessalonians 1:8**).

Anyone who knows Christ rightly longs to know him better.

People who have seen the dawn of the **Sun of Righteousness** desire his full rising.

Jesus said, **“Before Abraham was, I AM.”**

Abraham was a creature; Jesus is **the Creator**.

The name **I AM** is God's name (**Exodus 3:14**), revealing his self-existence.

Jesus is **the First and the Last**, unchanging through all ages (**Revelation 1:8**).

He existed not only before Abraham, but before every world (**Proverbs 8:23; John 1:1**).

As Mediator he was appointed Messiah before Abraham.

He was **the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world** (**Revelation 13:8**).

Jesus was made Wisdom, Righteousness, Sanctification and Redemption to Adam, Abel and all who lived and died in faith before Abraham.

His opponents prepared to stone him as a blasphemer.

He withdrew safely through his miraculous power.

Let us boldly confess what we know and believe about God.

If we share Abraham's faith, we may rejoice in the future day when Christ will appear in glory, defeat his enemies and **complete the salvation of all who trust him**.
