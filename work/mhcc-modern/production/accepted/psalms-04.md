# Psalm 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The children of men proved, and the happiness of godly people. | 1–5 |
| God's favour is happiness. | 6–8 |

## Reverence, self-examination and trust (verses 1–5)

Our strongest plea in prayer is to ask God to **hear us for his mercy's sake**.

People who refuse to ask for forgiveness, the righteousness that justifies and eternal life will perish because they have rejected blessings they desperately need.

It is a dreadful choice.

The psalmist warns against sin.

Keep a holy reverence for God's greatness and glory.

There is much we ought to say **to our own hearts**.

Do not leave it unsaid.

Examine your heart through serious reflection.

Fix your thoughts on what is good and hold to it.

Before going to sleep, review the day's conduct and ask especially what you did wrong, so that you can repent.

When you wake at night, meditate on God and what leads to peace with him.

During illness, give particular attention to examining your ways.

**Be still**.

When you question your conscience, remain serious and quiet long enough to hear its answer.

Do not rush to excuse sin.

All confidence must rest **in God alone**.

Therefore, after speaking of sacrifices offered in righteousness, the psalmist commands us to **trust in the Lord**.

## The happiness of God's favor (verses 6–8)

Worldly people ask for good things, but not usually **the greatest good**.

They want outward, immediate and partial benefits: good food and drink, a successful business, comfortable possessions and wealth.

But how much are these worth?

Most people will accept almost any benefit.

Someone whose heart is ruled by grace cannot be satisfied with these things alone.

Such a person prays: **Lord, let us know your favor. Let us be sure of your loving kindness. That is enough.**

Many people search for happiness.

David had found it.

When God gives grace to the heart, he also gives **gladness to the heart**.

Comforted in this way, David could pity prosperous sinners without envying or fearing them.

He committed his concerns to God and became willing to accept God's wise will.

But **salvation is in Jesus Christ alone**.

Those who reject him as Mediator and insult him through his disciples are in grave danger.

May they stand in awe, turn from sin and stop resisting **the only remedy**.
