# Daniel 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Nebuchadnezzar acknowledges the power of Jehovah. | 1–18 |
| Daniel interprets his dream. | 19–27 |
| The fulfilment of it. | 28–37 |

## Nebuchadnezzar acknowledges God's power (verses 1–18)

The beginning and end of this chapter encourage us to hope that **Nebuchadnezzar** became an example of **the power of divine grace and the riches of mercy**.

After recovering from his madness, he told people far away and left a written account for later generations of how God had **justly humbled and graciously restored** him.

When a sinner comes to his senses, he wishes to benefit others by telling them about God's wonderful mercy.

Before explaining the judgment brought upon him for pride, Nebuchadnezzar described the warning he had received in a dream.

The meaning was explained to him.

The person represented would lose his honor and **the use of his reason for seven years**.

This is surely among the most severe **temporal judgments**.

Whatever outward affliction God allows us to suffer, we should bear it patiently and be thankful if he preserves **our reason and a peaceful conscience**.

Even the dreadful loss of reason would be preferable to being allowed to multiply sins or disgrace God's name without restraint.

God as the righteous Judge had determined the matter, and heaven's angels approved it.

This does **not** mean that God requires the angels' counsel or agreement. The language expresses **the solemnity of the sentence**.

Henry also connects the demand with **the word of the holy ones—God's afflicted people**, whose cries the Lord hears.

Let us seek blessings that can never be taken away, and especially avoid **pride and forgetting God**.

## Daniel explains the dream (verses 19–27)

Daniel was astonished and frightened by the severity of the announced judgment against such a powerful king.

He gave his counsel with **tenderness and respect**.

Genuine repentance requires not only **ceasing to do evil** but also **learning to do good**.

Such repentance might not prevent every judgment.

Yet it might **delay the trouble or shorten its duration**.

Everyone who repents and turns to God will escape **everlasting misery**.

## The king is humbled and restored (verses 28–37)

Pride and self-importance are particular temptations for powerful people.

They readily claim for themselves the glory that belongs only to God.

**While the king's boast was still in his mouth, God's powerful word came**.

Nebuchadnezzar lost his understanding and memory, and the powers of his rational mind failed.

We should beware of conduct that provokes God to remove our reason.

**God opposes the proud**.

The king wanted to be greater than a human being.

God justly brought him into a condition **lower than a human being**.

The Most High God lives forever.

His kingdom, like himself, is **eternal and universal**. No one can resist his power.

People can truly hope for divine honor only when they **honor God**, confess their sins and acknowledge his sovereign rule.

God not only restores what was lost through the sin of **the first Adam**, but adds glory through **the righteousness and grace of the second Adam, Christ**.

Afflictions last no longer than the period needed to accomplish the purpose for which God sent them.

Henry concludes that **Nebuchadnezzar truly repented and became an accepted believer**.

He also reports the view that Nebuchadnezzar lived **no more than about a year after this restoration**.

Thus the Lord knows how to humble those who walk in pride and give grace and comfort to the **humble, brokenhearted sinner who calls upon him**.
