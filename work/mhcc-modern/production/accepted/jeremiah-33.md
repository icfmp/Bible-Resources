# Jeremiah 33 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The restoration of the Jews. | 1–13 |
| The Messiah promised; happiness of his times. | 14–26 |

## God promises restoration and peace (verses 1–13)

Those who expect comfort from God must **call upon him**.

His promises are not given to replace prayer, but to awaken and encourage it.

These promises lead us to **the gospel of Christ**. In the gospel, God reveals truth to guide us and peace to give our hearts rest.

His sanctifying grace cleanses sinners from the corruption of sin, and his forgiving mercy frees them from its guilt.

When people are thus justified, washed and sanctified **in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Holy Spirit**, they are enabled to walk before God in **peace and purity**.

Many observers then recognize the difference between God's people and the surrounding world, and they come to fear his righteous judgment.

The Lord promises that people who have endured sorrow for a long time will again be filled with joy.

Where he gives **righteousness and peace**, he also supplies what is necessary for earthly needs. All the things we possess become genuine comforts as they are sanctified through **his word and prayer**.

## The promised Messiah and his eternal kingdom (verses 14–26)

To complete the promised blessings, God gives the promise of **the Messiah**.

Christ gives his church righteousness. God has made him our righteousness, and in him believers become the righteousness of God.

**Christ is our Lord God, our righteousness, our sanctification and our redemption.**

His kingdom will never end.

In this world, however, prosperity and adversity alternate like light and darkness, day and night.

God will also establish the covenant concerning the priesthood.

All true believers form **a holy and royal priesthood**. They offer spiritual sacrifices pleasing to God, beginning with **themselves as living sacrifices**.

The covenant promises will find their complete fulfillment in **the Israel of the gospel**.

According to **Galatians 6:16**, those who walk according to the gospel's rule belong to **the Israel of God**, upon whom are peace and mercy.

Yet we must not despise **the families who were God's chosen people in ancient times**, even though for a season they appear to have been set aside.
