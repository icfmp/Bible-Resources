# Genesis 45 — Modern English

## Joseph reveals himself to his brothers (verses 1–15)

Joseph allowed Judah to finish his appeal and listened to everything he had to say.

He saw that his brothers had been humbled for their sins. They had not forgotten Joseph, whom Judah had mentioned twice.

They showed respect for their father and deep tenderness toward Benjamin.

Now they were ready to receive the comfort Joseph intended to give them by revealing himself.

Joseph sent all his attendants out of the room.

In a similar way, Christ reveals himself and his loving kindness to his people beyond the sight and hearing of the world.

Joseph wept with tender and powerful affection. His tears swept away the sternness he had shown his brothers until then.

This reflects God's compassion toward sinners who return to him in repentance.

“I am Joseph, your brother.”

Those words must have humbled them still more because they had sold him. Yet the words also encouraged them to hope for kindness from him.

When Christ confronted Paul, he said, “I am Jesus.” When he comforted his disciples, he said, “It is I. Do not be afraid.”

When Christ reveals himself to his people, he invites them to draw near with sincere hearts.

Joseph likewise drew his brothers near, explaining that God had brought good out of the evil they had intended against him.

Sinners must still grieve over their sin and be angry with themselves, even when God brings good from their wrongdoing. They deserve no credit for the good he produces.

The likeness between Joseph's reconciliation and Christ's revelation of himself to a sinner's soul is remarkable.

When Christ makes himself known, the sinner does not begin to think that sin is a smaller evil, but a greater one.

Yet knowing what God has done protects the sinner from despair.

The believer can rejoice in God's saving work even while trembling at the danger and ruin from which he has escaped.

Joseph promised to provide for his father and the entire family.

Children must support and provide for their parents, as fully as they are able, whenever their parents need it. This is how they show godliness at home (1 Timothy 5:4).

Joseph embraced Benjamin, then embraced all his brothers. Afterward they spoke freely with him about their father's household.

Likewise, the signs of true reconciliation with the Lord Jesus are followed by the sweetness of communion with him.

## Pharaoh welcomes Joseph's family (verses 16–24)

Pharaoh showed kindness to Joseph and, for Joseph's sake, to his relatives.

What they lost by moving would be made up to them in Egypt.

In the same way, those for whom Christ has prepared heavenly glory should not cling to worldly possessions.

Even the finest earthly treasures are only baggage. We cannot be sure we will keep them here, much less carry them away when we die.

We must not fasten our eyes or hearts on the world.

Better things await us in the blessed land where Christ, our true Joseph, has gone to prepare a place.

As Joseph sent his brothers away, he gave them a timely warning: “Do not quarrel on the way.”

He knew their tendency to argue. Having forgiven them all, he now forbade them to accuse one another.

Our Lord Jesus likewise commands us to love one another and not to quarrel, whatever has happened or may happen.

We are brothers and sisters, and we have one Father.

We have all been guilty. Rather than turning against one another, we have reason to be displeased with ourselves.

We have offended God, and we either have received or hope to receive his forgiveness. We should therefore be ready to forgive one another.

We, too, are on the way.

We are passing through a land like Egypt, where many eyes watch us and some seek an opportunity to accuse us.

But our road leads to the heavenly Canaan, where we hope to live forever in perfect peace.

## Jacob learns that Joseph is alive (verses 25–28)

The news that Joseph was alive seemed too good to be true. Jacob was overwhelmed because he could not believe it.

We, too, grow weak when we fail to believe.

At last he was convinced.

Jacob was old and did not expect to live much longer. He wanted to see Joseph once more before his eyes closed forever.

Then he would need nothing else in this world to make him happy.

Consider Jesus revealing himself as a Brother and Friend to those who had once despised him and been his enemies.

He assures them of his love and of the riches of his grace.

He commands them to cast aside envy, anger, malice, and strife and to live in peace with each other.

He teaches them to abandon the world for him and for all his fullness.

He supplies everything needed to bring them home to himself, so that they may be where he is.

When he finally calls his people to himself, they may still feel doubts and fears for a time.

But the thought of seeing his glory and being with him will enable them to say, “It is enough. I am willing to die, because I am going to see and be with the One my soul loves.”
