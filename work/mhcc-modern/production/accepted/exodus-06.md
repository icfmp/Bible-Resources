# Exodus 6 — Modern English

## God renews his promise (verses 1–9)

Our efforts to glorify God and serve other people are most likely to succeed when we have learned from experience that we can do nothing by ourselves.

We must depend wholly on God and expect everything from him.

Moses had been waiting to see what God would do.

Now he would see what God was about to do.

God would make himself known by the name *Jehovah*—the God who fulfills his promises and completes the work he has begun.

He intended his people's happiness:

“I will take you as my own people, and I will be your God.”

We can ask for nothing greater and receive nothing better to make us happy.

God also intended to display his own glory:

“You will know that I am the Lord.”

These gracious and comforting words should have revived the discouraged Israelites and helped them forget their misery.

But they were so overwhelmed by their troubles that they paid no attention to God's promises.

When we indulge dissatisfaction and agitation, we deprive ourselves of the comfort God's word and providence could give us.

We remain without comfort because we refuse to receive it.

## God sends Moses and Aaron back to Pharaoh (verses 10–13)

Moses' faith was so weak that he could scarcely be kept at his task.

Prompt obedience usually grows with the strength of our faith.

Our weaknesses should make us humble, but they should not discourage us from doing our best in the work God has given us.

When Moses repeated objections that had already failed, God did not continue arguing with him.

Instead, he gave Moses and Aaron a direct command, addressed both to the Israelites and to Pharaoh.

God's authority is enough to answer every objection.

It binds us to obey without complaining or arguing (Philippians 2:14).

## Moses and Aaron's family and calling (verses 14–30)

Moses and Aaron were themselves Israelites, raised up from among their brothers.

Christ would also come from among Israel as its Prophet and Priest, Redeemer and Lawgiver.

Moses returned to his account and repeated both the command God had given him to address Pharaoh and the objection he had made against it.

Those who have spoken rashly should look back on their words with regret, as Moses seems to do here.

Scripture sometimes uses the word *uncircumcised* to describe something unfit to fulfill its proper purpose.

In the same way, the fleshly heart and corrupted nature of fallen humanity are entirely unfit for God's service and glory.

It is good for us to have no confidence in ourselves.

Our entire ability must come from the Lord.

We can never distrust ourselves too much or trust God too much.

As the apostle says, “I can do nothing by myself, but I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.”
