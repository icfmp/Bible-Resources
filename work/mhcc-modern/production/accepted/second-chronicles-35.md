## Josiah celebrates the Passover (verses 1–19)

The book of Kings describes more fully how Josiah destroyed idolatry.

Chronicles instead gives an account of his solemn celebration of the Passover.

The **Lord's Supper resembles Passover more closely than any other Jewish festival**.

Observing it rightly is a sign of growth in true godliness and devotion.

Only God can make our hearts holy and prepare us to worship him properly.

Nevertheless, there are duties that we must perform, through which we seek and receive his blessing.

## Josiah dies in battle (verses 20–27)

Scripture does not directly condemn Josiah for confronting Pharaoh.

Yet **Henry considers it possible that Josiah was wrong** not to consult the Lord after receiving a warning.

His death might have corrected him for rashness.

At the same time, it was a judgment upon the hypocritical and wicked people of Judah.

For someone who has lived in repentance, faith and obedience, the unexpected or sudden manner of death cannot destroy the person's eternal hope.

The people mourned Josiah.

Yet many people grieve over disasters while refusing to abandon the sins that caused God to send them.

Only repentance can turn away divine judgment.

If we judge Josiah's conduct to have been wrong, let us also watch our own actions, lest we die in a way that dishonors our Christian confession.
