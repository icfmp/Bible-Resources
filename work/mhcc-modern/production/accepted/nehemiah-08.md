# Nehemiah 8 — Modern English

## The law is read and explained (verses 1–8)

Sacrifices had to be offered at the entrance of the temple.

**Prayer and preaching**, however, could be carried out acceptably in many different places, both then and now.

Household leaders should bring their families to public worship.

Women and children have souls that need salvation. They therefore need to know God's word and attend the means of grace.

Young children should be taught religion as soon as they become capable of understanding it.

Ministers should take their **Bibles** into the pulpit, as Ezra did.

Scripture must be the source of their knowledge and the standard for everything they teach.

Reading Scripture aloud in a gathered congregation is an ordinance God has appointed to honor himself and build up his church.

Listeners need to understand what they hear. Otherwise it is only an empty sequence of words.

Teachers must therefore explain the text and make its meaning clear.

Reading is valuable and preaching is valuable. But **explaining the meaning of Scripture** helps people understand what is read and makes preaching more convincing.

In almost every age God has raised up preachers and also writers who explain biblical truth.

Some commentators have made matters darker through ignorant words, but the work of others has been extremely useful.

Nevertheless, **every teaching must be tested by Scripture**.

The people listened eagerly and paid attention to every word.

God's word deserves our close attention. If carelessness makes us miss much while listening, forgetfulness may cause us to lose everything afterward.

## The people are told to rejoice (verses 9–12)

The people's tears when they heard God's law showed that their hearts had become tender.

They were told to send food to those who had nothing prepared.

Both a solemn feast and a solemn fast should move us to compassion for hungry people.

God's generosity should make us generous, not only to people who come asking but also to those we must actively seek out.

**Their strength lay in joy in the Lord.**

The more deeply we understand God's word, the greater comfort we find in it.

Much of the darkness we experience in trouble comes from ignorance.

## The Feast of Tabernacles (verses 13–18)

Through careful study, the people rediscovered the law concerning the Feast of Tabernacles.

People who diligently search Scripture often find instructions they had forgotten.

The feast pictured the believer's **temporary dwelling in this world** and the holy joy of the gospel church.

Zechariah's prophecy pictures the nations coming to faith in Christ through this feast (**Zechariah 14:16**).

True religion makes us recognize ourselves as strangers and pilgrims on earth.

We read and hear God's word rightly when we **do what it commands**, including renewing duties long neglected.

The people observed the reality signified by the ceremony. Without that, the outward rite would have been useless.

They obeyed while rejoicing in God and his goodness.

These are the appointed means through which the Holy Spirit brings sinners to reverence, repentance and humility before God.

But those who continually indulge sorrow—even sorrow for sin—and refuse the comfort offered through God's word and Spirit become **enemies of their own growth in holiness**.
