# Revelation 6 — Modern English

## The first four seals (verses 1–8)

**Christ the Lamb** opened the seals in succession.

At the first seal John saw **a white horse and its rider**.

Henry suggests that this **seems** to represent a period of peace or the early progress of Christianity in its purity, when the Lord sent his apostles to teach all nations and promised to remain with them to the end of the world.

The gospel advances under God's favor, spiritually equipped against its enemies and destined for final victory.

The second seal revealed **a red horse**, signifying destructive judgments.

War and persecution take away one of the greatest earthly blessings—peace—and turn people who should love and help one another into enemies who kill one another.

Henry sees a correspondence with later Christian history, when some church leaders neglected love and peace, fought among themselves and resorted to the sword, becoming guilty through violence.

The third seal disclosed **a black horse**, the color of mourning, suffering, darkness and ignorance.

Its rider carried a yoke.

Henry applies this to efforts to bind disciples under **superstitious religious requirements**, as Christian practice became corrupted when it moved away from its original purity.

The excessive prices of necessities, while costly luxuries went unharmed, can also picture **the scarcity of spiritual nourishment**.

God invites thirsty souls to receive what sustains spiritual life (**Isaiah 55:1**).

Yet where ignorance and superstition dominate, true knowledge and godliness become scarce.

When people despise their spiritual food, God may withdraw ordinary bread as a judgment.

**A famine of bread is dreadful, but a famine of God's word is worse.**

At the fourth seal came **a pale horse**, ridden by **Death**.

**Hades**, the state of eternal misery of those who die in sin in Henry's interpretation, followed him.

In times of widespread destruction, many die without preparing for eternity.

This seal pictures slaughter and devastation that destroy the supports of ordinary happiness and ravage people's spiritual lives.

Henry relates these images to the growth of the **mystery of iniquity**, exerting influence over both lives and consciences.

He explicitly cautions that **the exact periods covered by these four seals cannot be established** because the changes were gradual.

Every instrument of judgment remains subject to God's command.

Public calamities happen only when he permits them, and extend **no further than his permission allows**.

## The fifth seal: faithful martyrs await justice (verses 9–11)

At the fifth seal, John saw **the souls of martyrs beneath the altar**, at the feet of Christ in heaven.

Persecutors can kill the body, but cannot kill the soul.

God has prepared a better dwelling for those who remain faithful until death.

They enter heaven **not because their own death deserves that reward**, but because of **Christ's sacrifice**.

They suffered for **the word of God**, the worthiest cause for which a person could give earthly life.

They trusted and confessed God's word without abandoning it.

They entrusted their cause to the Lord, **to whom vengeance belongs**.

He comforts his afflicted people and regards their shed blood as precious.

As their persecutors' guilt grows, so the number of Christ's faithful martyrs is brought to completion.

Then the Lord will send judgment on those who afflict his servants and grant **unbroken rest and happiness** to the afflicted.

## The sixth seal: upheaval and the wrath of the Lamb (verses 12–17)

Opening the sixth seal brought **a great earthquake**.

John's images describe vast upheavals that shake the foundations of governments and churches.

Scripture's prophecies often use such bold symbols for great historical changes, while they also point toward **the world's end and the day of judgment**.

Every rank and condition of person will be terrified.

Wealth, strength, courage and worldly distinction will offer no defense.

People will wish to hide so completely that they might cease to exist.

Although Jesus is **the Lamb**, his wrath against sin is terrible.

If the Redeemer who normally intercedes and turns away judgment becomes our enemy, **who remains to plead for us?**

Human beings have days of opportunity and times of grace.

God also has a day when he will display righteous anger against evil.

**Henry understands the scene as apparently referring to the overthrow of paganism in the Roman Empire.** This is his historical interpretation of the symbol, not a certainty established by the text.

In that reading, idolaters hide in dens and caves and seek in vain to escape the approaching fall.

Whenever the signs of the times remind believers that the King of kings is coming, they should openly confess **Christ and his truth** before others.

However much contempt people may inflict now, bearing it briefly is better than experiencing **everlasting shame**.
