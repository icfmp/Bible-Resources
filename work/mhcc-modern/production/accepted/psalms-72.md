# Psalm 72 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David begins with a prayer for Solomon. | 1 |
| He passes into a prophecy of the glories of his reign, and of Christ's kingdom. | 2–17 |
| Praise to God. | 18–20 |

## David prays for Solomon (verse 1)

Part of this psalm concerns **Solomon**, but it belongs **more fully and clearly to Christ**.

Solomon was both a king and a king's son.

His godly father asked that God would give him wisdom, so that Solomon's reign might become a memorial and picture of **the Messiah's kingdom**.

This is a father's prayer for his child and a final blessing near the end of life.

The best thing parents can ask God to give their children is **wisdom and grace to understand and fulfill their duties**.

## The Messiah's enduring kingdom (verses 2–17)

The psalm foretells **the kingdom of Christ**, and many of its promises cannot be fulfilled by Solomon.

At the beginning of Solomon's rule there were righteousness and peace.

But before his reign ended, trouble and injustice appeared.

The kingdom described here lasts as long as the sun, whereas Solomon's kingdom soon came to an end.

Henry adds that even **Jewish interpreters** understood it as speaking of the Messiah.

The glorious promises find their complete fulfillment **only through Christ's reign**.

Where his kingdom is established, discord and conflict diminish in families, churches and nations.

Christ's law written upon the heart makes people honest and just, willing to give each person what is owed and to live in love.

This produces abundant peace.

**Holiness and love endure in his kingdom**.

The world and human lives change constantly, but Christ's kingdom remains.

He descends through the grace and comfort of his Spirit **like rain on freshly cut grass**—on the living grass remaining to grow again, not on the pieces already cut off.

His gospel has been preached, or will be preached, to every nation.

Christ does not need human gifts or services, yet **he deserves our best**.

People who possess worldly wealth should use it to **serve him and do good**.

Prayer is offered **through him and for his sake**. Whatever we ask the Father, we should ask in Christ's name.

He is also worthy of praise, because our obligations to him are beyond measure.

Christ alone will be reverenced throughout the generations.

His name will be praised to the end of time and throughout eternity, and **all nations will call him blessed**.

## Praise for redeeming love (verses 18–20)

We are taught to bless God **in Christ**, praising him for everything accomplished for us through his Son.

David earnestly prayed that the promise about the Messiah's kingdom would be fulfilled.

It is sad to see how little glory God receives from the world, although he is so generous toward it.

May we, like David, submit to Christ's authority and share in his **righteousness and peace**.

Let us bless God for the wonders of redeeming love.

May our days be spent—and our lives end—in prayer for the **spread of Christ's gospel**.
