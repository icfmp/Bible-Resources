# Job 36 — Modern English

## Elihu asks Job to listen (verses 1–4)

Elihu maintained that Job's suffering had been permitted **to test him**.

It continued because Job had not yet been humbled fully under it.

Elihu wanted to **ascribe righteousness to his Maker** and show that God is righteous in everything he does.

We cannot learn this truth fully from fallen nature alone.

It must be taught through **God's word and Spirit**.

Elihu's answer was relevant to Job's dispute with his friends.

He explained that the trials had been sent for Job's spiritual good.

This corrected the friends' false assumption that every such calamity was a punishment proving great wickedness.

God had treated Job with **mercy**, even amid severe suffering.

## God's purposes in affliction (verses 5–14)

God rules righteously.

He is always ready to defend people who are treated unjustly.

When our eyes remain turned to God in faithful service, his eyes remain upon us in mercy, even when we have reached our lowest point.

The Lord can use hardship to **remind us of past sins** and make us willing to receive instruction.

Through grace working with the trial, an afflicted person may learn lessons previously rejected.

God also uses trouble to **discourage future sin**.

The lesson is not only to recognize wrongdoing but to have nothing more to do with it.

Those who serve God faithfully have a promise of the good things of the present life, as far as those things promote **God's glory and their own true welfare**.

Who would wisely wish for more?

They also enjoy **inward pleasures and the deep peace of those who love God's law**.

When suffering fails to produce its intended effect and a person continues unrepentant, judgment may become more severe.

Hypocrisy is principally a condition **of the heart**.

It loves the world and bodily desires while outwardly appearing devoted to God and religion.

Whether sinners die young or live long enough to store up judgment, the end of unrepentance is dreadful.

The souls of the wicked continue beyond bodily death, but in misery.

## Elihu warns Job against contending with God (verses 15–23)

Elihu believed Job was prolonging his own suffering through proud resistance.

He urged Job not to persist in stubbornness.

Even godly people need reminders to fear God's anger because the best and wisest still have sinful tendencies.

Job should not continue quarreling with divine Providence.

Nor should we ever approve of sin, cherish it or make room for it.

In Elihu's judgment, Job had preferred the gratification of pride and an irritable temper over mortifying those tendencies through humble submission and acceptance of discipline.

It is absurd for human beings to try to **instruct the One who is himself the source of light, truth and all knowledge**.

God teaches us through **the Bible, the best book**, and **through his Son, the best Teacher**.

He is righteous in all his dealings.

## The marvels of creation (verses 24–33)

Elihu directed Job's thoughts to God's greatness, so that he might submit with renewed willingness.

Unlike animals, human beings can look at creation and recognize their Creator's hand.

They should therefore give him glory.

Evildoers should tremble before him.

Faithful children of God, however, should rejoice, even when their Father speaks in judgment against his enemies.

Even the clearest light may be hidden temporarily by a cloud.

The **light of God's favor** is the most blessed of all, yet sin can darken our experience of his love.

When sins come between us and his countenance, we may no longer perceive the sunshine of his kindness.
