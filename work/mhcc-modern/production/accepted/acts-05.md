# Acts 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The death of Ananias and Sapphira. | 1–11 |
| The power which accompanied the preaching of the gospel. | 12–16 |
| The apostles imprisoned, but set free by an angel. | 17–25 |
| The apostles testify to Christ before the council. | 26–33 |
| The advice of Gamaliel, The council let the apostles go. | 34–42 |

## Ananias and Sapphira are judged (verses 1–11)

Ananias and Sapphira wanted to be regarded as exceptionally devoted Christians, although their hearts were not genuinely devoted to Christ.

Hypocrites sometimes make a real material sacrifice in one area because they hope to gain a greater advantage elsewhere.

The couple loved worldly wealth and distrusted God's Providence, imagining they could serve **both God and money**.

They thought they could deceive the apostles.

Through Peter, the Holy Spirit recognized the unbelief ruling Ananias's heart.

Satan may have suggested the deception, but he could not have filled Ananias's heart with this wickedness without **Ananias's consent**.

The lie was an attempt to deceive **the Spirit of truth** who spoke and acted through the apostles.

Their sin was **not** keeping part of the proceeds from selling their land.

**They were free to keep the entire price** if they wished.

The crime was trying to gain an undeserved reputation for self-sacrifice by telling a terrible lie, motivated by vanity and greed.

Anyone imagining they can deceive God will only fatally deceive their own soul.

How sad when married people, who should encourage each other toward goodness, reinforce each other in evil!

The sudden judgment also served as a warning of mercy to many others.

It urged serious self-examination, prayer and fear of hypocrisy, greed and pride. It restrained false profession from spreading unchecked through the church.

God hates falsehood.

We must avoid not merely direct lies but also gaining advantage through **deliberately ambiguous words and double meanings**.

## The apostles' message and healing spread (verses 12–16)

God's judgment on hypocrites encouraged sincere believers to hold more firmly to one another and to the gospel ministry.

Whatever strengthens the church's **purity and good reputation** also promotes its growth.

Yet only the power that worked miracles through the apostles can rescue sinners from sin and Satan and bring them into God's people.

Christ works through all his faithful servants. Anyone who comes to him for healing will find him ready to help.

## An angel releases the apostles from prison (verses 17–25)

No prison can be so secure or dark that God cannot visit his people there and, if he chooses, bring them out.

Recovery from illness and release from suffering are not granted merely so we can enjoy earthly comforts.

They are opportunities **to honor God through renewed service**.

Gospel preachers should not hide in private places while they still have opportunities to proclaim Christ before large gatherings.

They must speak to the poor as well as to the powerful.

The souls of ordinary and overlooked people are as precious to Christ as the souls of rulers.

The message concerns everyone; therefore **speak to everyone**.

Speak as people prepared to stand by it in life and death.

Proclaim all the words of the **heavenly life**, compared with which ordinary earthly life hardly deserves the name.

These are the words the Spirit supplies, the gospel words **through which sinners are saved**.

People who become angry at gospel success are miserable indeed.

Even seeing God's power oppose their plans may not stop them.

## The apostles testify before the council (verses 26–33)

Some people dare to commit wicked actions but cannot endure having those actions described to them afterward.

We cannot receive Christ's healing and redemption without agreeing **to be ruled by him**.

Faith receives the Savior in **all his offices**.

Jesus came not to save us **while leaving us in our sins**, but to save us **from our sins**.

If Christ had been exalted merely to give political rule and power to Israel, the chief priests would gladly have welcomed him.

But they did not value or recognize their need of **repentance and forgiveness**, the blessings Christ came to give.

Wherever true repentance occurs, forgiveness is certainly granted.

No one is freed from sin's guilt and deserved punishment without also being freed from **its rule and power**, turning from sin and against it.

Christ gives repentance through his Spirit working with the word.

He awakens conscience, produces sorrow for sin and works **a real change in heart and conduct**.

The Spirit's gifts show that God commands obedience to the exalted Christ.

Those who refuse his reign face judgment.

## Gamaliel counsels restraint; the apostles rejoice (verses 34–42)

The Lord still holds every human heart in his hand.

Sometimes he uses even the good judgment of people without faith to restrain persecutors.

Ordinary wisdom calls us to caution, and history shows that religious frauds usually fail before long.

**Suffering disgrace for Christ is a true honor**, because it makes us more like him and offers opportunities to serve his cause.

The apostles rejoiced that they had been counted worthy of that disgrace.

If we suffer because we do right, and God's grace enables us to endure it well, we too have reason to rejoice.

The apostles did not preach themselves but **Jesus Christ**.

That was what most offended the priests.

Ministers must always preach **Christ crucified and Christ glorified**, treating other subjects only as they relate to him.

Whatever our station in life, we should seek to make Christ known and glorify his name.
