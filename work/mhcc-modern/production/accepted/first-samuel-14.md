## Jonathan attacks the Philistines (verses 1–15)

Saul seemed confused and unable to help himself.

No one who believes himself outside God's protection can truly feel secure.

Saul therefore called for a priest and the ark, hoping to put matters right with the Almighty through a partial religious reform.

Many people do this while their hearts remain proud and unchanged.

They prefer ministers who tell them only pleasant things.

Jonathan, however, felt an impression from God calling him to this daring undertaking.

The Lord will guide those who acknowledge him in all their ways, sincerely seek his direction and fully intend to follow what he shows.

Sometimes the greatest comfort comes from events we did not plan or arrange, when we recognize the unexpected ways God's providence has guided us.

The Philistine camp was seized with trembling.

It was described as a **trembling from God**.

The panic was not only severe and impossible to resist; it came from God's hand.

The One who made the human heart knows how to make it tremble.

## The Philistines are defeated (verses 16–23)

God's power turned the Philistines against one another.

The more clearly it became evident that God had achieved the victory, the more carefully Saul ought to have asked whether God wanted him to do anything further.

But he hurried to attack an enemy already falling apart.

He would not even finish his religious inquiry or wait for God's answer.

Those who believe God will not hurry in this way, as though any business were so urgent that there was no time to seek the Lord's guidance.

## Saul forbids food during the battle (verses 24–35)

Saul's severe order was foolish.

It may have saved some time, but it cost the soldiers the strength they needed to keep pursuing the enemy.

God made our bodies so that we cannot carry out the work of each day without the food of each day.

Our Father graciously provides our daily bread.

Saul was turning away from God.

Yet he became especially zealous for the **outward form of religion** just when he was denying its inward power, beginning to build altars.

## Saul seeks an offender among the people (verses 36–46)

If God seems unwilling to answer our prayers, we have reason to search for any cherished sin within our hearts and put it away.

We should examine **ourselves first**.

But an unrepentant person looks suspiciously at everyone else, searching everywhere except the heart for the cause of trouble.

Jonathan was identified as the one who had broken Saul's order.

People most indulgent toward their own sins may be the harshest judges of others.

Those who least respect God's commands are often most offended when people fail to obey **their** commands.

Careless curses can place both the speaker and the family in danger.

Saul's behavior on this occasion displayed rashness, pride, malice and irreverence.

It illustrates the corrupt dispositions of human nature when people are left to themselves.

## Saul's family and reign (verses 47–52)

The chapter gives a general account of Saul's household and military establishment.

He had little reason to boast of his royal dignity.

Nor did his neighbors have much reason to envy his position.

After gaining the throne, he enjoyed little lasting happiness.

Sometimes earthly glory shines most brightly immediately before a dark period of shame and sorrow.
