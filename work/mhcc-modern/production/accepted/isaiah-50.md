# Isaiah 50 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The rejection of the Jews. | 1–3 |
| The sufferings and exaltation of the Messiah. | 4–9 |
| Consolation to the believer, and warning to the unbeliever. | 10, 11 |

## God answers his people's complaints (verses 1–3)

People who profess to belong to God sometimes complain when he treats them severely, as though he had been unjust toward them.

The Lord answers such accusations.

He did not deprive his people of their privileges without cause.

The Jews went into captivity in Babylon because **idolatry violated the covenant**.

Henry also connects their later rejection with **the crucifixion of the Lord of glory**.

God called them to turn away from their sins and avoid ruin.

Finally, his own Son came to his people, but they did not receive him.

When God calls people toward happiness and they refuse to answer, they justly experience the misery resulting from their refusal.

The passage offers evidence of God's power to answer doubts.

Even the extraordinary events surrounding Christ's suffering and death testified that he was **the Son of God** (**Matthew 27:54**).

## The Messiah is taught, suffers and is vindicated (verses 4–9)

Because Jesus Christ is **God and man in one person**, Scripture sometimes describes him as **the Lord God** and sometimes as **a man and servant of Jehovah**.

He came to announce truths that bring comfort to **broken and repentant hearts**, people weary of sin and burdened by affliction.

The Holy Spirit rested upon him so that he could speak as no other person spoke.

That same divine help awakened him daily to **prayer, gospel preaching, and complete reception and proclamation of the Father's will**.

The Father vindicated the Son by accepting the satisfaction he made for human sin.

Christ also speaks on behalf of **every believer**.

Who can successfully oppose people whose Friend is Christ?

Who can condemn those whom he defends as Advocate?

The apostle Paul applies this very truth in **Romans 8:33**.

## Light for believers; warning to the self-confident (verses 10 and 11)

A child of God fears offending the Father.

That fear often becomes most evident during periods of spiritual darkness, when the believer's other graces seem hidden.

People who truly fear God **obey Christ's voice**.

A sincere servant of God may go through a long season without a clear sense of eternal happiness.

What should such a person do?

**Trust the name of the Lord.**

Rest upon the promises of his covenant and build hope upon them.

Trust **Jesus Christ, the Lord our Righteousness**, and lean upon God as **our God through the Mediator**.

But presumptuous sinners are warned against placing confidence in themselves.

Their supposed merit and strength appear to them like **warmth and light**.

Earthly comforts are mere **sparks**: they burn briefly and then disappear.

Yet worldly people try to warm themselves by them while enjoying the pride and pleasures of their passing light.

Those who make the world their comfort and **their own righteousness their confidence** will finally encounter bitterness.

A godly person's path may be dark now, but it leads to **peace and everlasting light**.

An ungodly person's path may appear pleasant, but it ends in **lasting darkness**.
