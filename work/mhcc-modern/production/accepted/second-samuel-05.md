# 2 Samuel 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David king over all Israel. | 1–5 |
| He takes the strong-hold of Zion. | 6–10 |
| David's kingdom established. | 11–16 |
| He defeats the Philistines. | 17–25 |

## David becomes king over all Israel (verses 1–5)

David was now anointed king **for the third time**.

His rise had been gradual so that his faith could be tested and he could gain experience.

In this way his rule pictured the kingdom of the Messiah, which also grows toward its full glory by stages.

Jesus became **our Brother**.

He took our human nature and lived within it so that he could become our Prince and Savior.

A humbled sinner can take courage from this close and loving relationship.

Such a person seeks his salvation, submits to his authority and asks for his protection.

## David takes Zion (verses 6–10)

God's enemies often feel most confident in their strength shortly before their fall.

The pride and insults of the Jebusites stirred David to action, and the **Lord God of hosts** was with him.

Similarly, on the day of God's power, **Satan's stronghold—the human heart—can become God's dwelling through the Spirit**.

It becomes the throne on which the Son of David rules and brings every thought into obedience to himself.

May Christ enter our hearts, claim them, cleanse them, overthrow every idol and reign forever!

## God establishes David's kingdom (verses 11–16)

David's house was not made less holy or less fit to be dedicated to God because foreign builders helped construct it.

The gospel church is described in similar language:

The sons of foreigners will rebuild its walls, and their kings will serve it (**Isaiah 60:10**).

David's kingdom became firmly established.

Likewise, the Son of David reigns securely, as do those who through him are made kings and priests to God.

Israel had never appeared so great as it did at this time.

Many people enjoy God's favor and love without recognizing it and therefore miss the comfort they might have had.

To receive his favor **and know that we have it** is great happiness.

David recognized that God had raised him to power **for the good of his people**, so he could bless them and they could live happily under his leadership.

## David defeats the Philistines (verses 17–25)

The Philistines overlooked the fact that David enjoyed God's presence, which Saul had lost.

In the same way, as soon as the Messiah's kingdom was established in the world, it came under attack from powers of darkness.

Nations raged and rulers stood against it, but in vain (**Psalm 2:1**).

The eventual defeat would fall upon Satan's own kingdom.

David confessed his dependence upon God for victory.

He asked whether the Lord would fight for him and submitted to God's decision.

The promise of victory over our spiritual enemies should give us courage in spiritual conflict.

David waited until God moved.

Then he acted—but **not before**.

He was being trained in dependence on God and his providence.

The Lord performed what he had promised, and David made good use of every opportunity provided.

Likewise, before the apostles began their work against Satan's kingdom, they had to wait for the promised Spirit.

He came upon them with a sound from heaven like a **rushing, powerful wind** (**Acts 2:2**).
