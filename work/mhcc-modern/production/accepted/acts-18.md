# Acts 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Paul at Corinth, with Aquila and Priscilla. | 1–6 |
| He continues to preach at Corinth. | 7–11 |
| Paul before Gallio. | 12–17 |
| He visits Jerusalem. | 18–23 |
| Apollos teaches at Ephesus and in Achaia. | 24–28 |

## Paul works with Aquila and Priscilla (verses 1–6)

Paul had the right to receive support from the churches he established and the people he taught.

Yet he continued working at his trade.

No one should despise **honest work that provides daily bread**.

Jewish families commonly trained their children in a trade even when they also provided education or property.

Paul took care to avoid needless prejudice, even when the prejudice was unreasonable.

**Love for Christ** is the best bond among believers.

Fellowship with other Christians makes labor, contempt and even persecution easier to bear.

Many Jews continued contradicting and blaspheming the gospel.

Not only did they refuse faith themselves, they tried to stop other people from believing.

Paul therefore left them.

But he did **not** abandon his ministry.

Even if Israel did not gather, Christ and his gospel would still be glorified.

The Jewish hearers had received the first opportunity to hear him and could not claim he had refused them the message.

When some oppose the gospel, we should turn to others willing to hear.

Our grief over the unbelief of many should not prevent us from **thanking God for those who are converted**.

## Paul continues serving in Corinth (verses 7–11)

The Lord knows who belong to him, including **those who will later become his** through his work within them.

We should not despair about any city or people.

Even in wicked Corinth, Christ had many people.

He will gather his chosen flock from the places where its members are scattered.

Encouraged by this promise, Paul continued teaching in Corinth, and the church grew strong and numerous.

## Paul appears before Gallio (verses 12–17)

Paul intended to show that his preaching did not encourage people to worship God unlawfully.

But **Gallio** would not accept the Jews' complaint about an issue outside his civil authority.

That was right: he should leave Jewish religious disputes to the Jews and refuse to let them use such arguments **as an excuse to persecute others**.

But Gallio was wrong to speak contemptuously about God's law and faith, which he could and should have learned about.

Whether Jesus is the Messiah, whether the gospel comes from God and how God must be worshiped are **not trivial disagreements about names and words**.

They are matters of tremendous importance.

Gallio acted as though ignorance of Scripture were something to boast about and God's law beneath his notice.

If he merely refused to be disturbed by wicked people's insults, that was commendable.

But if he was indifferent when innocent people were mistreated, **his indifference had gone too far**.

People who learn of God's servants' suffering but feel no sympathy, offer no prayer and take no interest in helping resemble Gallio in his worst attitude.

## Paul travels and encourages believers (verses 18–23)

While Paul saw that his work was fruitful, he continued working.

Our times belong in God's hands.

We make plans, but **he decides their outcome**.

Every promise concerning our plans should therefore be made in submission to God's will.

We must seek not merely permission from Providence but the Lord's own direction about our movements.

For a faithful minister, spending time with other believers is valuable refreshment.

Disciples remain weak and imperfect.

Ministers should help strengthen them by directing them to **Christ, their Strength**.

In whatever position God has given us, let us earnestly pursue Christ's cause.

We should plan as wisely as we can, yet depend on the Lord to accomplish those plans **if he judges them good**.

## Apollos teaches and learns more fully (verses 24–28)

Apollos understood and taught as much of Christ's gospel as **John the Baptist's ministry had revealed**, but no further.

Henry thinks Apollos probably knew Jesus had died and risen, but had not yet been instructed clearly about the meaning of those events.

Apollos did not have the apostles' miraculous gifts.

Nevertheless, he used **the gifts he actually possessed**.

The Spirit grants gifts according to his measure for the good of others.

Apollos was an energetic and affectionate preacher, **fervent in spirit**.

He cared deeply for God's glory and the salvation of precious souls.

He was a man devoted to God, equipped for the work God had given him.

**Aquila and Priscilla** encouraged Apollos by attending his ministry.

They did not despise him for what he lacked or lower his reputation in the eyes of others.

They understood the disadvantages under which he had learned.

Through long companionship with Paul, they had learned more about the gospel, and they **shared that knowledge with him**.

Younger students can benefit greatly from speaking with mature Christians.

Even people who believe through grace continue needing instruction.

While we remain in this world, some unbelief persists and our faith is still imperfect.

The Jews whom Apollos addressed already honored Moses.

If they accepted that Jesus is the Messiah, **Moses' own law directed them to listen to him**.

Ministers' work is to preach Christ.

They must not merely assert the truth, but **explain and defend it with gentleness as well as power**.
