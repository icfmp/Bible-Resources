# Nehemiah 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| Opposition of Sanballat and others. | 1–6 |
| The designs of the adversaries. | 7–15 |

## Sanballat and others oppose the work (verses 1–6)

Proud and arrogant mockers have despised many a good work.

People who disagree about almost everything may nevertheless unite in persecution.

Nehemiah did not answer foolish insults in kind. He looked up to God in prayer.

God's people have often been despised, but the Lord hears every insult directed toward them. This knowledge comforts them.

Nehemiah had reason to believe these enemies had become desperately hardened in heart; otherwise he would not have prayed that their sins never be wiped away.

Good work progresses well when people have the heart to do it. Enemy insults should **make us more diligent in duty**, not drive us away from it.

## The enemies plan to stop the building (verses 7–15)

Wicked people aim to obstruct good work and expect their plans to succeed. But work done for God is his work, and it will prosper.

God can expose and defeat the plans of his church's enemies in many ways.

If our enemies cannot frighten us away from duty or deceive us into sin, they cannot truly hurt us.

Nehemiah entrusted himself and his cause to God's protection, as we should.

He laid all his cares, sorrows and fears before God.

**He prayed before taking practical measures. Then, after praying, he appointed a guard.**

If we expect prayer to protect us without vigilance, we are lazy and put God to the test.

If we depend on vigilance without prayer, we are proud and disregard God.

Either way, we abandon the path of his protection.

God's care for our safety should encourage us to perform our duties vigorously.

Once the danger has passed, let us return to work and trust him when new trouble comes.

## Nehemiah's precautions (verses 16–23)

We must remain watchful against spiritual enemies and not imagine that the struggle ends before our work is finished.

The word of God is **the sword of the Spirit**.

It should always be within reach, so that we never have to search for it when we are working or fighting as Christians.

Every true Christian is **both a worker and a soldier**, building with one hand and fighting with the other.

Good work progresses when those doing it treat it seriously.

Satan fears the watchful Christian. If he does attack, the Lord fights for his servant.

We must continue until the end of life, never putting aside our armor until our work and warfare have finished.

Then we will be welcomed into our Lord's rest and joy.
