# 1 Samuel 23 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David rescues Keilah. | 1–6 |
| God warns him to escape from Keilah. | 7–13 |
| Jonathan comforts David. | 14–18 |
| He is rescued from Saul by an invasion of the Philistines. | 19–29 |

## David rescues Keilah (verses 1–6)

When rulers persecute God's people, they should expect trouble from other directions as well.

A country is most likely to enjoy peace when it allows God's church to live in peace.

While Saul pursued David, the Philistines attacked Saul's country.

But David still regarded himself as a protector of the land.

Jesus Christ, our Savior, likewise acted for the good of those who mistreated him and left us an example.

Those who refuse to do good merely because their past service has not been rewarded are not following David.

## God warns David to leave Keilah (verses 7–13)

David could rightly complain that his enemies repaid good with evil and love with hostility.

Christ was treated in the same shameful way.

David sought guidance from his great Protector.

As soon as the ephod was brought, he made use of it.

We possess the Scriptures.

Whenever we face a difficult choice, we should consult them: **bring the Bible here**.

David's prayer was both reverent and extremely specific.

God permits us to pray this way, explaining exactly which matter troubles us and asking for direction.

He knows not only **what will happen**, but also **what would happen under circumstances that never take place**.

Thus he knows how to deliver godly people from temptation and how to judge everyone according to their deeds.

## Jonathan encourages David (verses 14–18)

David made no attempt to harm Saul.

He continued along God's path, waited for God's appointed time and was content to remain hidden in forests and wildernesses.

The world's cruelty toward some of its best people should lessen our love for it and increase our longing for the kingdom in which goodness and holiness will receive everlasting honor.

Jonathan came to comfort David.

As a godly friend, he directed David's attention to **God, the foundation of all comfort**.

As a self-denying friend, he rejoiced in David's approaching rise to the throne.

As a faithful friend, he renewed their covenant of friendship.

We should regularly renew our own covenant commitment to God and sustain our fellowship with him.

If a single conversation with a true friend can strengthen and comfort us, how much more can we expect from the continual support and powerful love of Jesus Christ—the **covenant Friend of believers**?

## Saul is forced to abandon the pursuit (verses 19–29)

Even during his worst wrongdoing, Saul liked to speak as though he were deeply religious.

Such language, without corresponding actions, can deceive both the people hearing it and the people speaking it.

The mountain that separated Saul's forces from David became a picture of divine Providence standing between David and his destroyer.

Do not despair at the thought of problems yet to come.

Trust the Lord, whose counsel is wonderful and whose work is excellent.

Before he allows his promise to fail, he will use even an invasion by Philistines to bring about our escape—at the very moment when the danger appears greatest.

God requires complete dependence upon himself.

“If you do not believe, you will not be established” (**Isaiah 7:9**).
