# Psalm 98 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The glory of the Redeemer. | 1–3 |
| The joy of the Redeemer. | 4–9 |

## Singing a new song for redemption (verses 1–3)

The song of **redeeming love** is new because it celebrates a mystery hidden from earlier ages and generations.

Those converted to Christ sing in a new way, very different from their former songs.

If God puts **a new heart** within us, he also gives us **a new song**.

We should sing it to his praise because of the marvelous works he has accomplished.

The Redeemer overcame every obstacle in the way of our salvation.

Neither the services nor the sufferings appointed for him discouraged him.

Praise the Lord for revealing his salvation and righteousness to the world, fulfilling the Old Testament prophecies and promises.

To accomplish this purpose, God sent his Son Jesus not only as **a light to the nations**, but also as **the glory of Israel**.

We should examine whether his holy arm has won the victory **within our hearts**, overcoming Satan, unbelief and sin.

If he has, we will exchange shallow songs of vanity for songs of gladness and thanksgiving.

Our lives will proclaim the Redeemer's praise.

## The whole creation invited to rejoice (verses 4–9)

Everyone has reason to rejoice in the establishment of **Christ's kingdom**, because all may benefit from it.

The psalm seems to use figurative language to describe different orders of rational creatures throughout the universe rejoicing under the great Messiah's reign.

Christ's kingdom will bring blessing to the whole creation.

We await **his second coming**, when his glorious reign will be fully displayed.

Then heaven and earth will rejoice, and the joy of the redeemed will be complete.

But sin and its devastating consequences will not be entirely removed until **the Lord comes to judge the world in righteousness**.

Because we await these things, we should strive to be found by him **at peace, spotless and blameless**.
