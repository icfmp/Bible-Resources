# 2 Chronicles 12 — Modern English

## Original heading

**Rehoboam, forsaking the Lord, is punished.**

## Rehoboam is punished for abandoning God

Once Rehoboam became powerful enough to believe he had nothing to fear from Jeroboam, he abandoned even his **outward profession of godliness**.

It is common, and deeply regrettable, for people who appear eager to seek and serve God during danger, illness or fear of death to abandon religion after receiving merciful deliverance.

God soon brought trouble upon Judah to awaken the people to repentance before their hearts became hardened.

When Providence corrects us, we should acknowledge **God's justice and our own guilt**.

If humiliating circumstances produce a genuinely humble heart, the suffering has accomplished its purpose.

It may then be removed or changed in its character.

The more we compare God's service with other kinds of service, the more reasonable and easy it appears.

Do laws requiring self-control seem difficult?

The consequences of excess are far harder.

**Serving God is perfect freedom. Serving sinful desires is complete slavery.**

Rehoboam never possessed a stable inward faith.

He did not entirely reject God, but he also never set his heart on seeking him.

He failed to serve the Lord because he failed to **seek** the Lord.

He did not pray as Solomon had for wisdom and grace, consult God's word as his guide or follow its directions.

Religion mattered little to him because he never truly committed his heart to it.

He did wrong because he was never firmly resolved to do good.
