# Job 39 — Modern English

## Original heading

**God inquires of Job concerning several animals.**

## God teaches Job through animals

The Lord continued to humble Job through questions about the living creatures he had made.

The nature and circumstances of each animal reveal something of the **power, wisdom and variety of God's works**.

Consider the **wild donkey**.

It is better to work diligently and accomplish something useful than to wander without purpose and be of no use.

If Job could not command even the colt of a wild donkey, how unfit was he to issue instructions to **divine Providence**!

Henry also describes the powerful creature called the **unicorn** in his biblical witness.

It possesses the strength to perform useful service but is unwilling to do so.

God challenged Job to compel it to work.

When the Lord gives someone the strength to serve, it is a special mercy if he also gives **a willing heart**.

We should pray for that willingness and reason ourselves into it, because unlike animals we can reflect on our duty.

The most impressive-looking qualities are not always the most valuable gifts.

Henry contrasts the **nightingale's song** with the **peacock's beautiful tail**.

He would rather possess **the eagle's eyesight and soaring wings**, and **the stork's parental affection**, than the **ostrich's fine feathers**, since the ostrich cannot soar and appears to lack natural care for its young.

The description of the **warhorse** pictures presumptuous sinners.

As a horse rushes into battle, so people fully committed to evil hurry along the way their desires and passions have chosen.

They may refuse to fear divine judgment, however clearly their sinful habits lead toward ruin.

Some sinners believe themselves secure in their sin, like **an eagle nesting high among inaccessible rocks**.

But the Lord declares that even from such a height he can bring the proud down (**Jeremiah 49:16**).

Every wonderful image of animal life and behavior should teach us to honor **the Creator's wisdom**, which sustains everything he has made.

Job's failure to recognize how completely divine wisdom is present in God's works led him to speak in unworthy ways about Providence.
