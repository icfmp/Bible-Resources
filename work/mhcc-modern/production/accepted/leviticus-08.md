## Aaron and his sons are consecrated (verses 1–13)

Aaron's consecration and that of his sons took place only after the tabernacle had been prepared and the rules for sacrifice had been given.

They were washed with water. This signified that they must turn away from every sinful desire and keep themselves pure afterward.

Christ washes in his own blood those whom he makes kings and priests to God (Revelation 1:5–6).

Those who approach God must also be washed with pure water (Hebrews 10:22).

Aaron's anointing foreshadowed Christ's anointing with the Holy Spirit, who was given to him without measure.

Every believer also receives an anointing.

## The sacrifices accompanying consecration (verses 14–36)

These ceremonies picture our great High Priest, Jesus Christ.

By his own blood and the power of his Holy Spirit, he was appointed, anointed and set apart for his sacred office.

He sanctifies religious worship so that it benefits his people and honors God the Father.

For Christ's sake the Father accepts our worship, even though sin still mars it.

We can rejoice that Jesus is a merciful and faithful High Priest, full of compassion toward weak and troubled souls.

All true Christians are likewise consecrated as spiritual priests.

We should examine our daily lives carefully. Do we seek to live according to this calling?

Do we offer abundant spiritual sacrifices that are pleasing to God through Christ?

Even when the answer is yes, we have no reason to boast.

We should never despise other sinners. Remembering our own sins and the way God saved us, we should pray and work for their salvation too.
