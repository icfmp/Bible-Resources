# Ezekiel 3 — Modern English

## The prophet receives God's words (verses 1–11)

Ezekiel was to take God's truths into himself as **food for his soul**.

By feeding on them through **faith**, he would gain strength.

People changed by God's grace can receive with joy the same truths that bring terror to the wicked.

Ezekiel was to announce **everything God had told him, and nothing beyond it**.

How can anyone speak God's mind better than by using **his words**?

If people refused to listen, Ezekiel must not turn away in offense.

The people of **Nineveh** responded to **Jonah's preaching**, while Israel remained stubborn and unchanged.

We must leave such different outcomes under **God's sovereignty** and confess that his judgments are deep.

Those who refused the prophet's word also refused to learn from God's corrective judgments.

The Lord would strengthen Ezekiel.

He must continue earnestly preaching regardless of the immediate outcome.

## Ezekiel is appointed a watchman (verses 12–21)

The prophet's mission caused **holy angels to rejoice**.

These events taught him that God had enough power to sustain him in his work.

Ezekiel was overwhelmed by grief over his people's sins and suffering, and by the majesty of the vision.

Solitude, reflection and fellowship with God can be precious.

Yet the Lord's servant must be prepared to **serve the people of his generation**.

God appointed Ezekiel to be **a watchman for Israel**.

If a watchman faithfully warns the wicked, he is not responsible for their refusal and ruin.

Although the passage concerns **Israel's national covenant**, Henry also applies its warning to the **ultimate destiny of all people** under every period of God's dealings.

Those who appear righteous need **warnings as well as comfort**.

Many become proud or careless, fall away and die in their sins.

Listeners to the gospel should welcome **rebuke and warning**, not only encouragement.

## God regulates the prophet's speech (verses 22–27)

We owe every blessing of fellowship between God and humanity to **Christ's mediation**.

A genuine believer can say, **“I am never less alone than when I am alone with God.”**

When the Lord opened Ezekiel's mouth, the prophet was to speak boldly.

He must place **life and death, blessing and curse**, before the people, leaving them responsible for their response.
