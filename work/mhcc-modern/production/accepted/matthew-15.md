# Matthew 15 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jesus discourses about human traditions. | 1–9 |
| He warns against things which really defile. | 10–20 |
| He heals the daughter of a Syrophenician woman. | 21–28 |
| Jesus heals the sick, and miraculously feeds four thousand. | 29–39 |

## Jesus exposes human traditions (verses 1–9)

Adding rules to God's law reflects badly upon his wisdom, as though he had left out something necessary that human beings could provide.

Such additions ultimately draw people into disobedience.

We should be deeply thankful for **the written word of God**.

Never imagine that Scripture's religion can be improved by additions in either **doctrine or practice**.

Jesus described the Pharisees' traditions as **human inventions**.

He showed how they violated the **fifth commandment**, which requires honoring parents.

When parents needed assistance, some claimed they had dedicated all their available resources to the temple, even though they had not actually given the resources away.

On that pretext they refused their parents help.

The tradition thus made **God's command ineffective**.

Jesus summed up the fate of hypocritical worship: **they worship God in vain**.

Such worship neither pleases God nor benefits the worshiper.

They trust in what is empty and receive emptiness in return.

## The heart is the source of defilement (verses 10–20)

Jesus taught that what truly defiles is not food entering the mouth.

It is the sinful speech and conduct **coming out of a corrupt heart**.

Only the **regenerating graces of the Holy Spirit** remain forever in the soul.

Likewise, the church should admit nothing as its spiritual rule or foundation except what comes from above.

We need not be troubled simply because a clear and timely declaration of divine truth offends people.

The disciples asked Jesus to explain more fully.

An honestly willing heart seeks instruction when its understanding struggles with a word of Christ.

It is **the heart that is deceitful and desperately wicked** (**Jeremiah 17:9**).

No sinful word or deed appears outwardly before it has first arisen inwardly.

Sinful actions are fruit from this corrupt source.

When Christ teaches us, he exposes the deceit and wickedness within.

He humbles us and leads us to seek cleansing in **the Fountain opened for sin and uncleanness**.

## The Syrophoenician mother's faith (verses 21–28)

Christ's saving influence reached even remote parts of the country, anticipating the day when the ends of the earth would see his salvation.

A mother's distress for her daughter drove her to Jesus.

Even when suffering is what first brings us to Christ, he does not reject us on that account.

She did not prescribe a particular form of relief.

Her whole request was **mercy**.

She claimed no merit and trusted entirely in his kindness.

Parents should pray earnestly for their children, especially concerning the salvation of their souls.

A son or daughter enslaved by pride, impurity, malice or some other sin is in a state even more dreadful than bodily demonic oppression.

Such children should be brought to Christ through **faith and prayer**, for he alone can heal them.

God's dealings with his people may at times appear dark and perplexing.

This event teaches that **Christ may have love in his heart while his face appears to frown**.

We may trust him even when he seems ready to destroy our hopes.

He sometimes humbles most deeply those whom he intends to honor most greatly.

A proud heart would have rebelled at the apparent rejection.

But this woman transformed it into **a new reason to ask for mercy**.

She pictures the sinner who knows the desperate need of the soul.

Even the smallest **crumb from the Bread of Life** is precious to one who believes.

Faith especially honors Christ; he therefore especially honors faith.

He spoke, and her daughter was healed.

Those who seek the Lord and receive no immediate answer should learn to turn **their very unworthiness and discouragement** into reasons for pleading for mercy rather than abandoning prayer.

## Jesus heals many and feeds four thousand (verses 29–39)

Whatever troubles us, our best course is to lay it at **Christ's feet**, submit the matter to him and accept his decision.

Those seeking spiritual healing must be willing to be governed as he pleases.

Consider the harm sin has brought into the world and the many kinds of disease to which human bodies are exposed.

Some illnesses were beyond anyone's understanding or ability to heal, yet all were subject to Christ's command.

His **spiritual cures** are equally wonderful.

He makes spiritually blind people see through faith.

He opens speechless mouths in prayer.

He enables those spiritually lame or disabled to **walk in holy obedience**.

Jesus again demonstrated his power by providing enough food for a great crowd.

Everyone ate and was satisfied.

**Those whom Christ feeds, he fills.**

There is more than sufficient bread with him, and more than enough grace for both those already seeking it and those who might yet come.

After feeding the people, he sent them home.

Though he had miraculously fed them twice, they were **not to expect miracles instead of ordinary provision every day**.

They needed to return to their callings and their own tables.

Lord, increase our faith and forgive our unbelief.

Teach us to depend upon your fullness and generosity in all that concerns **this life and the life to come**.
