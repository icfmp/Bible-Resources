# 1 Samuel 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elkanah and his family. | 1–8 |
| Hannah's prayer. | 9–18 |
| Samuel, Hannah presents him to the Lord. | 19–28 |

## Elkanah's household (verses 1–8)

Elkanah continued to worship at the Lord's altar even though his household was divided by unhappy conflict.

When family worship does not bring an end to disagreements, **the disagreements must not bring an end to family worship**.

It is unkind to reduce our rightful affection for a relative because of some weakness that person cannot overcome and already suffers from.

Doing so would turn God's providence into an excuse for breaking his command to love and would add suffering to someone already afflicted.

It is an ugly disposition to enjoy grieving a person whose spirit is sorrowful or upsetting someone easily distressed.

We should carry one another's burdens, not increase them.

Hannah found the provocation difficult to bear.

People who are easily upset and take injuries too much to heart harm themselves, depriving themselves of many comforts in life and faith.

We should notice the comforts God gives us so that we do not become absorbed in our troubles.

Remember what is **for us**, not only what is **against us**.

## Hannah prays in distress (verses 9–18)

Hannah prayed with tears.

She trusted that the merciful God knew the grief of her soul.

The Lord permits us not only to ask generally for good things, but to name the **particular blessing** that we most desire and need.

Hannah prayed quietly; no one heard her words.

This showed that she believed God knows the heart and its desires.

Eli was the high priest and also a judge in Israel.

He misjudged Hannah.

We should not be quick to accuse others of serious wrongdoing when the matter is still uncertain and unproved.

Hannah did not respond by reminding Eli of his own sons' wicked conduct.

When someone judges us unfairly, we should be doubly careful about our words and not answer accusation with accusation.

Hannah simply explained herself.

We should be willing to do the same.

Eli, in turn, was ready to admit his error.

Hannah left with peace of mind.

She had committed her trouble to the Lord in prayer, and Eli had prayed on her behalf.

Prayer brings relief to a heart that trusts God.

It should even soften the face and visible expression of sorrow.

Those who rightly use the privilege of approaching the mercy seat of God, reconciled to us through Jesus Christ, will not remain forever without comfort.

## Hannah presents Samuel to the Lord (verses 19–28)

Elkanah's family had a journey to make, with children to bring along.

Even so, they would not set out until they had worshiped God together.

**Prayer and provision do not delay a journey.**

People who hurry into travel or business without taking time to worship may go forward without God's presence and blessing.

Although Hannah loved worshiping at God's house, she asked to remain at home for a time.

God desires mercy rather than sacrifice.

Those prevented from attending public worship because they must nurse or care for young children may take comfort from her example.

If they perform that work in a faithful spirit, God will graciously receive it as service to him.

When Hannah presented Samuel to the Lord, she gratefully acknowledged that he had answered her prayer.

Everything we give God was first something we asked for and received from him.

**Our gifts to him begin as his gifts to us**.

Even as a child, Samuel showed genuine devotion.

Young children should be taught to worship God.

Parents should teach them, bring them to worship and encourage them to participate as well as they can.

God will graciously accept their young efforts and teach them to serve him better.
