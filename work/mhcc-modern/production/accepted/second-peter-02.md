# 2 Peter 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Believers are cautioned against false teachers, and the certainty of their punishment shown from examples. | 1–9 |
| An account of these seducers, as exceedingly wicked. | 10–16 |
| But as making high pretences to liberty and purity. | 17–22 |

## False teachers face certain judgment (verses 1–9)

The path of error is harmful, yet many people readily enter it.

Let us give Christ's enemies no valid reason to speak against **his holy name or the salvation offered through him**, the Way, Truth and Life.

False teachers deceive by using **invented and manipulative words**.

They lead others astray and bring judgment upon themselves.

God's dealings in earlier history illustrate his justice.

Angels were cast down from their dignity and glory because they disobeyed God.

Even creatures dwelling in heaven will suffer judgment if they sin.

**Sin belongs to darkness; darkness is its deserved outcome**.

God judged the ancient world in Noah's days. The **number or social position** of wrongdoers cannot make sin acceptable.

When wrongdoing spreads throughout a society, judgment may spread widely too.

God can turn fruitful land to desolation and flourishing cities into ashes.

No political strategy can prevent his judgment on unrepentant sin.

The God who preserves his servants through **fire and water** (**Isaiah 43:2**) can use the same elements in judgment.

When judgment falls upon the wicked, the Lord can preserve the righteous.

Living among evil people brings either **grief over their sins or guilt through participating in them**.

Yet God's children can remain faithful even in deeply corrupt surroundings, because **Christ's grace within them is stronger than Satan's temptations or wicked examples**.

God often places surprising obstacles in people's path before they commit sin, as though warning them to reconsider.

His wisdom and power ensure that his merciful purposes will be fulfilled.

Some wicked people escape visible punishment now because they are **reserved for final judgment**.

## The arrogance and corruption of deceptive teachers (verses 10–16)

False teachers and their followers abandon themselves to sensual desires.

They refuse to submit their thoughts to **Christ's obedience**, violate God's holy commands and go progressively deeper into impurity.

They also despise lawful authority God requires them to respect.

Their hearts are fixed upon **temporary earthly gain**, which they both desire and promise.

Those who presume upon God's grace as permission to indulge sin have special reason to tremble.

Some treat the moral demands of Scripture as though the gospel freed them from obeying God.

Christians should keep their distance from such teaching and conduct.

## False promises of freedom lead to slavery (verses 17–22)

God's true word is **living water** that refreshes people who receive it.

False teachers resemble **empty springs or clouds without rain**, because they possess no saving truth.

As clouds can block the sunlight, false teaching can darken people's understanding with words lacking substance.

Those who spread darkness should not be surprised by the judgment awaiting them.

While promising freedom, these teachers are themselves **slaves of their sinful desires**.

Their passions have conquered them completely.

Anyone entangled by sin is more easily overcome. Christians must remain close to God's word and beware people who confuse and mislead them.

**Deliberate apostasy after knowledge is more serious than ignorance**.

Speaking falsely against God's good way of salvation exposes a person to severe judgment.

This condition is dreadful. Yet Henry adds a critical qualification: **it is not utterly without hope**.

Just as a leper may be cleansed and a dead person raised, a person grieved by their departure from faith may still find mercy.

**If your backsliding grieves you, believe in the Lord Jesus and you will be saved.**
