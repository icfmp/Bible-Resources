# Acts 25 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Paul before Festus, he appeals to Caesar. | 1–12 |
| Festus confers with Agrippa respecting Paul. | 13–27 |

## Paul appeals to Caesar (verses 1–12)

See how restless malicious people are.

Persecutors treat the opportunity to satisfy their hatred as a special favor.

But preaching **Christ as the fulfillment and goal of the law** was no offense against that law.

In times of persecution, God's people need **wisdom and prudence** as well as patience.

Innocent people may rightly insist on their innocence.

Paul was prepared to submit to lawful proceedings and let the legal process take its course.

If he truly deserved death, he would accept the penalty.

But if the accusations were false, no one could justly deliver him to his enemies.

For the moment Paul was **neither released nor condemned**.

This shows how gradually Providence sometimes unfolds.

We often discover that both our hopes and our fears were premature, as God keeps us waiting on him.

## Festus consults Agrippa about Paul (verses 13–27)

Agrippa governed Galilee.

Festus acknowledged an important Roman legal principle: a person accused of wrongdoing must have the opportunity to confront the accusers and answer the charges (**verse 16**).

How many hasty and unjust judgments does that principle condemn!

Even a non-Christian governor, guided only by natural understanding, could follow the law and established custom in this matter.

Yet many professing Christians neglect **truth, justice and charity** when judging brothers and sisters.

Worldly people and political officials may regard questions of divine worship, salvation and the gospel as doubtful or unimportant.

Notice how lightly Festus spoke of Christ and the controversy between Jewish leaders and Christians.

But the day is approaching when Festus and everyone else will recognize that the affairs of the entire Roman Empire were insignificant compared with **the truth of Christ's resurrection**.

Those who had the means to learn God's truth yet despised them will understand their folly and sin.

A distinguished company gathered to hear Paul.

They intended only to satisfy their curiosity about a prisoner, but they were about to hear the gospel.

People still sometimes come to hear God's word with **great pomp**, motivated by curiosity rather than a desire for truth.

Although ministers today do not ordinarily stand in the dock defending their lives, some hearers still sit in judgment upon them, looking for any statement they can use as a charge rather than listening to learn God's will for their salvation.

Yet all the grandeur of the assembly was eclipsed by **the real glory of the poor prisoner**.

What were their splendid clothes and rank beside Paul's **wisdom, grace, holiness, courage and steadfast endurance for Christ**?

It is a great mercy when God makes our innocence and upright conduct plain, leaving opponents without any proven charge.

Sometimes he causes even our enemies to do us justice.
