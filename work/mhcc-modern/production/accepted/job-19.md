# Job 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job complains of unkind usage. | 1–7 |
| God was the Author of his afflictions. | 8–22 |
| Job's belief in the resurrection. | 23–29 |

## Job grieves over his friends' harshness (verses 1–7)

Job's friends condemned him as wicked because of his suffering.

Job described how cruelly they treated him and how his words deserved more understanding than their criticism allowed.

Harsh words from friends add great weight to already painful troubles.

Yet we should be careful not to let those words produce resentment in our hearts.

Look instead to **Jesus Christ**, who endured hostile sinners speaking against him and was treated with far greater cruelty than Job or we have suffered.

## Job describes his abandonment (verses 8–22)

How painful Job's complaints sound!

What makes hell dreadful is **the wrath of God**.

People whose consciences have become hardened may feel no fear of it now, but they will experience it later.

Those whose consciences have been enlightened fear that judgment now, yet through Christ they will not experience it in eternity.

We must not assume that people whom God afflicts are therefore being treated as his enemies.

Every created thing affects us as God's Providence permits it.

But that truth **does not excuse** the cruelty of Job's relatives and friends.

Human friendship is uncertain.

If God is our Friend, however, he will never fail us in the hour of need.

Our bodies remain vulnerable to diseases arising within them, however much care we take.

Job appealed to his friends for compassion and rightly criticized their harshness.

It is deeply distressing for someone who loves God to lose both **earthly comforts and the inward feeling of God's consolation**.

Yet even such suffering does not weaken the evidence that a true believer is a child of God and an heir of glory.

## Job confesses faith in his living Redeemer (verses 23–29)

At this point the Spirit of God seems to have worked powerfully within Job.

He made a beautiful confession of faith and expressed sure hope.

His words are full of **Christ and heaven**.

They show that he sought a better homeland, a heavenly one.

God had taught Job to trust in **a living Redeemer**, to expect **the resurrection of the dead** and to look for the life of the world to come.

These hopes comforted him.

Job believed this Redeemer would free sinners from Satan's power and sin's condemnation.

He trusted that the Redeemer was **his own Redeemer** and expected salvation through him.

He believed the Redeemer was alive already, **though he had not yet appeared in human flesh**.

At the last day the Redeemer would appear as the world's Judge, raise the dead and complete his people's redemption.

How gladly Job spoke of this hope!

May the Holy Spirit engrave these truths deeply upon our hearts.

We all need to make sure that **the root of true religion** is in us.

It is a living, strengthening, governing work of grace in the heart—necessary to faith as the root is necessary to a tree's stability and fruitfulness.

Job and his friends disagreed about the ways of Providence.

But they agreed about this root principle: **there is another world**.
