# James 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Cautions against proud behaviour, and the mischief of an unruly tongue. | 1–12 |
| The excellence of heavenly wisdom, in opposition to that which is worldly. | 13–18 |

## The tongue can set a life on fire (verses 1–12)

A tongue that is not controlled is one of the greatest dangers in human life.

Human speech has repeatedly thrown private and public affairs into confusion, in every age and social condition.

**Hell contributes far more to the sinful fire of human speech than people usually realize.** When words serve sin, they are set ablaze by that evil source.

No one can tame the tongue without God's grace and help.

James does not suggest control is absolutely impossible, but that it is **extremely difficult**.

Some sins diminish with age as opportunities decrease. But uncontrolled speech can grow worse when physical strength fails and people become increasingly irritable, bitter or dissatisfied.

A person contradicts himself by praising God's perfection with one breath and condemning good people for using different words or expressions with the next.

**Genuine religion will not accept this contradiction**.

A sanctified heart should produce holy and useful speech. Just as one tree does not produce another kind of tree's fruit, true believers should not normally produce **curses, lies, boasting and abuse**.

Nevertheless, many professing Christians find it easier to control bodily appetites than to govern their words.

Let us depend on God's grace, refuse to **bless and curse with the same tongue**, and strive for consistency between speech and behavior.

## Wisdom from above is pure, gentle and peaceable (verses 13–18)

There is a great difference between **claiming wisdom** and **possessing it**.

Those who think or speak cleverly are not wise according to Scripture unless they also **live and act wisely**.

True wisdom is demonstrated by **humility and gentleness**.

Envy, malice and rivalry produce disorder and make people ready for almost any evil work.

Such wisdom comes from earthly principles, seeks earthly goals and operates through worldly motives.

By contrast, **the wisdom James describes closely resembles the Christian love Paul describes**. Each provides a clear test for our professed spiritual progress.

Heavenly wisdom is not disguised, crafty or deceitful.

It is **sincere, open, steady and consistent**, refusing the clever manipulations the world admires.

May purity, peace, gentleness, teachability, mercy and the **fruits of righteousness** in our conduct show that God has given us this excellent gift.
