# 2 Samuel 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Absalom's army defeated. | 1–8 |
| He is slain. | 9–18 |
| David's over-sorrow. | 19–33 |

## David's forces defeat Absalom (verses 1–8)

David repaid evil with good.

Absalom had wanted only **David** to be killed.

David wanted **Absalom** alone to be spared.

This contrast partly resembles the difference between humanity's rebellion against God and God's mercy toward humanity.

Which is more astonishing: human wickedness or divine kindness?

Israel now learned what happens to those who conspire against the Lord and his anointed king.

## Absalom dies (verses 9–18)

Young people should remember Absalom's terrible end.

He hung helplessly from a tree, condemned and abandoned.

His death displayed God's displeasure against rebellion toward parents.

Nothing can finally protect people from misery and contempt except heavenly wisdom and the grace of God.

## David grieves too intensely (verses 19–33)

**Ahimaaz** prepared David to hear of his son's death by first encouraging him to thank God for the victory.

The more our hearts are fixed upon sincere gratitude for God's mercies, the better prepared we are to bear suffering that accompanies them.

Some interpreters believe David's wish to die in Absalom's place arose from concern about his son's eternal condition.

Henry thinks it more likely that David spoke **without adequate reflection**.

He criticizes David's excessive affection for a son who had lived without godliness.

He also reproaches him for disputing God's justice and for resisting the judgment that David, as king, was responsible for administering.

The rightful claims of public justice should have been placed above his private natural affection.

Even the best believers are not always in a healthy spiritual state.

We often **grieve too much for what we have loved too much**.

Let us learn from David's example to avoid indulging or neglecting our children sinfully.

But even in David's love and sorrow, may we see a faint picture of the Savior's greater love.

Jesus **wept over, prayed for and died for** sinful, rebellious human beings who had set themselves against him.
