# 2 Samuel 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Nathan's parable—David confesses his sin. | 1–14 |
| The birth of Solomon. | 15–25 |
| David's severity to the Ammonites. | 26–31 |

## Nathan confronts David (verses 1–14)

God will not allow his own people to remain undisturbed in sin.

Through a parable, **Nathan led David to pronounce judgment upon himself**.

Those who correct others need wisdom and care in choosing how to speak.

But once Nathan applied his story, he was completely faithful.

He told David plainly: **“You are the man.”**

God's response demonstrates how much he hates sin, even when it occurs among his own people.

He will not simply ignore it wherever it is found.

David made no excuses and did not minimize his wrongdoing.

He confessed it freely.

When David said that he had sinned, Nathan recognized genuine repentance and assured him that his sin had been forgiven.

“You shall not die” meant, according to Henry, that David would not suffer **eternal death** or be separated from God forever.

But David would continue to suffer the Lord's discipline during his earthly life.

He would not be condemned with the world.

One especially dreadful consequence of public sin by people who profess faith is that it gives God's enemies material for mockery and blasphemy.

David's case shows that even when sin is forgiven, God may still correct his children severely.

A fleeting gratification of shameful desire brought David many years of painful distress.

## David's child dies; Solomon is born (verses 15–25)

Henry connects **Psalm 51** with this period of David's life.

Although David had been told he was forgiven, he prayed earnestly for pardon, lamented his wrongdoing and remained willing to bear its shame.

He was prepared to remember it repeatedly and receive reproof for it.

God permits us to pray earnestly for **particular blessings** on the basis of his general mercy and power, even when no specific promise assures that those requests will be granted.

David submitted patiently when his child died.

Later God compensated that loss through the birth of another child.

The best way to retain earthly comforts or have a loss made up in some other way is to surrender them willingly to the Lord.

God showed special favor to Solomon and directed that he be called **Jedidiah**, meaning **“Beloved of the Lord.”**

Henry expresses confidence that prayers for one's children are graciously answered both when children die in infancy—being well cared for by God—and when others live and are loved by the Lord.

## David treats the Ammonites harshly (verses 26–31)

David subjected the people of Ammon to severe slavery.

Henry regards this harshness as evidence that **David's heart had not yet been softened by repentance when these events occurred**.

The more deeply we recognize our own need for the Lord's forgiveness and experience its sweetness, the more compassionate, kind and forgiving we should become toward others.
