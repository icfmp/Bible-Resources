# Nahum 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The sins and judgments of Nineveh. | 1–7 |
| Its utter destruction. | 8–19 |

## Nineveh's sins and judgment (verses 1–7)

When proud sinners are brought down, others should learn not to become proud themselves.

The destruction of this great city warns individual people who accumulate wealth through deception and oppression.

By mistreating others, they create enemies for themselves.

If the Lord decides to punish them in this life, they may find no one willing to pity them.

Everyone who seeks personal prosperity, safety and peace should act not only **honestly and honorably**, but also **with kindness toward all**.

## Nineveh's complete downfall (verses 8–19)

Even the strongest fortresses cannot protect people from God's judgments.

When he determines that they must fall, they will be unable to defend themselves.

The **Chaldeans and Medes** would consume the land like swarms of destructive insects.

Henry also understands the multitude of hired soldiers to be among the forces that would consume Assyria itself.

He suggests that the term translated **“merchants”** in this passage may refer to those troops.

Those who have done evil to their neighbors will eventually experience the consequences.

Nineveh, along with many other cities, kingdoms and empires, has been ruined.

Their histories ought to warn us.

Are we any better?

Only the presence of true believers among a people provides a stronger defense than favorable geography or military strength.

When the Lord sets himself against a nation, everything it trusts in will either fail or become a disadvantage.

But he continues to show goodness to Israel.

**The Lord himself is an unconquerable stronghold for every believer in trouble.**

No enemy can storm or capture that refuge.

He knows those who trust in him.
