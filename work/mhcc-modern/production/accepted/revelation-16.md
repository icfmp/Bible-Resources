# Revelation 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The first vial is poured out on the earth, the second on the sea, the third on the rivers and fountains. | 1–7 |
| The fourth on the sun, the fifth on the seat of the beast. | 8–11 |
| The sixth on the great river Euphrates. | 12–16 |
| And the seventh on the air, when shall follow the destruction of all antichristian enemies. | 17–21 |

## The first three bowls and God's righteous judgment (verses 1–7)

We should pray that **God's will may be done on earth as it is in heaven**.

Here John sees a succession of terrible acts of divine Providence.

These judgments seem to recall **the plagues of Egypt**: similar sins are met by similar punishments.

Henry connects the seven bowls with the earlier seven trumpets, which he interprets as representing the **rise of Antichrist**.

In that reading, the downfall of the church's enemies resembles the manner in which their power first grew.

The earth, air, sea, rivers and cities associated with this wicked power are subjected to judgment.

The destruction expresses God's opposition to the evil in which that society had participated.

The angels witnessing and carrying out God's judgment praise **his justice and truth**.

They honor him for avenging his servants, whose persecutors had inflicted cruel suffering on the saints and prophets.

## The fourth and fifth bowls: suffering without repentance (verses 8–11)

The human heart is so deeply corrupted that even the most severe misery does not by itself bring a sinner to repentance.

**God's special grace is necessary**.

Hell itself is filled with blasphemy.

People who understand human history, Scripture and their own hearts should know that suffering sometimes makes sinners **more furious against God**, even when they recognize his hand in their troubles.

Let sinners seek **repentance through Christ** and the renewing grace of the Holy Spirit now.

Otherwise, they will remain unhumbled and unrepentant, adding guilt to misery.

**Darkness** is the opposite of knowledge and wisdom.

It therefore pictures the confusion and folly of idolatrous followers of the beast.

Darkness also opposes pleasure and joy and thus pictures **anguish and inward distress**.

## The sixth bowl: Euphrates, deception and watchfulness (verses 12–16)

Henry offers alternative possible interpretations of the drying of the Euphrates.

It **probably** symbolizes, in one reading, **the destruction of Turkish power and idolatry**, preparing a way for the return of the Jews.

In another reading, **Babylon represents Rome** under a symbolic name, because the city could not then be named openly.

In that case, the fall of Rome would also devastate the trade and resources on which its power depended.

Henry adds that **perhaps** an opening would follow for the Eastern nations to join Christ's church.

These are **his tentative historical interpretations**, not established identifications or a timetable supplied by the prophecy.

Before losing his power, the great dragon gathers his remaining forces for one desperate conflict.

God warns the church so that his people may prepare for severe trials and temptation.

Christ therefore calls his professed servants to **watch**, expect his sudden return, and avoid the shame of being found apostates or hypocrites.

Christians disagree about **the dates and seasons** of future events.

Yet on one essential point they agree: **Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, will return suddenly to judge the world**.

For people who live near him, that coming is a joyful hope.

They do not wish to postpone it.

## The seventh bowl and Babylon's overthrow (verses 17–21)

When the seventh angel emptied his bowl, Babylon's destruction was completed.

The church triumphant in heaven saw it and rejoiced.

The church still struggling on earth saw its deliverance and could rejoice in victory.

God remembered the great and wicked city.

For a time he had seemed to overlook its idolatry and cruelty, but he had not forgotten.

Even things that had appeared most secure were carried away in the collapse.

Yet people continued to blaspheme.

The severest judgments cannot produce repentance **without God's grace**.

Indeed, becoming more stubborn against God in response to his righteous judgments is a sure warning of final destruction.
