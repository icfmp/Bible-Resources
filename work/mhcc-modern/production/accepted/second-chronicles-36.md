# 2 Chronicles 36 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The destruction of Jerusalem. | 1–21 |
| The proclamation of Cyrus. | 22, 23 |

## Jerusalem is destroyed (verses 1–21)

The ruin of Judah and Jerusalem unfolded gradually.

God repeatedly tried to call the people back through **his word, ministers, conscience and the events of Providence**.

These were all signs of his compassion and his desire that sinners not perish.

Consider the dreadful destruction that sin brings!

If we value our earthly blessings and want them to last, we should keep sin—the worm that attacks their root—away from them.

The people had often plowed and sown the land during **seventh years** when it should have rested.

Now it remained unsown and uncultivated for **seventy years, ten times seven**.

In the end, human disobedience cannot deprive God of the glory due to him.

If the people would not let the land rest, God would make it rest.

O Lord, what place will your justice spare if **Jerusalem itself** has fallen?

If the city where you delighted could be destroyed for wickedness, let us not grow proud, but fear.

## Cyrus announces the return (verses 22 and 23)

God promised the restoration of the exiles and the rebuilding of Jerusalem after **seventy years**.

At last the appointed time to favor Zion came.

God's church may be **cast down**, but it is not **cast off**.

His people may be corrected, but they are not abandoned.

They may be put into a furnace, but they will not be destroyed or left there longer than necessary for the worthless material to be refined away.

God may contend with his people for a long time, but he will not contend forever.

As the books of Chronicles close, we should reflect on the destruction that sin has brought into the whole world—and even into God's own church.

Let us tremble at these records while recognizing in a few faithful lives that God has never left himself without witnesses.

Having considered this truthful picture of fallen human nature, compare it with human nature renewed through **God's almighty grace**, through the justifying righteousness of our Savior Jesus Christ that adorns the soul.
