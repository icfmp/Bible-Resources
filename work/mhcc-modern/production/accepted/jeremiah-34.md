# Jeremiah 34 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Zedekiah's death at Babylon foretold. | 1–7 |
| The Jews reproved for compelling their poor brethren to return to unlawful bondage. | 8–22 |

## Zedekiah is warned of his captivity (verses 1–7)

Zedekiah learned that Jerusalem would fall and that he would die in captivity.

Nevertheless, **his death would be natural**, not an execution.

It is better to live and die **repentant in a prison** than to live and die **unrepentant in a palace**.

## The people force their freed servants back into bondage (verses 8–22)

Under Israel's law, an Israelite was not to remain in this kind of service **beyond the appointed period of seven years**.

The people and their ancestors had violated that obligation.

They finally released their servants, but when it seemed possible that the siege might be lifted, they **forced the people they had freed back into servitude**.

People who try to deceive God with **pretended repentance and incomplete reform** end up deceiving themselves most of all.

Imagining that they are free to sin merely gives them the freedom to expose themselves to **the most severe judgments**.

When we disappoint God's rightful expectations that we will do our duty, he may justly disappoint our expectation of receiving mercy.

A reform motivated **only by terror** seldom lasts.

Solemn promises made to God and then deliberately broken **profane his appointed ordinances**.

Those most eager to bind themselves with an appeal to God's name may also be the quickest to violate their own promises.

Let us examine our hearts so that our repentance is **genuine**, and let **God's law** govern how we act.
