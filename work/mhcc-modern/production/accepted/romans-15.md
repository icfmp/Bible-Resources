# Romans 15 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Directions how to behave towards the weak. | 1–7 |
| All to receive one another as brethren. | 8–13 |
| The writing and preaching of the apostle. | 14–21 |
| His purposed journeys. | 22–29 |
| He requests their prayers. | 30–33 |

## The strong should care for the weak (verses 1–7)

Christian liberty was given **not for our own pleasure**, but for **God's glory and other people's good**.

We should please our neighbors for **the benefit of their souls**.

This does not mean indulging their sinful wishes or participating in wrongdoing to gain their favor.

If we try to please people in that way, we cease acting as servants of Christ.

**Christ's whole life was marked by self-denial**, not self-indulgence.

The most mature Christian is the one who most resembles him.

Nothing was more foreign to Christ's spotless holiness than **being made sin and a curse for our sake**, the righteous suffering for the unrighteous and bearing the reproaches directed against God.

He bore **sin's guilt and its curse**.

We are called to bear only some of the trouble that results from sin.

He bore the presumptuous crimes of wicked people; we are asked only to bear **the failings of weaker believers**.

How can we refuse to be humble and self-denying toward one another when we are members of one body?

The Scriptures were written for our benefit as much as for their original readers.

Those who know God's word deeply and can use it wisely are truly learned.

Comfort arising from **God's word** is the surest and sweetest support of Christian hope.

The Holy Spirit, our Comforter, is the pledge of the inheritance to come.

Unity of mind must be formed **according to Christ's command and example**.

It is God's precious gift, and we should ask him earnestly for it.

Christ shows himself **gentle and humble** to draw disciples toward him.

His servants, especially those strong in faith dealing with weaker believers, should imitate that spirit.

**God's glory** must be the final purpose of our deeds.

Few things promote it more than love and kindness among those who confess Christ.

People united in Christ have every reason to agree in affection.

## Jews and Gentiles should welcome one another (verses 8–13)

Christ fulfilled the promises made to the Jewish people.

Therefore Gentile believers have no justification for despising them.

Gentiles brought into God's church become fellow participants in patience, suffering and praise.

The Scriptures call **all nations to praise the Lord**.

This shows that God intends them to come to know him.

People will not sincerely seek Christ until they **trust him**.

The entire plan of redemption is designed to reconcile us both **to the gracious God** and **to one another**.

This produces lasting hope of eternal life through **the Holy Spirit's sanctifying and comforting power**.

Our own strength cannot accomplish these things.

Where hope is genuine and abundant, therefore, **the Spirit deserves all the glory**.

The apostle prays for **all joy and peace**—every kind of true spiritual joy and peace sufficient to overcome fear and doubt through the Spirit's power.

## Paul's ministry serves God's sanctifying work (verses 14–21)

Paul was convinced the Roman Christians possessed knowledge, goodness and loving dispositions.

He wrote nevertheless to remind them of duties and dangers, because God had appointed him **a minister of Jesus Christ to the Gentiles**.

Paul proclaimed the gospel.

Yet the thing that made his Gentile converts acceptable as **offerings to God** was **their sanctification**.

That was **the Holy Spirit's work**, not something Paul could produce by himself.

Nothing unholy can please the holy God.

Conversion belongs to the Lord.

Paul therefore took satisfaction in God's work, not in merely human achievements.

Even a great preacher cannot make a single soul obedient unless **God's Spirit accompanies the ministry**.

Paul particularly sought the good of people still living in spiritual darkness.

Whatever genuine good we accomplish, **Christ works through us to do it**.

## Paul's intended journeys and the gift for Jerusalem (verses 22–29)

Paul placed Christ's work above his personal wishes.

He would not abandon the urgent work of founding churches simply to make a desired visit to Rome.

We should always do **what is most necessary first**.

Do not feel insulted when friends put duties pleasing to God ahead of visits or compliments that would please us.

Every Christian should support good work, especially **the conversion of souls**.

Fellowship among Christians is like **a foretaste of heaven on earth**, anticipating their final gathering to Christ.

Yet even its richest joy is incomplete compared with **fellowship with Christ himself**, which alone fully satisfies the soul.

Paul was traveling to Jerusalem carrying material assistance for believers there.

**God loves cheerful giving**.

Every exchange between Christians should express the union they share in Jesus Christ.

Because Gentiles received the gospel through Jewish believers, they owed their Jewish brothers and sisters material help in their bodily needs.

Paul spoke cautiously about what he expected from human companions, but confidently about what he expected from God.

**We should expect little of fallible people and much of the faithful God**.

How delightful it is when God's gospel comes with the fullness of its blessings through the Spirit's power!

## Paul asks for prayer (verses 30–33)

Learn to value **the earnest, effective prayers of righteous people**.

We should avoid conduct that would forfeit the loving prayers of those devoted to God.

If we have known the Holy Spirit's love, we ought not neglect this service of praying for one another.

Those who want to prevail in prayer must **strive earnestly in it**.

Those asking others to pray for them should not neglect **their own prayer**.

Though Christ knows our needs perfectly, he wants us to bring them to him.

We must pray that God will restrain enemies' hostility and preserve and strengthen our friends' goodwill.

Every joy we receive depends on his will.

Let us pray earnestly with and for one another, asking that **through Christ and the Holy Spirit's love**, great blessings will rest upon Christians and upon the work of their ministers.
