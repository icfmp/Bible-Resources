# Isaiah 65 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The calling of the Gentiles, and the rejection of the Jews. | 1–7 |
| The Lord would preserve a remnant. | 8–10 |
| Judgments upon the wicked. | 11–16 |
| The future happy and flourishing state of the church. | 17–25 |

## God calls the Gentiles and confronts unbelief (verses 1–7)

The Gentiles began seeking God and found him because **he had first sought and found them**.

Sometimes the Lord confronts a careless pleasure-seeker or a person openly hostile to him with the words, **“Here I am!”**

A sudden change follows.

Throughout the gospel age, Christ has waited to show mercy.

But the Jews, though invited, **would not come**.

Henry therefore says their rejection did not happen without reason.

They insisted on following whatever pleased themselves.

They grieved and resisted **the Holy Spirit**.

They abandoned God's temple to sacrifice in the groves.

They disregarded the difference between clean and unclean foods **before the gospel removed those dietary distinctions**.

Henry suggests this behavior may represent all forbidden pleasures and everything people think they will gain through sin—that hateful thing God opposes.

Jesus spoke many warnings against the **pride and hypocrisy** he encountered.

The charge against such conduct is clear.

We too must watch against pride and preferring ourselves to others.

Every sin, including **the most secret thought of the heart**, is known to God and will come under his judgment.

## The Lord preserves a remnant (verses 8–10)

A cluster of grapes that seems unripe and worthless may still contain **new wine**.

The Jewish people have continued as a distinct people, providing witness to the fulfillment of ancient promises and prophecies.

God's chosen ones, the spiritual offspring of **praying Jacob**, will inherit his mountains of happiness and joy.

He will bring them safely there through **the valley of tears**.

Everything ultimately displays God's glory in **the redemption of sinners**.

## The different ends of faithful and unfaithful people (verses 11–16)

Here the prophet contrasts **the godly with the wicked** and **Jews who believed with those who continued in unbelief**.

The wicked spread a table for a host of pagan deities and poured out offerings to the many false gods.

Those worshipers were willing to spend generously for their idols.

This should shame people who claim to worship the true God while begrudging his service.

See how malicious sin really is.

It means **choosing to do what we know displeases God**.

In every nation and age, the Lord leaves people who persist in wrongdoing and despise his gospel's call to face the consequences.

But his servants receive **the bread of life** and will lack nothing truly good for them.

Those abandoning God will be ashamed of their false confidence in **their own righteousness** and the hopes built upon it.

Worldly people congratulate themselves over possessions and prosperity.

God's servants rejoice **in God himself**.

He is their strength and their portion.

They honor him as **the God of truth**.

He promised that **all the families of the earth** would be blessed in him.

His people count themselves happy to belong to the God who has made them forget their troubles.

## The promised new creation and peace (verses 17–25)

Through the grace and comfort believers receive from **Jesus Christ**, we should understand the promise of **a new heaven and a new earth**.

The earlier disorder, sin and misery of humanity will no longer be remembered or repeated.

The prophet describes the coming happiness of the church through many images.

A person who dies at **a hundred years old** will be regarded as dying young; such long life will be expected.

**Henry explicitly leaves the precise fulfillment open**, saying that only events will determine what these images mean.

Nevertheless, he reasons that if Christianity became universal, the decline of violence and evil could greatly extend human life.

During those blessed times, God's people will enjoy **the fruits of their labor**.

Children will no longer bring misery to their parents or endure misery themselves.

Sinful dispositions will be completely brought under control, and everyone will live in harmony.

The church on earth will enjoy a happiness resembling heaven.

This prophecy assures Christ's servants that a time is approaching when they will enjoy undisturbed all that they need for true happiness.

As **God's fellow workers**, let us attend his ordinances and obey his commands.
