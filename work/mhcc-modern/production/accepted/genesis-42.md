# Genesis 42 — Modern English

## Jacob sends his sons to Egypt (verses 1–6)

Jacob saw that his neighbors had bought grain in Egypt and brought it home. Seeing others receive what they need can spur us to act.

Will other people find food for their souls while we starve, even though that food is available?

Once we know where help can be found, we should seek it promptly, without shrinking from the effort or resenting the expense.

This is especially true when our immortal souls are at stake.

Christ has made provision for us, but we must come to him and seek it from him.

## Joseph deals sternly with his brothers (verses 7–20)

Joseph treated his brothers harshly, not to take revenge but to lead them to repentance.

When he did not see Benjamin with them, he suspected they might have disposed of him as they had disposed of Joseph.

He therefore gave them opportunities to speak about their father and brother.

In his providence, God sometimes seems harsh toward those he loves and speaks sternly to people for whom he has great mercy in store.

Joseph finally decided that one of the brothers would remain behind while the others returned home to bring Benjamin.

His statement, “I fear God,” should have encouraged them.

It was as though he said, “You can trust me not to wrong you. I dare not do so, because I know that someone greater than I am stands over me.”

We can expect fair treatment from those who truly fear God.

## The brothers remember their guilt (verses 21–24)

One task of conscience is to bring back to mind things said and done long ago.

When Joseph's brothers first committed their crime, they treated it lightly and sat down to eat.

But now, many years later, their consciences accused them.

This shows one benefit of suffering: it can awaken conscience and bring forgotten sins back to our attention.

It also shows the burden of guilt for wrongs done to others.

When we think someone has wronged us, we ought to remember how we ourselves have wronged other people.

Reuben alone could look back with some comfort. He had done what he could to prevent the crime.

When others suffer, it is a great comfort to know that we did not join in their wrongdoing but spoke against it as best we could.

Joseph withdrew and wept.

His judgment told him to continue treating his brothers as strangers because they were not yet humbled enough.

But his natural love for them still moved him deeply.

## The brothers return with grain and money (verses 25–28)

The brothers came seeking grain, and grain they received.

More than that, each found that his money had been returned.

Christ, like Joseph, provides what we need without money and without price. Even the poorest are invited to come and receive.

But a guilty conscience often interprets God's kindness as a threat.

People burdened by guilt may read evil meanings into events that are actually working for their good.

## Jacob refuses to send Benjamin (verses 29–38)

Jacob was deeply troubled by the report his sons brought home.

Even the money Joseph had kindly returned frightened him.

He blamed his sons, fearing that they had provoked the Egyptians and had wrongfully taken back the money.

Jacob plainly distrusted them. He remembered that he had never seen Joseph again after Joseph went away with them.

A family is in a sad state when children behave so badly that their parents no longer know whether they can trust them.

Jacob regarded Joseph as lost and Simeon and Benjamin as endangered.

“All these things are against me,” he concluded.

But it turned out that all of them were working for his good and the good of his family.

We often think that something is against us when it is actually working in our favor.

We suffer in our health, possessions, reputation, and relationships and suppose that everything is against us.

Yet those very troubles can be working for us an eternal weight of glory.

So the Lord Jesus sometimes hides himself and his favor, correcting people for whom he has loving purposes.

Through painful correction and humbling conviction, he breaks the stubbornness and pride of the heart and brings people to true repentance.

Even before sinners fully know him or experience his grace, he seeks their good and sustains them as they wait for him.

May we do the same: refuse discouragement, look for no other refuge, and humble ourselves more and more under his mighty hand.

At the right time, he will answer our prayers and do more for us than we expect.
