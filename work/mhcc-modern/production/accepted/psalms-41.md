# Psalm 41 — Modern English

## God's care for those who care for the needy (verses 1–4)

God's people are not exempt from poverty, illness or outward suffering. But the Lord considers their condition and gives them what they need in due time.

Following their Master's example, believers learn to show concern for poor and suffering brothers and sisters. This kind of godliness often receives earthly blessings.

Yet nothing troubles a repentant believer more than the fear of **God's displeasure** or the discovery of sin still living in the heart.

**Sin is the sickness of the soul**. Pardoning mercy heals its guilt, and renewing grace heals its corruption.

We should therefore seek spiritual healing with greater earnestness than we seek bodily health.

## David suffers betrayal and praises God (verses 5–13)

We rightly lament the scarcity of sincere friendship among human beings, but the past was no better.

Someone whom David deeply trusted joined his enemies.

We should not be surprised when people we considered friends treat us badly.

Yet we ourselves have broken promises made to God. We receive food from his hand every day and still raise our heel against him.

We must not delight in the downfall of our enemies. We can, however, rejoice when their harmful plans are defeated.

A mercy becomes doubly precious when we recognize **God's favor** behind it, whether the blessing concerns one person or the public good.

Without his constant sustaining grace, we could not remain standing.

While still on earth, let us join wholeheartedly in the praise that God's redeemed people offer to their God and Savior both here and in heaven.
