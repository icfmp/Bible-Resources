# Micah 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The general prevalence of wickedness. | 1–7 |
| Reliance on God, and triumph over enemies. | 8–13 |
| Promises and encouragements for Israel. | 14–20 |

## Wickedness spreads even through families (verses 1–7)

The prophet grieved that he was living among people **rapidly approaching judgment**.

Many righteous people would suffer alongside them.

Even families and close relatives no longer brought comfort.

Neglect and contempt for **family responsibilities** are sad signs of widespread corruption.

People who refuse proper respect and service to their parents are unlikely to live well.

Micah found safety and consolation only in **looking to the Lord and waiting for God his Savior**.

During suffering, we should continually look to **our divine Redeemer** for faith and strength.

He will enable us to trust him and be good examples to those around us.

## Trusting God while waiting for deliverance (verses 8–13)

People who have truly repented of sin find strong reasons to be **patient under affliction**.

When we complain to God about the wickedness of our age, we should also confess **the sins of our own hearts**.

We must rely on him to bring rescue **at the proper time**.

We should not only **look to him** but also **look expectantly for him**.

Even during our worst distress, we need not despair of salvation when we trust the Lord **as the God of our salvation**.

Enemies may celebrate our ruin, but eventually their pride will be silenced.

Zion's walls may lie broken for a long time, but a day will come when **they are rebuilt**.

Israel will return from distant places, without turning back because of discouragement.

When enemies seem to prevail, we should not become hopeless.

**Though cast down, we are not destroyed**.

We can combine hope in God's mercy with **submission to his correction**.

No obstacle can prevent the blessings the Lord has decided to give his church.

## God's shepherding and complete forgiveness (verses 14–20)

When God is preparing to deliver his people, he moves their friends **to pray for them**.

Henry understands the prophet's prayer as applying spiritually to **Christ, the great Shepherd of his sheep**.

Christ leads and cares for his people while they travel through **this world, where they are present but do not belong**.

God's promise in answer to prayer is that he will act for them with power reminiscent of **the miracles of earlier ages**.

**Sin brought the people into bondage**.

**God's forgiveness releases them from it**.

Everyone who experiences pardoning mercy has reason to be amazed.

When the Lord removes **the guilt of sin**, so that it can no longer condemn us, he also breaks **the ruling power of sin**, so that it cannot continue to dominate us.

Our own strength is insufficient to defeat remaining sinful desires.

But **God's grace is enough to subdue them**.

If sin no longer rules us, it will not ruin us.

When God forgives, he ensures that the sin is **no longer remembered against the sinner**.

He throws the sins into **the depths of the sea**, not merely into shallow water near shore where they might reappear.

They will **never rise again**.

He casts **all** their sins into that depth, because when he pardons, **he pardons completely**.

The Lord will finish the work he began for us and do everything our condition needs according to his promises.

Henry connects these promises with **Christ**, the success of the gospel through the end of time, **the future restoration of Israel** and the final spread of true religion throughout the nations.

God will fulfill **every part of his truth and mercy**.

Nothing he promised will fail.

The One who made the promises is faithful and will accomplish them.

The security of his covenant gives strong consolation to everyone who **flees to Christ for refuge and holds onto the hope set before them**.
