# Job 42 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job humbly submits unto God. | 1–6 |
| Job intercedes for his friends. | 7–9 |
| His renewed prosperity. | 10–17 |

## Job submits humbly to God (verses 1–6)

Job now recognized his guilt.

He stopped making excuses and hated the sin he saw in his heart and conduct, especially his **complaints against God**.

He was ashamed and repented.

When the Spirit of grace enlightens our understanding, the new knowledge we gain of God's character is as much greater than what we previously knew as **seeing something for ourselves** is greater than hearing reports about it.

God reveals his Son **to us** through other people's teaching.

But through the Spirit he reveals his Son **within us** (**Galatians 1:16**), changing us into his likeness (**2 Corinthians 3:18**).

When we recognize our sins, we must humble ourselves deeply for them.

True repentance brings sincere sorrow over one's wrongdoing.

God will bring those he loves to worship him humbly.

Saving grace moves us to **confess sin without trying to justify ourselves**.

## Job prays for his friends (verses 7–9)

After Job was convinced of his faults and humbled in repentance, the Lord recognized him, comforted him and honored him.

Satan had tried to prove Job a hypocrite.

His three friends had condemned him as a wicked man.

But when God approves a servant, the accusations of others matter little.

The friends had misrepresented God.

They had made **earthly prosperity a sign of the true church** and **suffering certain evidence of God's anger**.

Job had recognized more clearly that **judgment and reward belong chiefly to the coming world**.

In that respect he had spoken more rightly about God than they had.

Job prayed and offered sacrifice on behalf of the very friends who had hurt him.

In this he was like **Christ**, who prayed for his persecutors and always lives to intercede for sinners.

Henry regards Job's friends as **good men who genuinely belonged to God**, despite their errors.

The Lord would not let them continue in their mistakes any more than he had allowed Job to continue in his.

He had humbled Job by speaking from the whirlwind.

He humbled the friends in another way.

They were no longer to argue.

They had to unite through **a sacrifice and a prayer**, so that reconciliation could be achieved.

Believers may differ over lesser matters of judgment, yet they belong together in **Christ, the great Sacrifice**.

Therefore they should love one another and bear with one another.

When God was displeased with Job's friends, he still showed them **the way to peace**.

The offense begins on our side, but reconciliation begins through God's mercy.

Peace with him comes only **in his appointed way and on his terms**.

Those who understand how precious that peace is will accept even humbling conditions with gratitude.

Job did not use his vindication to insult his friends.

Once God had received him graciously, he readily forgave them.

In all our prayers and service, we should seek **God's approval**, not people's admiration.

## Job's prosperity is restored (verses 10–17)

At the beginning of the book, Job's patience during suffering provided an example.

The conclusion gives encouragement to follow that example by showing his joyful end.

His trials began through **Satan's hostility**, which God restrained.

His restoration began through **God's mercy**, which Satan could not oppose.

Notice the turning point.

Job's possessions were not restored while he continued **arguing** with his friends.

Restoration came when **he prayed for them**.

The Lord is pleased by **warm devotion, not heated disputes**.

He doubled Job's possessions.

We may lose much for the Lord, but we will never ultimately lose anything **because of the Lord**.

Whether he grants health or material advantages in this life or not, those who endure suffering patiently according to his will will be happy in the end.

Job's wealth increased.

The Lord's blessing gives people the ability to gain wealth and grants success to honest work.

Sometimes the last days of a godly life become its best days, the latest acts its best service and the final comforts its sweetest.

The believer's path resembles **the light of morning**, growing brighter and brighter until the perfect day.
