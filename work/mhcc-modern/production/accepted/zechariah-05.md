## The flying scroll (verses 1–4)

The Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments are scrolls, in which God has written the great truths of his law and gospel.

They are **flying scrolls**: the Lord sends his word swiftly (**Psalm 147:15**).

This vision reveals his righteous anger against sin.

If only we could see, by faith, the curse of God's law hanging over the guilty world like a dark storm cloud, shutting out the warmth of divine favor and ready to break in thunder and lightning!

Then how welcome we would find the news of **the Savior who redeemed us from the law's curse by becoming a curse for us**!

Sin destroys homes and families, particularly when people hurt others or give false testimony.

Nothing can shut out God's judgment—not locks, bars or walls.

Some of its consequences ruin a person's earthly possessions; others reach the soul and lead to everlasting punishment.

We have all transgressed God's law.

We therefore have no escape from its judgment except to **flee for refuge to the hope announced in the gospel**.

## The woman inside the ephah (verses 5–11)

Zechariah saw an ephah, a container shaped like a measure used for grain.

Henry takes this as a picture of **the Jewish nation filling up the measure of its iniquity**.

When that measure was full, God would allow its enemies to take it captive as punishment.

The woman seated inside represents, **in Henry's interpretation**, the Jewish church and nation in a later corrupt period.

The heavy lead cover expresses the guilt weighing upon a sinner, carrying the unrepentant toward judgment.

Henry further understands the vision as foretelling the **condemnation and dispersion of the Jews after the crucifixion of Christ and the rejection of his gospel**.

The container carried into a distant land would picture their removal from their country and prolonged life elsewhere, as in the earlier captivity to Babylon.

Henry considers this later period of suffering much longer than the Babylonian exile, and interprets the picture in terms of **Israel's blindness and unbelief**.

These are **Henry's historical and theological identifications**, not independent findings or a silent revision of Scripture.

The universal application is to beware of storing up wrath for judgment.

Every crime added to the record fills the measure further.
