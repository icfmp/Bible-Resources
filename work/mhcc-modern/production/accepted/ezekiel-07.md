# Ezekiel 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The desolation of the land. | 1–15 |
| The distress of the few who should escape. | 16–22 |
| The captivity. | 23–27 |

## The end of the land's prosperity (verses 1–15)

The abruptness and repetition of this prophecy show **how deeply the prophet was moved** by the disaster he foresaw.

The final destruction of unrepentant sinners will be just as inescapable.

Oh, may the wicked person's wrongdoing come to an end **before it brings the person to an end**!

For someone who refuses repentance, trouble does nothing but harm: it hardens the heart and stirs sinful desires.

Yet God can, through his grace, **make the very same suffering a means of much good** for another person.

The day of genuine trouble was near—not merely rumors or distant echoes.

Whatever visible effects God's judgment produces, **our sin is the root beneath them**.

The judgments would spread everywhere.

God would be glorified in all of them.

Now is the time of the Lord's **patience and mercy**.

But the time when sinners face judgment is approaching.

## The surviving people suffer (verses 16–22)

Sooner or later **sin brings sorrow**.

Those who will not repent may justly be left to waste away under the sins they refuse to abandon.

For many people, wealth is a **snare** leading to ruin.

Gaining the whole world can mean **losing the soul**.

Riches do not help in the day of God's anger.

The world's possessions cannot meet the soul's deepest needs or bring lasting satisfaction in distress.

Even **God's temple** would be no protection for people who rejected his ways.

Those unwilling to be ruled by the **power of godliness** are unworthy to keep the honor of its outward **form**.

## God's judgment brings captivity (verses 23–27)

People who break free of **the obligations of God's law** will find themselves bound by **the chains of his judgment**.

Because they encouraged one another in sin, the Lord would cause them to lose courage.

When God judges people according to what they deserve, distress reaches everyone.

May he enable us to seek **the good portion that can never be taken away**.
