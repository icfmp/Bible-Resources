# 2 Kings 22 — Modern English

## Josiah repairs the temple; the book of the law is found (verses 1–10)

Josiah became king at a young age, as Manasseh had done, yet their lives developed very differently.

This must be attributed to the distinguishing grace of God.

Those who raised Josiah probably also helped shape that difference.

His character was excellent.

If the people had joined his reforms as sincerely as he continued them, wonderful blessings could have followed.

But the people had become deeply corrupt and foolish through idolatry.

To understand Judah's true spiritual condition, we should read not only its historical records but also the writings of prophets who lived at the time.

While the temple was being repaired, the book of the law was discovered and taken to the king.

It appears to have been missing for a long time.

Perhaps it had been **carelessly set aside**, as some people leave their Bibles neglected in corners.

Or perhaps some idolaters had **deliberately concealed** it.

God's providential care of Scripture shows how precious it is to him.

Whether or not this was the **only surviving copy**, the truths it contained seemed new to both king and high priest.

No collection of summaries, extracts or lessons can preserve knowledge of God and his will as reliably as **Scripture itself**.

We should not be surprised that the people became so corrupt when the law had become scarce.

Those who led them astray probably tried to keep the book away from them.

Possessing many Bibles makes the sin of neglecting Scripture even more serious.

What greater contempt can we show for God than refusing to read his word—or reading it without believing and obeying it?

The holy law teaches us to recognize sin.

The blessed gospel reveals salvation.

When sinners discover the strictness and excellence of God's commands, they begin to ask, **“What must I do to be saved?”**

The ministers of the gospel point them to Jesus Christ, **the fulfillment and goal of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes**.

## Josiah consults the prophetess Huldah (verses 11–20)

The law was read before the king.

Those who honor their Bibles most deeply are the people who study them, feed on them daily and let their light direct their steps.

Awareness of sin and divine judgment should lead us to ask how we may be saved, what to expect and how to prepare.

People who truly recognize the seriousness of God's anger cannot remain indifferent to the question of salvation.

Huldah told Josiah what judgments were coming upon Judah and Jerusalem.

Most of the people remained hardened and unrepentant.

But **Josiah's heart was tender**.

He humbled himself before the Lord.

Those who fear God's judgment most sincerely are less likely to experience its final condemnation.

Although Josiah would be mortally wounded in battle, Henry says he **died at peace with God and entered glory**.

Whatever suffering faithful people must endure or witness, they will be gathered to the grave in peace and enter the rest God has prepared for his people.
