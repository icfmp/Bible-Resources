# Psalm 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David rejoices in the deliverances God wrought for him. | 1–19 |
| He takes the comfort of his integrity, which God had cleared up. | 20–28 |
| He gives to God the glory of all his mighty deeds. | 29–50 |

## David praises the Lord for deliverance (verses 1–19)

The opening words, **“I will love you, Lord, my strength,”** express the main message of the Psalm.

Those who truly love God can rejoice in him as their **Rock and Refuge** and confidently call upon him.

When God grants deliverance, we should consider all the details that reveal **his power and kindness**.

David prayed, and God proved himself a God who hears prayer.

When we pray as David did, we may trust the same gracious God.

The description of God's majestic appearing, especially in **verses 7–15**, goes beyond what happened in the ordinary history of Jesse's son.

Henry sees **someone greater than David** in the extraordinary language of divine power.

God will not merely bring his people **out of** trouble at the proper time.

He will also **support them under it** while it lasts.

The eighteenth verse directs Henry's thoughts to **Gethsemane and Calvary**.

Christ faced his deepest distress when Judas betrayed him, his friends abandoned him, the crowd mocked him, the comfort of his Father's love was withheld and the powers of darkness assailed him.

Surrounded by the sorrows of death, he prayed in deep anguish (**Hebrews 5:7**).

At the Savior's death the earth trembled and rocks split.

The Father brought him from the grave through resurrection, showing his pleasure in the Son and the work he had undertaken.

## David recalls his integrity and God's favor (verses 20–28)

Those who abandon God's ways turn away from God himself.

Even when we recognize many personal missteps, we must avoid a **deliberate and settled departure** from him.

David kept God's commands before his eyes.

Our careful resistance to **the sin that most easily traps us** provides evidence of an upright heart.

Even people who show mercy to others still need mercy from God.

Those faithful to him will discover that he fulfills his promises.

God's word is **pure, reliable and sweet**.

People who resist him and deliberately live contrary to him will find that he opposes them (**Leviticus 26:21–24**).

David described a gracious reward that, in general, belongs to those acting from sincere motives.

He spoke comfort to the humble and warning to the proud.

He also found courage in the promise that God would **light his lamp**.

The Lord would revive his sorrowful spirit, guide him around danger and give him opportunities and strength to work for God.

People walking through discouragement and darkness can take courage:

**God himself will be their light**.

## All strength and victory belong to God (verses 29–50)

When we praise God for one mercy, we should also remember the countless others that have surrounded us throughout life.

Many things helped bring David to his present position.

He recognized **God's hand in all of them**, teaching us to do the same.

From **verse 32 onward**, Henry sees the gifts by which God equips the spiritual warrior for conflict, following the example of the victorious Leader.

We must seek deliverance **through Christ**, not through our own strength.

Prayer cannot be accepted apart from reconciliation with God through Jesus.

David, as a type, points to **Jesus our Redeemer**.

Christ endured enemies, overwhelming sorrow, violent ungodliness, death itself and **God's wrath for our sake**.

He called upon the Father with strong cries and tears.

He was delivered from the grave.

He continues overcoming his enemies until even **death, the last enemy**, is destroyed.

Therefore love the Lord, our strength and salvation.

Call upon him in every trouble and praise him for each deliverance.

Seek to walk with him in righteousness and holiness, resisting sin.

If we belong to Christ, **he conquers and reigns for us**.

We will conquer and reign through him and share the mercy promised to the Anointed King and all his descendants forever.

Amen.
