# Joshua 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Five kings war against Gibeon. | 1–6 |
| Joshua succours Gibeon, The sun and moon stand still. | 7–14 |
| The kings are taken, their armies defeated, and they are put to death. | 15–27 |
| Seven other kings defeated and slain. | 28–43 |

## Five kings attack Gibeon (verses 1–6)

When sinners abandon Satan's service and friendship with the world and seek peace with God, their former friends may turn into enemies.

They should not be surprised when the world hates them.

Satan uses fear of opposition to discourage many people who are convinced of their danger and almost persuaded to become Christians, but who fear the cross.

Such experiences should encourage us to ask God for protection, help and deliverance.

## Joshua rescues Gibeon; the sun and moon stand still (verses 7–14)

Those who have only recently begun trusting God have as much right to protection and care as people who have served him faithfully for many years.

We should defend those who suffer because of their connection with us or because of the gospel.

Joshua did not abandon the Gibeonites, who had only recently become his dependents.

How much less will Jesus, our true Joshua, abandon anyone trusting him!

Our trust may be weak, but its object never fails.

God's promises should encourage our efforts, not replace them.

Joshua acted with great faith, and God answered through a miraculous prolonging of the day.

Henry explains that Joshua acted upon an **impulse from the Holy Spirit**.

It was not necessary for Joshua or the account of this miracle to use modern astronomical terms.

To the Israelites, the sun appeared above Gibeon and the moon above the valley of Aijalon.

They appeared to remain there for an entire day.

“Is anything too hard for the Lord?” answers every objection to God's power.

The miracle announced to the nations around Israel that the Lord was near his people in a remarkable way.

## The five kings are judged (verses 15–27)

No one dared speak against Israel during its victory, showing how thoroughly God protected the people.

The kings were called to account as enemies of God's Israel.

Their wickedness had reached its full measure.

The act of public justice against them warned Israel how seriously God hated the sins of the nations being driven out.

Henry also sees this as a picture of Christ's victory over the powers of darkness and of believers' victories through him.

In spiritual warfare, one important victory is not enough.

We must pursue the remaining sins that arise within our hearts and continue the struggle until it is complete.

The Lord will give light and help for that work.

## Other kings and cities are defeated (verses 28–43)

Joshua acted quickly.

How much can be accomplished in a short time when we are diligent and make good use of our opportunities!

The judgments displayed God's hatred of idolatry and the other serious sins in which Canaan had become involved.

Henry understands the destruction of those nations as a warning of the final judgment upon people who reject Jesus and despise the riches of his grace.

The Lord fought for Israel.

The people could not have won without his undertaking the battle on their behalf.

We overcome because God fights for us.

If he is for us, who can stand against us?
