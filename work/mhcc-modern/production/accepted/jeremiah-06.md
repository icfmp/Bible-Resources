# Jeremiah 6 — Modern English

## The Chaldeans invade Judah (verses 1–8)

No human strategy can succeed in resisting **God's appointed judgments**.

The more we indulge the pleasures of this life, the less prepared we become to endure its troubles.

The Chaldean army would enter Judah and soon consume what lay before it.

A day of visitation is coming upon those who continue carelessly in sin, convinced that they are secure.

How foolish it is to waste our time in trivial pursuits when **eternal salvation** is at stake and we have spiritual enemies to resist!

The attackers hurried forward, not because they wished to carry out God's purposes, but because they wanted to **fill their own treasuries**.

Yet in doing what they desired, they unknowingly served **his purpose**.

In its natural, unrenewed state, the human heart produces evil thoughts as constantly as **a fountain pours out water**. The stream continues, and the spring never runs dry.

The God of mercy is reluctant to depart even from a people who repeatedly provoke him.

He earnestly calls them to **repentance and reform**, so that they may avert the worst consequences of their sin.

## Judah refuses the ancient good way (verses 9–17)

When the Lord rises to judge, sinners of every **age, social rank and sex** are accountable to him.

These people had given themselves over to **love of the world**.

If we examine that sin by the standard of Scripture, we find it among people of every position and background.

The most dangerous enemies are sometimes those who **flatter us while we sin**.

May people learn to act wisely concerning their souls!

**Ask for the ancient paths.** The way of godliness and righteousness has always been the way God approves and blesses.

Seek the ancient paths made known in **his written word**.

When you find the good way, **walk in it**. You will be abundantly rewarded when the journey ends.

But those who refuse God's voice and will not flee to **the Refuge he has appointed** will learn at judgment that they have been ruined by **rejecting his word**.

## Every warning and means of grace has been rejected (verses 18–30)

God rejected the people's outward ceremonies as worthless to atone for their sins.

Sacrifice and incense were meant to direct worshipers toward **the Mediator**.

When people offered them as if they could buy permission to continue sinning, their offerings **provoked God**.

The sins of those professing to belong to the Lord made them easy prey for their enemies. They scarcely dared show themselves.

Believers may rejoice in the **hope of God's mercies**, even while they see those mercies only in his promises.

Sinners should tremble at **his judgments**, even while they see those judgments only in his warnings.

Judah's people were deeply rebellious, and each sinner became a source of corruption to others.

Those who surrender to sin soon become **tempters themselves**.

They resembled ore that appears to contain valuable metal, but after refining proves to be **nothing but dross**.

No warning, correction or rebuke could persuade them to separate from their sins.

They would be called **rejected silver**—material of no use or worth.

When **every means of grace** leaves people unrenewed, they risk being rejected by God and left to everlasting misery.

Let us pray instead that **the Lord would refine us as silver is refined**.
