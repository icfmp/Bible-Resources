# Psalm 34 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David praises God, and encourages to trust him. | 1–10 |
| He exhorts to fear. | 11–22 |

## David praises God and invites others to trust (verses 1–10)

If we hope to spend eternity praising God, we should devote much of this life to praising him.

He has never told anyone to seek him without hope of an answer.

David's prayers helped overcome his fears. Many others have looked to God through faith and prayer and found renewed courage and comfort.

Looking to the world leaves us perplexed and uncertain. **Looking to Christ** is essential to our salvation and every blessing that belongs to it.

The poor person whom society neither respects nor notices can still approach the throne of grace. The Lord hears and delivers.

Holy angels minister to the saints and defend them against powers of darkness. Yet **all glory belongs to the Lord of the angels**, not to his servants.

Taste and sight allow us both to discover and to enjoy. **Taste and see that the Lord is good**: recognize his kindness and take comfort in it.

Whoever truly trusts him is blessed.

For the coming life, God's people receive grace sufficient to sustain them spiritually. For this life, they receive what is genuinely necessary from him.

Paul could say he possessed everything and abounded because he had learned contentment (**Philippians 4:11–18**).

Those who trust only their own strength and efforts will eventually find themselves lacking. Those who rely on the Lord and quietly carry out their responsibilities will be cared for.

## Learn the fear of the Lord (verses 11–22)

Young people should begin life by learning to fear the Lord. Those who start early in his service enjoy a greater opportunity for comfort now and happiness forever.

Everyone wants to be happy. But human life is short and troubled, so genuine happiness must reach beyond this world.

The way of faith requires **watchfulness over heart and tongue**.

Not harming others is not enough: we must strive to **be useful and do good**.

We should seek peace and pursue it, willingly giving up some personal advantage for its sake.

When genuine believers suffer, they habitually call upon God and are comforted that he hears them.

The righteous are humble about their sins and do not think highly of themselves.

Nothing is more necessary for true godliness than **a contrite heart free from self-confidence**. Grace flourishes in such soil, and the free, rich gospel of Jesus Christ is its encouragement.

The Lord specially protects righteous people, yet they still experience many trials and hatred from others. Divine mercy itself may correct them, and the enemy of their souls also attacks them.

But their troubles cannot ultimately destroy their souls, because the Lord keeps them from falling into sin during suffering.

**No one is truly desolate except the person whom God has abandoned**.
