# 1 Corinthians 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The great privileges, and yet terrible overthrow of the Israelites in the wilderness. | 1–5 |
| Cautions against all idolatrous, and other sinful practices. | 6–14 |
| The partaking in idolatry cannot exist with having communion with Christ. | 15–22 |
| All we do to be to the glory of God, and without offence to the consciences of others. | 23–33 |

## Israel's privileges did not guarantee salvation (verses 1–5)

Paul reminded the Corinthians of the ancient Israelites to warn them against fellowship with idolaters and **false confidence while continuing in sin**.

Through a miracle Israel passed through the Red Sea, while the Egyptians pursuing them drowned.

That passage was **a type of baptism**.

The manna with which God fed the people pictured **Christ crucified**, the Bread from heaven. Everyone who receives him truly will live forever.

Christ is **the Rock on which the Christian church is built**.

From him streams of spiritual refreshment flow to believers, picturing the Holy Spirit's gracious work through Christ.

Yet Israel's privileges did not prevent judgment upon unbelief and sin.

**Neither possession of religious privileges nor a profession of truth can secure heavenly happiness** for someone whose heart remains unconverted.

## Learn from Israel's sins and God's faithful protection (verses 6–14)

Sinful appetites grow stronger when they are indulged.

Therefore, **restrain them as soon as they arise**.

If we want to avoid Israel's judgments, we should fear falling into its sins.

Those who deliberately tempt Christ may be left under the power of **the ancient serpent**, their enemy.

Complaining against God's providential dealings and his commands greatly offends him.

Nothing written in Scripture is useless.

We are obligated to learn from its warnings.

Others have fallen; **we may fall too**.

A Christian's protection against sin includes **distrusting personal strength and self-confidence**.

God has not promised to preserve us from falling while we neglect watchfulness.

But Paul's warning is joined to great encouragement.

Others have carried burdens and faced temptations like ours.

Through divine grace, we can also endure and overcome.

God is both **wise and faithful**.

He knows what we can bear and will not permit temptation to overwhelm his people beyond what he enables them to endure.

He will provide **a way of escape**.

That may mean removing the trial or preventing it from destroying us spiritually.

We have every reason to flee sin and remain faithful.

When we hold firmly to him, temptation cannot finally defeat us.

Whether the world smiles upon us or treats us harshly, it remains a danger.

Through Christ, believers are strengthened to resist **both its threats and its enticements**.

Reverence for the Lord, planted within the heart, is a great means of protection.

## Idolatrous worship cannot be combined with Christ's table (verses 15–22)

Participating in the Lord's Supper is a public profession of faith in **Christ crucified** and grateful worship of him for his saving work.

Through this ordinance, believers express their unity in Christ.

Like grains gathered together in **one loaf**, or members forming **one body**, Christians share fellowship with Christ and with each other.

Paul confirms this meaning by referring to the Old Testament sacrifices.

Those eating from the sacrifices participated in the worship associated with the altar.

Similarly, eating food **as part of an idol's sacrifice** meant taking part in worship of that idol and having fellowship with what stood behind the idolatry.

A person cannot share communion with **Christ and demons** at the same time.

It would contradict Christianity itself.

Christians should also beware of places and occasions that encourage them to offer themselves to **sinful bodily pleasures, the desires of their eyes or the pride of life**.

Such conduct provokes God.

## Use liberty for God's glory and others' good (verses 23–33)

Some food previously offered to idols could lawfully be eaten.

For example, meat given to a pagan priest might later be sold **in a public market as ordinary food**.

But believers must ask more than **“Is this permitted?”**

They must also ask **“Is this beneficial and does it build up others?”**

Christianity does not forbid ordinary kindness or justify rude conduct toward people who differ from us in their religious beliefs or practices.

The freedom to eat common food must, however, be distinguished from **participating in a religious feast involving idol worship**.

Christian liberty must never be exercised so carelessly that it harms others or brings unnecessary disgrace on us.

**Whatever we eat, drink or do, we should do everything for God's glory**, seeking to honor him.

This is religion's great purpose and a guide to conduct even where Scripture does not supply a detailed rule.

A holy, peaceable and generous spirit can overcome the hostility of even determined opponents.
