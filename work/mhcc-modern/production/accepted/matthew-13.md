# Matthew 13 — Modern English

## The parable of the sower (verses 1–23)

Jesus preached from a boat to avoid the crowd pressing too closely and to allow his voice to be heard.

This teaches us to use faithfully the practical arrangements Providence provides for worship, without seeking outward splendor.

Parables make divine truth clear to those willing to learn while leaving it obscure to those determined to remain ignorant.

**The seed is God's word**. Christ sows it, either personally or through his ministers.

Preaching to a crowd is like scattering grain: we cannot know where the seed will take root.

Four kinds of soil describe **four responses of the heart**.

The **path** represents careless hearers. Satan is both the great murderer of souls and **the thief of sermons**, stealing the word from those who neglect to keep it.

**Rocky soil** represents hypocrites who quickly display enthusiasm, sometimes exceeding the outward enthusiasm of genuine Christians.

They enjoy messages about free salvation, Christian privileges and heaven.

Yet without a changed heart, lasting conviction of sin, awareness of their need for a Savior or love for holiness, they claim assurance without foundation.

When persecution comes, or a tempting sinful advantage appears, they abandon or conceal their profession and seek an easier form of religion.

**Thorny soil** represents worldly cares.

Thorns came through sin and are part of the curse. They may serve a practical purpose, yet they entangle, hurt and eventually burn (**Hebrews 6:8**).

Worldly anxiety prevents us from benefiting from God's word.

The **deceitfulness of riches** harms us precisely when we trust wealth as a source of safety and happiness. It chokes the seed.

**Good soil** is marked by **fruitfulness**, which distinguishes genuine believers from hypocrites.

Christ does not say the good ground has no stone or thorn at all, only that nothing is allowed to destroy its fruit.

True believers differ in fruitfulness.

We should seek to bear the greatest possible harvest.

Hearing God's word is one of the best uses of our hearing. Let us examine ourselves: **What kind of hearer am I?**

## The weeds among the wheat (verses 24–30 and 36–43)

The weeds picture the condition of Christ's church **in this life and at the final judgment**.

The parable describes Christ's care, the devil's hostility, good and bad mixed together now, and their final separation.

Sin grows readily in fallen humanity, just as weeds grow without tending.

Good seed requires care, watering and protection.

When the servants complained about weeds in a good field, their master had not caused the problem.

Whatever is wrong in Christ's church certainly **does not originate with Christ**.

Openly wicked people and those persistently opposing the gospel should not be treated as faithful church members.

Yet **no human judgment can separate genuine believers from hypocrites perfectly**.

Even opponents should be instructed **with meekness**, not destroyed as though we were authorized to carry out final judgment ourselves.

The great day will reveal clearly what is hard to discern in this world.

God's coming judgment should restrain us from wrongdoing.

At death believers will recognize their own heavenly glory; on judgment day they will shine before the whole world.

Their light is borrowed from **the Fountain of light**.

Then their sanctification will be perfected and their justification publicly made known.

May we be found among them.

## The mustard seed and leaven (verses 31–35)

The gospel began in apparent smallness but would expand greatly.

So it is with God's grace in an individual heart. If grace is genuine, it grows even when its first beginnings are almost imperceptible.

Over time it becomes strong and useful.

The gospel also works like **leaven**, gradually but certainly transforming the heart.

Its work is often quiet and unseen (**Mark 4:26–29**) yet powerful and unstoppable.

The apostles introduced a small amount of gospel leaven into the great mass of humanity.

The Lord's Spirit empowered its growth.

When gospel grace enters a soul, it changes all its faculties and alters even the ways the bodily members are used (**Romans 6:13**).

These parables teach us to expect spiritual progress rather than instant completion.

Are we **growing in grace and in holy habits**?

## The treasure, pearl, net and householder (verses 44–52)

Jesus gave **four parables** here.

**First, treasure hidden in a field.** People who look only at Scripture's surface may undervalue it.

Those who search Scripture and find **Christ and eternal life** (**John 5:39**) recognize a treasure worth surrendering everything else for.

**Salvation cannot be purchased**, yet many things may need to be relinquished for its sake.

**Second, the precious pearl.** Everyone is occupied pursuing something: wealth, recognition, learning or pleasure.

Most settle for counterfeit pearls.

**Jesus Christ is the Pearl of great price**. Having him is sufficient for genuine happiness now and forever.

Gold can be purchased too dearly; Christ never can. When sinners recognize the grace of the Savior, other gains lose their ultimate importance.

**Third, the net cast into the sea.** The world resembles a great sea, humanity its fish, and gospel preaching the net.

The Lord who rules the sea uses the gospel to gather people for his glory.

At the end, **genuine believers and hypocrites will be separated**. Those cast away will be miserable.

**Fourth, the householder.** A faithful gospel minister is a skilled teacher who draws out useful truth, like a host offering food from **last year's store and this year's harvest**.

Long-held experience and new understanding both serve the church.

We must remain students at Christ's feet, **relearning old truths and discovering new lessons every day**.

## Jesus is rejected at Nazareth (verses 53–58)

Christ renewed his offer to people who had previously rejected him.

They dismissed him as **the carpenter's son**.

He was commonly believed to be so, and there is no shame in being the child of an honest tradesman.

His neighbors ought to have respected him more because he came from among them.

Instead familiarity became a reason for contempt.

Jesus performed few miracles there because of their unbelief.

**Unbelief hinders people from receiving Christ's blessings**.

Let us remain faithful to the Savior who has made peace for us with God.
