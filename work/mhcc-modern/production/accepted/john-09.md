## Jesus heals a man blind from birth (verses 1–7)

Christ restored sight to many who had lost it through illness or injury. Here he healed someone **blind from birth**, demonstrating power to help in the most hopeless condition.

This pictures grace giving spiritual sight to sinners who are naturally blind. The man could not see Jesus, but **Jesus saw him**. If we know anything of Christ, it is because he first knew us.

Unusual afflictions must not always be interpreted as **special punishment for particular sins**. They may instead be occasions for God to reveal his glory through his works.

Our lifetime is a working day. Let us not waste its opportunities, for we shall have time to rest when the day ends. Death's approach should make us diligent in both doing and receiving good.

Do the good you can **promptly**. Whoever waits until no possible objection can be raised may accomplish nothing (**Ecclesiastes 11:4**).

Jesus used a means that might seem more likely to blind a seeing man than to heal a blind one. Human reason cannot judge all God's methods. He often uses instruments the world despises. Those seeking healing from Christ must follow his instructions.

The man went to the pool blind and returned seeing. So God's appointed ordinances can send people away **strengthened instead of weak, assured instead of doubtful, joyful instead of grieving, and enlightened instead of blind**.

## The healed man tells his story (verses 8–12)

People transformed by grace remain recognizably the same individuals but become remarkably different in character. Their lives testify to the Redeemer's power and recommend his salvation to others.

We can see the change produced in a soul without seeing the hand that produced it. The Spirit works like the wind: we hear its effects but cannot trace its origin or destination.

## The Pharisees question the man (verses 13–17)

Jesus performed miracles on the Sabbath in a manner that offended the religious authorities. He refused to give way to interpretations that elevated human ceremonies above the substance of religion.

Works of **necessity and mercy** are proper on the Sabbath. Rest serves the day's sacred work rather than preventing it.

How many spiritually blind eyes have been opened through gospel preaching on the Lord's day!

Uncharitable judgments often arise because people **add their own inventions to God's commands**.

Christ was perfect in holiness and wisdom. His enemies found no better accusation than their repeatedly refuted charge of Sabbath-breaking. May our conduct likewise silence foolish and ignorant criticism.

## The man's parents are questioned (verses 18–23)

The Pharisees hoped to discredit the miracle. They expected a Messiah, but refused to accept Jesus because his teaching contradicted their traditions and he did not display the outward pomp they desired.

**Fear of people brings a snare** (**Proverbs 29:25**). It may lead us to deny Christ, abandon his ways or violate our conscience.

Poor or uneducated people with sincere hearts can draw sound conclusions from the gospel's evidence. People driven by contrary desires can keep studying and never reach the truth.

## The man is expelled (verses 24–34)

Christ's mercy is most precious to those who know how deeply they needed it. Strong and lasting love for him grows through personal experience of his grace.

We may not know precisely **when, how or by what steps** the Spirit changed us. Yet we may still find comfort in saying, **“I was blind, but now I see.”**

I once lived for earthly and sensual pleasures, but God's grace has changed me (**Ephesians 5:8**).

People who have experienced Jesus' saving power marvel at the determined unbelief of those surrounded by evidence of his goodness.

The man made a strong argument: Jesus was not the sinner his accusers claimed but must have come **from God**.

Let us examine ourselves. What are we doing for God, for our own souls and for others?

## Jesus receives the man (verses 35–38)

Christ acknowledges those who acknowledge him, especially when they suffer for his truth and the witness of a good conscience.

He graciously revealed himself to the man. The gift of sight had become an even greater mercy because the man could now **see the Son of God**.

Only God may receive worship. By worshiping Jesus, the man acknowledged his divine nature. All true believers will worship him.

## Christ rebukes the Pharisees (verses 39–41)

Jesus came to open the eyes of the spiritually blind, while those proud of their supposed wisdom remained shut up in ignorance.

People relying on worldly wisdom regarded the preaching of the cross as foolishness.

Nothing hardens the heart more than supposing that the applause of others guarantees God's approval.

Christ silenced the Pharisees, but their sin remained: they rejected the gospel of grace, so **its guilt remained unpardoned and its power remained unbroken**.
