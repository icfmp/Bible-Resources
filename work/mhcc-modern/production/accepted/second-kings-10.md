# 2 Kings 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Ahab's sons and Ahaziah's brethren put to death. | 1–14 |
| Jehu destroys the worshippers of Baal. | 15–28 |
| Jehu follows Jeroboam's sins. | 29–36 |

## Ahab's descendants are killed (verses 1–14)

Even in the most terrible events, including those marked by human crimes, we must acknowledge God's truth and justice.

The Lord never commands anything unjust or unreasonable.

Jehu destroyed the surviving members of Ahab's household who had shared in that family's wickedness.

When we think about the immense amount of human suffering and death, and when we consider the resurrection and final judgment, we may be overwhelmed by the multitude of unrepentant sinners awaiting judgment.

We may ask what ultimately caused all this death and misery.

The answer is **sin**.

Why then should we welcome sin into our hearts and look for happiness in what has caused so much suffering?

## Jehu destroys Baal worship (verses 15–28)

**“Is your heart right?”**

This is a question we should often ask ourselves.

We may make an impressive profession of faith and gain a good reputation.

But are our hearts truly right before God?

Are we sincere with him?

**Jehonadab** supported Jehu in both the acts of judgment and religious reform he undertook.

An upright heart is satisfied with God's approval and seeks nothing more.

If our purpose is to win people's applause, our foundation is wrong.

We cannot be sure whether Jehu himself aimed higher than human recognition.

Under the **civil law of ancient Israel**, idolatry was subject to death.

For the time being, that worship was removed from Israel.

May we desire equally that **idolatry be uprooted from our own hearts**.

The recorded civil penalty is part of this ancient history, not a command for present-day religious persecution.

## Jehu continues in Jeroboam's sin (verses 29–36)

It is right to question whether Jehu acted from a godly principle and whether he made wrong choices even while doing the work of reform.

Nevertheless, God does not leave genuine service rendered to him unrewarded.

True conversion requires turning not only from **grossly wicked acts**, but from every sin.

It requires abandoning not only false gods, but also false ways of worshiping the true God.

It demands leaving behind not only sins that damage our earthly interests, but also those **from which we profit** and through which we protect our wealth and standing.

Abandoning profitable sins especially tests our ability to deny ourselves and trust God.

Jehu displayed great zeal for removing false religion.

But he neglected faithful service in true religion.

He did not take care to please God or fulfill his duty.

Where such care is absent, we have reason to fear that saving grace is absent as well.

The people were equally careless.

It is therefore unsurprising that the Lord began to reduce Israel's territory, wealth and strength.

The people had fallen short in their duty toward him, and he caused their nation to fall short in power and prosperity.
