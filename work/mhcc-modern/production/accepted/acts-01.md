# Acts 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Proofs of Christ's resurrection. | 1–5 |

| The apostles unite in prayer. | 12–14 |

## The risen Christ prepares the apostles (verses 1–5)

The Lord Jesus explained to the disciples the work they would undertake.

He commanded them to remain in Jerusalem until the promised **Holy Spirit** had been poured out.

This would be **baptism with the Holy Spirit**, granting miraculous power while also enlightening and sanctifying their souls.

Because Jesus himself spoke the promise, we may depend confidently upon it.

**All God's promises find their Yes and Amen in Christ**.

## Jesus ascends to heaven (verses 6–11)

The disciples eagerly asked about matters their Master had never encouraged them to investigate.

Jesus knew that his ascension and the Spirit's teaching would soon correct their misunderstandings.

He therefore rebuked their curiosity rather than giving the political timetable they sought.

This warns the church in every age against **desiring knowledge God has not authorized us to seek**.

Christ had already told them their duties before his death and again after his resurrection.

That revealed knowledge was sufficient for faithful service.

It is enough that Jesus promises believers strength suited to the trials and tasks before them.

Through the Spirit they can bear witness to him on earth in the ways he gives, while Christ governs their concerns in heaven with **perfect wisdom, truth and love**.

When we stand idly watching or wasting time, thinking of his return should **awaken us to work**.

When we are trembling with fear, the same promise should **comfort and encourage** us.

May we await his return steadfastly and joyfully, diligently seeking to be found blameless.

## The disciples pray together (verses 12–14)

God knows how to provide safe gathering places for his people.

The disciples devoted themselves to prayer.

**Everyone belonging to God should be a praying person**.

The disciples lived amid danger and uncertainty.

Prayer is the proper response to affliction, because it brings fears and cares before God.

An enormous task lay ahead of them. Before beginning, they earnestly sought God's presence.

They waited for the Spirit's coming **by praying continually**.

People are best prepared to receive spiritual blessings when their hearts are inclined to prayer.

Jesus' promise that the Spirit would soon come did not make prayer unnecessary. It **strengthened and encouraged their praying**.

Even a small congregation can grow rapidly when its members are united in love, exemplary in conduct, earnest in prayer and wisely zealous for Christ's cause.

## Matthias is chosen to replace Judas (verses 15–26)

The apostles' central calling was to bear witness to **the resurrection of Jesus**, the great proof that he is the Messiah and the foundation of our hope.

Apostolic office was not given to create **worldly prestige or dominion**, but to preach Christ and the power of his resurrection.

The believers appealed to God, who knows every heart **better than any human being knows it—including its owner**.

God should choose his own servants.

When he shows us his choice through Providence or through gifts supplied by the Spirit, we should gladly accept his will.

Let us recognize his hand in all that occurs, especially when a responsibility or trust is entrusted to us.
