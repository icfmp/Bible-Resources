# Jeremiah 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God expostulates with his people. | 1–8 |
| Their revolt beyond example. | 9–13 |
| Guilt the cause of sufferings. | 14–19 |
| The sins of Judah. | 20–28 |
| Their false confidence. | 29–37 |

## God reminds his people of their early devotion (verses 1–8)

People who begin well and then fail to persevere will rightly be reminded of how promising their beginnings once were.

Those who abandon a faith they once professed often oppose it more fiercely than people who never knew it.

Such people have **no adequate excuse** for their departure.

God's spiritual Israel should acknowledge the debt owed to him for bringing them safely through **the wilderness of this world**, where so many dangers threaten the soul.

How sad that people who once appeared devoted to the Lord later live in ways that make their earlier religious profession **an added witness against their sins**!

As we grow in knowledge, let us be careful not to lose **zeal and warmth of devotion**.

## The fountain of life is exchanged for broken cisterns (verses 9–13)

Before the Lord punishes sinners, he **pleads with them**, calling them to repentance.

The charges he brings are the very matters we should confront within ourselves.

We should tremble at the anger and curse toward which people run when they reject God's favor and grace.

**Grace in Jesus Christ** is like water from a spring.

It refreshes, cools, cleanses and brings fruit.

It is **living water** because it gives life to dead sinners, revives believers who are growing weary, sustains spiritual life, and leads to eternal life.

It flows without being exhausted.

**Abandoning this Fountain** is the first great evil.

God's professing people do so when they neglect his **word and appointed worship**.

Instead they dig **broken cisterns that cannot hold water**.

These are the world and its possessions, along with human inventions that people trust and follow.

Let us cling wholeheartedly to the Lord alone.

**Where else could we go?**

How readily we surrender **the consolations of the Holy Spirit** for the worthless excitements enjoyed by enthusiastic but hypocritical people!

## Israel suffers the consequences of rebellion (verses 14–19)

Was Israel intended to live as a slave?

No. They were the descendants of Abraham.

Henry makes a spiritual application.

Was the human soul created to be enslaved?

No. Yet through sin it has **sold its freedom** and made itself captive to many sinful desires and passions.

Assyrian rulers, like **lions**, overpowered Israel.

People from Egypt also destroyed the nation's strength and splendor.

Israel brought those calamities upon itself by **turning away from God**.

The practical warning is simple:

**Repent of sin, so that correction does not become destruction**.

What business does a Christian have pursuing forbidden pleasures, empty sinful amusements, greed or ambition?

## Judah's fruitless pursuit of sin (verses 20–28)

Despite all the advantages Judah had received, the people became like **a wild vine producing poisonous fruit**.

People can become as enslaved to uncontrolled desires and lusts as irrational animals are to their instincts.

God warned them not to exhaust themselves pursuing things that could bring only **distress and misery**.

We should never despair of the extent of **God's mercy**, which is sufficient to pardon our sins.

Nor should we despair of **the power of his grace**, which can overcome even the strongest corruption in our hearts.

## Empty confidence and continued self-justification (verses 29–37)

God's judgments had failed to change the people.

Instead, they tried to **justify themselves**.

For people who make the world their true home and final portion, it becomes **a wilderness and a dark country**.

But those who dwell in God can say their inheritance has fallen in pleasant places.

The people's words reveal the presumption of sinners who have long stopped thinking seriously about the Lord.

How many days pass in our own lives without remembering him as we should!

The Lord was displeased with Judah's false confidences and would not make them succeed.

People may use all their cleverness, but they cannot find happiness through sin or discover any honest excuse for it.

They may move from one sin to another.

But **no one ever hardened the heart against God, turned away from him and truly prospered**.
