# Jeremiah 26 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The destruction of the temple and city foretold. | 1–6 |
| Jeremiah's life is threatened. | 7–15 |
| He is defended by the elders. | 16–24 |

## God's messenger must speak without fear (verses 1–6)

People sent to speak on God's behalf must not shape their message **to please human beings** or merely to protect themselves from harm.

Notice how patiently God waits for an opportunity to **show mercy**.

If the people continued refusing obedience, Jerusalem and its temple would be ruined.

What else could they expect?

**People unwilling to submit to God's commands make themselves subject to his judgment**.

## Jeremiah is accused and threatened (verses 7–15)

The priests and prophets accused Jeremiah of deserving death and **gave false testimony against him**.

The leaders of Israel came to investigate.

Jeremiah explained that **the Lord had sent him** to deliver this warning.

Ministers who remain faithful to the word they have received from God need not be ruled by fear.

It is deeply unjust to blame a minister simply for preaching **judgment and condemnation**, when the purpose is to lead hearers toward **heaven and salvation**.

Jeremiah warned the people that killing or injuring him would bring still more guilt upon them.

Anyone who harms, kills or hates a faithful person who corrects wrongdoing **hastens and increases their own judgment**.

## The elders defend the prophet (verses 16–24)

God's word gives sufficient reason to warn secure sinners that continuing in sin may lead to the removal of **the Spirit's gracious work and the privileges of God's kingdom**.

Consider the contrasting examples.

**Hezekiah** protected **Micah** and prospered.

**Jehoiakim** killed **Urijah**. Did he prosper?

Evil people's behavior and the consequences of their actions should deter us from following them.

Urijah faithfully proclaimed the message given to him.

But Henry judges that he **acted wrongly by abandoning his appointed work**.

The Lord permitted Urijah to die, while Jeremiah was protected amid danger.

Those who rely most simply upon the Lord are safest in the truest sense, regardless of outward circumstances.

Knowing that **all human hearts are in God's hands** encourages believers to trust him while fulfilling their duties.

He will honor and reward those who treat kindly **people persecuted for his sake**.
