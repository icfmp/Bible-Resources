# Esther 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Providence recommends Mordecai to the king's favour. | 1–3 |
| Haman's counsel honours Mordecai. | 4–11 |
| Haman's friends tell him of his danger. | 12–14 |

## Providence brings Mordecai to the king's attention (verses 1–3)

God's Providence governs even the smallest human concerns.

Not even a sparrow falls to the ground without him.

Consider the steps through which Providence brought Mordecai into a place of honor.

The king could not sleep because God had a purpose to accomplish through his wakefulness.

Nothing suggests an illness interrupted his sleep.

God simply withheld it, though sleep itself is a gift from him.

A man who could command **127 provinces** could not command **one hour of sleep**.

## Haman advises the king to honor Mordecai (verses 4–11)

See how pride deceives people.

Nothing reveals the deceitfulness of our hearts more clearly than our exaggerated opinion of ourselves and our achievements.

We must continually watch and pray against this tendency.

Haman assumed the king could not possibly honor anyone but him.

He was mistaken.

We should be cautious about assuming the admiration other people express is as great as it appears.

That caution protects us against self-importance and excessive dependence upon people's favor.

What a shock Haman received when the king ordered him to honor **Mordecai the Jew**, the very person Haman hated most and was planning to kill!

## Haman returns humiliated (verses 12–14)

Mordecai did not become proud because of his new honor.

He returned to his place and his duty.

Honor is well given to those who do not believe themselves above ordinary work.

Haman, however, could not bear the humiliation.

What real harm had Mordecai's honor done him?

Yet something that scarcely interrupts a humble person's sleep can break a proud person's heart.

Haman's wife and friends now foretold his downfall.

They recognized plainly that the scattered Jewish people enjoyed God's special protection.

But they were miserable comforters.

They did not tell Haman to repent, only that destruction was unavoidable.

God's wisdom appears in the timing of his church's deliverance, which brings glory to his own name.
