# Psalm 91 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The safety of those who have God for their refuge. | 1–8 |
| Their favour with Him. | 9–16 |

## God is the believer's refuge (verses 1–8)

Those who choose God as their protector **through faith** will find in him everything they need or can rightly desire.

People who have experienced that comfort want others to find refuge in him too.

**God's grace preserves spiritual life** from Satan's temptations, pictured as the traps of a birdcatcher, and from the contagion of sin, pictured as a deadly disease.

Great security is promised to believers even when they are surrounded by danger.

Wisdom prevents **groundless fear**; faith keeps us from being **overwhelmed by justified fear**.

Whatever happens, our heavenly Father's will is fulfilled. We need not be ruled by terror.

God's people will see both his **promises fulfilled** and his **warnings carried out**.

Therefore sinners should approach his mercy seat **through the Redeemer's name** and encourage others to trust him.

## God's care in trouble and at life's end (verses 9–16)

No event can ultimately harm a believer.

Trouble and suffering may still come.

They can be painful rather than pleasant for the present, but God will make them work **for the believer's good**.

Those who truly know God will love him and continually call upon him in prayer.

His promise is to deliver the believer **in his appointed time** and, meanwhile, **to be with that believer in trouble**.

The Lord will govern their earthly concerns and preserve their lives as long as remaining here is truly good for them.

They look to Jesus for encouragement.

The believer will live **long enough to complete the work assigned by God and become ready for heaven**.

Why would anyone want an additional day after God had no further work to do in them or through them?

Someone may die young yet have a satisfying life.

A wicked person, by contrast, may live for many years without ever being satisfied.

At last the believer's struggle ends.

Then sin, suffering and temptation are gone forever.
