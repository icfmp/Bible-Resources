## The privileges and holiness of God's people (verses 1–21)

Moses described **three privileges** God had given Israel to distinguish the people from other nations.

They were honors in themselves and pictures of the spiritual blessings believers receive in Christ.

**First, election.** “The Lord has chosen you.”

God did not choose Israel because the people had already made themselves superior to other nations.

He chose them through grace so they would become his own.

Believers are chosen in the same way (Ephesians 1:4).

**Second, adoption.** “You are children of the Lord your God.”

God did not need children, but the people needed a Father.

Every true spiritual Israelite is a child of God and shares in his favor and in the character he forms within his people.

**Third, sanctification.** “You are a holy people.”

God's people are called to holiness, yet any genuine holiness they possess comes from his grace.

Those whom he chooses as his children, he also forms into a people devoted to good works.

They should avoid whatever could bring disgrace upon their confession of faith in the eyes of those watching for their failures.

Our heavenly Father forbids nothing except for our benefit.

Do not destroy your health, reputation, family happiness or peace of mind.

Above all, do not ruin your soul.

Do not become a slave to bodily appetites or passions, making both yourself and others miserable.

Seek what is best and most useful.

The ceremonial rules regarding unclean animal flesh helped keep Israel separate from idolatrous neighbors.

The gospel makes clear that Christians are **no longer bound by those food laws**.

But we should ask whether we are truly God's children, separated from worldly ungodliness and dedicated to his glory through Christ's blood.

Does the Holy Spirit work within us?

Lord, teach us through these laws how pure and holy your people ought to live!

## Tithes, generosity and care for the poor (verses 22–29)

A second portion of the land's produce was required.

The arrangement opposed human greed, mistrust and selfishness.

It encouraged friendliness, generosity and cheerfulness.

It also established provision for people who were poor.

Israel learned that earthly possessions are enjoyed most comfortably when shared with brothers and sisters in need.

God promised to bless the work of those who served him and did good with what they had.

His blessing is essential to earthly prosperity.

Without it, our efforts can achieve nothing lasting.

Yet that blessing rests upon **working hands**.

We should not expect God to bless idleness and the love of ease.

It rests also upon **giving hands**.

People who distribute their possessions generously may find their resources increased.

Free and generous support of religion and other good work is a safe and fruitful way of using wealth.
