# Isaiah 26 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Divine mercies encourage to confidence in God. | 1–4 |
| His judgments. | 5–11 |
| His people exhorted to wait upon Him. | 12–19 |
| Deliverance promised. | 20,21 |

## God's unchanging covenant and perfect peace (verses 1–4)

The words **“that day”** seem to point, according to Henry, to the time when the **New Testament Babylon** will have been completely overthrown.

The **unchanging promise and covenant of the Lord** are the walls around his church.

The gates are open. Let sinners be encouraged to enter and belong to the Lord.

He keeps the person who trusts him **in perfect peace**: inward peace, outward peace, peace with God, peace of conscience, peace at every time and through every event.

Trust the Lord for such peace and for a lasting portion.

Everything sought from the world survives only briefly.

But those who trust God receive from him not merely comfort for the present, but **strength that carries them toward everlasting happiness**.

Let us acknowledge him in everything we do and depend upon him through every trial.

## Waiting for God through his judgments (verses 5–11)

The righteous person's path is marked by **steadiness, obedience and holy conduct**.

God makes their path plain.

Their responsibility and comfort are to wait upon him and keep their desires directed toward him, even in the longest and darkest hours of distress.

We must never let suffering turn us away from God.

In a prolonged night of adversity, let our hearts desire him and pray for grace to remain faithful.

**Religion without the heart has no real value**, regardless of outward profession.

Even those who seek God early discover that he is already willing to receive them.

Affliction is intended to **teach righteousness**. Blessed are the people who learn from it.

But rebellious sinners continue resisting God because they do not consider whose commands they despise.

Those who mock his warnings will eventually feel what they now refuse to believe: **falling into the hands of the living God is fearful**.

They refuse to see sin's evil now, but will have to see it later.

May they abandon sin and return while the Lord is ready to show mercy.

## Prayer, freedom and resurrection hope (verses 12–19)

Every creature, occupation and circumstance that brings comfort does so because **God makes it helpful to us**.

Even what seemed hostile may, under his Providence, become a means of good.

His people had known slavery under sin and Satan, but through divine grace they learned to hope for freedom from every former master.

Everything opposed to God and his kingdom will finally be overcome.

Suffering reveals our need of prayer.

Before distress, prayer may come **a drop at a time**. Under the pressure of trials, it can flow **like water from a spring**.

Affliction drives us into earnest, private communion with the Lord.

Henry suggests considering **Christ himself as the speaker addressing his church**.

Christ's resurrection is the guarantee of the deliverances foretold here.

His grace, like **dew or rain reviving plants that seemed dead**, can restore a church brought very low.

The promise may also refer to **the bodily resurrection of the dead**, particularly those united to Christ.

## A place of safety during the storm (verses 20 and 21)

When danger comes, withdrawing to a safe place can be wise.

Entrust yourself to God as the One able to hide you. He will shelter his people **either beneath heaven or in heaven**.

Their tribulations are limited to a little while. Once ended, they will seem small compared with the happiness that follows.

God's place is the **mercy seat**, where he delights to dwell.

When he comes to punish, he is described as going **out of his place**, for he takes no pleasure in the death of sinners.

Yet no truth is more frequently taught in Scripture than his settled purpose to judge those who continue in wrongdoing.

Keep close to the Lord and separated from the world's sins.

Seek strength through **secret prayer**.

A day of vengeance is approaching, and believers should expect suffering before it arrives.

But that prospect need not leave them restless or terrified.

**Rest in God. Remain in him. Wait patiently for his promises.**
