# Revelation 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The state of the church is represented under the figure of a temple measured. | 1, 2 |
| Two witnesses prophesy is sackcloth. | 3–6 |
| They are slain, after which they arise and ascend to heaven. | 7–13 |
| Under the seventh trumpet, all antichristian powers are to be destroyed and there will be a glorious state of Christ's kingdom upon earth. | 14–19 |

## God's temple and its worshipers are measured (verses 1 and 2)

The symbolic measuring of the temple seems to recall **Ezekiel's vision**.

Henry proposes several possible purposes: **preserving the church** during public danger, **testing its condition**, or **reforming it**.

God's people must be examined to see whether they pursue **his glory as the purpose** of worship and follow **his word as the rule** for everything they do.

Those remaining in the outer court picture people who worship wrongly or pretend to have sincere hearts; their hypocrisy will eventually be exposed.

God will preserve **a temple and an altar**—true worship—somewhere in this world until the end of time.

He carefully watches over his church.

The holy city represents the visible church.

It may be trampled and filled with **idolaters, unbelievers and hypocrites**, but its period of devastation is limited.

The Lord will ultimately rescue his people from their troubles.

## The witnesses suffer and are restored (verses 3–13)

During periods when the church was oppressed, God maintained **faithful witnesses** to declare the truth of his word and worship and the goodness of his ways.

They were few, yet enough to accomplish the task.

They prophesied in **sackcloth**, signifying both the persecution and hardship they endured and their grief at the sins against which they testified.

God sustained them through their long and difficult service until their assigned work was complete.

Henry interprets the **1,260 years** as an extended period of testimony during which **Antichrist**, understood as a great instrument of Satan, would eventually attack the witnesses with force.

He believed the witnesses' final suffering and restoration were **probably still future in his own time**.

He also acknowledged that Christians might again endure severe outward persecution, and that there was already abundant reason to mourn the state of religion.

He suggested that the depressed condition described could refer particularly to the **Western church**.

These are Henry's historically situated proposals about prophetic fulfillment, **not dates or identifications certain from the biblical text**.

People hardened against divine truth rejoice when they manage to silence, banish or kill faithful witnesses, because the witnesses' teaching and conduct trouble their consciences.

But the **Spirit of life from God** revives the spiritually dead.

He will restore his people and renew the church's seemingly dying witness.

Such revival terrifies its enemies.

Where there is guilt, there is fear; a persecuting spirit can be cruel but remains cowardly.

The vindication and public honor of those who suffered for God will itself be part of the judgment against their persecutors, both in this world and at the last day.

Christ's witnesses must not grow tired of serving or suffering, nor impatiently seize a reward before its time.

They must wait until **their Master calls them**.

The revival and exaltation of the witnesses brings a mighty upheaval to their opponents' dominion.

Henry cautions that **events alone will disclose the precise meaning** of those prophecies.

But the broader principle is clear: when God's work and witnesses revive, Satan's work is defeated.

## The seventh trumpet and God's kingdom (verses 14–19)

Before the seventh and final trumpet, a solemn announcement demands attention.

The saints and angels in heaven recognize God's and the Savior's right to rule the entire world.

But the nations answer God's righteous anger with their own rebellion.

The Lord begins rewarding his people's faithful work and suffering.

His enemies become more hostile and thereby increase their own guilt and hasten judgment.

The **opening of God's heavenly temple** might picture freer fellowship between heaven and earth: more frequent prayer and praise rising to God and blessings descending.

Henry nevertheless considers it **more likely** that the image represents changes in the church on earth.

During what he calls Antichrist's reign, God's law was set aside in favor of human traditions and decrees, and **Scripture was withheld from ordinary people**.

Now God's word is brought openly before everyone.

The ark's reappearance then symbolizes **God's returning presence and favor** through **Jesus Christ, the atoning sacrifice for sins**.

Henry relates this to **the Protestant Reformation** and the extraordinary events surrounding it.

The God who heard prayer answered it through mighty acts of righteousness.
