## Giving without hypocrisy (verses 1–4)

Our Lord warned his followers against hypocrisy and outward display in religious duties. What we do must arise from **an inward principle** and aim at God's approval, not people's praise.

Giving to the needy is good and necessary. The fact that hypocrites misuse it to promote themselves does not lessen its value.

Vanity is subtle. We can begin seeking admiration before we even notice the temptation.

Jesus said that those who give in order to be seen already have their reward: people see them. That is all they wanted, and it is a miserable reward compared with God's promise.

The less we dwell upon our good deeds, the more fully God notices them.

He rewards not as an employer paying a worker no more than the wages earned, but as **a Father generously blessing his child**.

## Praying without hypocrisy (verses 5–8)

Jesus assumed his disciples would pray. Finding a living Christian who never prays would be as unnatural as finding a living person who never breathes.

**Prayerlessness betrays gracelessness.**

The scribes and Pharisees fell into two errors: **seeking human admiration** and **using empty repetitions**.

If our prayers, in which we approach God about matters of eternal importance, are made for the sake of people's praise, it is just that praise should be our only reward.

God hears even a brief, silent longing directed toward him.

His answer is a gift of **grace, not a debt**; there is no human merit in asking for help.

When he does not grant exactly what his people request, it is because he knows the gift is not needed or would not be good for them.

The length and wording of a prayer do not manipulate him. Some of the strongest intercessions come through **groanings too deep for words**.

Let us learn daily from Christ how to pray, with the right disposition of heart.

## Jesus teaches us how to pray (verses 9–15)

The Lord's Prayer teaches both the **subjects** and the **order** of Christian prayer.

We are not restricted to reciting this prayer alone, or required to recite it every time. Yet using it is good, provided we understand what we say and do not repeat it without purpose.

It contains much in few words.

Its **six petitions** are arranged meaningfully: the first **three** focus especially on God's honor, and the final **three** concern our temporal and spiritual needs.

We seek God's kingdom and righteousness first. Only after praying for his name, kingdom and will do we ask for the support and comforts required in earthly life.

Every word teaches something.

**Bread** teaches moderation. We ask for what we need, rather than every luxury we might desire.

**Our bread** teaches honesty and diligence. We do not ask for another person's bread, or bread gained through deception (**Proverbs 20:17**), or through idleness (**Proverbs 31:27**). We seek what is honestly earned.

**Daily bread** teaches continual dependence upon God's Providence.

We ask him to **give** it, not to sell or lend it. Even the greatest people depend upon his mercy for everyday food.

We say **give us**, not merely give me. We must care for the poor and pray with and for our families.

We ask for it **today**. Just as our bodies need daily food, our souls should daily renew their desire for God. We need prayer as surely as bread.

The prayer also teaches us to **hate and fear sin** while hoping for mercy. We must distrust our own strength, depend on God's protecting grace, prepare to resist the tempter and avoid tempting others.

Jesus promises that those who forgive others will receive their heavenly Father's forgiveness. We must show mercy if we hope to receive it.

Christ came not only to reconcile sinners **to God**, but also to reconcile them **to one another**.

## Fasting without display (verses 16–18)

Religious fasting is a duty of Christ's disciples. Yet it is principally **a means of preparing the heart for other duties**, not a performance with value merely in itself.

Fasting expresses the **humbling of the soul** (**Psalm 35:13**). That inward reality must be our chief concern.

Do not seek public recognition for its outward appearance.

God sees what is done in secret and will reward it openly.

## Treasures, spiritual sight and masters (verses 19–24)

Worldliness is a common and deadly sign of hypocrisy.

Few things allow Satan to hold the heart more firmly while a person still maintains an outward religious profession.

Every soul looks for something to treasure above all else—something in which it places its happiness, confidence and delight.

Christ directs us toward **the invisible, eternal treasures of heaven**.

Through Jesus Christ we should make every effort to be sure of our interest in eternal life. Nothing earthly can compare with its incorruptible inheritance, secure beyond the changes of time.

A worldly person begins with a false principle about what matters most; therefore, the thoughts and decisions built upon that principle also go wrong.

The teaching about the eye and light also applies to **false religion**. A person may call something light that is actually deep darkness.

We should therefore test the principles guiding our lives **by God's word**, praying earnestly for the Spirit's instruction.

A person may perform occasional tasks for two employers but cannot devote the heart entirely to two masters.

**God requires the whole heart. He does not share it with the world.**

When their demands conflict, one master must be chosen.

Whoever clings to the world as the supreme good will despise God. Whoever loves God must renounce the world's friendship.

## Trusting God's fatherly care (verses 25–34)

Our Lord repeatedly warned his disciples against **anxious, distracting and distrustful concern** about everyday needs.

The poor may fall into this temptation just as easily as the wealthy fall into love of riches.

There is also a **proper and necessary care** for earthly duties. Jesus did not abolish that responsibility; he warned against carrying it into unbelieving anxiety.

Entrust the length of your life to God. Our times are in his hands, and his hands are good.

Entrust life's pleasures and hardships to him as well. He knows whether to make our circumstances sweet or bitter.

God has promised food and clothing; we may therefore look to him for them without being anxious over next year, old age or what we shall leave behind.

We should neither **boast about tomorrow** nor **torment ourselves over tomorrow**.

The One who gave us life and our bodies is surely able to supply the lesser necessities that sustain them.

If we attend to our souls and to eternity, we may trust him for food and clothing.

We must learn to accept the condition Providence has assigned us, just as we accept our physical stature. Worry cannot change his plans.

**Concern for our souls is the best remedy for consuming concern about worldly things.**

Seek first God's kingdom. Do not imagine that pursuing true religion will cause us to starve; God knows how to supply his people even in this present life.

Through **daily prayer** we receive strength for daily troubles and resistance to their temptations.

Happy are those who trust themselves entirely to God's wise care. May the Spirit expose our lack of trust and remove the worldliness remaining in our hearts.
