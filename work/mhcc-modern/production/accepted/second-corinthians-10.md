# 2 Corinthians 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle states his authority with meekness and humility. | 1–6 |
| Reasons with the Corinthians. | 7–11 |
| Seeks the glory of God, and to be approved of him. | 12–18 |

## Ministry uses persuasion, not coercion (verses 1–6)

Some people thought poorly of Paul and spoke about him with contempt.

Yet he recognized his own weaknesses and spoke humbly of himself.

We, too, should remember our limitations and remain humble even when others criticize or insult us.

The work of Christian ministry is **spiritual warfare**, directed against spiritual enemies and toward spiritual purposes.

**Physical force is not the way of the gospel**.

Its methods are **earnest persuasion, the power of truth and the gentleness of wisdom**.

A person's conscience answers to **God alone**.

People must be persuaded to follow the Lord and obey their duty, **not forced into religious obedience**.

Nevertheless, the Christian's spiritual weapons are powerful.

The evidence supporting the truth is compelling.

Sin and Satan resist the gospel fiercely within human hearts.

Yet see what victories God's word achieves!

Even means that appear weak to human eyes become powerful when the Lord works through them.

The preaching of **Christ's cross by faithful, praying people** has repeatedly overthrown idolatry, ungodliness and wickedness.

## Do not judge ministry by outward appearance (verses 7–11)

Paul's outward appearance seemed unimpressive to some who despised him.

But this was a false standard of judgment.

We must not assume that lacking impressive worldly qualities proves someone is neither a genuine Christian nor a capable and faithful minister.

Remember that **the Savior himself was humble**.

External prestige is not the measure of spiritual authority or service.

## Seek God's approval, not self-congratulation (verses 12–18)

Comparing ourselves with people who surpass us can help keep us humble.

Paul adopted a better standard than the self-praise of his opponents.

He would speak only about the responsibility and field of service **God had actually assigned him**, not boast beyond his appointed measure.

Few things lead to so much error as judging people and opinions through **our own prejudices**.

How often people assess their religious character according to the expectations and values of the world around them!

But **God's word** provides a different standard.

Among all forms of flattery, **self-flattery is the most dangerous**.

Instead of praising ourselves, we should strive to be **approved by God**.

Our boasting belongs in **the Lord who saves us**.

We should value other things only as evidence of his love or as opportunities to advance his glory.

Rather than seeking the applause of people, let us desire the honor that **comes from God alone**.
