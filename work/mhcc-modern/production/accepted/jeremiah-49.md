## The Ammonites face judgment (verses 1–6)

Among human beings, **power often wins against what is right**.

But the Almighty governs even powerful people and judges fairly.

Like the **Ammonites**, those who suppose everything they can seize must rightfully belong to them will discover their error.

The Lord calls people to account for **every dishonest act**, especially offenses committed against those who have no resources or defenders.

## Judgment comes upon Edom (verses 7–22)

The **Edomites** were longstanding enemies of God's Israel.

Their day of judgment had now drawn near.

The warning concerned them, but it was also given for the sake of **God's people**, whose suffering the Edomites had made worse.

Divine judgment moves from nation to nation.

The earth is full of upheaval, and no one can escape the agents God appoints to execute his just purposes.

Even amid human violence, we should recognize **the Lord's righteousness**.

## Syria and Damascus are humbled (verses 23–27)

How easily God can take away the courage of nations celebrated for their bravery!

**Damascus** became weak.

It had been a joyful city with many of the pleasures people desire.

But those who place their happiness in **earthly and bodily enjoyment** deceive themselves.

## Kedar is scattered (verses 28–33)

**Nebuchadnezzar** would devastate the people of **Kedar**, who lived in the deserts of Arabia.

A ruler who had captured many strong cities would not spare those who lived in tents.

He acted to satisfy **his own greed and ambition**.

Yet God directed the events to correct an ungrateful people and warn a careless world that danger can arrive when it feels most secure.

The inhabitants would flee far away, withdrawing into the depths of the desert and becoming scattered.

But **privacy and obscurity do not always bring safety**.

## Elam is judged, but not abandoned forever (verses 34–39)

Henry identifies the **Elamites with the Persians**; that is his historical identification in this commentary.

They had acted against God's Israel and would be called to account.

Evil pursues those who practice sin.

God would show them that he reigns.

Nevertheless, **Elam's destruction would not last forever**.

Henry understands the promise of restoration as finding its full realization **in the days of the Messiah**.

When believers read God's warnings against the enemies of his church, they can take courage that the result of the spiritual conflict is not uncertain.

**The One who is for us is greater than all who oppose us.**

He will subdue the enemies of our souls.
