## The vineyard and its wicked tenants (verses 1–12)

Through the parable, Jesus taught that God would set aside **the Jewish church of his day** as an outward institution that had rejected him.

The treatment faithful ministers have received in many ages is shameful.

Some have been persecuted by people who enjoyed religious privileges but failed to produce the corresponding fruit.

At last God sent **his beloved Son**.

We might expect people to honor someone whom the master himself loved.

Instead the tenants hated the Son **precisely because he was the heir**.

Christ's exaltation was the Lord's doing.

It is also God who raises up Christ in our hearts and establishes his rule there.

When he does so, it is wonderful to us.

Scripture, faithful preachers and above all Christ's incarnation summon us to honor God through our lives.

Sinners should beware of a proud, worldly attitude.

Those who mock or despise Christ's faithful ministers show the kind of reception they would have given **Christ himself** if they had met him during his earthly ministry.

## The question about taxes (verses 13–17)

Christ's opponents pretended to seek guidance about duty, but their real purpose was to obtain an answer they could use to accuse him.

One of the most dangerous traps for Jesus' followers is becoming absorbed in **worldly political quarrels**.

Jesus escaped by directing them to recognize the civil obligations their nation had already accepted.

Everyone marveled at the wisdom of his answer.

Many praise the beauty or cleverness of a sermon without **obeying its teaching**.

## Jesus teaches the resurrection (verses 18–27)

A correct understanding of Scripture—the fountain and foundation of revealed religion—is our best protection against error.

The Sadducees mocked the doctrine of resurrection.

Jesus corrected them by explaining the difference between **earthly marriage** and **life in heaven**.

Although marriage was established in the first earthly paradise, it will not continue in the heavenly one in the same way.

We create absurd ideas when we try to explain the world of spirits entirely through the arrangements of our present bodily life.

It would make no sense for the living God to remain the eternal portion and happiness of someone who had ceased to exist forever.

Thus **Abraham's soul continues to exist and act**, though for a time it is separated from his body.

Those who deny resurrection are seriously mistaken.

Let us pass through this dying world with a joyful expectation of **eternal happiness and a glorious resurrection**.

## The greatest commandment (verses 28–34)

Jesus willingly teaches those who genuinely wish to understand God's will.

He explained that the greatest commandment, which contains all others in principle, is **to love God with the whole heart**.

When such love governs the soul, it creates a willingness to fulfill every other duty and to do what pleases him.

The old sacrificial offerings only pictured **atonement for disobedience to God's moral law**.

They were not themselves effective apart from **repentance and faith in the promised Savior**, producing moral obedience.

Since we have failed to love God and our neighbors in this way and have often done the opposite, **we are condemned sinners in need of repentance and mercy**.

Christ approved the scribe's understanding and encouraged him.

The man was well placed to go further.

Knowledge of God's law awakens conviction, leads toward repentance and the awareness of needing mercy, and enables us to understand **justification through Christ**.

## David's Son and David's Lord (verses 35–40)

When we understand Scripture's teaching about Christ's person and offices, we are led to confess him as **our Lord and God** and obey him as the exalted Redeemer.

If ordinary people receive these truths gladly while the educated and powerful oppose them, the first have reason for joy and the second for pity.

Sin concealed beneath **a display of piety** is doubled wickedness and will meet an especially serious judgment.

## Jesus praises the poor widow (verses 41–44)

Jesus still watches what his people give.

He knows both **the amount** and **the motives**.

He sees whether our gifts come from love for him or from a wish to be admired.

Many people would criticize the poor widow's offering.

Few would willingly imitate her.

Yet Christ commended her, so we know her action was good and wise.

At the final judgment, modest efforts of poor people to honor their Savior will receive his approval, while magnificent works of unbelievers done from wrong motives will be exposed as worthless.
