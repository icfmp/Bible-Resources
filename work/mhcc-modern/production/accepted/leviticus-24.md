## Oil for the lamps and the bread of the Presence (verses 1–9)

The loaves of bread in God's sanctuary represented Christ, the Bread of Life, who nourishes his people's souls.

He is also the Light of the church and of the whole world.

His light shines through his word.

By that light, we recognize the spiritual food prepared for us.

We should feed upon it in our hearts each day, and especially from Sabbath to Sabbath, with thanksgiving.

The loaves remained before the Lord in his sanctuary.

Likewise, we should remain in God's presence until he sends us on our way.

## The judgment of blasphemy (verses 10–23)

The person who offended had an Egyptian father and an Israelite mother.

Henry understands the mention of both parents as illustrating the harm that can come from religiously mixed marriages.

This event led to a permanent law prescribing the stoning of blasphemers.

Scripture places serious weight on the command.

It applied to foreign residents as well as native-born Israelites.

Foreign residents were to receive the **protection** of the law against injustice.

They were also subject to its **penalties** if they broke it.

People who dishonor God's name may escape human punishment, but they cannot escape God's righteous judgment.

What hostility to God must be in the human heart when people speak blasphemies against him!

If those who rejected Moses' law died without mercy, Henry asks how much greater the judgment must be for those who scorn and misuse the gospel of God's Son.

We should guard against anger, avoid wrongdoing, refuse harmful associations with wicked people and revere the holy name that sinners dishonor.
