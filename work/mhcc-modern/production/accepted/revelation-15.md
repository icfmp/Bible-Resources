# Revelation 15 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A song of praise is sung by the church. | 1–4 |
| Seven angels with the seven plagues; and to them one of the living creatures gives seven golden vials full of the wrath of God. | 5–8 |

## The redeemed sing of God's holiness and deliverance (verses 1–4)

John saw **seven angels in heaven** preparing to carry out the last judgments.

Henry interprets their task as bringing the destruction of **Antichrist** to completion.

In his reading, Babylon's wickedness had reached its full measure, and therefore the time had come for God's full judgment against it.

Yet the scene also contains extraordinary consolation.

Believers still living amid the world's distress are like people standing upon **a sea of glass mixed with fire**.

They can look forward to their final deliverance even before it arrives.

Every new mercy they receive gives them reason to sing a new song of praise.

The more we discover of God's wonderful works, the more we will celebrate his greatness as **the Lord God Almighty, Creator and Governor of all worlds**.

But his title **Emmanuel, King of the saints**, makes him especially dear to those he has redeemed.

Anyone who understands God's power in judgment, the value of his favor and the glory of his holiness should worship **him alone** with deep reverence.

His praise rises above heaven and earth.

## The angels receive the bowls of judgment (verses 5–8)

In judging Antichrist and those associated with that power, God fulfills **the prophecies and promises of his word**.

The angels are prepared for their work.

Their **clean white clothing and golden bands across their chests** symbolize the purity, holiness, righteousness and excellence of God's judgments.

They are ministers of divine justice and carry out their task without wrongdoing.

They receive the bowls containing God's righteous anger.

Even the weakest creature becomes irresistible when the Lord appoints it as the means of his judgment.

The angels receive the bowls from **one of the four living creatures**.

Henry interprets the living creature as representing **a minister of the true church** and regards the action as an answer to the prayers of God's servants and people.

He expects the removal of Antichrist to shake the entire world.

Even sincere believers might face confusion and suffering while that judgment unfolds.

God's greatest deliverances for the church sometimes come through **awesome and astonishing acts of Providence**.

The church will not enter its fully blessed condition until its stubborn enemies have been removed and **halfhearted or merely formal Christians** have been purified.

When everything contrary to Scripture has been cleansed away, the church will become **spiritual, pure and united**, firmly established in faithful worship and service.
