# Hebrews 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An exhortation to be constant and persevere, The example of Christ is set forth, and the gracious design of God in all the sufferings believers endured. | 1–11 |
| Peace and holiness are recommended, with cautions against despising spiritual blessings. | 12–17 |
| The New Testament dispensation shown to be much more excellent than the Old. | 18–29 |

## Run the race by looking to Jesus (verses 1–11)

Believers are called to a race of **faithful perseverance in Christ** whose outcome is either everlasting glory or everlasting misery.

The sin that **so easily entangles** us is especially the sin to which our own habits, circumstances or age make us vulnerable.

As long as a cherished sin remains unrestrained, it hinders spiritual progress and strengthens discouragement.

When tired and fainthearted, remember **the holy Jesus**, who endured suffering to rescue us from eternal misery.

Fixing our thoughts upon him strengthens holy affection and restrains sinful desires.

Our trials are small compared with his agonies, what we deserve, and the hardships borne by many others.

Even believers are inclined to lose heart because of remaining sin and imperfect grace.

Although enemies may cause our troubles, God our heavenly Father can use those troubles as **fatherly correction**.

We should not treat affliction as meaningless, but neither should we collapse in bitterness or despair.

Our Father may let others continue in sin for a time, but he corrects his own children for their good.

Human parents sometimes discipline out of anger or selfishness; the Father of our souls **never afflicts his children merely to satisfy passion**.

Earthly life is a kind of spiritual childhood in which we still need teaching and correction.

When made perfect in heaven, we will understand his wisdom more fully.

**God's discipline is not condemnation**. Received with faith and patience, it promotes holiness.

## Pursue peace and holiness; do not despise grace (verses 12–17)

Affliction can make believers' hands hang down and their knees weak.

Yet they must gather courage for the spiritual race.

**Faith and patience** enable us to pursue peace and holiness as diligently as a person pursues a calling.

Peace with people from **different groups and parties** is helpful for holy living.

But peace and holiness must remain together: there is no genuine peace in continuing sin.

An unmortified desire may seem dead and then spring up like a poisonous root, spreading trouble through the community.

The danger of apostasy appears when someone, like **Esau**, values momentary sensual pleasure above God's blessing and heavenly inheritance.

People who despise these blessings now will not always regard them so lightly.

A profane person may desire God's blessing while refusing **the means God appointed to receive it**.

But God will not detach the blessing from his appointed way or combine it with indulgence in sin.

No one who sincerely seeks his mercy and blessing will ultimately seek in vain.

## Zion's covenant and reverent worship (verses 18–29)

Sinai was a physical mountain, illustrating how much of the **Mosaic order** concerned outward and earthly matters.

The gospel is kinder and suited to our weakness. Through Christ we may approach God with boldness.

But without a Savior, even the most outwardly righteous person must despair before Sinai's holy law.

The gospel church is **Mount Zion**. Believers receive clearer views of heaven and heavenly desires.

Every child of God is an **heir with the privileges of the firstborn**.

A person still devoted to selfish desires and earthly pride would find heaven's holy fellowship strange and uncomfortable.

**Christ is the Mediator of the new covenant**, bringing God and humans together, keeping them in fellowship, interceding for us, calling us back to God and finally gathering his people in heaven.

His blood establishes this covenant.

Unlike a cry for vengeance, **Christ's sprinkled blood speaks mercy for sinners**.

We must not refuse his gracious voice from heaven.

God's patient appeal under the gospel does not remove judgment against those who reject such kindness.

Acceptable worship requires **reverence and godly fear**, empowered by grace.

God is as holy and just under the gospel as under the law.

Believers' inheritance is secure and every saving blessing is given freely as they pray.

Seek grace to serve him with reverent love.
