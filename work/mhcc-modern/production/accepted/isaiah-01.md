# Isaiah 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The corruptions prevailing among the Jews. | 1–9 |
| Severe censures. | 10–15 |
| Exhortations to repentance. | 16–20 |
| The state of Judah is lamented; with gracious promises of the gospel times. | 21–31 |

## Judah's widespread corruption (verses 1–9)

**Isaiah** means *the salvation of the Lord*, a fitting name for a prophet who speaks so frequently about **Jesus the Savior and his salvation**.

God's professing people failed to recognize that every comfort and even life itself came from his fatherly kindness.

Many people are equally careless about their souls. **Failing to consider truth we already know can harm us as much as not knowing truth we ought to learn.**

Wickedness had spread throughout the land.

The prophet pictured the nation as a sick body whose disease threatened death. From foot to head—from the poorest worker to the most powerful ruler—there was no spiritual health.

Guilt and corruption were everywhere, the sorrowful effects of **Adam's fall**. Henry understands this picture as declaring **the total depravity of fallen human nature**.

When people refuse to repent, their spiritual wounds remain untreated, and ruin approaches.

Jerusalem stood exposed like the temporary huts built to watch over ripening fruit. Such shelters were familiar in the East.

Yet the Lord preserved **a small faithful remnant** in the city.

It is because of his mercy that we have not been destroyed.

The evil nature described here exists in each of us. **Only Jesus Christ and his sanctifying Spirit can restore our souls to spiritual health.**

## Sacrifice without repentance is unacceptable (verses 10–15)

Judah's countryside lay desolate and cities had burned. Those judgments moved the people to offer sacrifices.

But they approached worship as though they might **pay God to remove punishment while continuing in sin**.

Many are willing to surrender expensive offerings who remain unwilling to surrender their sinful practices.

They regard religious forms as good deeds that entitle them to reward.

Yet even the costliest worship is unacceptable without a genuine change of **heart and life**.

God not only refused such sacrifices; he hated their hypocrisy.

Sin is deeply offensive to him.

If we continue secretly cherishing forbidden sins or reject Christ's salvation, **even our prayers become offensive rather than acceptable**.

## Turn from evil and begin doing good (verses 16–20)

Repentance is not merely sorrow over past actions. **Stop practicing evil and begin doing the good God commands.**

We must not stand idle when there are duties to fulfill.

The sacrifices prescribed in Moses' law could not of themselves atone even for the nation's outward crimes.

But God has opened a **fountain of cleansing**, available to sinners of every age and social rank.

Our sins may be like scarlet and crimson, dyed deeply and repeatedly: first in the wool of **original corruption**, then through the many threads of our own deliberate transgressions.

Even after repeated backsliding, God's pardoning mercy can remove the stain (**Psalm 51:7**).

Life and death, good and evil, are set before us.

**Lord, incline us all to live for your glory.**

## Zion's corruption and promised redemption (verses 21–31)

Neither a holy city nor a royal capital fulfills its purpose if true religion no longer lives within it.

Impure metal may still resemble silver. Diluted wine may still look like wine. In the same way, outward appearances may conceal spiritual corruption.

People who **oppress the vulnerable rather than help them** bear grave responsibility.

Human rules and restraints can accomplish something outwardly. But only God can change hearts effectively through **his Spirit acting in judgment and renewal**.

**Sin is the worst form of captivity and slavery.**

Henry regards these verses as fitting most fully the redemption of **spiritual Zion through Christ's righteousness, death and powerful grace**.

The unrepentant face complete ruin. They resemble a tree scorched by heat or a garden without water, quickly ruined in a dry land.

Those who put their trust in idols or human strength will perish.

Even a strong person will become like **dry tinder**, easily broken and readily set on fire.

When sinners make themselves like stubble and meet God as a **consuming fire**, nothing can prevent their destruction unless they receive his mercy.
