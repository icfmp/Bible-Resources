# 2 Samuel 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Ishbosheth murdered. | 1–7 |
| David puts to death the murderers. | 8–12 |

## Ish-bosheth is murdered (verses 1–7)

Consider how Ish-bosheth was killed!

When difficulties arise, they should encourage us to act wisely and vigorously.

If instead they drain away our courage and effort, we may lose both our earthly lives and the heavenly crowns toward which we should be striving.

Do not love sleep so much that it leads to poverty and ruin.

A soul made idle is an easy target for the destroyer.

None of us knows where or when death will come.

Whenever we lie down to sleep, we cannot be certain we will wake again.

Nor can we know from whose hand death may reach us.

## David judges the murderers (verses 8–12)

We may be glad to receive something to which we have a rightful claim while sincerely grieving over the unlawful means by which it came into our hands.

We may mourn the death of a person even if that death appears to benefit us.

The men who killed Ish-bosheth shed innocent blood for wicked, self-interested reasons.

David was right, in Henry's judgment, to carry out the historical punishment against them.

He refused to accept help toward the throne when it came through wrongdoing.

The Lord had already brought David through many difficulties and dangers.

David trusted him to **complete the work he had begun**, without the help of criminal schemes.

David spoke of being delivered from every adversity as though it were already accomplished.

He knew that the God who had delivered him in the past would continue to deliver him, though many storms still lay ahead.
