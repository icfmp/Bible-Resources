# Psalm 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God is to be glorified, for making known himself to us. | 1, 2 |
| And for making even the heavenly bodies useful to man, thereby placing him but little lower than the angels. | 3–9 |

## God's name is glorious (verses 1 and 2)

The psalmist wanted to give God the glory his name deserves.

How brightly that glory shines even in our earthly world!

God is **our Lord** because he created us, protects us and cares for us specially.

The birth, earthly life, preaching, miracles, suffering, death, resurrection and ascension of **Jesus Christ** have become known throughout the world.

No other name is so widely recognized and no influence so generally felt as the Savior's.

But divine glory shines even more brilliantly in heaven.

Here on earth we hear God's excellent name and praise it.

The angels and blessed spirits **see his glory** and praise him for it.

Nevertheless, God is exalted beyond even their worship and thanksgiving.

Sometimes divine grace is seen wonderfully in **little children**.

At other times God accomplishes great things within his church through **weak and unlikely people**, so that everyone can recognize that the power came from him, not them.

Through such actions he silences his enemies.

## Humanity's dignity under God and Christ (verses 3–9)

Considering the heavens should lead us to turn our desires toward **heavenly things**.

What are human beings—so small—that God should honor them?

What are human beings—so sinful—that he should show them favor?

Humanity has been given authority over lesser creatures, subject to God.

This passage also points specifically to **Jesus Christ**.

In **Hebrews 2:6–8**, the apostle uses these words to demonstrate Christ's supreme authority.

Jesus is **the Man and Son of Man** who has received dominion over the works of God's hands.

The greatest honor ever given to human nature, and the greatest mercy ever shown to our race, are displayed in Christ.

The psalmist therefore had every reason to finish as he began:

**Lord, how excellent is your name in all the earth!**

The earth has been honored by the Redeemer's presence and continues to receive the light of his gospel and the governance of his wisdom and power.

How could our words be sufficient to praise the One who has every right to our obedience as Redeemer?
