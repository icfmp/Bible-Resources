# Genesis 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Sodom is destroyed and Lot is delivered. | 1–29 |
| Lot's sin and disgrace. | 30–38 |

## Sodom's destruction and Lot's rescue (verses 1–29)

Lot was a godly man, but there was not another person of the same character in the city.

The people of Sodom had become thoroughly wicked.

God therefore took care to save Lot and his family.

Yet Lot hesitated and wasted time.

Many people are like him.

They become convinced that their spiritual condition is wrong and that they must change, but they delay the very change they know is necessary.

Even the most righteous people are saved through God's mercy, not through any merit of their own.

**We are saved by grace.**

We must also recognize God's power in bringing people out of a sinful condition.

If God had not shown mercy to us, our hesitation would have destroyed us.

Lot had to flee for his life.

He must not keep longing for Sodom or looking back toward it.

Similar commands are given to everyone whom grace has delivered from sin.

Do not return to sin and Satan.

Do not settle down in yourself or in the world as though they could save you.

Reach toward Christ and heaven. That is what it means to flee to the mountain.

Do not stop before you reach them.

The destruction of Sodom reveals God's wrath against sin and against sinners in every age.

Let it teach us how evil and destructive sin truly is.

Sin leads to ruin.

## Lot's sin and disgrace (verses 30–38)

Notice the danger of becoming too sure of ourselves.

In Sodom, Lot remained pure.

He grieved over the city's wickedness and testified against it.

Yet later, while alone in the mountains, believing himself safely beyond temptation, he fell into a shameful sin.

Whoever thinks he is standing high and securely must be careful not to fall.

Notice also the danger of drunkenness.

It is not only a serious sin in itself. It opens the door to other sins that leave deep and lasting wounds of shame.

People often do things while drunk that would horrify them to contemplate when sober.

There is also danger in temptations that come from relatives and friends.

These are people we love and respect, and from whom we expect kindness.

Yet we must fear a trap wherever we are and remain constantly on our guard.

Nothing can excuse the actions of Lot's daughters, or Lot's own conduct.

There is scarcely any explanation but the truth that the human heart is deceitful above all things and desperately wicked.

Who can understand it?

Scripture says nothing more about Lot from this point on.

That silence teaches us something about drunkenness: it not only makes people forget, but also causes them to be forgotten.

## Chapter-level editorial review

Compared with the [original Concise Commentary on Genesis 19](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc/mhcc.ii.xix.html) in a separate editorial pass. Primary production source remains published `MHCC 0.1.1`.

- **1–29:** Lot the lone godly man in Sodom, family rescue, hesitation, postponed spiritual repentance, grace **not merit**, God's effective rescue, flight without nostalgia, Christ/heaven as the true destination and God's righteous judgment—all retained.
- **30–38:** Lot standing faithfully in Sodom yet falling when secluded, warning against self-assurance, drunkenness opening other sin and lasting disgrace, temptations from trusted family/friends, clear moral responsibility of **both** daughters and Lot, Jeremiah-like heart-deceitfulness language and the final wordplay about forgetting/being forgotten—all retained.
