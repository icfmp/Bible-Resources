# Ezekiel 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A type of hair, showing the judgments about to come upon the Jews. | 1–4 |
| These awful judgments are declared. | 5–17 |

## The prophet's divided hair symbolizes the judgments (verses 1–4)

Ezekiel was commanded to **shave his head and beard**.

This signified God's complete rejection and abandonment of the people as they persisted in rebellion.

One portion of the hair would be **burned inside the city**, representing multitudes who would die from **famine and disease**.

Another would be **cut apart**, representing those killed by **the sword**.

A third would be **scattered by the wind**, representing people carried captive by the conqueror and others fleeing for safety to neighboring countries.

A small part of the scattered hair would be **bound in the folds of his clothing** to show how carefully a small remnant would be preserved.

Only **a few people** would be saved.

But sinners cannot escape God's anger merely by choosing a different hiding place.

The fire and sword of divine judgment will reach those who persist without repentance.

## God announces the judgment upon Jerusalem (verses 5–17)

The sentence against Jerusalem is terrifying, and the language used makes it even more dreadful.

Who could stand before God when his anger is revealed?

Those who **live and die without repentance** face lasting judgment and will not be spared forever.

Neither people nor churches that **change the Lord's commands** should assume they will escape the judgment Jerusalem received.

Let us seek to **honor the teaching of God our Savior** in all we do.

Sooner or later, **God's word will prove true**.
