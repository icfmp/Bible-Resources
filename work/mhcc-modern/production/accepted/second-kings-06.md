# 2 Kings 6 — Modern English

## The prophets build more living space; the iron ax head floats (verses 1–7)

Fellowship with God's servants has a sweetness that can make people forget the tiredness and pain of physical labor.

But even the sons of the prophets needed to work.

No one should think that honest employment is shameful or a burden beneath them.

Work done with the mind can be as difficult as work done with the hands—and often more demanding.

We ought to be as careful with borrowed possessions as with our own, doing to others what we would want them to do to us.

The man who lost the borrowed ax head cared about this obligation.

For a person with an honest conscience, the hardest part of poverty may not be hunger or damaged reputation, but the inability to pay just debts.

The Lord attends to even the smallest concerns of his people.

His grace can also raise up a heart as heavy and hard as **iron**, sunk into the mud of earthly concerns, and lift affections that naturally cling to the ground.

## Elisha reveals Syria's plans (verses 8–12)

Israel's king paid attention to Elisha's warnings of danger from the Syrians.

Yet he ignored the prophet's warnings concerning the danger of his sins.

Most people behave similarly.

They are eager to save their lives from death but show little concern about saving their souls from judgment.

Nothing anyone does, says or thinks—at any time or place—is hidden from God's knowledge.

## Elisha and the Syrian army (verses 13–23)

Elisha's words to his servant also comfort all God's faithful servants when there are conflicts outside and fears within.

Do not fear with the kind of fear that brings torment and confusion.

**Those who are with us to protect us are more numerous than those seeking to destroy us.**

Elisha's servant's physical eyes were open, and they could see the danger.

Lord, open the **eyes of our faith** to see your protecting hand!

The more clearly we recognize heaven's authority and power, the less we fear earthly troubles.

Satan, the god of this world, blinds people's eyes and thereby leads them toward ruin.

But when God opens their eyes, they recognize that they are surrounded by enemies, enslaved to Satan and exposed to judgment—even if they once believed themselves secure.

When Elisha had the Syrian soldiers in his power, he demonstrated **divine goodness as well as divine power**.

Let us not be overcome by evil, but **overcome evil with good**.

The Syrians realized it was useless to continue attacking such a godly and good man.

## Samaria is besieged and suffers famine (verses 24–33)

Hardship should make us value ordinary abundance and give thanks for it.

In famine, money becomes almost worthless when people will surrender it gladly for anything edible.

King Jehoram's words to the woman may have expressed despair.

The events fulfilled God's earlier warnings about the consequences of Israel's sin.

Among those warnings was the horrific possibility that people would eat their own children (**Deuteronomy 28:53–57**).

The episode showed both the truth and the dreadful justice of God's word.

What terrible misery human sin has brought upon the world!

Yet a person's foolishness ruins the path, and the heart then turns in anger against God.

The king threatened to kill Elisha.

Wicked people blame almost anyone for their troubles rather than admit their own wrongdoing or give it up.

Tearing clothing is no substitute for a **broken and repentant heart**.

Wearing sackcloth is useless without being renewed inwardly.

If external signs were sufficient, sinners would not remain stubbornly opposed to the Lord.

May the whole word of God grow in us a reverent fear and a holy hope.

May we remain firm and immovable, always abounding in God's work, knowing that labor done for the Lord is never wasted.
