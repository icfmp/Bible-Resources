# Leviticus 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The first offerings of Aaron for himself and the people. | 1–21 |
| Moses and Aaron bless the people, Fire cometh upon the altar from the Lord. | 22–24 |

## Aaron makes his first offerings (verses 1–21)

All these many sacrifices were brought to an end through the death of Christ.

They teach us that even our best services need to be washed in his blood.

Even our most careful acts of worship carry guilt that can be removed only through an offering purer and greater than ours.

Let us thank God that we have such a High Priest!

The priests were never excused from service for an entire day.

God's spiritual priests likewise have work to do continually, according to each day's responsibilities.

If we want to complete our service joyfully and give a good account of our lives, we must make good use of our time.

The glory of the Lord appeared openly before the people, showing his approval of their work.

We should not now expect this kind of visible manifestation.

Yet God still draws near to those who draw near to him.

He accepts the offerings made in faith.

Because Christian sacrifices are spiritual, the signs of their acceptance are spiritual as well.

When Aaron finished the sacrificial work, he raised his hands and blessed the people.

Aaron could **ask** God to bless them; God alone could **command** the blessing.

## Fire from the Lord consumes the sacrifice (verses 22–24)

After the service had ended and the blessing had been pronounced, God openly declared his acceptance.

Fire came out from before the Lord and consumed the offering.

The same fire could justly have consumed the guilty people.

Instead it consumed their sacrifice, showing that God accepted the offering as atonement for the sinner.

This pointed toward greater blessings to come.

The Holy Spirit later descended upon the apostles in the form of fire.

The Spirit's holy fire also comes into our hearts, kindling love, reverence and zeal for God, while consuming fleshly desires.

That inward work is a sign of God's gracious acceptance of both our persons and our service.

We cannot offer God anything that did not first come from him.

His grace must provide the holy fire that makes our worship acceptable (Hebrews 12:28).

The people responded with the greatest joy and deepest reverence.

They rejoiced that God had come near, yet bowed humbly before his majesty.

Fear of God is wrong when it drives us away from him. Gracious reverence causes us to bow before him.
