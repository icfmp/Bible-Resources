# Romans 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The freedom of believers from condemnation. | 1–9 |
| Their privileges as being the children of God. | 10–17 |
| Their hopeful prospects under tribulations. | 18–25 |
| Their assistance from the Spirit in prayer. | 26, 27 |
| Their interest in the love of God. | 28–31 |
| Their final triumph, through Christ. | 32–39 |

## No condemnation for those united to Christ (verses 1–9)

The Lord may **discipline believers**, but they will never be **condemned with the world**.

Through union with Jesus Christ by faith, they are safe from that condemnation.

The essential question is what governs their conduct: **flesh or Spirit, old or new nature, corruption or grace**?

For which do they make provision, and which directs their plans?

The unrenewed will cannot fully obey any commandment.

God's law requires **inward obedience** as well as outward conduct.

God showed his hatred of sin through the suffering of his Son in human flesh.

Through that sacrifice, believers may be pardoned and justified.

Christ satisfied God's justice and opened the way of salvation.

By the Holy Spirit, **the law of love is written on believers' hearts**.

They do not perfectly fulfill the law's righteousness by their own strength.

Nevertheless, God's grace produces within them something answering the law's true purpose.

Spiritual people give attention to **God's favor, the good of the soul and eternal concerns**.

Which subjects do we enjoy thinking about?

What governs our decisions and plans?

Are we wiser about the world, or about our own souls?

Someone living only for pleasure is spiritually dead (**1 Timothy 5:6**).

A sanctified soul possesses true life, and that life brings peace.

The unregenerate mind is not merely hostile toward God at times; **it is hostility itself**.

Divine grace can turn a previously worldly person toward obedience.

But **the carnal mind itself must be broken and cast out**; it cannot become obedient while remaining carnal.

We can examine our state by asking whether **the Spirit of God and Christ lives in us**.

Having Christ's Spirit means being given a mind that in some measure resembles **the mind of Jesus**, expressed through conduct consistent with his commands and example.

## The Spirit assures and leads God's adopted children (verses 10–17)

If the Spirit dwells in us, Christ himself dwells in our hearts **through faith**.

Grace within the soul becomes **a new nature**.

The soul is made alive toward God and begins a holy happiness that will last forever.

**Christ's imputed righteousness** guarantees its preservation from death.

Therefore, we must walk according to the Spirit rather than the flesh.

Anyone habitually living for corrupt desires will die in sin, **whatever religious profession they make**.

No pleasure the present world offers deserves comparison with the glory promised to believers.

By the Spirit, let us increasingly put the flesh to death.

Regeneration gives a new divine life to the soul, even if its earliest expression is weak.

God's children receive the Spirit who teaches them to behave as children, not as slaves.

Henry contrasts this with the Old Testament believers' relative **bondage under the shadows and uncertainty of that dispensation**, before the Spirit of adoption was poured out abundantly.

He also refers to the sense of bondage and fear experienced by many saints **at the beginning of conversion**.

Some people falsely reassure themselves that God is at peace with them.

But the Spirit bears witness to truly sanctified people by bringing **divine peace to the conscience**.

Even when following Christ appears costly now, no one will finally lose by belonging to him.

## Present suffering and future glory (verses 18–25)

The troubles of God's people belong to **this passing life**.

Compared with eternal glory, they are light and momentary.

How differently Scripture and the world judge present sufferings!

All creation seems to wait eagerly for God's children to be revealed in glory.

Through humanity's fall, creation has suffered **disorder, weakness and corruption**.

Creatures now often harm one another, and humans misuse them as instruments of sin.

Yet creation remains under **hope**.

God will finally free it from bondage caused by human wickedness.

Human misery caused by ourselves and one another shows that the world will not remain forever as it is.

Believers have received the **firstfruits of the Spirit**, strengthening their longing, hope and expectation.

Sin is the guilty source of the suffering introduced into God's creation.

It brought misery upon earth and the judgment of hell.

No tear, groan or pain in human body or soul exists apart from the effects of sin.

Beyond human suffering, consider **sin's dishonor to God's glory**, which so many people disregard.

Believers are already brought into safety through Christ.

Yet their present comfort rests chiefly in **hope of what is coming**, rather than full enjoyment now.

Nothing in the passing world can provide the satisfaction promised by that hope.

We need patience on a long and difficult road.

The One who is coming **will come**, even if his arrival seems delayed.

## The Spirit helps believers to pray (verses 26 and 27)

Christians have many weaknesses.

If left to themselves, they would be overwhelmed.

But **the Holy Spirit supports them**.

As the Spirit who enlightens, he teaches them **what to pray for**.

As the Spirit who sanctifies, he awakens and sustains the graces needed for prayer.

As the Spirit who comforts, he calms fears and carries them through discouragement.

The Holy Spirit is the source of every genuine desire directed toward God, including desires too deep to express in words.

The Spirit who searches hearts knows the mind of the Spirit and the renewed desires of believers, and **intercedes for them**.

Their enemy cannot overcome this gracious help.

## God's unbreakable purpose works for believers' good (verses 28–31)

What is genuinely good for God's people is whatever **does their souls good**.

Every providential circumstance serves the spiritual good of those who love the Lord.

It loosens their attachment to sin, brings them closer to God, frees them from love of the world and prepares them for heaven.

When believers depart from their proper character, God uses **correction to bring them back**.

Paul describes the causes and order of salvation as **a golden chain that cannot be broken**.

**First, those whom God foreknew he predestined to become like his Son.**

Those he appointed for final happiness and glory he also appointed to the necessary path of grace and holiness.

The whole human race deserved judgment.

Yet for reasons not fully known to us, God determined to recover some people through **regeneration and the power of his grace**.

He planned that they should be conformed to Christ's likeness.

In this life, that transformation begins as they are renewed and follow his example.

**Second, those he predestined he also called.**

This is **an effective call** from self and earth toward God, Christ and heaven as our final end, and from sin and emptiness toward grace and holiness as the path.

It is the gospel's call.

When God's love begins to rule hearts once hostile to him, that demonstrates his calling according to his purpose.

**Third, those he called he justified.**

None receive this justification apart from God's effective call.

Those who continue resisting the gospel remain under guilt and judgment.

**Fourth, those he justified he also glorified.**

Effective calling breaks sin's reigning power; justification removes sin's guilt.

**Nothing can finally separate that soul from the glory God intends**.

This strengthens faith and hope because God's way and work are perfect.

Paul responds with amazement at the immeasurable dimensions of Christ's love.

With most other subjects, familiarity can weaken our sense of wonder.

But deeper knowledge of the gospel's mysteries produces even greater amazement.

While God stands for us and we abide in his love, we may confidently resist the powers of darkness.

## Final victory through Christ's inseparable love (verses 32–39)

No gift in heaven or earth displays God's free love so greatly as giving **his coequal Son** to make atonement on the cross for human sin.

Every other saving blessing follows from our **union with Christ** and participation in him.

God will provide everything that can serve the believer's genuine good.

The One who prepared a crown and a kingdom will provide what is needed **on the way to them**.

People may claim they are innocent even while charges remain against them.

But when **God justifies**, the verdict ends the matter.

Christ paid the debt through his death.

**More than that, he rose again**, proving divine justice had accepted his sacrifice.

We have a Friend at God's right hand, possessing all authority and **continually interceding for us**.

Believer, does your soul cry, *If only I belonged to him, and he belonged to me; if only I could please him and live for him*?

Do not endlessly trouble yourself with unproductive doubts.

Recognize your sin, then trust **the One who justifies the ungodly**.

You were condemned, but **Christ died and rose again**.

Flee to him.

Having shown love by giving his Son, will God suddenly abandon that love?

Troubles neither cause nor demonstrate any reduction in it.

Believers may lose many things, but what truly matters remains.

**No one can take Christ from the believer or take the believer from Christ**.

By contrast, worldly possessions cannot promise such permanence.

Homes, loved ones and estates will all eventually be left behind.

Death dissolves every worldly attachment.

Those without Christ will be left with what they would gladly lose but cannot: **the guilt of unforgiven sin**.

Yet people united to Christ cling to him even as everything else is taken away.

Death breaks the union of body and soul, but it brings the believer's soul into **closer communion and everlasting joy with the beloved Lord Jesus**.
