# Acts 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The mission of Paul and Barnabas. | 1–3 |
| Elymas the sorcerer. | 4–13 |
| Paul's discourse at Antioch. | 14–41 |
| He preaches to the Gentiles, and is persecuted by the Jews. | 42–52 |

## Paul and Barnabas are sent on mission (verses 1–3)

What a remarkable gathering of believers!

The different names reveal how God chooses servants from **varied places and walks of life**.

Zeal for his glory can lead people to abandon attractive relationships and worldly prospects to advance his cause.

The Spirit of Christ makes his ministers **both willing and able** to serve, and draws them away from distractions that would prevent their work.

Christ's ministers are appointed to **labor, not to seek status**.

Their task is to carry out Christ's work under the Spirit's guidance for the Father's glory.

The congregation prayed for Barnabas and Saul to receive blessing and the Holy Spirit's power for their mission.

Whatever procedures are followed or means employed, **the Holy Spirit alone calls and equips gospel ministers** for their calling.

## Paul confronts Elymas (verses 4–13)

Satan particularly tries to keep powerful and influential people from genuine faith because their example affects many others.

Here Saul is first called **Paul** and afterward continues under that name.

Henry explains the two names as reflecting Saul's Jewish heritage and Paul's Roman citizenship.

Under the Holy Spirit's direct influence, Paul exposed Elymas's true character **without acting from uncontrolled anger**.

A combination of deliberate deceit and harm to others is a fitting description of one acting as the devil's child.

Those who oppose Jesus' teaching are enemies of genuine righteousness.

**Christ's way alone leads to heaven and lasting happiness**.

Some people not only leave that way themselves, but deliberately persuade others to reject it.

Such people can become hardened in sin.

The Roman governor was astonished both by the gospel's effect upon his conscience and by the divine power confirming it.

The more deeply we know Christ's teaching, the more reason we have to marvel.

Someone who begins work for God but looks back is not fit for the kingdom.

Likewise, people unwilling to face opposition or endure hardship are not prepared for gospel ministry.

## Paul preaches in Pisidian Antioch (verses 14–41)

### Israel's history and the promised Savior (verses 14–31)

When we meet to worship God, we should not only pray and praise; we must also **read and hear his word**.

Public reading is important, but **reading alone is not enough**.

Scripture should be explained and the people exhorted from it, so they can apply it to themselves.

Paul's sermon touched on the truths most likely to lead Jewish listeners to receive Jesus as their promised Messiah.

Even a brief view of God's dealings with Israel reveals divine mercy and patience amid human ingratitude and stubbornness.

Paul moved from **David to the Son of David**, presenting Jesus as the promised descendant.

Jesus accomplishes what the judges of ancient Israel could not: he saves sinners from **their worst enemies—their sins**.

When the apostles preached Christ as Savior, they never hid the cross.

They preached **Christ crucified**.

Our complete separation from sin is pictured by **burial with Christ**.

But Jesus also rose again and did not undergo corruption.

**That resurrection was essential to the message**.

### The resurrection and the sure mercies of David (verses 32–37)

Christ's resurrection was the great evidence that he is **the Son of God**.

Death could not hold him, for he possesses life in himself.

He freely laid it down with the intention and power to take it up again.

The **sure mercies promised to David** concern everlasting life.

Jesus' resurrection guarantees that life; even now the blessings of redemption are a pledge of what is coming.

David served his own generation well.

We were not born only for ourselves.

We should seek to benefit the people placed around us.

But Jesus' service reaches **every generation**.

May we trust the One declared God's Son by his resurrection, walk with God by faith, serve our own generation according to his will and finally fall asleep with the joy of a blessed resurrection.

### Forgiveness and justification through Christ (verses 38–41)

Everyone hearing the gospel must recognize **two truths**.

**First**, through the One who died and rose again, **forgiveness of sins** is proclaimed.

However numerous or serious our sins, they can be forgiven without dishonoring God's justice.

**Second**, only people who believe in Christ receive **complete justification** from guilt they could never be justified from through Moses' law.

The great need of a convicted sinner is to be acquitted from all guilt and accepted as righteous before God.

If even one charge remains, the sinner is ruined.

Christ brings complete justification because he has made **complete atonement**.

He justifies us not only as our Judge but as **the Lord our Righteousness**.

What the law could not accomplish because of human weakness, the gospel accomplishes.

This blessing lays the foundation for every other saving gift.

The warnings against unbelief are given so sinners will take heed.

People are ruined by treating religion with contempt.

Those who refuse to wonder at God's grace and be saved will finally wonder at his judgment and perish.

## Paul turns toward the Gentiles (verses 42–52)

Some Jewish hearers resisted the apostles' teaching.

When they could not answer its truth, they blasphemed Christ and the gospel.

Contradiction can harden into blasphemy.

When Christ's opponents are bold, his advocates should become **more courageous**.

While some treated themselves as unworthy of eternal life, others—people who might have seemed less likely—eagerly wanted to hear more.

The Old Testament had foretold the Gentiles' inclusion.

What light, power and treasure the gospel brings!

Its truths, commands and promises are excellent.

Those whom the Father drew and in whom the Holy Spirit made the gospel call effective came to Christ (**Romans 8:30**).

Henry describes those who were **disposed toward eternal life**—concerned for their eternal condition and intent on gaining that life—as believers in Christ, in whom God has stored everlasting life.

He immediately attributes the disposition and faith to **God's grace working within them**.

Godliness among people of honorable standing is a good thing.

But outward devotion becomes grievous when used as a pretext for hating Christ.

The more we cherish the comforts of true faith, the better prepared we will be to bear hardships for its sake.
