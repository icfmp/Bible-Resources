# 1 Kings 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Abijah being sick, his mother consults Ahijah. | 1–6 |
| The destruction of Jeroboam's house. | 7–20 |
| Rehoboam's wicked reign. | 21–31 |

## Jeroboam sends his wife to Ahijah (verses 1–6)

When Jeroboam was doing evil, his child became sick.

When sickness enters a family, we should examine whether we have been cherishing a particular sin from which God means to turn us through this hardship.

Jeroboam would have acted more faithfully by asking **why God was contending with him**, requesting the prophet's prayers and throwing away his idols.

But many people would rather learn their **future fortunes** than hear about their faults and duties.

Jeroboam sent to Ahijah because that prophet had foretold his rise to the throne.

People who make themselves unfit to receive comfort through their sins may still expect good ministers to speak only peace to them.

In doing so they wrong both themselves and the ministers.

Jeroboam sent his wife in disguise so the prophet would answer only her question about their son.

Some people similarly try to limit their ministers to pleasant messages.

They fear hearing the full counsel of God because it might warn of judgment rather than promise comfort.

But Jeroboam's wife would learn from the prophet's first words what she must expect.

The news of a hypocrite's final portion will be dreadful.

God judges people by what they truly are, not how they appear.

## Judgment is announced against Jeroboam's family (verses 7–20)

Whether or not we remember God's former mercies, he remembers them.

If we respond with ingratitude, he can bring those gifts back before us as evidence against us.

Ahijah foretold the child's approaching death.

Henry interprets this as a **mercy to the child**.

Of all Jeroboam's family, the boy alone showed affection for the true worship of God and disliked the worship of the calves.

God's sovereign grace can save someone from the worst household, wherever there is a genuine inclination toward the Lord God of Israel.

The righteous may be taken away from coming evil in this world and brought to the good awaiting them in a better world.

When the best member of a family dies first, it can be a warning to the others; yet death does not deprive that righteous person of any eternal blessing.

The death of the boy should have instructed both the household and the nation.

The prophet also announced judgment on Israel because its people had adopted Jeroboam's false worship.

After the ten tribes left David's house, their kingship rarely remained long in a single family.

One ruling house displaced and destroyed another.

Families and kingdoms are ruined by sin.

When influential people act wickedly, they draw others into both their guilt and its consequences.

Their condemnation is especially severe because they must answer not only for their own wrongdoing but also for the sins they encouraged others to commit and continue.

## Rehoboam's evil reign (verses 21–31)

Scripture says nothing good about Rehoboam here and much that condemns the behavior of his subjects.

Jerusalem was the city God had chosen for his temple and worship.

Yet its people practiced some of the most shameful sins of the pagan nations.

This shows that nothing can transform fallen human hearts except **the sanctifying grace of the Holy Spirit**.

We should depend upon that grace and pray daily for it, for ourselves and everyone around us.

The splendor of the temple, the ceremonies of the priesthood and every outward advantage of the nation's religion could not keep the people faithful to God.

Only an outpouring of the Spirit can do that.

Sin exposes a people to danger, makes it poor and weakens it.

**Shishak, king of Egypt**, came and took away Jerusalem's treasures.

Sin tarnishes gold, makes what was precious grow dim and replaces it with inferior brass.
