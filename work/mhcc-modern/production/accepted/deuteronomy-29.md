# Deuteronomy 29 — Modern English

## Remembering past and present mercies (verses 1–9)

God's former kindnesses and his recent acts of mercy should both encourage us to obey him.

Hearing ears, seeing eyes and understanding hearts are gifts from the Lord.

Everyone who possesses them owes them to him.

God gives food, clothing, wealth and considerable possessions to many people whom he has not given saving grace.

Many enjoy the gifts without recognizing their Giver, or understanding their proper purpose.

Gratitude and wise self-interest, as well as faithful duty, therefore call us to keep the words of God's covenant.

## The danger of self-confidence while sinning (verses 10–21)

The **national covenant** made with Israel pictured the covenant of grace with genuine believers.

It also represented the outward administration of religion under the gospel.

People who have consented to Christ's new covenant of mercy, and have dedicated themselves to be God's people, should take every opportunity to renew the public confession of their relationship to him.

Their lives should match that confession.

The sinner described here first turns away from God **in the heart**.

This is where the trouble begins.

Unbelief leads people to abandon the living God for lifeless idols.

The same temptation exists today.

People are drawn away by their own sinful desires and imagination.

Henry describes them as roots growing poisonous plants.

If not stopped, those weeds spread through an entire field.

Satan may disguise the bitterness of sin for a time.

But its true character will become clear, if not sooner, at the final judgment.

The presumptuous sinner hears God's warnings and still assumes there is no danger.

The threats in this passage are among the most serious in Scripture.

If only people who deliberately persist in wrongdoing would read them and tremble.

They are true declarations of God's anger against human ungodliness and injustice.

## The danger of national idolatry (verses 22–28)

Moses warned that idolatry would destroy Israel as a nation.

God has sometimes brought ruin upon communities that claimed to be especially close to him.

He does not do so without just reason.

We should seek to recognize that reason, give glory to God for his justice and take warning ourselves.

The law of Moses leaves sinners under its curse and expelled from the Lord's land.

But through Christ's grace, sinners who repent and believe are planted again into his favor.

God's power then protects them from finally being torn away.

## The revealed and hidden things of God (verse 29)

Moses ended his prophecy of Israel's rejection in a way comparable to Paul's conclusion on the same subject (Romans 11:33).

We are not meant to pry into God's secret purposes or claim certainty about what he has not revealed.

But we are commanded and encouraged to study diligently what he **has** revealed.

He has withheld nothing necessary or truly profitable for us.

Only what is good for us **not** to know remains hidden.

The aim of divine revelation is not to provide endless material for curious speculation or argument.

It is to enable us to obey God's law and receive his blessing.

Scripture plainly reveals what is needed for that purpose.

Going beyond it will not help us.

Through the light God has given, we may live and die in peace and finally be happy forever.
