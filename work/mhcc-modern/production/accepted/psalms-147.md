# Psalm 147 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The people of God are exhorted to praise him for his mercies and care. | 1–11 |
| For the salvation and prosperity of the church. | 12–20 |

## God gathers, heals and provides (verses 1–11)

**Praising God is work that carries its own reward**. It is fitting for all rational creatures and especially for people bound to him by covenant.

He gathers outcast sinners through grace and brings them into his holy dwelling. Through the comfort of his Spirit, he heals the brokenhearted, speaks peace and assures them that **their sins are pardoned**.

Our knowledge quickly reaches its limits, but God's wisdom cannot be measured. **He counts the stars and listens to the brokenhearted**. He feeds young ravens; surely he will not neglect his praying people.

Clouds appear dark, yet without them there would be no rain or fruit. Likewise **clouds of affliction can yield the peaceful fruit of righteousness**.

God does not admire the outward powers and achievements in which sinners glory. He values sincere reverence and trust. Faithful people should act under the gracious influence of **hope and fear joined together**.

## God's word and Spirit melt frozen hearts (verses 12–20)

God's wisdom, power and goodness build and preserve his church as they preserved Jerusalem. His blessings in nature picture his gracious work.

The word that makes winter ice melt can represent **the gospel of Christ**, while the thawing wind pictures **the Spirit of Christ**, whom Scripture compares to the wind (**John 3:8**).

Converting grace softens a heart that was **frozen hard**, melting it into tears of repentance and releasing good thoughts and affections once held back. The change brought about by the thaw is visible although its workings are mysterious. So is the transformation when God's **word and Spirit** bring a soul to repentance and life.
