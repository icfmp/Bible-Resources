## Beware of careless financial guarantees (verses 1–5)

Living according to God's word benefits us even in the present life.

We are **stewards**, not absolute owners, of our possessions. We will answer to the Lord for how we use them.

It is wrong to waste them through rash projects or agreements that may entangle us in difficulty and temptations.

No one should promise to pay another person's debt unless the amount is something they are **able and willing to pay**, without depriving their own family of what it needs.

When we guarantee a debt, we should consider it **our own obligation**.

If such care is necessary to escape debts owed to other people, how much more earnestly should we seek release from our debt before God!

Humble yourself before him. Seek **Christ as your Friend and Advocate**, and pray earnestly for forgiveness of sin and deliverance from destruction.

## Learn diligence from the ant (verses 6–11)

Diligence in our work is both wise and right.

Its main purpose is not simply to become wealthy, but to avoid burdening others or bringing disgrace upon God's church.

Ants show more industry than lazy people. We can learn from even the smallest creatures and be ashamed by their example.

Habits of idleness and excessive self-indulgence grow stronger through repetition.

A wasted life leads toward poverty. At first poverty seems distant, like a traveler slowly approaching. When it arrives, it becomes like **an armed man whom the careless person cannot resist**.

The same warning applies to the soul.

How many people delight in **the sleep of sin** and dream of worldly happiness!

We should seek to wake them. We must also be diligent concerning **our own salvation**.

## Turn away from the things God hates (verses 12–19)

If people who do nothing useful deserve rebuke, those who devote themselves to doing as much evil as possible deserve still stronger condemnation.

The wicked person described here plans speech and conduct cunningly and deliberately.

Destruction can overtake such a person **suddenly and without remedy**.

The passage lists **seven things God hates**.

Sins that destroy the peace and well-being of other people are particularly offensive to him.

We must learn to hate these sins **in ourselves**. Merely condemning them in other people accomplishes nothing.

Avoid such conduct; remain watchful and pray against it.

Show clear disapproval of those who practice it, whatever their rank or position.

## Walk in God's commandments (verses 20–35)

God's word has something to teach us in **every circumstance of life**.

We should never resent faithful correction.

The repeated warnings against adultery are necessary because the sin is widespread and deeply evil.

Its consequences are destructive, and it can ruin the soul's spiritual life.

Notice how this chapter brings together its warnings.

Remember **the One who willingly became our Surety—Christ—while we were strangers and enemies**.

With such promises, motives and an example before us, how could Christians remain idle or careless?

Will we neglect the things pleasing to God and worthy of the gracious reward he promises?

We must **guard every sense** through which sinful influence can enter our thoughts and affections.
