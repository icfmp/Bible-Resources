# Isaiah 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortations and warnings. | 1–8 |
| Comfort for those who fear God. | 9–16 |
| Afflictions to idolaters. | 17–22 |

## The coming Assyrian invasion (verses 1–8)

Isaiah was instructed to write on a large scroll or metal tablet words meaning **“Hurry to the plunder; rush to the prey.”**

They foretold how quickly the Assyrian army would arrive and seize enormous spoils.

Soon the wealth of **Damascus and Samaria**, cities that then appeared secure and powerful, would be carried away by Assyria.

The prophet appealed to **the promised Messiah**, who would one day appear in this land. Henry understands this appeal as resting on the Messiah's **divine power to protect it in the meantime**.

A gentle stream represents a mild, peaceable government. A raging flood pictures the overwhelming power of an invading conqueror and tyrant.

The invader's success is also described as a bird of prey spreading its wings across the land.

Those who reject Christ may call their rejection freedom, but they will discover it is **the deepest slavery**.

Yet no enemy can take a believer from **Emmanuel's hand** or deprive that person of the heavenly inheritance.

## God as sanctuary or stumbling stone (verses 9–16)

Isaiah challenged Judah's enemies: their plans would come to nothing, and their strength would be broken.

During trouble, we must guard against fears that lead us to **dishonest or sinful methods of protecting ourselves**.

Reverent fear of God protects us from the tormenting fear of human beings.

If we properly understood his majesty and power, we would see that our enemies' strength remains under his control.

The Lord is **a sanctuary** for those who trust him.

But to those who put their fear and hope in created things, he becomes **a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense**.

If God's truth itself offends us, it will become the cause of our ruin.

The apostle applies this warning to those persisting in unbelief toward Christ's gospel (**1 Peter 2:8**).

Crucified **Emmanuel** offended unbelieving Jews and continues to offend many who call themselves Christians.

They regard preaching of the cross as foolishness and reject his teachings and commandments.

## Seek God's word instead of spiritual deception (verses 17–22)

Isaiah foresaw a time when the Lord would hide his face, yet he resolved to continue waiting for God's return in mercy.

His children's names served as divinely given reminders, calling the people to consider God's message, even though they were **not miraculous signs**.

Unbelieving Israelites sought guidance from diviners, employing foolish and sinful practices.

Where should we turn if we wish to know God's mind and our duty?

**To the law and the testimony.**

There we discover what is good and what the Lord requires.

We must speak about divine things in words taught by **the Holy Spirit** and submit ourselves to their authority.

Those who consult spirits while neglecting God's word invite distress and darkness.

Leaving God means leaving the path of all true good.

A resentful heart punishes itself through its own bitterness.

People who curse God and turn away from his light will find their fears deepening, with no way of relief.

Those who deliberately close their eyes to Scripture may rightly be left in spiritual darkness.

All human troubles are small compared with the destruction awaiting people who abandon **the words of Christ** to pursue deception.
