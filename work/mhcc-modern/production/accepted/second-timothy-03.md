# 2 Timothy 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle foretells the rise of dangerous enemies to the gospel. | 1–9 |
| Proposes his own example to Timothy. | 10–13 |
| And exhorts him to continue in the doctrine he had learned from the Holy Scriptures. | 14–17 |

## Dangerous times and false religious appearance (verses 1–9)

Perilous times can arise even while the gospel is being preached. Persecution **from outside the church** is dangerous; corruption **inside it** is more dangerous still.

People increasingly gratify sinful desires instead of pleasing God and fulfilling their duties.

When everyone is eager to obtain more and anxiously protect possessions, people become dangerous to one another.

Those who do not fear God will not respect others. Children disobeying parents make life unstable.

Ingratitude for God's mercies feeds irreverence. We misuse God's gifts when we make them fuel for sinful passions.

Lack of natural affection in parents, unchecked temper and contempt for what is good also create perilous times.

God deserves our highest love, yet the sinful mind opposes him and prefers almost anything—especially bodily pleasure.

**The appearance of godliness is entirely different from its power.** Christians must distance themselves from people shown to be religious hypocrites.

Throughout the church's history, manipulative people have used flattery and pretended spirituality to gain the trust of the gullible and uninformed.

Their followers are always embracing new ideas but never seek **the truth found in Jesus**.

Like the Egyptian magicians, these deceivers resist truth with corrupt minds and lack genuine faith. Yet Satan and the spirit of error can mislead nations or churches **only as far and as long as God permits**.

## Follow Paul's teaching and endurance (verses 10–13)

The more fully we understand Christ's doctrine as the apostles taught it, the more firmly we should hold to it.

When we know only part of the persecution believers suffer, we may be tempted to abandon their cause.

People may tolerate a merely formal profession of Christianity, while openly holding fast to Christ's truth and faithfully practicing godliness arouses the world's scorn.

By God's grace, faithful people grow in holiness. But through Satan's schemes and their own corrupt desires, wicked people grow worse.

The road of sin goes downhill: those who deceive others also deceive themselves. The history of the visible church sadly demonstrates **the Holy Spirit's warning through Paul**.

## Remain in the God-breathed Scriptures (verses 14–17)

Anyone who seeks firm knowledge of God's ways must learn **the Holy Scriptures, God's own revelation**.

Childhood is especially suitable for learning. The greatest education children can receive begins with Scripture.

We must not leave the Bible neglected and scarcely ever opened.

The Bible is a trustworthy guide toward eternal life.

**Prophets and apostles did not invent their message; they delivered what God gave them** (**2 Peter 1:21**).

Scripture is profitable for **every part of Christian living**. Everyone needs instruction, correction and rebuke; the Bible addresses every situation.

If we loved our Bibles more deeply and remained closer to their teaching, we would benefit now and at last enjoy the happiness they promise through faith in **Jesus Christ, the central subject of both Testaments**.

The best defense against error is **a solid knowledge of God's truthful word**.

The greatest kindness to children is to teach them the Bible from an early age.
