# Deuteronomy 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's mercies to Israel after their rebellion. | 1–11 |
| An exhortation to obedience. | 12–22 |

## God's renewed kindness after Israel's rebellion (verses 1–11)

Moses reminded the Israelites how merciful God had been to them even after they had repeatedly provoked him.

He identified **four signs** that the Lord had reconciled himself to the people.

**First, God gave them his law.**

The Lord has likewise entrusted us with Scripture, Sabbaths and sacraments as signs of his presence and favor.

**Second, he led them onward toward Canaan.**

Their earlier rebellion had not brought his covenant promises to an end.

**Third, he established a continuing ministry** to care for holy things among the people.

Under the gospel, the Holy Spirit is poured out more abundantly and powerfully.

The Spirit continues to raise up ministers in every age, preparing their hearts and making them willing to serve.

**Fourth, God accepted Moses as an intercessor.**

He also appointed Moses to remain Israel's leader.

In this, Moses pictured Christ, who lives forever to intercede for us and possesses all authority in heaven and on earth.

## Our obligations to God, neighbor and ourselves (verses 12–22)

Moses explained our responsibilities in both conviction and conduct.

**Toward God**, we must fear the Lord, love him, delight in fellowship with him, walk in his appointed ways, and serve him sincerely with all our hearts and souls.

Our service should be willing and cheerful.

We must keep his commands.

Obedience offers true honor and joy.

We should also honor God and cling to him as the One whom we love, trust and delight in, and from whom we expect every good thing.

**Toward our neighbors**, God's generosity to humanity obligates us to honor all people.

Those who once suffered and have received God's mercy should be especially ready to show kindness to others in similar distress.

**Toward ourselves**, we are commanded to circumcise our hearts.

We must put aside the corrupt desires and affections that keep us from loving and fearing God.

By nature, human beings do not love him.

This is original sin, the source of our wrongdoing.

The sinful mind is hostile to God.

It neither submits to his law nor has the power to do so.

Those who live under the flesh's rule cannot please God (Romans 8:5–9).

Let us therefore come without delay or reservation to God, who has reconciled us to himself through Jesus Christ.

In fellowship with him, we can love, serve and obey him acceptably.

Through the Spirit, we can be transformed into Christ's likeness, from one degree of glory to another.

God's greatness, glory, goodness and grace all encourage us to perform our duty.

Blessed Spirit, give us your cleansing, persevering and renewing power!

May we no longer remain strangers as our ancestors once were, but be counted among God's children and receive our inheritance with his saints.
