# Psalm 143 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David complains of his enemies and distresses. | 1–6 |
| He prays for comfort, guidance, and deliverance. | 7–12 |

## Pleading God's righteousness rather than our merit (verses 1–6)

We possess no righteousness of our own that would justify our prayers.

We must therefore appeal to **God's righteousness** and the promises he has freely given us.

David asked for the **forgiveness of sin before asking to be relieved from suffering**.

He relied on **mercy alone** for pardon.

He confessed the heaviness of his heart under trouble.

But he also looked back to remember God's earlier deliverances—those granted to Israel and those he had personally experienced.

He looked around at God's works in creation.

The more we consider **God's power**, the less we will fear the power or threats of other people.

He lifted his thoughts toward God and longed earnestly for his favor.

This is the best response when our spirits are overwhelmed.

The believer never forgets that **even the best acts remain mixed with sin**.

Meditation and prayer help restore our souls.

A sorrowing heart reaches toward God like **an infant stretching out its hands to a tender mother**, and longs for consolation like **dry earth thirsting for rain**.

## Asking to know and do God's will (verses 7–12)

David prayed for God's favor and for the assurance that he enjoyed it.

He knew how miserable he would be if the Lord withdrew his consoling presence.

But the night of affliction will eventually end in **a morning of comfort and praise**.

He asked to understand God's will.

Henry calls this **the first work of the Spirit**.

The faithful person does not ask merely which road is easiest or most pleasant.

**The right road matters more than the comfortable road**.

Lord, do not only reveal your will to me; teach me **how to carry it out**.

Those who know the Lord as their God have **the Holy Spirit as their Guide**.

David also asked for renewed life and strength to obey.

Above all, we should ask for **our sins to be destroyed**, since they are our most dangerous enemies, so that we can serve the Lord faithfully.
