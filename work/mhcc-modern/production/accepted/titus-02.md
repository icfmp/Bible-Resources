# Titus 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The duties which become sound doctrine. | 1–8 |
| Believing servants must be obedient. | 9, 10 |
| All is enforced from the holy design of the gospel, which concerns all believers. | 11–15 |

## Sound teaching should shape every age (verses 1–8)

Older disciples of Christ should make every part of life consistent with **Christian teaching**.

Older men are to be sober. Growing frailty is no excuse for excess; they should seek consolation in **closer fellowship with God**, not self-indulgence.

Faith operates through love: loving God for himself and people for his sake.

Older people may become irritable or complain excessively and should guard against those temptations.

Scripture does not spell out a rule for every word, gesture or expression, but gives **general principles** governing all of life.

Young women should behave with discretion and self-control. A careless decision can expose them to dangerous temptation.

Their conduct should prevent **God's word from being dishonored**. Neglecting ordinary responsibilities brings reproach on Christianity.

Young men are often eager and thoughtless. They need earnest encouragement toward sound judgment and self-control.

Henry regards **pride** as a particularly destructive danger for young people.

A Christian should seek to silence unjust accusations through upright conduct. What an honor when even an enemy seeking fault has no genuine wrongdoing to report!

## Serve faithfully in earthly responsibilities (verses 9 and 10)

Those serving earthly masters should fulfill lawful responsibilities as service rendered ultimately to **Christ**; he rewards faithful duty.

They should avoid provoking or disrespectful speech and receive deserved correction without argumentative excuses.

Trying to justify a wrong already committed makes it worse.

They must not steal or misuse entrusted possessions, but should handle them faithfully and contribute to the good of those they serve.

**“If you have not been faithful with what belongs to someone else, who will give you what is your own?”** (**Luke 16:12**).

A faithful life **adorns Christian teaching**.

## God's grace trains us to holy living (verses 11–15)

The message of grace and salvation is for **people of every rank and condition**.

It teaches us to renounce sin and refuse an earthly, sensual life inconsistent with our heavenly calling.

We are called to live **self-controlled, upright and godly lives**, despite temptations, bad examples, mistreatment and the sinful desires still remaining in believers.

The gospel also teaches us to anticipate another world's glory. Our **blessed hope** will be complete when Christ appears in glory.

The purpose of his death was to bring us into **holiness and happiness**.

**Jesus Christ is the great God and our Savior**—not a Savior merely as God, still less merely as a human being, but **the God-man, two natures united in one person**.

He loved us and gave himself for us. We should love him and give ourselves to him.

Redemption from sin and sanctification belong together. Jesus frees his people from guilt and condemnation and purifies them **through the Holy Spirit**, making them his own people eager for good works.

All Scripture helps us perform our duties rightly.

Ask whether we truly rely on **grace that saves the lost, pardons the guilty and makes the impure holy**.

The less we boast of supposed good works or trust them for acceptance, and the more we boast in Christ alone, **the more eager we should become to perform genuinely good works**.
