# Luke 21 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ commends a poor widow. | 1–4 |
| His prophecy. | 5–28 |
| Christ exhorts to watchfulness. | 29–38 |

## Jesus commends the poor widow (verses 1–4)

What we rightly give to care for poor people or to support the worship of God is, in a real sense, **given to God**.

Jesus receives with pleasure what his servants sincerely desire and are able to offer for his service and the good of his people.

Even the poorest of God's servants has **two small coins of another kind: a body and a soul**.

May Christ persuade us and enable us to present **both** to him!

What happiness to know that he receives them.

## Jesus foretells Jerusalem's distress and his return (verses 5–28)

The disciples were curious about the date of the coming catastrophe.

Jesus answered clearly enough to teach them **what they needed to do**.

Knowledge is valuable insofar as it helps us act faithfully.

Although divine judgments during the gospel age often affect people's spiritual condition, God still sometimes uses **outward and temporal judgments**.

Jesus warned his followers that they would endure serious suffering because of his name.

He encouraged them to remain steadfast in their duty despite opposition.

God would stand beside them, acknowledge them and help them.

After the Holy Spirit was poured out, the promise was strikingly fulfilled as Jesus gave his disciples **wisdom and words** through the Spirit.

We may suffer losses **for Christ**, but in the end we will never be losers **because of Christ**.

In every age, and especially in times of great danger, we should carefully guard our souls.

Through **Christian patience** we retain our inward composure and resist the harmful emotions that trouble the heart.

Henry compares this prophecy to Old Testament prophecies in which a great future event is described together with nearer events that foreshadow it.

He understands Jesus to describe **about thirty-eight years** leading to **Jerusalem's destruction and the dispersal of the Jewish nation**.

This historical judgment is also a figure and warning concerning **Christ's second coming**.

Henry regards the continuing historical existence of the dispersed Jewish people as a witness to the reliability of Christ's predictions.

He also urges prayer for the day when neither Jerusalem nor God's spiritual people will remain under oppression and when **both Jews and Gentiles will turn to the Lord**.

In Henry's interpretation, the first historical judgment delivered Christians oppressed by hostile authorities, bringing the churches a period of rest.

When Jesus comes to judge the world, he will deliver **all his people** from suffering.

The destruction of Jerusalem is thus a solemn demonstration that God does not ignore sin.

His warnings to unrepentant sinners will be fulfilled, as his prophetic word was fulfilled in that judgment.

## Watch and pray (verses 29–38)

Jesus taught his disciples to notice signs that helped them recognize the approach of Jerusalem's catastrophe.

Yet Henry says **Abraham's descendants would not disappear altogether** and would remain until the time of Christ's revelation.

Jesus warned against complacency and indulgence.

**Take care of yourselves** so that temptation and inward corruption do not overcome you.

No one is safe while careless about the soul.

The day of death or judgment must not find us unprepared.

It would be terrible if, when summoned to meet Jesus, we were least concerned with the very matter that should have occupied our hearts.

Those absorbed only in earthly life and lacking any fellowship with heaven will be overtaken in this way.

Seek to be ready to stand before Christ.

If you have never sought him, come to him now.

If you have not humbled yourself over your sins, begin.

If you have begun, continue in humility and faith.

**Watch and pray always**.

Watch against sin.

Remain watchful in every duty and use every opportunity to do good.

Those who live a life of prayer here will enjoy a life of praise in the world to come.

May we begin, use and finish every day by attending to Christ's word, obeying his commands and following his example.

Then whenever he comes, we will be found ready.
