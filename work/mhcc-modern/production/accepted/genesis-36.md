# Genesis 36 — Modern English

## Esau and his descendants

The family records in this chapter show that God was faithful to his promise to Abraham.

Esau is called *Edom* here. That name kept alive the memory of his selling his birthright for a single meal.

Esau continued to show the same disregard for sacred and heavenly things.

In outward prosperity and honor, people who belong to God's covenant often fall behind, while those outside the covenant get ahead.

Imagine what a test of faith it must have been for the Israelites, living as slaves in Egypt, to hear about the splendor and power of Edom's kings.

Yet those who expect great things from God must be willing to wait for them.

God's time is always the best time.

Mount Seir is described as the land the Edomites possessed.

Canaan, at that point, was still only the land God had promised to Abraham's descendants.

The Edomites already held Seir, while Israel awaited Canaan.

The children of this world possess all they expect to have now, with nothing left to hope for beyond it (Luke 16:25).

God's children, by contrast, have almost nothing of their full inheritance in hand, but everything to hope for.

When all things are considered, it is incomparably better to have Canaan as a promise than to possess Mount Seir already.
