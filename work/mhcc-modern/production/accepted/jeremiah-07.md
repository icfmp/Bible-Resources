# Jeremiah 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Confidence in the temple is vain. | 1–16 |
| The provocation by persisting in idolatry. | 17–20 |
| God justifies his dealings with them. | 21–28 |
| And threatens vengeance. | 29–34 |

## The temple offers no protection from unrepentant sin (verses 1–16)

Neither outward religious observances nor professions of faith nor even claims to have received revelations from God will benefit people who **refuse to amend their ways and actions**.

No one has a right to claim a share in **free salvation** while willingly continuing in **known sin** or deliberately neglecting a **known duty**.

The people imagined that the very temple they had polluted with their wrongdoing would protect them.

Those who continue sinning because grace abounds, or in the hope that grace will abound even more, effectively turn **Christ into a servant of sin**.

The cross of Christ, rightly understood, is the strongest remedy against that poisonous thinking.

**God's Son gave himself for our transgressions**. His sacrifice displays both the excellence of **God's law** and the dreadful evil of sin.

Never suppose that we can continue in wickedness without facing its consequences.

## The people's zeal for idols condemns their indifference to God (verses 17–20)

Judah's people were proud of their enthusiasm in serving idols.

Even from that terrible example we should learn how earnestly we ought to serve the **true God**.

It should be an honor to be entrusted with any work for him.

We should work diligently ourselves and take care to teach our children **the truths of God**, just as many people diligently teach others corrupt practices.

Their idolatry expressed hostility toward God, but its consequences would fall upon **themselves**.

They would discover there was no escape from his judgment.

**God's wrath is like an unquenchable fire**.

## The Lord required attentive obedience (verses 21–28)

God demonstrated that what he required from them was **obedience**.

His command was to **listen carefully to the voice of the Lord their God**.

The promise attached to that command was full of encouragement.

Let **God's will be our rule**, and let **his favor be our happiness**.

The people's disobedience displeased him.

We misunderstand the gospel as badly as those Israelites misunderstood the law if we imagine that even **Christ's sacrifice reduces our obligation to obey God**.

## Judgment comes upon a polluted people (verses 29–34)

Jerusalem would be brought low, in token of both sorrow and slavery.

The city once set apart to the Lord would now be separated from him.

The human heart is the place where God has chosen to put his name.

But when sin holds the highest and deepest place within it, **we pollute the Lord's temple**.

The approaching destruction of Jerusalem was dreadful.

Many would be killed in the very place they had made a center of wrongdoing.

Evil pursues sinners **even beyond death**.

People unwilling to abandon empty pleasures through God's grace may have **every pleasure taken away through his judgment**.

How many people ruin their health and possessions without complaint while serving **Satan**!

May we learn to enjoy **holy joys** and hold every other pleasure lightly, even those that are lawful.
