# Ezekiel 38 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The army and malice of Gog. | 1–13 |
| God's judgments. | 14–23 |

## Gog and the hostile army (verses 1–13)

These events belong to **the latter days**.

Henry says **it is supposed** that these enemies will unite to invade Judea and that God will defeat them.

God not only knows who is presently hostile to his church; he also knows **who will become its enemies**.

Through his word, he warns them that he is against them.

Even if the wicked join together, they will not escape his judgment.

## God's judgment and protection (verses 14–23)

The enemy would launch a formidable attack upon Israel.

When Israel dwells safely under God's protection, the attackers will discover how useless their attempts to destroy it are.

God's word contains promises of security that his people may treasure in advance of dangers and troubles facing the church in the latter days.

In judging sinners, God makes his **greatness and holiness** known.

We should therefore pray daily:

**Father, glorify your own name.**
