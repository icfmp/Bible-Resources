# Ecclesiastes 7 — Modern English

## A good name, sober reflection and godly correction (verses 1–6)

A reputation for godliness and honesty is worth more than all this world's wealth and pleasure.

It can be more beneficial to visit **a funeral than a feast**.

Both can be lawful at the proper time.

Our Savior attended a wedding celebration at **Cana** and wept at the grave of a friend in **Bethany**.

But because we are easily led toward vanity and indulgence of sinful desires, visiting mourners may be particularly useful.

There we learn what becomes of every human life in this world.

Serious thought is better for us than noisy amusement when it teaches what truly matters.

What benefits the soul is best for us, even when it does not feel pleasant.

It is better for our sinful inclinations to be corrected by **a wise person's rebuke** than to have them flattered by the songs of foolish people.

The laughter of fools quickly vanishes, and its end is sorrow.

## Be patient; do not idealize the past (verses 7–10)

The outcome of trials and difficulties is often better than we first imagined.

A patient spirit is far better than one proud, rash and impatient.

Do not become angry quickly or respond immediately to every insult.

Nor should anger remain for a long time.

Even a wise person may feel anger, but it should **pass through like a traveler**, not take up permanent residence.

Anger makes its home only among fools.

It is foolish to complain continually about the wickedness of the present age when we have even more reason to lament **the wrongdoing within our own hearts**.

Even now we enjoy many mercies.

It is equally foolish to imagine that earlier generations were uniformly better.

They had their own reasons for complaint and trouble.

Nostalgia of this kind often springs from discontent and a readiness to quarrel **even with God**.

## Wisdom, correction and the limits of self-righteousness (verses 11–22)

Wisdom is as valuable as an inheritance, indeed **more valuable**.

It protects the heart during life's storms and scorching heat.

Money cannot lengthen the natural life appointed to us.

But true wisdom brings **spiritual life** and strengthens people to serve God amid suffering.

We should regard our circumstances as governed by him; the end will show the justice and goodness of his decisions.

Even while pursuing righteousness, do not be carried away by passionate temper—**not even by zeal for God**.

Avoid being impressed by your own abilities, finding fault with everything or intruding needlessly into other people's affairs.

Some people who are unmoved by fear of God or judgment still avoid sins that might destroy their health, fortune or public reputation.

But those who truly fear God have **one great purpose**, so they act more consistently.

If we claim we have no sin, we deceive ourselves.

Every sincere believer should be ready to say, **“God, be merciful to me, a sinner.”**

At the same time remember: personal obedience and a life renewed in holiness are the only genuine **evidence that we have received by faith the Redeemer's righteousness**.

Wisdom teaches us not to take offense quickly.

We need not go looking for what people say about us.

Praise can feed our pride, while criticism can excite anger.

Seek God's approval and a clear conscience, then give less attention to what others say.

It is easier to overlook **twenty insults** than to avenge just one.

When someone hurts us, ask whether we ourselves have hurt other people in a similar way.

## Solomon discovers sin's corruption (verses 23–29)

Solomon had been deceived in his efforts to understand human affairs through personal inquiry.

Now he speaks with **godly sorrow**.

Someone who continually seeks to please God may hope to escape the worst temptations.

But a careless sinner may fall and never recover.

Solomon now recognized more clearly the evil of the sin he had committed by **loving many foreign women** (**1 Kings 11:1**).

He said he had not found a thoroughly faithful and godly woman among them.

How could he expect to find one among the women he had gathered under those circumstances?

Even anyone originally well disposed might have been badly influenced by that setting.

Henry says Solomon's warning was directed against his own sinful choices, **not against the possibility of finding a virtuous wife**.

Many godly husbands can gratefully acknowledge having found a prudent and faithful woman in marriage.

But someone who follows Solomon's path has little reason to expect the same blessing.

Solomon traced every stream of actual transgression back to its source.

Human nature is corrupted and has turned away from its original creation.

God made humanity upright, but human beings have invented countless ways of making themselves wicked and miserable.

Let us thank God for **Jesus Christ** and seek his grace, that we may belong to his redeemed people.
