# Ezra 3 — Modern English

## The altar is restored (verses 1–7)

Israel's conduct on returning to Jerusalem teaches us to **begin with God**.

We should do what we can in God's worship even when we cannot do everything we wish.

They could not build a temple immediately, but they would not remain without an altar.

Fear of danger should move us **toward duty**, not away from it.

If enemies surround us, all the more reason to seek God's friendship and continue in fellowship with him.

Our fears should drive us **to our knees**.

For a poor community, the prescribed sacrifices were costly.

Nevertheless, many people gave additional voluntary offerings.

They also began preparing for the temple without delay.

Whatever God calls us to do, we may trust his Providence to supply the means required.

## The temple foundation is laid (verses 8–13)

As the foundation was laid, the people experienced remarkably different emotions.

Those who had known only life without a temple shouted with joy.

To them, even the foundation appeared magnificent.

We should give thanks when mercy begins, even before its work is complete.

But people who remembered the glory of the former temple wept loudly because this new building seemed likely to be so much poorer.

They had reason to grieve, especially if they mourned the sins that brought about the loss of the first temple.

Yet it was wrong to cast gloom over everyone else's joy.

They were in danger of **despising the day of small things** and failing to thank God for the gifts now before them.

Do not let the memory of former troubles overwhelm your gratitude for present mercies.
