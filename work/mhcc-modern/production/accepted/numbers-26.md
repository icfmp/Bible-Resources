# Numbers 26 — Modern English

## The new census in Moab (verses 1–51)

Moses did not number Israel whenever he wished.

He did so only when the Lord commanded him.

This record lists not only Israel's tribes but also the families within them.

The total number of people was almost the same as at the earlier census at Mount Sinai.

Scripture specifically mentions the sons of Korah, who did not die as the children of Dathan and Abiram did.

They seem not to have joined even their own father in rebellion.

If we refuse to participate in other people's sins, we will not share the judgment brought upon them.

## The division of Canaan (verses 52–56)

The inheritance was to be divided justly.

Larger tribes were to receive larger portions; smaller tribes, smaller portions.

Although the ruler's judgment played a part, the final outcome would be settled through God's providence.

The people should therefore accept his decision.

## The Levites are counted separately (verses 57–62)

Levi was the tribe set apart for God.

It was therefore counted separately from the others.

The Levites were not included in the judgment that prevented the earlier generation from entering Canaan, with the exceptions of Caleb and Joshua.

## The earlier generation has passed away (verses 63–65)

The judgment pronounced on the complaining generation had now been fulfilled (Numbers 14:29).

Not one of the people counted in the first census remained in the new census—except Caleb and Joshua.

This showed God's righteousness and the certainty of his warnings.

It also demonstrated his faithfulness in keeping the particular promises he had made to those two men.

Death brings enormous changes to families and nations.

Yet God's perfect wisdom, justice and truth govern every such change.

These truths should make us think seriously about the evil of sin, which brought death and so much destruction into the world.

Let us renew our repentance, seek forgiveness and value Christ's salvation.

Let us remember how frail we are, prepare for death and use the days given to us in serving our generation according to God's will.
