# Judges 3 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Nations left to test Israel (verses 1–7)

Israel was a picture of God's church on earth.

The people were not meant to live carelessly or lazily.

The Lord allowed some of the Canaanite nations to remain so that Israel's faith would be tested.

Temptations and trials expose the wickedness in sinners' hearts.

They also strengthen the graces of believers as they struggle each day against Satan, sin and this evil world.

Christians must live within the world, but they do not belong to it and must not adopt its sinful patterns.

This distinguishes Christ's followers from people who merely profess faith.

Friendship with the world is more deadly than the world's hostility.

Hostility can kill the body, but worldly friendship has destroyed many precious souls.

## Othniel delivers Israel (verses 8–11)

Othniel was the first judge.

He had already become known during Joshua's lifetime.

Soon after Israel settled in Canaan, the nation's purity was corrupted and its peace disturbed.

Suffering can make people cry out to God when they had scarcely spoken to him before.

The Lord returned to them in mercy and raised up a deliverer.

The Spirit of the Lord came upon Othniel.

The Spirit gave him **wisdom and courage** to fit him for the task, and **power** to move him to perform it.

Othniel first governed Israel, rebuked its sins and worked for its reform.

Only then did he go to war.

Let us first conquer the sin within us, the worst enemy of all.

Then it will be easier to face outward enemies.

Let Christ be our Judge and Lawgiver, and he will save us.

## Ehud delivers Israel from Eglon (verses 12–30)

Israel sinned again, and God raised up another oppressor.

The Israelites did wrong, and the Moabites did even worse.

Yet because God corrects his own people in this life, Israel grew weaker and Moab stronger against it.

If small troubles do not bring repentance, God may send greater ones.

When Israel prayed again, the Lord raised up **Ehud**.

As a judge carrying out divine justice, Ehud killed Eglon, king of Moab.

Henry understands this act as the execution of God's judgment against an enemy of the Lord and his people.

But he immediately makes an essential distinction.

**Our rule today is to obey rulers and authorities in everything that is lawful. No such special commissions are given now. Claiming one would be blasphemy against God.**

Ehud's words to Eglon remind us that a proud rebel can expect a message of judgment from God.

Scripture contains such a warning.

Ministers must declare it boldly, neither fearing the anger of sinners nor showing favoritism.

But blessed be God, his ministers also bring **a message of mercy and free salvation**.

The warning of judgment is for people who reject God's gracious offer.

After Ehud's victory, the land had peace for **eighty years**.

That was a long rest, but it cannot compare with the everlasting rest of the saints in the heavenly Canaan.

## Shamgar delivers Israel (verse 31)

The Philistines threatened the southwestern region.

The Lord raised up Shamgar to deliver it.

Without a sword or spear, Shamgar took the nearest available tool—an **oxgoad**.

God can use people for his glory and the good of his church even when their birth, education or occupation seem humble and obscure.

The weapon itself is not what matters if God directs and strengthens the person using it.

He often works through unlikely means so that everyone can see the excellence of his power.
