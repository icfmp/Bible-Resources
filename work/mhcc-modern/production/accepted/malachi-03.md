# Malachi 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The coming of Christ. | 1–6 |
| The Jews reproved for their corruptions. | 7–12 |
| God's care of his people; The distinction between the righteous and the wicked. | 13–18 |

## The messenger prepares the way for Christ (verses 1–6)

These opening words answer those who mocked God's promise of judgment.

The prophet announces **John the Baptist**, the forerunner of Christ, who would prepare the way through a call to repentance.

For a long time, believers had expected the Messiah, **the One who was to come**.

Now his arrival was near.

He is **the Messenger of the Covenant**.

Those who sincerely seek Jesus will find delight in him, sometimes when they least expect it.

Through his word and the convictions brought by the Holy Spirit, Christ prepares the sinner's heart **to become his temple**.

He enters as the Messenger of peace and consolation.

Hypocrites and people content with empty religious forms cannot endure his teaching or stand before his judgment.

Christ came to distinguish the precious from the worthless.

He sits like a **refiner**, purifying his church through the gospel and renewing souls through the Spirit who works with it.

He removes the dross of inward corruption that makes human capacities useless in God's service.

A believer need not be afraid of the fiery trials of suffering and temptation through which the Savior refines his gold.

**Christ will not allow the refining fire to be hotter or to last longer than is truly needed** for his servant's good.

Its outcome for believers differs completely from the punishment of the wicked.

Through his intercession, Christ makes his people acceptable to God.

Without reverence for God, no spiritual good can be expected.

Wrongdoing pursues sinners.

**God never changes**.

Although his judgment may be delayed, it will come; he is still as opposed to sin as ever.

Yet his unchangeableness is also our comfort.

**We have not been consumed because his compassion has not failed.**

## Return to the Lord and honor him (verses 7–12)

The people of that generation had turned from God and neglected his appointed ways.

He graciously called them back, yet they asked, **“How shall we return?”**

The Lord notices how our hearts respond when his word summons us.

It is a serious perversity to use suffering **as an excuse to sin**, when God may have sent that suffering to separate us from sin.

The prophet called the people urgently to reform.

God must receive first place in our service.

The welfare of **our souls** should matter more than the comfort of **our bodies**.

Trust him to provide what is needful.

He has blessings ready for us, but weak faith and narrow desires often leave us without room to receive them.

Those who honor him with their possessions will discover that doing so brings no ultimate loss.

## The Lord remembers those who fear him (verses 13–18)

Some people openly revealed wickedness through their arrogant words.

**Christ's yoke is easy**, but people who willfully persist in sin test God's patience.

We should judge such ways in light of the final judgment, when the outcome of arrogant wrongdoing will become clear.

Meanwhile, those who feared the Lord **spoke kindly to one another**, preserving and encouraging mutual love while sin multiplied around them.

They talked together as people who **fear God and think upon his name**.

Evil companionship corrupts character.

Good conversations strengthen faith and integrity.

A **book of remembrance** was written before the Lord.

God will not permit his children to perish alongside those who refuse faith.

They are vessels of mercy and honor, while unrepentant people face judgment.

Believers are **God's precious jewels**.

He preserves them when the world is burned like worthless material.

Those who now acknowledge him as their God will be acknowledged as his own.

We must serve him as loving children, **not as idle children who expect everything while doing nothing**.

Even his children continually need **sparing mercy**.

All people finally belong among those who serve God or those who refuse.

We often misjudge both categories.

But at **Christ's judgment seat**, every person's true character will be known.

We must examine where our own lot will lie while refusing to pronounce premature judgment upon others.

Then everyone will recognize that the truly wise and happy people were those who trusted and served the Lord.
