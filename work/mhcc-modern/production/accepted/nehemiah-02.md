# Nehemiah 2 — Modern English

## Nehemiah asks the king (verses 1–8)

Our prayers must be accompanied by serious efforts; otherwise we are making a mockery of prayer.

We are not restricted to fixed times when approaching the King of kings. We may turn to him at any moment. It is never the wrong time to approach his throne of grace.

Yet God's displeasure and the suffering of his people cause sorrow to his children—a sorrow that no earthly pleasure can remove.

The king encouraged Nehemiah to explain what troubled him. This gave Nehemiah courage to speak.

How much greater courage should we take from **Christ's invitation to pray** and his promise that our prayers will be heard! We can approach the throne of grace with confidence.

Nehemiah prayed to the God of heaven, who is infinitely greater than the powerful earthly king standing before him.

He raised his heart to the God who understands the language of hearts.

We should never undertake anything for which it would be wrong to seek and expect God's guidance, help and blessing.

His prayer received an immediate answer: the descendants of Jacob have never sought the God of Jacob in vain.

## Nehemiah comes to Jerusalem (verses 9–18)

After studying the situation, Nehemiah told the Jews that God had put it into his heart to rebuild Jerusalem's wall.

He did not intend to accomplish this work without them.

When we encourage ourselves and one another toward what is good, we strengthen one another to do it.

We perform our duties weakly when we are indifferent or careless.

## Opposition to the work (verses 19 and 20)

The hostility of the serpent's offspring toward Christ's cause is not limited to any particular age or country.

The lesson for us is clear. God's church needs our help. Is it not exposed to attacks and sometimes left desolate?

Does its low condition grieve us?

We must not become so absorbed by business, amusement or support for a particular group that Zion and its welfare no longer matter to us.
