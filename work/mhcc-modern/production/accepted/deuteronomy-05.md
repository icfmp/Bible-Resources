# Deuteronomy 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The covenant in Horeb. | 1–5 |
| The ten commandments repeated. | 6–22 |
| The request of the people that the law might be delivered through Moses. | 23–33 |

## Israel is called to hear and obey (verses 1–5)

Moses urged Israel to pay attention.

When we hear God's word, we must **learn** it.

Once we have learned it, we must **put it into practice**.

That is the purpose of listening and learning.

We are not meant merely to fill our heads with religious information or our mouths with talk.

God's word should direct our affections and conduct.

## The Ten Commandments are repeated (verses 6–22)

This account of the commandments differs in some wording from Exodus 20.

Henry compares that difference with the variations between the Lord's Prayer recorded in **Matthew 6** and **Luke 11**.

We must care more about faithfully receiving the truths and commands than about insisting that every repeated account use identical words.

The original reason for observing the Sabbath—the Lord's rest after creating the world on the seventh day—is not mentioned here.

That reason remains valid, but it is not the only one.

Here Moses appeals to Israel's deliverance from Egypt.

That deliverance pointed to our redemption through Jesus Christ.

Henry connects it with the Christian Sabbath, which remembers that redemption.

Through Christ's resurrection we were brought into the glorious freedom of God's children, by his strong hand and outstretched arm.

How gentle and life-giving the gospel sounds to a soul burdened by the terrors of a broken law!

## The people ask Moses to speak for them (verses 23–33)

Moses recalled how terrified the people had been when God gave the law.

Ever since humanity's fall, divine appearances have been frightening to guilty people.

But Christ has removed sin, and through him we are invited to approach the throne of grace boldly.

The people seemed sincere while they were deeply convicted by the word they had heard.

Yet conscience can be alarmed by the law without becoming clean.

Strong feelings may force people to make excellent promises without creating new and lasting principles within them.

God commended the words Israel spoke.

He truly desires the good and salvation of sinners.

He has supplied abundant evidence.

He gives people time and opportunity to repent.

He sent his Son to redeem us, promised the Spirit to those who ask, and declared that he takes no pleasure in sinners' destruction.

How good it would be if people always had the hearts they sometimes seem to have under conviction, painful providence or the approach of death.

The only way to be truly happy is to become holy.

It will go well with the righteous.

Believers should increasingly delight in learning and doing everything the Lord has commanded.
