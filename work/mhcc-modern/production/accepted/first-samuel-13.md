# 1 Samuel 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

| Saul sacrifices, He is reproved by Samuel. | 8–14 |
| The policy of the Philistines. | 15–23 |

## The Philistines invade Israel (verses 1–7)

Saul reigned for a year without any remarkable events.

In his second year, the events in this chapter occurred.

For more than a year, he had allowed the Philistines time to prepare for war and weaken and disarm the Israelites.

When people grow proud and trust their own sufficiency, they often make foolish decisions.

The enemies of God's people gain many of their best opportunities through the misconduct of those who claim to be the people's friends.

When Saul finally sounded the alarm, the Israelites were dissatisfied with his leadership or terrified by the enemy's strength.

Many failed to come to his aid, and others soon deserted him.

## Saul offers an unlawful sacrifice (verses 8–14)

Saul broke the specific instruction Samuel had given about what to do in an emergency (1 Samuel 10:8).

He offered the sacrifice himself without Samuel.

But Saul was neither a priest nor a prophet.

When challenged about his disobedience, he defended himself and showed no repentance.

He wanted to present a rebellious act as **prudent leadership and genuine religious devotion**.

People who lack inward godliness often place great importance on outward acts of worship.

Samuel told Saul that he had harmed himself.

Those who disobey God's commands act foolishly against their own good.

Sin is folly, and people deeply committed to it are deeply foolish.

Our response to seemingly small matters often reveals whether our hearts truly desire to obey God.

Human observers might consider Saul's act a minor offense.

But the Lord saw the **unbelief and distrust**, contempt for divine authority and justice, and rebellion against conscience that lay behind it.

Blessed Savior, may we never bring you our poor offerings or imagined peace offerings without trusting in your precious, entirely sufficient sacrifice.

You alone have made peace for us through **the blood of your cross**.

## The Philistines keep Israel without weapons (verses 15–23)

The Philistines acted cleverly when they gained the advantage.

They not only prevented the Israelites from making weapons.

They forced them to depend upon their enemies even for the tools needed to cultivate the land.

Saul had acted foolishly by failing to correct this danger when his reign began.

Lack of true judgment accompanies lack of grace.

Sins that seem small to us may have very dangerous consequences.

How miserable a guilty, defenseless nation is!

Yet even more miserable are those who lack **the whole armor of God**.
