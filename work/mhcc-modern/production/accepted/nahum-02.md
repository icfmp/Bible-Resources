## Nineveh's destruction is foretold (verses 1–10)

Nineveh would not be able to turn away the judgment approaching it.

**Neither human advice nor military strength can prevail against the Lord.**

God looks upon proud and powerful cities and brings them low.

Nahum described in detail the terrifying appearance of the attacking army and the fear it would produce.

The Assyrian empire is pictured as a **queen** about to be led away captive to Babylon.

When a guilty conscience is awakened in a day of trouble, the mind may be filled with terror.

What help can stored wealth and public glory offer in such a moment, or when the day of divine wrath arrives?

Yet how many people sacrifice their souls to obtain those very things!

## Assyria's cruelty brings judgment (verses 11–13)

The Assyrian kings had long terrorized their neighbors through their cruelty.

Now the Lord would break their power.

Some people excuse robbery and deceit by saying they have families to provide for.

But possessions gained through such wrongdoing will not bring lasting good to them or their households.

Those who fear the Lord and earn their living honestly may depend upon his care for themselves and those who belong to them.

God may justly remove children or the comfort parents expected to receive from them when the parents have used sinful methods to enrich the family.

People who have insulted God with their speech may find that their voices are no longer heard.

Therefore, rather than trusting in dishonest gain or worldly power, let us approach **God's throne of mercy**.

Through **our Lord Jesus Christ** we may have peace with God and know that he is for us.

Then we can trust that all things will work together for our everlasting good.
