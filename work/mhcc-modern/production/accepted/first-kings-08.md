## The ark is placed in the temple (verses 1–11)

Bringing the ark into the temple completed the work.

The people did so with great solemnity.

The ark was placed inside the Most Holy Place, where Israel expected God to speak from his mercy seat.

The poles were drawn out so that they pointed toward the mercy seat and guided the high priest when he entered once a year to sprinkle blood.

They were still useful, even though they were no longer needed for carrying the ark.

God's glory appeared in a cloud.

Henry sees **two images** in this darkness.

**First**, the earlier religious order was comparatively dark when set beside the light of the gospel, through which believers behold the Lord's glory more openly.

**Second**, our present lives are comparatively dark when set beside heaven, where God's glory will be fully revealed and seeing him will make his people happy.

## Solomon encourages the priests (verses 12–21)

The priests were astonished by the dark cloud.

Solomon encouraged them.

When God's providence seems dark, we should hurry toward the hope given in the gospel.

The best comfort comes from remembering God's word and considering his works in its light.

Whenever we accomplish something good, we should see it as **God fulfilling his promise to us**, not merely us fulfilling promises we made to him.

## Solomon prays to God (verses 22–53)

Solomon's prayer is a model for ours.

He gave **glory to God** before seeking further blessings.

New experiences of God's faithfulness should lead to fuller praise.

We should appeal to his promises, depending upon them in our requests.

People who desire new mercies should remain thankful for those already received.

God's promises should guide what we desire and form the ground of what we hope for in prayer.

The sacrifices, incense and ministry of the temple pictured **Christ's priestly office, offering and intercession**.

That is why people were to keep the temple in mind.

One word expresses all Solomon sought for Israel: **“forgive.”**

Every misery ultimately springs from sin.

Forgiveness therefore opens the way for the removal of evil and reception of good.

Without pardon, even an outward deliverance cannot become a true blessing.

Solomon also asked God to teach the people through his word **and through his discipline**, making their suffering spiritually useful.

Each person must recognize **the plague of his own heart**.

This includes the painful burden of guilt and the sinful desires still living within us.

Every true believer should learn to recognize these spiritual diseases, watch against their return and put them to death.

Awareness of inward sin drives us to prayer.

Solomon ultimately asked the Lord to hear his praying people.

Under the gospel, no special building or physical direction adds anything to a prayer.

**Christ is the reality to which the temple pointed.**

Whatever we ask in his name according to God's promise may be granted.

Through Christ, God's people are established and sanctified, the wanderer restored, the foreigner welcomed, mourners comforted and God's name glorified.

Sin causes our troubles; repentance and forgiveness lead toward human happiness.

## Solomon blesses and exhorts Israel (verses 54–61)

Solomon's farewell words were especially likely to remain with the people.

The blessings he requested are now obtained through **Christ's intercession**, of which his prayer was a type.

We receive sufficient, suitable and timely grace whenever we truly need it.

No human heart is naturally willing to obey the gospel call to repentance, faith and a renewed life.

Yet Solomon called the people to walk wholeheartedly in God's commands.

That is the way Scripture teaches us.

We must obey the law and the gospel's summons.

Because we know how sinful and weak we are, we pray for divine help, through which Christ enables us to serve God.

## Solomon celebrates with offerings (verses 62–66)

Solomon made many sacrifices.

He apparently observed the Feast of Tabernacles after the temple dedication.

Let us likewise return home **rejoicing** after worship, thankful for the Lord's goodness.
