# Acts 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The martyrdom of James, and the imprisonment of Peter. | 1–5 |
| He is delivered from prison by an angel. | 6–11 |
| Peter departs, Herod's rage. | 12–19 |
| The death of Herod. | 20–25 |

## James is killed and Peter imprisoned (verses 1–5)

James was a son of Zebedee.

Jesus had told him that he would drink his Master's cup and share his baptism of suffering (**Matthew 20:23**).

That prediction was now fulfilled.

Those who suffer with Christ will reign with him.

Herod imprisoned Peter.

Persecution, like other kinds of sin, is a **downhill path**.

Once people begin following it, stopping becomes increasingly difficult.

Anyone whose main purpose is **pleasing people** makes an easy target for Satan.

James finished the work appointed to him.

Peter, however, still had tasks to perform and was therefore safe despite appearing destined for an early death.

In a generation that prays coldly or scarcely prays at all, we may struggle to imagine the seriousness and intensity of the church's prayers for Peter.

If persecution became as severe as it was under Herod, faithful Christians would learn again what **prayer from the depths of the soul** means.

## An angel delivers Peter (verses 6–11)

A clear conscience, living hope and the Holy Spirit's comfort can make people calm even when they expect death.

Such peace may come to people who once were terrified by the thought of dying.

God often intervenes **when trouble has reached its most desperate point**.

Peter could trust that the Lord would bring his trial to the conclusion most suited to God's glory.

People delivered from **spiritual imprisonment** should follow their Deliverer just as the Israelites followed God out of Egypt.

They may not know the road ahead, but they know **whom they are following**.

When God chooses to save his people, obstacles cannot prevent him.

Even iron gates open before his power.

Peter's rescue pictures the redemption accomplished by Jesus.

Christ does more than **announce release to captives**.

He **brings them out of prison**.

Only afterward did Peter fully realize what God had done.

Similarly, believers may have genuine renewing grace before they possess a clear assurance that God has changed them.

The Comforter will eventually enable them to recognize that blessed transformation.

## Peter returns to the praying believers (verses 12–19)

God's promise to finish what he begins still leaves room for **human prudence**.

The disciples continued praying earnestly.

We ought to pray persistently and not lose heart.

As long as we are waiting for a needed mercy, keep asking God for it.

Yet sometimes we find it hardest to believe the very answer we desire most deeply.

Christ commands self-denial and willingness to suffer.

But these commandments do **not cancel our duty to protect our own safety through lawful means**.

During public danger, believers may trust God as a hiding place that persecutors cannot discover.

Those who carry out persecution are themselves exposed to danger under God's judgment.

Their rage often falls indiscriminately upon innocent people nearby.

## Herod is judged (verses 20–25)

Many pagan rulers claimed divine honors.

Herod's guilt was even greater because he knew the word and worship of the living God and yet accepted such honors without rebuking the blasphemy.

His pride and vanity prepared him for judgment.

God guards his own glory.

Those who refuse to glorify him may encounter his justice.

Our physical bodies are frail and already contain the beginnings of their own decay.

God need only speak, and human life can quickly end.

We may learn from the people of **Tyre and Sidon**, who sought peace with Herod because they depended upon him for provisions.

We have offended the Lord by sin, yet **depend upon him for life, breath and everything else**.

We should therefore humble ourselves and seek reconciliation through **the appointed Mediator**, who stands ready to help us, before judgment overtakes us.
