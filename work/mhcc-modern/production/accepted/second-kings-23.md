# 2 Kings 23 — Modern English

## Josiah reads the law and renews the covenant (verses 1–3)

God had told Josiah that Jerusalem's destruction could not now be prevented and that he would escape the coming disaster personally.

Nevertheless, Josiah carried out his duty and left the results with the Lord.

He solemnly called the people to abandon idolatry and serve God with genuine righteousness and holiness.

Most probably followed only outward forms or acted hypocritically.

Yet the reform would still prevent much public wickedness.

Each person remained accountable before God for personal conduct.

## Josiah destroys idols (verses 4–14)

What extraordinary wickedness was found in Judah and Jerusalem!

It scarcely seems possible that such practices could flourish where God was known and where his name was honored—in Salem and Zion, where his dwelling place stood.

Josiah had already reigned **eighteen years**.

He had personally set an excellent example and upheld religion according to God's law.

Yet when he investigated idolatry, he discovered that it reached very deeply and widely.

Both ordinary history and Scripture teach that **all genuine godliness and goodness** found on earth come through the recreating Spirit of Jesus Christ.

## Reform reaches Israel; the Passover is celebrated (verses 15–24)

Josiah extended his reform to the cities of Israel he could reach.

He preserved the grave of the man of God from Judah who had foretold the destruction of **Jeroboam's altar**.

After the land had been cleansed of the old leaven of idolatry, the people returned to celebrating the feast.

No Passover like this had been held during the previous reigns.

The revival of a neglected ordinance brought holy joy.

God rewarded their zeal in removing idolatry with unusual signs of his presence and favor.

We have reason to think true religion flourished through the rest of Josiah's reign.

## Josiah is killed by Pharaoh Neco (verses 25–30)

Reading these events, we must confess that although God's righteousness is as clear and solid as great mountains, **his judgments are deep and beyond our understanding**.

The reforming king died while he was still useful.

This was a mercy to Josiah because it spared him from seeing the approaching disaster.

But it was a judgment on his people, since his death opened the way for the nation's devastation.

## Jehoahaz and Jehoiakim rule wickedly (verses 31–37)

After Josiah's burial, one disaster followed another.

Within **twenty-two years**, Jerusalem was destroyed.

Many wicked people perished.

The remaining people were purified.

Josiah's reform had helped raise up others who joined the small number of faithful people, preserving the precious seed of the future church and nation.

Sometimes a short period and people with limited abilities are enough to undo the good for which faithful servants labored over many years.

But, blessed be God, the good work he begins through **his regenerating Spirit cannot be undone**.

That work withstands every change and temptation.
