# 2 Chronicles 32 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The invasion of Sennacherib, His defeat. | 1–23 |
| Hezekiah's sickness, His prosperous reign, and death. | 24–33 |

## Sennacherib invades Judah (verses 1–23)

Those who trust God to keep them safe must still use **appropriate means of protection**; otherwise they are testing him.

God will provide, but we must also take responsible action.

Hezekiah gathered his people and spoke words of comfort to them.

Confidence in the Lord raises believers above the fear of human beings.

Let faithful subjects and soldiers of Jesus Christ rest upon his word and boldly declare, **“If God is for us, who can be against us?”**

Through God's favor, enemies are defeated and friends are gained.

## Hezekiah's illness and death (verses 24–33)

God allowed Hezekiah to experience a trial without the usual supporting grace.

This exposed what remained in his heart and showed that he was not as mature in grace as he imagined.

It is good to discover our own weakness and sinfulness so that we do not become conceited or self-confident.

We must live in continual dependence on divine grace.

We do not know how corrupt our hearts may become or what we might do if God leaves us to ourselves.

Hezekiah's sin was that **his heart became proud**.

People who are great, godly or useful need especially to examine their weaknesses and remember how much they owe to freely given grace.

Let us ask God earnestly to keep us humble.

Hezekiah repaid the Lord poorly by turning the very gifts he had received into fuel for his pride.

We should avoid occasions of sin: **company, amusements, books and even sights** that feed our sinful desires.

Let us continually commit ourselves to the Lord's care and ask him never to abandon us.

Blessed be God, death will finally end the believer's conflict.

Then pride and every other sin will be abolished.

Never again will a believer be tempted to withhold the praise that belongs to the God of salvation.
