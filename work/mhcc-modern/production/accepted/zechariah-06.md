# Zechariah 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The vision of the chariots. | 1–8 |
| Joshua, the high priest, crowned as a type of Christ. | 9–15 |

## The chariots and divine Providence (verses 1–8)

The vision **may** represent the ways Providence governs the affairs of the world.

In all that happens, whether to the public or to private individuals, we should see events coming from **between the two mountains of bronze**, representing the Lord's immovable purposes and decrees.

It is as foolish to quarrel with these events as it is our duty to submit to God.

His providential purposes move with the speed and strength of chariots, but are always directed by his infinite wisdom and sovereign will.

The **red horses** picture war and bloodshed; the **black** depict its consequences—famine, disease and desolation.

The **white horses** indicate the return of comfort, peace and prosperity.

The **mixed colors** stand for events that bring prosperity to some and adversity to others, or a mixture of both.

Angels go out as messengers of God's purposes and ministers of his justice and mercy.

God also works through secret movements in human hearts by which Providence brings its plans to completion.

These are pictured by the four spirits of heaven, sent by the God of the spirits of all flesh.

All events spring from God's unchanging counsel, founded on perfect wisdom, justice, truth and goodness.

Henry considers it likely that certain historical events near the time of this vision correspond to it, but does not identify that correspondence as certain.

## Joshua's crown points toward Christ (verses 9–15)

Some Jews returning from Babylon brought contributions to God's house.

Those who cannot advance good work by being physically present should contribute what they can from their resources.

If some people supply their hands, **others may fill them**.

Crowns were placed on Joshua the high priest.

The sign drew attention to the promise that God would raise up, in the fullness of time, **a great High Priest**, whom Joshua foreshadowed.

Christ is not only **the Foundation of the spiritual temple but also its Founder**, who builds it by his Spirit and grace.

Glory is a burden, but not too heavy for the One who upholds everything.

The cross was his glory, and he carried it.

He also bears the exceeding weight of his crown.

The **counsel of peace** unites Christ's **priestly and royal offices**.

The gospel church's peace and the believer's welfare are achieved not through two separate persons, but through **two offices united in Jesus Christ**.

**As Priest, he purchases peace. As King, he maintains and defends it.**

The crowns were to remain in the temple as enduring witnesses to the promise of the Messiah.

We must not separate what God has joined in his purpose of peace.

We cannot approach God through Christ our **Priest** while refusing to submit to him as our **King**.

We have no sound reason to think ourselves at peace with God if we make no effort to obey his commandments.
