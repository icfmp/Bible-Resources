# Deuteronomy 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The blessings for obedience. | 1–14 |
| The curses for disobedience. | 15–44 |
| Their ruin, if disobedient. | 45–68 |

## The blessings promised for obedience (verses 1–14)

This long chapter develops two words: **blessing** and **curse**.

Both refer to real conditions with real consequences.

The blessings are described first.

God is slow to become angry and quick to show mercy.

He delights to bless.

It is better to be attracted to goodness by the hope of God's favor, as children who trust their Father, than to be driven to obedience merely through the fear of slaves.

The promised blessing depended upon Israel's careful attention to God's voice.

If the people maintained both the outward form and inward reality of religion within their families and nation, God's providence would prosper their earthly affairs.

## The curses associated with disobedience (verses 15–44)

Those who refuse God's commands not only lose the promised blessing but also place themselves under the curse.

The blessing includes true happiness; the curse brings misery.

Notice that the curse is just.

It does not come without reason or for a trivial offense.

Notice also its range and power.

Wherever the sinner travels, God's judgment follows.

Every possession and comfort becomes affected by the curse.

Earthly pleasures can turn bitter when God's anger is joined with them.

No genuine comfort remains.

Moses described many judgments that would come upon the Jewish people because of apostasy and disobedience.

Henry interprets them in light of what he believed had already happened to the Jewish people in his own day.

The final misery described includes loss of hope and comfort, leaving people close to despair.

People who live only by what they see, rather than by faith, risk losing even their sound judgment when the world around them becomes frightening.

## Israel's ruin and the curse's final meaning (verses 45–68)

Consider how terrible the calamities can be when God brings judgment even in this life.

Yet these earthly sufferings are only the beginning of sorrows for those who remain under his curse.

What then will be the suffering of the place where the worm does not die and the fire is not extinguished?

Henry finds something astonishing in the history of Israel.

He describes a nation formerly favored by God as later rejected and scattered among other nations while retaining a distinct identity.

He cites the Babylonian exile, the Roman destruction of Jerusalem and later prohibitions on Jewish access to Jerusalem as fulfillments of the warnings.

**These are Henry's historical and theological interpretations**, retained as part of the commentary rather than new editorial judgments about Jewish people today.

He contrasts Israel's refusal to serve God joyfully with the prospect of being made to serve enemies.

Everyone will finally recognize God's greatness, willingly or under judgment.

Henry highlights the predicted loss of the land (verse 63), bodily rest (verse 65) and even peace of mind.

He observes repeated migrations, banishments and returns.

He considers the correspondence between these historical events and Mosaic prophecy evidence of Scripture's truth.

He also anticipates a future conversion of Jewish people to Christ.

He expects this to precede a wider spread of Christianity among the nations.

This, again, is Henry's reading of the prophecies, not an independent new historical claim.

Moses, speaking through God's Spirit, warned sinners before their ruin.

Such warning gives people an opportunity to repent in time, and leaves them without excuse if they refuse.

Let us be thankful that **Christ redeemed us from the law's curse by becoming a curse for us**.

He bore in his own person the judgment our sins deserve—judgment that otherwise would have remained upon us forever.

Let sinners flee to him for refuge.

Let believers rejoice and serve their reconciled God wholeheartedly, grateful for the abundance of his spiritual blessings.
