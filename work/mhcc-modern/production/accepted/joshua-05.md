# Joshua 5 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

| The Captain of the Lord's host appears to Joshua. | 13–15 |

## Circumcision is renewed at Gilgal (verses 1–9)

How dreadful is the condition of people who see God's righteous judgment approaching but cannot turn it away or escape it!

That will be the final condition of the wicked.

The anguish and fear cannot adequately be described.

If only sinners would heed the warning now, before it is too late, and flee to the refuge offered in the gospel!

God brought such fear upon the Canaanites, breaking their courage.

This gave Israel a brief period of rest.

Circumcision was renewed and the disgrace associated with Egypt was rolled away.

The people were publicly acknowledged as God's freeborn children, bearing the seal of his covenant.

When God completes his people's salvation, he silences their enemies and turns their reproaches back upon them.

## Passover is observed and manna ceases (verses 10–12)

Israel celebrated the Passover at its appointed time on the plains near Jericho, despite the hostile Canaanites nearby.

The Lord fulfilled his promise that, when the people gathered for the appointed feasts, their land would remain under his special protection (Exodus 34:24).

Manna ceased as soon as the Israelites ate the old grain of Canaan.

God had provided it when they needed it.

He kept providing it for precisely as long as it was necessary.

We should not expect miraculous provision when the same needs can be met through ordinary means.

God's word and appointed acts of worship are like spiritual manna, feeding his people through the wilderness of this world.

Although we repeatedly forfeit these gifts through our sin, he graciously continues giving them while we remain here.

When we enter heavenly Canaan, we will no longer need them in the same way.

## Joshua meets the Captain of the Lord's army (verses 13–15)

This is the first recorded appearance of God's glory to Joshua.

Someone appeared before him in the form of a man.

Henry identifies him as **the Son of God, the eternal Word**.

Joshua gave the visitor divine honor.

He received that honor, which a merely created angel would refuse.

He is also called **Jehovah** in Joshua 6:2.

Christ appeared to Abraham as a traveler and to Joshua as a warrior.

He meets his people in the way their faith and circumstances require.

The Captain held a drawn sword.

This encouraged Joshua to prosecute the war courageously.

It showed that Christ was ready to defend and rescue his people.

Joshua needed to know whether this person was a friend or enemy.

In the conflict between Christ and Satan, no one can remain neutral as they might in ordinary disputes.

Joshua's question showed his genuine desire to know and obey the Lord's will.

Every true Christian must fight under Christ's banner.

Through his presence and help, they will overcome.
