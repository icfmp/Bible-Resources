## Micah and his mother turn to idols (verses 1–6)

The events described here and in the remaining chapters of Judges took place soon after Joshua died (see Judges 20:28).

The history shows how unhappy Israel could become without a judge, in contrast with the comparative peace experienced under the judges.

**Love of money** made Micah dishonor his mother by stealing from her.

That same love of money made his mother curse her own son.

Losses drive godly people toward prayer but can drive ungodly people toward cursing.

This woman's silver was already her god **before** it was shaped into a carved or cast image.

Micah and his mother agreed to turn their wealth into an idol and establish false worship in their home.

What caused this corruption?

Everyone did whatever appeared right in his own eyes.

Soon they began doing what was evil in the Lord's sight.

## Micah hires a Levite as priest (verses 7–13)

Micah saw the arrival of a Levite at his house as evidence that God favored him and his idols.

This is how people can deceive themselves.

When they are already pleased with their own false beliefs, they may treat any unexpected circumstance that helps them continue in wrongdoing as proof that God approves of it.

But an apparent success in Providence is no evidence that a sinful course has become right.
