## The Levites replace Israel's firstborn (verses 1–13)

Aaron and his two sons had a great deal of priestly work to carry out.

God appointed the Levites to help them.

Whenever he provides work for his servants, he can also provide the help they need.

The Levites were taken in place of Israel's firstborn.

When God saves us—as he saved Israel's firstborn—we receive an additional obligation to serve him faithfully.

The right God has over us through redemption confirms the right he already possesses as our Creator.

## The families and duties of Levi (verses 14–39)

The Levites were grouped according to Levi's three sons: **Gershon, Kohath and Merari**.

Each group was further divided into families.

Moses' descendants received no special rank or privilege above the other Levites.

This showed that Moses was not using his position to advance his own household or obtain special honors for his family.

Levi was by far the smallest of Israel's tribes.

God's chosen people are likewise a small flock compared with the world.

## The number of the firstborn and their ransom (verses 40–51)

The number of Israel's firstborn came close to the number of Levites.

God knows all his works before they happen.

The numbers were arranged in an exact relationship, which became clear when they were compared.

The small number of firstborn who exceeded the number of Levites were to be redeemed by a payment given to Aaron.

The church is called the **church of the firstborn**.

But it is not redeemed with silver or gold.

Because our sins have placed us under God's just judgment, only the precious blood of his Son can ransom us.

Every human being belongs to the Lord by creation.

Every true Christian belongs to him also by redemption.

We should each know our own responsibility.

No service required by such a Master should be dismissed as beneath us or too difficult.
