# 1 Kings 20 — Modern English

## Ben-hadad besieges Samaria (verses 1–11)

Ben-hadad sent Ahab an arrogant demand.

Ahab answered with a disgracefully submissive reply.

Sin brings people into such difficulties because it leaves them without God's protection.

If the Lord does not rule us, our enemies will.

Guilt saps people's confidence and makes them cowardly.

Ahab became desperate.

People will surrender even the things they love most in order to preserve their earthly lives.

Yet many would rather lose their souls than give up a single sinful pleasure or interest in order to save them.

Amid this folly, Ahab spoke one of his wisest sayings.

No one should boast before an approaching battle, since none of us knows what tomorrow will bring.

We should apply this to spiritual conflicts.

Peter fell because he was overconfident.

Blessed is the person who never neglects to keep watch.

## Ben-hadad is defeated (verses 12–21)

The proud Syrian army was defeated, and the despised Israelites won.

The drunken king's orders confused his troops and prevented them from attacking effectively.

People who feel most secure are often the least courageous when danger comes.

Ahab inflicted a severe defeat.

God sometimes makes one wicked person an instrument of judgment against another.

## The Syrians are defeated again (verses 22–30)

Ben-hadad's advisers suggested changing the battlefield.

They assumed that Israel's **gods**, rather than Israel itself, had won the earlier battle.

But their view of Jehovah was deeply mistaken.

They imagined that Israel worshiped several gods whose powers were limited to particular kinds of land.

They showed the emptiness of pagan ideas about God.

Great practical cleverness in worldly affairs can coexist with astonishing folly in understanding divine things.

## Ahab makes peace with Ben-hadad (verses 31–43)

Sinners have strong reason to repent and humble themselves before God.

Have we not heard that the God of Israel is merciful?

Have we not experienced his mercy?

**Gospel repentance arises from recognizing God's mercy in Jesus Christ**, through whom forgiveness is available.

Ben-hadad's conduct showed how quickly a person can change from arrogance in prosperity to humiliation in adversity.

An evil disposition may shape behavior in both conditions.

Ahab was one of those people whom prosperity failed to improve.

He did not know how to use success in serving God, helping his generation or even protecting his own true interests.

A wicked person may receive favor and still fail to learn righteousness.

A prophet confronted Ahab through a parable.

An obedient prophet was called upon to strike another by divine command.

Refusing that command brought punishment.

How much more blameworthy was Ahab for sparing **God's enemy and his own enemy** when the Lord had required judgment?

Ahab returned home resentful and displeased.

He did not truly repent or seek to put right what he had done.

His victory had not made him happy.

Many hear the good news of Christ and yet remain so occupied and distracted that the day of salvation passes them by.

## Editorial review — 1 Kings 20

**Full secondary original-English witness read:** [CCEL, 1 Kings 20](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xii.xx.html). Exact published **MHCC 0.1.1** remains primary, pending original-native SWORD key/reference and independent readback checks.

- **1–11:** arrogant demand/disgraceful submission, sin and lost protection, **surrendering worldly treasures to save life but not sin to save soul**, wise warning about boasting before battle and **Peter's self-confidence** — retained.
- **12–21:** drunken king disordering troops, proud Syrians defeated, God using **one wicked person against another** — retained.
- **22–30:** ignorant view that **Israel's many gods had limited geographical powers**, worldly skill together with religious ignorance — retained.
- **31–43:** **mercy in Christ as motive for true repentance**, arrogance/humiliation in changing circumstances, prosperity not sanctifying Ahab, **prophet's parable as a stricter comparison** between obedience of God's servants and Ahab's sparing of his enemy, **resentment without repentance**, missing salvation through distraction — retained.
