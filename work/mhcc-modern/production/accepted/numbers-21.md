# Numbers 21 — Modern English

## Israel defeats the people of Arad (verses 1–3)

Before Israel began traveling around Edom, the Canaanite king of Arad attacked the Israelites in the wilderness and captured some of them.

The incident was meant to lead the people to trust the Lord more completely.

## The bronze serpent and the healing of Israel (verses 4–9)

Israel became exhausted by the long journey around Edom.

The people complained bitterly about what God had already done and distrusted what he might do next.

If manna could not satisfy them, what would?

We must not let other people's contempt for God's word make us value it less.

That word is the real Bread of Life.

It nourishes to eternal life everyone who receives it by faith, no matter who dismisses it as worthless.

God justly judged the people for their complaints by sending venomous serpents into the camp.

Many were bitten and died.

They might never have admitted their sin without first suffering from its consequences.

But suffering led them toward repentance.

God then provided a remarkable cure.

Henry observes that Jewish interpreters themselves explain that merely seeing the bronze serpent did not heal people.

Rather, by looking up at it, the sufferers were looking toward God, the One who healed them.

The act was filled with gospel meaning.

Jesus himself said that just as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, the Son of Man must be lifted up, so that those who believe in him will not perish (John 3:14–15).

Our sickness is like theirs: sin bites and poisons the soul.

Their cure pictured ours.

They looked and lived.

We believe in Jesus and do not perish.

Faith turns our eyes toward him (Hebrews 12:2).

Anyone who looked was healed, however severe the bite, weak the eyesight or distant the sufferer.

God can save us through means that human reasoning would never invent.

How different people would live if they felt the poison of the ancient serpent's temptation as clearly as Israel felt those bites!

If we truly recognized the eternal danger of sin, we would not close our eyes to Christ or turn away from his gospel.

We would count everything else as loss compared with a crucified Savior.

We would call on him without delay.

Nor would we abuse the generosity of Christ's salvation once we understood the price he paid to provide it.

## Israel's journeys and the well of water (verses 10–20)

These verses describe Israel's movements until the people reached the plains of Moab, from which they would later cross the Jordan into Canaan.

The end of their journey was near.

It would be good for us, as we draw nearer to heaven, to grow more active and fruitful in the Lord's work.

The record also recalls God's extraordinary victories for Israel, including events around the River Arnon, Waheb in Suphah and neighboring places.

At every stage—and indeed every step—of life, we should remember what God has done for us.

We should recall distinctly the help he gave at particular times and places.

The Lord provided water, and Israel received it with joy and thanksgiving.

Their gratitude made the gift sweeter.

When we reach heaven, we will come to the fountain of living water.

Even now we should draw joy from the wells of salvation (Isaiah 12:3).

The bronze serpent pictured Christ lifted up to heal us.

The well pictured the Holy Spirit poured out to comfort us, from whom flow rivers of living water (John 7:38–39).

If that water is flowing within our souls, we should enjoy its comfort and give God all the praise.

God promised water, but the Israelites still had to open the ground to receive it.

We should expect his gifts while using the lawful means he has placed within our reach, remembering that all real power belongs to him.

## Sihon and Og are defeated (verses 21–35)

Sihon led his forces outside his own borders against Israel without provocation.

In doing so, he brought about his own defeat.

Enemies of God's church often destroy themselves through the plans they consider most clever.

Og, king of Bashan, could have learned from Sihon's defeat and sought peace.

Instead he made war and suffered the same destruction.

Wicked people make enormous efforts to protect themselves and their possessions from God's judgment.

But when judgment comes, their precautions cannot save them.

God gave Israel victories while Moses was still alive.

Moses could witness the beginning of the great conquest even though he would not live to see it completed.

These early victories were small compared with what lay ahead, but they promised greater things.

Christians must prepare for new struggles and new enemies.

We should make no compromise with the powers of darkness, expecting no permanent ceasefire with them.

Trusting God and obeying his commands, we will overcome every spiritual enemy.
