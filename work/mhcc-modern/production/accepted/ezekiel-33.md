# Ezekiel 33 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Ezekiel's duty as a watchman. | 1–9 |
| He is to vindicate the Divine government. | 10–20 |
| The desolation of Judea. | 21–29 |
| Judgments on the mockers of the prophets. | 30–33 |

## Ezekiel must warn people in danger (verses 1–9)

The prophet is **a watchman for the house of Israel**.

His work is to warn sinners about their misery and danger and call them **to abandon wickedness and live**.

If people perish because he **neglects that duty**, he brings guilt upon himself.

Consider the responsibility borne by those who excuse sin, flatter sinners and encourage them to suppose they will enjoy peace **while persisting in evil**.

Human beings often show far greater wisdom about temporary dangers than about spiritual dangers.

They appoint guards for houses and sentries to warn of approaching enemies.

Yet when their **everlasting happiness or misery** is at stake, they resent ministers who faithfully obey the Master's command to warn them.

They would rather perish while listening to **pleasant assurances**.

## God offers mercy to those who repent (verses 10–20)

God answered those who despaired of receiving mercy with a solemn declaration of **his readiness to forgive**.

The destruction of Jerusalem and its government had been determined.

But that did **not** determine the eternal destiny of every individual.

God promises life to the righteous.

Yet people who have made an outward profession of faith may ruin themselves through **proud confidence in their own righteousness**.

Depending on themselves, they fall into sin.

By contrast, people who have lived wicked lives may **repent, abandon their evil conduct and be saved**.

God's grace has brought about many astonishing and blessed changes like this.

When a person makes a settled break with sin, the separation between **that person and God** also ends.

## Judah fails to learn from its ruin (verses 21–29)

People are deeply resistant to instruction when they do not recognize their dependence upon God **even after every created comfort has failed them**.

Many claim the special promises given to true believers while their conduct proves them to be **God's enemies**.

They call their unfounded expectation **strong faith**.

But God's own testimony declares that they have earned the warnings of judgment rather than the promised blessings.

## Hearers do not necessarily obey God's word (verses 30–33)

People often come to faithful preaching for **unworthy or selfish reasons**.

Some attend merely to find something to criticize.

Far more come because of **curiosity or habit**.

Nevertheless, God can change their hearts.

Whether people listen or refuse, the outcome will eventually show that **God's servant was among them**.

Those who fail to appreciate God's mercies by making good use of them may justly discover their value **only when those mercies are taken away**.
