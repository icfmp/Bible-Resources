# Joshua 13 — Modern English

## The territory that remains to be taken (verses 1–6)

Joshua had grown old.

Henry considers him to have been around **one hundred years old**, but that is an estimate rather than a date stated in this passage.

It is a reminder that even the most useful servants of God must eventually be set aside from active work.

We are all moving toward the end of our earthly lives.

Whatever our hands find to do, we should do it with our strength (Ecclesiastes 9:10).

It is good when people who are advanced in age can look back on fruitful labor and encourage others to continue what remains unfinished.

Joshua still had work to arrange, even though he could not personally conquer all the remaining territory.

God's promises never fail merely because one human worker's strength is ending.

The Lord had promised to drive out the remaining Canaanites and would fulfill that promise at the proper time.

His people nevertheless had to exert themselves and act upon what he promised.

## The distribution of land east of the Jordan (verses 7–33)

The territories were assigned to Israel's tribes according to God's direction, not seized merely by whoever was most powerful.

The Lord gives different people their rightful places.

We should accept our own portion with gratitude instead of grasping at what belongs to others.

Under the gospel, Joshua pictures Jesus Christ, who obtains the heavenly inheritance for his people and gives them their share in it.

Moses had already granted Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh land east of the Jordan.

Their possessions were to remain within their appointed boundaries.

They had no right to claim additional land merely because God had shown them favor.

The Levites, unlike the other tribes, did not receive an ordinary territorial inheritance.

The Lord himself was their inheritance (Numbers 18:20).

Those serving in sacred ministry should not be driven by desire for riches or entangled in worldly concerns.

The community ought to provide for their legitimate needs, allowing them to devote themselves to the work entrusted to them.

We should all seek a better inheritance than earthly wealth.

God himself is a more precious portion than all the possessions we could gain.
