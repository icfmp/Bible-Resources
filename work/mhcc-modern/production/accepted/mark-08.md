# Mark 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Four thousand fed by a miracle. | 1–10 |
| Christ cautions against the Pharisees and Herodians. | 11–21 |
| A blind man healed. | 22–26 |
| Peter's testimony to Christ. | 27–33 |
| Christ must be followed. | 34–38 |

## Jesus feeds four thousand (verses 1–10)

Jesus welcomed even the humblest people who came to him for life and grace.

He knows our human weakness and takes our needs seriously.

Christ repeated the miracle of multiplying food to show that **his generosity is always ready**.

His favors continue to be renewed as our needs arise.

Those who live upon Christ by faith and receive his gifts with thanksgiving need not fear that his provision will fail.

## Jesus warns against hardened unbelief (verses 11–21)

Obstinate unbelief always finds an excuse, however unreasonable.

Jesus refused to satisfy the demand for a new sign.

People determined never to be convinced cannot be convinced by multiplying evidence.

How much reason have we to grieve for people whose hostility to the gospel harms **both themselves and others**!

When we forget God's former works and distrust him, we should correct ourselves as seriously as Jesus corrected his disciples.

How often we misunderstand his words, neglect his warnings and fail to trust his Providence!

## Jesus opens a blind man's eyes gradually (verses 22–26)

A blind man's friends brought him to Jesus, revealing their own **faith and concern**.

People spiritually blind may not yet pray for themselves.

Their family and friends should pray for them, asking Christ to touch and heal them.

This particular cure was **gradual**, unlike most of Jesus' miracles.

Henry sees in that pattern an image of how spiritually blind people often receive grace.

At first their understanding is **confused**.

But like early daylight growing toward the full light of day, their knowledge becomes clearer and clearer.

Those who despise Christ's gracious gifts risk losing the benefits they neglect.

They may discover their value only after those privileges have been withdrawn.

## Peter confesses Christ and is corrected (verses 27–33)

The gospel records these things so that readers may believe **Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God**.

His miracles show that he was not overcome but **the Conqueror**.

Once the disciples had acknowledged his identity, they were ready to hear more about his approaching sufferings.

Jesus began teaching them directly.

Christ sees what is wrong in our speech and actions even when we cannot see it ourselves.

He knows what spirit moves us more clearly than we do.

Human wisdom is foolish when it tries to set limits on God's purposes.

Peter still misunderstood **the nature of Christ's kingdom**.

## Every disciple must follow Jesus (verses 34–38)

Large crowds repeatedly came to Christ for help.

Everyone who wants him to heal the soul must hear his teaching about **self-denial**.

We must not make the body's ease our supreme good.

Eternal happiness with Christ more than compensates for losing even earthly life for his sake.

But winning the whole world through sin can never compensate for **the destruction of a soul**.

A day is coming when Christ's cause will be revealed as glorious, though some now despise it.

Let us learn to regard all earthly things as we will regard them **on the day of his appearing**.
