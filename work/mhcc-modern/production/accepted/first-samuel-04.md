# 1 Samuel 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Israelites overcome by the Philistines. | 1–9 |
| The ark taken. | 10, 11 |
| The death of Eli. | 12–18 |
| The birth of Ichabod. | 19–22 |

## Israel is defeated by the Philistines (verses 1–9)

Israel was defeated because sin—the accursed thing—remained in its camp, giving its enemies every advantage they needed.

The people recognized that God had allowed their trouble.

But instead of submitting, they complained angrily, as though they had done nothing to provoke him.

A person's own foolishness spoils the way, and then the heart becomes angry with the Lord (Proverbs 19:3), finding fault with him.

Israel supposed that bringing the ark into camp would force God to appear and fight for them.

People who have declined spiritually can sometimes become extremely attached to **outward religious observances**, imagining that these will save them.

The ark represented God's throne.

But bringing that throne into a military camp could not bring anyone to heaven while **the world and the flesh ruled the heart**.

## The ark is captured (verses 10 and 11)

The Philistines took the ark.

This was a terrible judgment on Israel and a sure sign of God's displeasure.

Let no one imagine that an outward profession of religion can shelter a person from God's wrath.

## Eli dies (verses 12–18)

The army's defeat grieved Eli deeply as a judge of Israel.

As a father, he also suffered when he learned that his two sons had died—the very sons he had treated with such indulgence, and who, he had reason to fear, died without repentance.

But the greatest blow came when the messenger announced that **the ark of God had been taken**.

That news struck Eli to the heart, and he died at once.

A person can die in terrible circumstances without necessarily being lost eternally.

Someone may experience an untimely and distressing death yet have **peace with God** in the end.

## Ichabod is born (verses 19–22)

The wife of Phinehas appears to have been a godly woman.

As she was dying, she grieved most over the ark being captured and God's glory departing from Israel.

What value could earthly happiness have for someone who was facing death?

At that moment, only joy that is spiritual and divine can truly comfort.

And how can people enjoy the comforts of creation when they have lost God's word and appointed worship—especially when they no longer enjoy the blessing of his gracious presence and the light of his face?

If God departs, the glory departs, and with it all that is truly good.

Woe to us if he leaves!

Yet even when God's glory is withdrawn from one sinful nation, town or village after another, it never disappears from the earth altogether.

When his light is hidden in one place, he causes it to shine somewhere else.
