# Hebrews 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The superior worth and dignity of Christ above Moses is shown. | 1–6 |
| The Hebrews are warned of the sin and danger of unbelief. | 7–13 |
| And of necessity of faith in Christ, and of stedfastly following him. | 14–19 |

## Christ is greater than Moses (verses 1–6)

Consider Jesus as **the Apostle of our confession**, sent by God to reveal the faith we proclaim and the hope we hold.

Consider him as **Christ**, the anointed Messiah, both Apostle and High Priest.

Consider him as **Jesus**, our Savior, healer and **great Physician of souls**.

Reflect upon what he is in himself, what he means to us and what he will be to us for eternity.

Careful meditation on Christ teaches us more of him.

The Jews rightly valued Moses' faithfulness, but Moses was only a **type** of Christ.

Christ **made and owns the house**—his church and people—and governs it as sovereign Lord.

Moses served faithfully **within** the house; Jesus rules it as **the eternal Son of God**.

It is not enough to begin Christian life well. We must persevere in faith to the end.

Meditating on Christ's person and salvation continually supplies wisdom and fresh reasons to trust, love and obey him.

## Do not harden your hearts (verses 7–13)

Periods of testing can easily become periods of provoking God.

Provoking him when he is exposing our complete dependence upon him is especially serious.

**Hardness of heart gives rise to every other sin**.

Other people's sins—including relatives' sins—should warn us.

The sins of people professing God's name grieve him as well as offend him.

The Lord is slow to destroy and waits patiently to show grace.

But prolonged, unrepented sin ultimately brings judgment. There is **no rest under God's wrath**.

All who wish to reach heaven must watch themselves.

Once we permit distrust of God, deserting him may soon follow. Those who believe they stand should beware of falling.

Tomorrow does not belong to us, so make good use of **today**.

Even the strongest believers need fellow Christians' encouragement.

And the weakest or least esteemed members deserve that same care from the whole church.

Sin disguises itself in many ways. It seems beautiful but is vile, promises pleasure but destroys, promises much and delivers nothing.

**Allowing one sin prepares the way for another**, and repeated wrongdoing strengthens the habit.

The deceitfulness of sin hardens the soul.

## Share in Christ through persevering faith (verses 14–19)

The privilege of God's people is being **partakers of Christ**—of his Spirit, new nature, graces, righteousness and life, and of what he has done and will do.

We must continue in the same sincere faith with which we began.

**Perseverance gives evidence of the genuineness of our faith**.

Listening to Scripture often can be a means of salvation, yet hearing without obedience increases responsibility under divine judgment.

Both the happiness of sharing Christ's complete salvation and the warning of everlasting misery should move us to persevere.

Do not put confidence in outward membership or religious professions.

Pray to be among the true believers who enter heaven, rather than among those who fall through unbelief.

Our obedience grows as faith grows; neglect and sin flourish where **unbelief** gains the upper hand.
