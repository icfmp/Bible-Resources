# 1 Samuel 22 — Modern English

## David at Adullam (verses 1–5)

Consider how God can use people who seem weak and unsuitable to carry out his purposes.

The Son of David is ready to receive suffering people who willingly submit to his leadership.

He welcomes everyone who comes to him, however sinful and miserable they may be.

He transforms them into a holy people and employs them in his service.

Those who desire to reign with Christ must be willing first to suffer **with him and for him**.

Notice how tenderly David cared for his elderly parents.

His first concern was to find them a quiet, safe place to live, whatever might happen to himself.

Children should learn to honor their parents by caring for their comfort and well-being.

Even if they rise to positions of honor or become occupied with demanding work, they must not forget their aging parents.

The Lord directs the steps of a righteous person.

He will preserve his people for the work he has appointed, however hated and exposed they may be.

## Saul orders the slaughter at Nob (verses 6–19)

Saul's conduct shows the cruelty of jealous malice and the miserable tricks it employs.

He considered everyone around him an enemy simply because they did not agree with everything he said.

Ahimelech answered Saul with the clear conscience of an innocent man.

But the evil spirit ruling Saul drove him to increasingly terrible wrongdoing.

He made accusations that were completely false and unproved.

Cruel rulers seldom lack instruments willing to carry out their cruelty.

After Doeg killed the priests, he went to **Nob** and killed the other inhabitants.

People whom God abandons to their own sinful desires can commit the vilest deeds.

Yet this judgment fulfilled the Lord's earlier warning concerning the house of Eli.

Saul acted wickedly, but God was righteous in permitting these events.

Not a single word of God's will and warning will fail.

## Abiathar escapes and finds David (verses 20–23)

David mourned the calamity deeply.

It pains a good person to discover that something he did contributed to other people's suffering.

David must have felt particularly distressed because his earlier **lie** had helped bring about this terrible event.

He assured Abiathar that he would protect him and spoke confidently of his own preservation.

All who belong to the Son of David may be certain of finding refuge with him (**Psalm 91:1**).

Despite the disorder, danger and constant urgency of his circumstances, David still made time for fellowship with God and drew strength and comfort from it.
