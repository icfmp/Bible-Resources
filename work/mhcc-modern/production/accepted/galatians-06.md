# Galatians 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Carry one another's burdens (verses 1–5)

Christians should **carry one another's burdens**.

Doing this fulfills **the law of Christ**, which calls us to mutual patience, compassion and practical support.

We travel through life together and should help fellow travelers under heavy loads.

It is easy to assume that we are wiser and better than others and therefore entitled to lecture them.

But that attitude is **self-deception**.

Someone who claims a superiority that does not exist will eventually discover the bitter consequences.

Such pride wins no genuine respect from God or other people.

Paul calls every believer to **examine their own work**.

The better we know our hearts and conduct, the less likely we are to despise others—and the more willing we become to help them through weakness and suffering.

Sins that seem small when committed become a **heavy burden** when their account must be given before God.

No one can pay another person's ransom through personal ability.

Sin is a spiritual weight upon the soul.

When someone feels **no weight of guilt at all**, that is reason for especially serious self-examination.

Many people are spiritually dead and therefore have no awareness of sin's burden.

When we recognize its weight, we should seek release through **the Savior** and learn to avoid every sin.

## Sow to the Spirit and do good without growing weary (verses 6–11)

Some people neglect religious duties while continuing to display an outward profession.

They may deceive other people, but **they cannot deceive God**.

He knows both conduct and motive, and he **will not be mocked**.

This present life is **sowing time**.

In the world to come, we shall reap the result of what is sown now.

There are two kinds of sowing: **to the flesh** and **to the Spirit**.

Those who live under the rule of sinful desires should expect **misery and ruin**, not eternal happiness.

But those who, under the Holy Spirit's guidance, live by **faith in Christ** and cultivate the graces of Christian living will reap **everlasting life** from the Spirit.

We often become tired of doing our duty, particularly **doing good for others**.

We must guard against that discouragement.

The promised reward is given to those who **persevere in good work**.

Every person should seek opportunities for useful service within their own responsibilities and means.

Do good **while you are alive**, making it a central purpose of your life.

When new opportunities arise, act on them as far as your abilities allow.

## Boast only in Christ's cross and become a new creation (verses 12–15)

Proud and worldly-minded people often want just enough religion to preserve an impressive public appearance.

Paul's greatest source of joy and honor, however, was **the cross of Jesus Christ**.

By the cross he meant **Christ's suffering and death**, the saving work of the crucified Redeemer.

Through Christ's cross, **the world is crucified to the believer and the believer to the world**.

The more we contemplate what the Savior endured from this world, the less attractive its praise and pleasures become.

Henry illustrates this with the dreadful appearance of someone dying by crucifixion.

The person beholding such suffering is not charmed by the victim's former physical beauty.

Nor would a dying person hanging upon a cross be captivated by the earthly scenes still visible in the final moments.

Likewise, to those who truly believe in Jesus Christ, worldly attractions lose their power **in comparison with him**.

There is **a new creation**.

Old attitudes and desires give way to a new understanding and way of living through **the regenerating work of the Holy Spirit**.

Believers enter a new world.

Created in Christ for good works, they receive a life increasingly marked by holiness.

This change affects **the mind and heart**, leading us to trust Jesus and live for God.

Where such genuine inward and practical faith is missing, outward religious labels and professions accomplish nothing.

## Peace and mercy in the grace of Christ (verses 16–18)

The greatest distinction between people is not status or religious ceremony, but **a new creation formed in Christ's likeness through faith**.

God promises **peace and mercy** to those who walk according to this rule.

Peace includes reconciliation with God, an untroubled conscience and the earthly comforts genuinely needed for our good.

Mercy means a share in the free love and favor of God in Christ, the fountain from which every other blessing flows.

**God's written word** must guide us, in both doctrine and conduct.

May his grace always be with our spirits, sanctifying, enlivening and comforting us.

May we remain ready to defend the honor of the truth that gives us life.

Paul carried **the marks of Jesus in his own body**—scars received from persecutors because he remained faithful to Christ and the gospel.

He addressed the Galatians as **brothers and sisters**, displaying humility and tender love.

He ended by praying that they would receive **Christ's gracious favor**, both in its actual benefits and in the assurance that it was theirs.

We need no greater blessing for happiness than **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ**.

Paul prayed not that Moses' law or righteousness earned by works would remain with them, but that **Christ's grace** would live in their hearts and spirits, giving life, comfort and strength.

His final **Amen** expressed both his earnest wish that this blessing might be theirs and his confidence that it would be granted.
