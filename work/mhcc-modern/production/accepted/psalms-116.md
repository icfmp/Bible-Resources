# Psalm 116 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist declares his love to the Lord. | 1–9 |
| His desire to be thankful. | 10–19 |

## Loving the God who saves (verses 1–9)

We have many reasons to love the Lord, but his kindness affects us especially deeply **when he rescues us from terrible trouble**.

When sinners become aware of their true condition and fear that God's righteous judgment will destroy them, they experience great sorrow and distress.

Let them call upon the Lord to save their souls.

They will find him **gracious and faithful to his promises**.

Neither a person's former ignorance nor guilt will prevent salvation **when that person trusts the Lord**.

We should describe God according to our experience of him.

Have we ever found him anything other than **just and good**?

Only his mercy prevents us from being consumed.

Let those who labor and carry heavy burdens come to him and receive **rest for their souls**.

If they wander from that rest, let them return quickly, remembering how generous he has been.

We should live with a continual sense of being in his presence.

It is a great mercy to be kept from being swallowed up by overwhelming sorrow.

It is also a mercy when God upholds our right hand so that temptation does not overpower and overthrow us.

When we enter **the heavenly rest**, deliverance from sin and sorrow will be complete.

We will see the Lord's glory and walk in his presence with a joy we cannot yet imagine.

## Gratitude expressed in service and devotion (verses 10–19)

During trouble, it is often best to be quiet because pain may lead us to speak rashly.

**Genuine faith may coexist with struggles of unbelief**, but faith will eventually prevail.

When we humble ourselves for distrusting God's promises, we can experience his faithfulness.

What can a forgiven sinner, or anyone rescued from danger, offer in return for the Lord's goodness?

We cannot add anything to his wealth or happiness.

Our very best falls short of what he deserves.

Yet we should dedicate **ourselves and all we possess** to him.

The psalmist resolves to take **the cup of salvation**.

This includes the drink offering appointed by the law as a thankful response to God.

It also includes receiving **the cup of affliction**: bitter suffering can be sanctified to God's people and become a means of spiritual healing.

And it includes **the cup of consolation**: accepting God's comforts as gifts from his hand and recognizing his love in blessings enjoyed now as well as in the coming inheritance.

The psalmist chooses to be God's servant regardless of whom others serve.

People may become servants in two ways.

**First, through birth**: the psalmist was born in the Lord's household as the son of one of his servants.

Having godly parents is a great mercy.

**Second, through redemption**: God broke his bonds and rescued him.

Those loosened bonds made him **more firmly devoted to his Redeemer**.

Doing good is a sacrifice that pleases God, and it must accompany thanksgiving.

Why would we offer the Lord something that costs us nothing?

The psalmist resolved to fulfill his vows immediately.

He would do so **publicly**, not to boast, but to show that he was not ashamed of God's service and to invite others to join in.

Such are God's genuine saints, whose lives and deaths will bring him glory.
