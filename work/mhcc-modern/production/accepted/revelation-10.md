# Revelation 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Angel of the covenant presents a little open book, which is followed with seven thunders. | 1–4 |
| At the end of the following prophecies, time should be no more. | 5–7 |
| A voice directs the apostle to eat the book; | 8–10 |
| and tells him he must prophesy further. | 11 |

## The glorious angel and the seven thunders (verses 1–7)

John saw another vision.

Henry suggests that the figure communicating the revelation was **probably Jesus Christ himself**, or at least appeared in a way intended to reveal Christ's glory.

The Lord veils the greatness of his glory because mortal eyes cannot bear it.

He also conceals some of his dealings with the world.

The **rainbow over the figure's head** reminds us that Christ continually remembers his covenant.

The dreadful voice was answered by **seven thunders**, solemn and awe-inspiring expressions of divine revelation.

We do **not** know what those thunders announced or **why their message was withheld**.

Events of great importance—possibly concerning the history of Christ's church—may not be disclosed in the public prophecies of Scripture.

Nevertheless, the Lord has pledged through his unfailing word that the righteous will finally be saved and that true religion will ultimately triumph on earth.

Although the fulfillment may not yet have come, it cannot be far away compared with eternity.

For each of us personally, the time when earthly life ends is certainly near.

Then time will be over for us.

Believers will enter a happy eternity, from which they will see and rejoice in the triumph of **Christ and his cause**.

## John eats the little scroll and must prophesy again (verses 8–11)

Most people find it appealing to learn about events that lie ahead.

Those who love God also rejoice to receive his word.

But when John fully absorbed the contents of the prophetic scroll, their meaning became **bitter**.

They foretold terrible things: fierce persecutions of God's people and devastating judgments upon the world.

Knowing these sufferings in advance would bring grief, despite the initial sweetness of receiving the revelation.

Let us seek to be taught by **Jesus Christ** and to obey his commands.

Meditate on his word daily, allowing it to nourish your soul.

Then share that word faithfully in whatever responsibilities God has given you.

The sweetness of studying Scripture will sometimes be mixed with sorrow when we compare its truths with the condition of the world, the visible church **and our own hearts**.
