# Daniel 11 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The vision of the Scriptures of truth.**

## Persian and Greek rulers, and their conflicts (verses 1–30)

The angel showed Daniel **the succession of the Persian and Greek empires**.

The kings of **Egypt and Syria** were also described.

**Judea lay between their territories** and suffered because of their conflicts.

Henry notes that **verses 5–30** are generally understood as describing events under those governments.

He also says that **from verse 21 onward**, the prophecy is generally understood as referring to **Antiochus Epiphanes**, a cruel and violent persecutor of the Jews.

Consider how quickly worldly splendor, wealth and the power used to obtain them **decay and disappear**.

In his Providence, God raises one ruler and removes another as he chooses.

This world is filled with wars and conflicts arising from **human desires**.

God foresees perfectly **every change of government, revolution and event**.

Not one word he has spoken will fail.

His purposes and declarations will certainly come to pass.

While the rulers of this world fight one another like **potsherds of the earth**, they sometimes win and sometimes lose, deceive others and are themselves deceived.

But those who know God will trust him.

He will enable them to **stand firm, carry their cross and endure their conflict**.

## Later conflicts and the final hope (verses 31–45)

Henry calls this remaining part of the prophecy **very difficult**.

He notes that commentators **disagree considerably** about its meaning.

He suggests that the account may pass from **Antiochus** to **antichrist**.

He sees possible references to **the Roman Empire**, the fourth monarchy, in its **pagan, early Christian and papal forms**.

These remain **Henry's tentative historical-prophetic identifications**, not explicitly named entities in the prophecy.

The end of God's anger toward his people approaches, as does the end of his patience toward his enemies.

To escape the ruin awaiting **unbelief, idolatry, superstition, cruelty and open ungodliness**, let us make **God's word** our standard for truth and conduct, the foundation of hope and the light along our path.

It will guide us through this dark world toward **the glorious inheritance above**.
