# Romans 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jewish converts cautioned against judging, and Gentile believers against despising one the other. | 1–13 |
| And the Gentiles exhorted to take heed of giving offence in their use of indifferent things. | 14–23 |

## Do not despise believers with different convictions (verses 1–6)

Differences of opinion existed even among Christ's first followers and their disciples.

Paul did not try to erase every such difference.

Forcing people to agree with a doctrine or follow outward observances **without personal conviction** would be hypocritical and useless.

Trying to produce absolute uniformity of opinion among Christians would be unproductive.

Therefore, do not let fellowship be spoiled by endless arguments over words.

When tempted to despise a brother or sister, ask: **Has God received this person? If so, how dare I reject them?**

A Christian exercising liberty should not ridicule a cautious believer as ignorant or superstitious.

A scrupulous believer should not condemn someone who uses liberty, because God accepts the believer without treating food distinctions as grounds for acceptance.

We take God's place when we claim to judge **the hidden motives and intentions of other people**.

The question of observing certain days is similar.

Those understanding that Christ fulfilled the old ceremonial observances no longer considered Jewish festivals binding.

But merely being personally satisfied about a practice is not enough.

Our confidence must be **grounded in God's word**.

Do not act against **a doubtful conscience**.

We readily treat our own convictions as the standard everyone else should follow, even when the matter is genuinely disputable.

Christians should not condemn each other over lesser matters.

Thankfulness to God, the Giver of our blessings, makes ordinary enjoyments good and sweet.

## We all belong to the Lord who judges us (verses 7–13)

Though some believers are strong and others weak, all must agree on this: **we do not live for ourselves**.

Anyone bearing Christ's name who deliberately makes personal pleasure the highest goal contradicts genuine Christianity.

Our life's purpose is to **please God**, not ourselves.

True Christianity makes Christ central in everything.

Believers may differ in knowledge, spiritual strength and conduct over lesser matters, yet they all belong to the same Lord and seek to serve him.

Christ rules the living and will raise the dead.

Do not despise or condemn one another, because all will soon give an account before him.

Thinking seriously about final judgment would quiet much rash criticism.

Let each person examine their own heart and life.

Those careful to judge and humble themselves are less likely to despise their neighbors.

We must avoid saying or doing anything likely to make others **stumble or fall**.

Henry distinguishes a lesser offense causing grief from a more serious action drawing someone into **actual guilt**.

## Freedom must be governed by love (verses 14–18)

Christ deals gently with people who possess genuine grace even when that grace is weak.

Remember **why Christ died for our brothers and sisters**.

Leading someone into sin threatens that person's soul.

Christ denied himself so far as **to die for them**.

Should we not deny ourselves **some lawful enjoyment** to protect them?

We cannot stop every careless tongue from criticizing us.

But we should avoid giving needless grounds for such criticism.

Sometimes we should forgo what is otherwise lawful because using our liberty selfishly would damage our testimony or another person's conscience.

Good actions can be spoken against when we perform them **without charity**.

Protect the honor of the faith we profess.

**Righteousness, peace and joy** are central blessings of God's kingdom.

**Toward God**, our concern is to stand justified through **Christ's death** and sanctified through **the Spirit's grace**.

**Toward our neighbors**, our concern is peace, love and charity toward everyone.

**Within ourselves**, the blessing is **joy in the Holy Spirit**, a joy arising from knowing God as our reconciled Father and heaven as our promised home.

Only devotion **to Christ** makes our service acceptable to God.

People most pleased with God tend to please him most.

They enjoy and promote peace and spiritual joy, and wise and good people recognize the value of their conduct.

Other people's disapproval should not rule us.

## Pursue peace without violating conscience (verses 19–23)

Many people speak loudly about wanting peace yet fail to practice the things that create it.

**Gentleness, humility, self-denial and love** make for peace.

We cannot strengthen one another spiritually while fighting and arguing.

Some ruin God's work in themselves through excess in food and drink, yielding to bodily desires.

Others injure fellow believers by **deliberately provoking their consciences**.

An act may be lawful in itself and yet **wrong because of the circumstances**, especially when it leads another person toward sin or distress.

This applies to every morally indifferent practice that injures a fellow Christian's grace, comfort or good resolve.

When Paul asks, “Do you have faith?” he refers here to **knowledge and certainty about Christian liberty**.

Enjoy your freedom thankfully, but do not use it in a way that troubles others.

Nor should you act while your own conscience remains in doubt.

How excellent are the blessings of Christ's kingdom!

It does not consist in outward ceremonies but in **righteousness, peace and joy in the Holy Spirit**.

Serving God is better than any other service.

We are not called to live and die for ourselves but **for Christ, to whom we belong**.
