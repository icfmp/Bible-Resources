# Ruth 2 — Modern English

## Ruth goes to glean in Boaz's field (verses 1–3)

Notice Ruth's humility.

When Providence made her poor, she accepted her circumstances cheerfully.

Proud people might rather starve than take a humble position.

Ruth was different.

She herself proposed gathering leftover grain.

She asked permission with modesty.

We should not demand another person's kindness as though it were owed to us, but ask for it and accept it gratefully, even in small matters.

Ruth was also diligent.

She had no desire to eat the food of idleness.

Her example is especially valuable for young people.

Diligence promises good both for this life and for the next.

We must not be ashamed of any honest work.

No lawful occupation is beneath us.

Sin should be beneath us; nothing else is shameful when Providence calls us to it.

Ruth also cared for her mother-in-law and trusted in God.

The Lord wisely orders even events that seem unimportant or uncertain, directing them to his glory and the good of his people.

## Boaz treats Ruth kindly (verses 4–16)

The godly, kindly words exchanged between Boaz and his workers show that there were faithful people in Israel.

How different this was from the immoral and corrupt speech heard too often in workplaces!

A stranger who heard them would gain a very different impression of Israel from what someone might gather from the careless speech common elsewhere.

Genuine faith teaches people to behave rightly whatever their social position.

It produces kind employers, faithful workers and harmony within households.

It fosters love and kindness between people of different ranks.

Boaz prayed for his workers when he arrived.

They replied with respect rather than speaking bitterly about him as soon as he was gone.

Relationships work well where employers and workers share such goodwill.

They expressed kindness by **praying for one another**.

Boaz noticed the unfamiliar woman and ordered that she be treated well.

Employers must ensure not only that they themselves do no wrong but also that people under their authority do not mistreat others.

Ruth humbly acknowledged that she had no right to expect these favors, having been born and raised among pagans.

We should all think humbly of ourselves, regarding others as worthy of honor.

Boaz's kindness toward poor Ruth also points to **the Lord Jesus Christ's kindness toward needy sinners**.

## Ruth returns with the grain she gathered (verses 17–23)

Even humble work like gleaning produces something useful.

This should encourage diligence.

Ruth valued what her hard work had obtained and took care to preserve it.

We should likewise guard against losing the spiritual good we have worked for (2 John 1:8).

Parents should ask children about their daily activities as Naomi did.

They should not question them so harshly that they dread being home or feel tempted to lie.

Instead, they should praise what is good and gently correct or caution them when needed.

Each evening we should ask ourselves: **Where have I gleaned today?**

Have I grown in knowledge and grace?

What have I done that will bear lasting good fruit?

When the Lord deals generously with us, let us not search for happiness or satisfaction in another field.

We lose divine favors when we treat them lightly.

Ruth carefully followed Naomi's instructions.

When the harvest ended, she stayed home to keep her elderly mother-in-law company.

Dinah once went out merely to visit the daughters of the land, and her vanity ended in shame (Genesis 34).

Ruth went out only to obtain food and help provide for Naomi.

Her humility and hard work finally led to honor.
