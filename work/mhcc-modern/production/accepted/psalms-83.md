# Psalm 83 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The designs of the enemies of Israel. | 1–8 |
| Earnest prayer for their defeat. | 9–18 |

## The enemies of God's people make plans (verses 1–8)

At times God seems not to be responding to unjust treatment of his people.

Then we may call upon him as the psalmist did.

All wicked people oppose God in some sense, and persecutors especially show themselves to be his enemies.

The Lord's people are **his hidden ones**.

The world does not truly know them, but he takes them under his special care.

If the enemies of the church can agree in their effort to destroy it, should not its friends unite in defending it?

Wicked people often wish religion would disappear from human society.

They would welcome the removal of every restraint it places upon their conduct, and would silence those who preach, profess and practice it.

They would go further if they had the power.

There have always been many enemies of God's church.

Their number makes the Lord's power in **preserving a people for himself** still more wonderful.

## Prayer for God's judgment and enemies' repentance (verses 9–18)

Everyone who persists in opposing Christ's kingdom should take seriously the judgment described here.

God remains the same toward his people and toward those who attack them.

The psalm asks God to make the enemies' purposes **as unstable as a turning wheel**, and to scatter them like stubble.

It also uses the image of stubble consumed by fire to speak of final judgment.

But Henry makes an essential pastoral distinction:

**We should desire no humiliation for our enemies or persecutors except what may help lead them to repentance and conversion**.

The judgment of God will overtake those who continue to resist him unless they turn and seek the pardoning mercy of the Lord they have offended.

God's victories demonstrate that **Jehovah** possesses all power and perfection in himself.

Let us fear his judgment, surrender willingly to him and seek his mercy.

Above all, let us seek deliverance through the destruction of **our own sinful fleshly desires, which fight against the soul**.
