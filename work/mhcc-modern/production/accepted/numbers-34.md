## The boundaries of Canaan (verses 1–15)

Canaan was a relatively small country.

Henry reckons it at about **160 miles long and 50 miles wide** within the boundaries described here.

Yet this was the land promised to Abraham, the father of the faithful, and to Israel's descendants.

For many centuries, this small region was the place where God alone was truly known.

It was the Lord's vineyard, a garden enclosed.

Just as small gardens and vineyards can be extraordinarily productive, Canaan's limited area was compensated for by the fruitfulness of its soil.

The whole earth belongs to the Lord, with everything in it.

Still, relatively few people know him and serve him.

Those few are blessed, because they bear fruit for God.

Notice how little of the world he gives even to his chosen people.

People who have their inheritance in heaven have good reason to be content with a small share of this earth.

The little that a righteous person possesses, received with God's love and blessing, is better and brings more comfort than the riches of many wicked people.

## The people appointed to divide the land (verses 16–29)

God named the men who would distribute the inheritance among the tribes.

The certainty of Israel's coming victory was so great, since God would fight for the people, that he identified in advance the very men who would be entrusted with dividing the conquered land.
