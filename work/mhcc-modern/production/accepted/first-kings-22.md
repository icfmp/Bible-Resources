## Jehoshaphat makes an alliance with Ahab (verses 1–14)

Some genuinely godly people are naturally agreeable and accommodating.

That disposition can tempt them into friendship with open enemies of true religion.

It is especially dangerous because they may begin tolerating speech and behavior they ought to oppose.

A godly person should take faith along wherever he goes and should not be ashamed to acknowledge it among people who disregard it.

Jehoshaphat had not left his respect for God's word behind in Jerusalem.

He openly requested it even at Ahab's court.

Ahab's prophets used the name of Jehovah **to please Jehoshaphat**.

But their message of guaranteed success was designed **to please Ahab**.

False prophets may imitate true prophets' speech, but those with mature spiritual discernment can recognize the deception.

**One faithful prophet of the Lord was worth all of them.**

Worldly people have always been unreasonable about religion.

They expect preachers to adjust their doctrine to current fashions and the audience's taste, while still claiming divine authority for words people want to hear.

They treat someone who refuses such manipulation as foolish or uncivil, as though deceiving others were merely a sensible way of protecting one's interests.

## Micaiah foretells Ahab's death (verses 15–28)

One of the greatest acts of kindness we can do for a person on a dangerous path is to warn that person clearly.

Micaiah described his vision to leave Ahab without excuse and teach everyone listening.

The scene is represented in language suited to human understanding.

We must not imagine that God needs to form new plans, consult angels or other creatures about what to do, or change his mind after receiving advice.

**Nor should we imagine that God is the author of sin or the cause of a person telling or believing a lie.**

Micaiah did not strike Zedekiah back when the false prophet struck him.

Zedekiah loudly claimed the Spirit's authority, as people ignorant of the Holy Spirit's true work often do.

Micaiah left him to discover his error when the prophecy was fulfilled.

People unwilling to let God's word correct their mistakes in time will eventually be undeceived by divine judgment, when it is too late.

We might be ashamed of calling our own difficulties severe if we considered what faithful servants of God have endured.

Yet a life without trouble may prove even more dangerous.

We can be drawn toward unfaithfulness and worldly conformity more easily through **flattery and rewards** than by threats.

## Ahab dies in battle (verses 29–40)

Ahab intended to expose Jehoshaphat to danger while protecting himself.

See the reward of joining oneself to wicked people!

How could Ahab be trustworthy toward a friend when he had been unfaithful to God?

Jehoshaphat had told Ahab, **“I am as you are.”**

Now, because of the way they went into battle, he was mistaken for Ahab.

People who associate with evildoers risk sharing the harm that comes upon them.

God rescued Jehoshaphat, showing that though he was displeased with the alliance, he had not abandoned his servant.

The Lord is a friend who never fails when other friends do.

No one can hide from divine judgment.

God directed the arrow that struck Ahab.

Those whom God has appointed to die cannot escape that sentence by their own planning.

Ahab survived long enough to see part of Micaiah's prophecy come true.

He had time to feel himself dying and to remember the evil he had done.

What terror those memories must have brought!

## Jehoshaphat's reign over Judah (verses 41–50)

Jehoshaphat's reign was among the best for both true devotion and prosperity.

He pleased God, and God blessed him.

## Ahaziah's reign over Israel (verses 51–53)

Ahaziah's reign was very brief—**less than two full years**.

God brings some sinners to judgment quickly.

Scripture gives Ahaziah a terrible character.

He would not receive instruction or learn from the warnings he saw.

He imitated his wicked father and followed the counsel of his still-living mother, **Jezebel**, who was even more corrupt.

Children are deeply miserable when their parents not only pass on a sinful nature but actively teach them to increase their wickedness.

Parents who encourage their children toward destruction bring dreadful consequences upon their own households.

Hardened sinners can rush forward without fear along the same paths that have already brought others to eternal ruin.
