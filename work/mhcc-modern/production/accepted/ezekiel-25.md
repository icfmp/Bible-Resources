# Ezekiel 25 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Judgments against the Ammonites. | 1–7 |
| Against the Moabites, Edomites, and Philistines. | 8–17 |

## The Ammonites are judged for rejoicing in Judah's ruin (verses 1–7)

It is wicked to rejoice when **anyone suffers**, especially when the sufferers belong to God's people.

God will certainly call such cruelty to account.

Even while he permits Israel to be held captive in Babylon, he will show that **he remains the God of Israel**.

It is better to **know the Lord while poor** than to be wealthy and ignorant of him.

## Moab, Edom and Philistia face judgment (verses 8–17)

The same outward event may happen to both righteous and wicked people, yet its meaning and final consequence may be **entirely different**.

Those who boast in any protection apart from **God's power, Providence and promises** will eventually be ashamed of their confidence.

People who refuse to **leave vengeance in God's hands** may expect his judgment to fall upon them.

The Lord's judgments are fair.

Sometimes he avenges an injury through **the people who suffered it**, and the justice of his action remains visible.

Those who nurture **old hatreds**, waiting for a chance to act upon them, are storing up **wrath for the day of wrath**.
