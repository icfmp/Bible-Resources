# Psalm 150 — Modern English

## Original heading

**A psalm of praise.**

## Everything that breathes, praise the Lord

We are summoned to praise God in his sanctuary and for the privilege of worshiping him.

The heavens display his power and glory in ways that their blessed inhabitants see far more fully than we do.

Yet **his greatest mighty work has been revealed in his earthly sanctuary**: the redemption of human beings.

**God's holiness and love shine more clearly in redemption than in all his other works**.

We should therefore praise our God and Savior.

The precise names of the musical instruments are not the heart of the passage. What matters is that **we spare no effort or expense in serving God**.

Praise him with **strong faith**.

Praise him through **holy love and delight**.

Praise him with **complete confidence in Christ**.

Praise him in **believing triumph over the powers of darkness**.

Praise him through **obedience to every command**.

Praise him with **cheerful submission to all his appointments**.

Praise him by **rejoicing in his love and taking comfort in his goodness**.

Praise him by **promoting the interests of the kingdom of grace**.

Praise him with **hope in the kingdom of his glory**.

Soon each of us will draw a final breath. Therefore, while we still have breath, **let us praise the Lord**, so that even death may find us full of comfort.

Let everything that breathes praise the Lord. **Praise the Lord!**

This is a fitting conclusion to the book of Psalms, inspired by the Spirit and written for praise.

Henry emphasizes that it has provided songs for God's people for **more than three thousand years**, and he calls it the book most frequently quoted by Christ and his apostles.

It speaks of God's majesty and government, addresses every human condition and every sort of spiritual experience, and displays **clear marks of divine inspiration**.
