# Isaiah 21 — Modern English

## Babylon's capture (verses 1–10)

Babylon lay in a flat country with abundant water.

Isaiah repeatedly predicted its destruction, which Henry also sees as a picture of **the downfall of the great enemy of the New Testament church** foretold in Revelation.

For oppressed captives, the news of Babylon's ruin would bring relief.

For proud oppressors, it would bring distress.

Let this teach us not to rely on empty amusement and bodily pleasure, because we do not know how suddenly laughter may end in grief.

Isaiah raises the alarm as Babylon is threatened by **Cyrus**.

The images of a **donkey and a camel** appear to Henry to represent **the Medes and Persians**.

Babylon's idols would be unable to protect the city; instead, they would themselves be broken.

Genuine believers are like **grain on God's threshing floor**.

Hypocrites resemble the chaff and straw mixed among the wheat until the harvest separates them.

God's grain should expect to be **threshed by suffering and persecution**.

Israel endured such afflictions, yet even in them **the Lord still called the people his own**.

Whatever happens to the church in the past, present or future, look to God.

He has the power to do anything for his church and the grace to do **everything that serves her true good**.

## The watchman's message to Edom (verses 11 and 12)

God's prophets and ministers are like watchmen.

In peaceful times they guard the city to see that all is well.

In times of war they look for approaching danger and warn the people.

After long spiritual sleep, it is time to **wake up and work**.

Our journey is long and our duties many.

The question about the night asks whether dawn and relief are approaching.

Yet many people ask God's watchmen questions only out of curiosity, demanding difficult predictions or obscure prophecies explained.

They fail to inquire about **their own souls, salvation and daily duty**.

The prophet's reply points to **morning followed by night**.

Periods of light, health, youth or prosperity may be followed by illness, old age and adversity.

Use the present morning well to prepare for the night to come.

The call is urgent: **Inquire. Return. Come.**

There is no time for idle delay.

Those who return to God discover how much good work remains and how little time they have to accomplish it.

## The danger awaiting Arabia (verses 13–17)

The peoples of Arabia lived in tents and kept flocks and herds.

An invading army would threaten them and make them vulnerable.

None of us knows what severe distress may arrive before death.

People who now eat abundantly may later lack even necessary bread.

Neither the ability of expert archers nor the courage of strong soldiers can protect a nation from God's judgments.

What a poor kind of glory can vanish so quickly!

**The Lord has spoken, and none of his words will fail**.

The Strength of Israel cannot lie.

Those who possess riches and glory beyond an invader's reach are truly blessed.

Every other form of prosperity is passing away.
