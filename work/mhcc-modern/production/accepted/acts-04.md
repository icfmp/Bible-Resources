## Peter and John are imprisoned (verses 1–4)

The apostles proclaimed **the resurrection from the dead through Jesus Christ**.

That resurrection opens the whole happiness of the future life, and it can be obtained **through Christ alone**.

Those who are saddened by the glory of Jesus' kingdom are in a miserable position, for his kingdom's glory will last forever.

Harmless and useful servants of Christ have often suffered persecution for faithful service and loving work, while wicked people escaped consequences.

Even today people may face reproach for **reading Scripture, praying with others or discussing faith**.

But if we obey Christ's commands, he will sustain us.

## The apostles testify boldly before the rulers (verses 5–14)

Filled with the Holy Spirit, Peter declared that the miracle had taken place by **the name and power of Jesus of Nazareth**.

Jesus is the Messiah whom these leaders crucified.

The healing confirmed the apostles' testimony that Christ had risen and was indeed the promised Savior.

The rulers had to face a choice: they must be **saved by the Jesus they had crucified** or perish in their sins.

The name of Jesus is offered to every nation and generation as the **only means of salvation from divine judgment**.

When greed, pride or corrupt desires rule the heart, people close their minds to light and treat those devoted to Christ crucified as ignorant.

Christ's disciples should live in such a way that those who meet them recognize **they have been with Jesus**.

His presence makes them **holy, heavenly-minded, spiritual and joyful**, lifting their affections above this world.

## Peter and John refuse to remain silent (verses 15–22)

The authorities wanted to prevent Christ's teaching from spreading.

But they could not prove the message false or dangerous.

They were unwilling to admit that their real objection was the way it exposed their **hypocrisy, injustice and oppressive conduct**.

People who properly value Christ's promises do not give undue weight to the world's threats.

Peter and John cared deeply for people in danger of eternal ruin and knew that **only Jesus could save them**.

They therefore continued faithfully warning others and explaining the way of salvation.

No one can enjoy inward peace or walk uprightly while directing conduct by changing social opinions rather than **the stable standard of truth**.

Beware of trying to serve both **God and the world**.

Such divided allegiance eventually makes people unfaithful to both.

## The believers pray for courage (verses 23–31)

Christ's servants benefit greatly from fellowship with others who share their faith.

The believers prayed to the God who created everything and governs every event. The Scriptures would be fulfilled.

Jesus had been anointed as Savior and was therefore appointed to become **the sacrifice making atonement for sin**.

Nevertheless, divine Providence's ability to bring good from wickedness **does not make human sin less evil**.

In a time of threatening persecution, the believers did not pray mainly that all difficulty would disappear.

Instead they asked for **steadfastness and courage to continue their work**.

They did not say, “Lord, allow us to abandon the task now that it is dangerous.”

They asked for grace to carry out their duty without fear of people.

Anyone who seeks God's help for faithful service may trust him for strength to continue.

God answered with a visible sign.

The place shook **so that their faith would remain unshaken**.

The believers received a greater filling of the Holy Spirit than before, enabling them not only to feel encouraged but to **speak God's word boldly**.

When the Lord strengthens us through his Spirit, we know we need not be ashamed (**Isaiah reference in the source is abbreviated as “Isa 17,” to be checked against the original-native edition**).

## The believers care for one another (verses 32–37)

The disciples' love fulfilled Christ's last commandment and his prayer for their unity.

When God's Spirit is poured out upon believers, the same love grows.

The apostles preached **Christ's resurrection**, a historical event which, properly explained, summarizes believers' privileges, duties and consolation.

The converts' words and deeds showed clear evidence of divine grace.

They were no longer attached to the world.

They did **not confiscate other people's property**.

Rather, they held their own possessions lightly, treating them as available to serve their brothers and sisters.

They had surrendered earthly security in their affections for Christ and expected persecution might soon strip them of their goods.

That is why they were **of one heart and soul** and why no one among them lacked what was needed.

The proceeds of gifts were entrusted to the apostles.

**Public charity should be distributed carefully to people with genuine need**, particularly those unable to support themselves or reduced to poverty because they did what was right or remained faithful under persecution.

**Barnabas** was a notable example of this generosity.

Preparing for gospel service, he disentangled himself from some of this life's business.

When love and generosity are practiced appropriately for the circumstances, they make the church's testimony especially persuasive.
