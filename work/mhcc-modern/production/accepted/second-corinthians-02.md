# 2 Corinthians 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Reasons for the apostle not coming to Corinth. | 1–4 |
| Directions about restoring the repentant offender. | 5–11 |
| An account of his labours and success in spreading the gospel of Christ. | 12–17 |

## Paul explains his reluctance to bring sorrow (verses 1–4)

Paul wanted his next visit to Corinth to be a joyful meeting rather than an occasion for painful confrontation.

He wrote his earlier letter trusting that the Corinthians would respond in ways that were **good for them** and comforting to him.

He hoped they would gladly remove the causes of distress between them.

We should **never take pleasure in causing another person pain**, even when our responsibilities require us to give a warning or correction that hurts.

Necessary discipline should be undertaken reluctantly, from love and for the other person's good.

## Forgive and restore the repentant offender (verses 5–11)

Paul called the Corinthians to restore to fellowship the person who had done wrong.

That person had recognized the offense and had been deeply distressed by the correction already received.

Even **grief over sin** must not be allowed to make people unable to perform their remaining duties or drive them into hopeless despair.

Satan can take advantage of excessive severity by persuading a penitent person to think harshly of God and of religion, until that person loses hope altogether.

The enemy can also use the church's refusal to forgive as a weapon against **Christ's congregations and ministers**.

When Christians appear unforgiving, their public witness suffers, divisions arise and the gospel's ministry is hindered.

Therefore correction requires wisdom.

Church leaders must avoid **two opposite errors**: tolerating wrongdoing as though it were harmless, and treating repentant sinners with excessive harshness.

Satan has many schemes and knows how to use even our mistakes to cause harm.

## The gospel triumphs through Christ (verses 12–17)

Every victory enjoyed by a Christian comes **through Jesus Christ**, who deserves all the honor and praise.

When the gospel spreads and bears fruit, believers have good reason to rejoice.

In ancient victory processions, sweet-smelling perfumes were spread around the participants.

Similarly, the name and salvation of Jesus are like fragrant ointment poured out wherever his gospel is proclaimed.

But people respond differently to this fragrance.

For some, the gospel becomes **an aroma of death leading to death**. They reject it and are ruined by that refusal.

For others it becomes **an aroma of life leading to life**. It awakens people who were spiritually dead in sins, continues strengthening their spiritual life and finally brings them to everlasting life.

Paul felt the dreadful seriousness of this task.

We should share that concern.

The ministry is far greater than our personal abilities. **We have no sufficient strength in ourselves; our sufficiency comes from God**.

Everything we do in religion must be done **sincerely and consciously before God**.

What is done for show or through dishonest motives does not come from him and cannot be acceptable to him.

May we watch our motives and seek the inward witness of conscience, instructed by the Holy Spirit, so that we speak **in Christ and of Christ with genuine sincerity**.
