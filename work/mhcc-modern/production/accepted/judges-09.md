# Judges 9 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Abimelech murders his brothers and becomes king (verses 1–6)

The people of Shechem chose Abimelech as king without consulting God.

They did not ask whether Israel should have a king at all, much less whom God would appoint.

If parents could foresee everything their children would do and suffer, their present joy would often turn to sorrow.

We should be thankful that the future is hidden from us.

Above all, we should watch carefully against sin.

Our wrongdoing may damage our families long after we ourselves are dead and buried.

## Jotham rebukes Shechem through a parable (verses 7–21)

The trees had no need to choose a king.

They were already all trees planted by the Lord.

Likewise, Israel had no need to appoint a king, because the Lord himself was the nation's King.

Trees that bear fruit and contribute to the good of others deserve more honor from wise people than trees that merely look impressive.

The fruitful trees gave much the same reason for refusing promotion.

Ruling brings a great deal of labor and anxiety.

Those entrusted with public power must give up private advantages and interests for the good of others.

A position of honor can also threaten a person's fruitfulness.

That is why people whose main desire is to do good may be reluctant to become too important.

Jotham compared Abimelech to a **bramble or thornbush**—a worthless plant destined to be burned.

That is what Abimelech proved to be.

## Shechem conspires against Abimelech (verses 22–29)

Abimelech occupied the throne Gideon had declined.

But how long did his glory last?

After only **three years**, the bramble was withering and beginning to burn.

The prosperity of the wicked is brief and uncertain.

The Shechemites suffered from Abimelech himself.

They had wrongly placed him on the throne, and they were the first to feel the weight of his rule.

## Abimelech destroys Shechem (verses 30–49)

Abimelech meant to punish the Shechemites because they no longer respected him.

But God was punishing them for having helped Abimelech murder Gideon's sons.

When God uses human beings as instruments of his judgment, **he may intend something very different from what those people themselves intend**.

What the Shechemites expected would bring them safety became a trap.

Anyone who seeks shelter in idols will discover that they are a false refuge.

## Abimelech's death (verses 50–57)

The people of Shechem were destroyed by Abimelech.

Now he, who had led them into wickedness, was called to account.

Evil pursues sinners and can overtake them even when they feel secure and triumphant.

Wickedness may prosper temporarily, but it will not prosper forever.

Much of human history, if told truthfully, would resemble this chapter.

Many events celebrated as great achievements are actually struggles for power.

Such scenes reveal the Bible's teaching about the deceitful wickedness of the human heart, the force of sinful desires and Satan's influence.

Lord, you have given us your word of truth and righteousness.

Pour upon us your Spirit of **purity, peace and love**, and write your holy law upon our hearts.
