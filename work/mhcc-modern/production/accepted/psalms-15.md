# Psalm 15 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The way to heaven, if we would be happy, we must be holy. We are encouraged to walk in that way.**

## The character of a citizen of Zion

This Psalm asks who belongs among the citizens of Zion.

The glorified saints are happy because they dwell on God's holy mountain.

They are at home there and will remain there forever.

We should examine whether we have a place among them.

The Psalm gives a clear and practical answer.

Scripture faithfully directs anyone sincerely wanting to know the path of duty, and **conscience** helps apply that direction.

A citizen of Zion is **sincere in faith**.

Such a person is genuinely what they claim to be and seeks to obey all God's will.

They deal justly with God and other people, speaking **the truth from the heart**.

They reject deceit, fraud and dishonest gain.

No bargain obtained through lies can truly be a good or profitable one.

Anyone who cheats a neighbor will eventually discover that the greatest injury fell upon the cheater.

The faithful person is careful not to harm anyone.

Such people do not slander others or turn their faults into daily entertainment.

They interpret others' conduct charitably.

When a malicious story is told, they try to correct it.

If they cannot, **they do not pass it on**.

They evaluate people according to character and true godliness rather than wealth or public reputation.

They do not despise a person's faith because that person is poor or occupies a humble position.

They recognize that **sincere godliness confers more honor than wealth or a famous name**.

They seek fellowship with godly people, appreciate their prayers and gladly show them kindness and respect.

By these marks we can examine ourselves.

A good and wise person may make a promise that later proves costly.

But a promise must still be kept even if this requires personal or family sacrifice.

It is better to bear loss than to wrong a neighbor.

God's people refuse to gain wealth through **extortion or bribery**.

No hope of personal advantage should persuade them to injure a righteous cause.

Every genuinely living member of Christ's church, like the church as a whole, is **built upon a Rock**.

The person who lives this way will not ultimately be shaken.

God's grace will remain sufficient.

But such attitudes and conduct can arise together only through **repentance from sin, faith in the Savior and love for him**.

Let us examine and test ourselves accordingly.
