# Joshua 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Israelites come to Jordan. | 1–6 |

| The Israelites pass through Jordan on dry land. | 14–17 |

## Israel approaches the Jordan (verses 1–6)

The Israelites approached the Jordan in faith.

They had been told they would cross and therefore came as far as they could.

When we know our duty, we should proceed as far as possible and trust the Lord to open the way.

Joshua rose early.

Scripture makes special mention of his early rising here and on other occasions.

He valued doing God's work more than personal ease.

People who accomplish important things must not be ruled by a love of sleep.

Anyone occupying public office should attend faithfully to the duties of that position.

The people were to follow the ark.

Likewise, we must follow God's word and the Holy Spirit's guidance in everything.

We should follow ministers only as far as **they follow Christ**.

The way across the Jordan was unlike anything Israel had encountered before.

Our lives also bring roads we have never walked and challenges we have never faced.

Yet in the path of duty we can go forward with courage and joy.

Poverty, suffering, exhausting work, persecution, public shame and even death may lie along that road.

But Christ, the Author and Perfecter of our faith, has already traveled it.

By faith we can see his footsteps along every dangerous stretch of the journey.

He invites us to follow him toward the glory where he now is.

Israel had to consecrate itself.

If we wish to experience God's love and power, we must put away sin and avoid grieving the Holy Spirit.

## The Lord strengthens Joshua and Israel (verses 7–13)

God announced that the Jordan's waters would be cut off.

This would repeat the wonder of the Red Sea, though in different circumstances.

The Lord has just as much power to **finish** the salvation of his people as he has to **begin** it.

God's **word** was with Joshua as truly as it had been with Moses.

His acts of favor toward his people should strengthen our faith and hope.

The Lord's work is perfect.

He will preserve those who belong to him.

The Jordan's overflowing water could not keep Israel out of Canaan.

And the power of Canaan's inhabitants could not drive Israel out again while God stood with the people.

## Israel crosses the river on dry ground (verses 14–17)

The Jordan had overflowed all its banks.

This made the miracle of crossing even more remarkable and displayed God's kindness.

However strong the opposition to God's saving work may become, he can overcome it.

After the long and weary wilderness journey, entering Canaan through the Jordan pictured a believer's passage through death into heaven.

Jesus, pictured by the ark, has gone before his people.

He crossed the river when it was overflowing.

Let us treasure our experiences of his faithful, tender care.

Their memory will strengthen our faith and hope in our final struggle.
