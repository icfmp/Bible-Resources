# Deuteronomy 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Edomites to be spared. | 1–7 |
| The Moabites and Ammonites to be spared. | 8–23 |
| The Amorites to be destroyed. | 24–37 |

## Israel must not attack Edom (verses 1–7)

Moses gives only a brief account of Israel's many years in the wilderness.

God had corrected the people for complaining and unbelief.

But he was also preparing them for Canaan—humbling them over sin, teaching them to fight against sinful desires, follow him and find comfort in him.

God's people may wait a long time for deliverance and for their circumstances to improve.

Yet the promised time will eventually come.

Before God brought Israel into conflict with its enemies in Canaan, he taught the people to **forgive their enemies in Edom**.

They were not to use God's covenant or his guidance as an excuse to seize everything they could obtain.

**Property rights are not founded simply on possessing God's grace.**

Israel would have a suitable place to live, but that did not mean the people could claim the whole earth for themselves.

**Religion must never become a disguise for injustice.**

Israel did not need to depend upon Edom's generosity; the people had the all-sufficient God to trust.

They were to use what they already possessed wisely and gratefully.

Since God had supplied their needs through Providence, they were never to use crooked methods to obtain more.

These principles also apply to individual believers.

## Israel must spare Moab and Ammon (verses 8–23)

The history explains the origins of Moab, Edom and Ammon.

It also reaches back to an even older population movement: the Caphtorites displaced the Avvim from their land.

Such changes show how uncertain earthly property and social position are.

Families lose their estates while other families increase.

Earthly possessions have never remained fixed forever.

These histories were meant to encourage Israel.

If God had given lands to Moab and Ammon through his providence, how much more surely would he fulfill his own promise to Israel!

But the Lord commanded Israel not to fight Moab or Ammon.

We must not wrong others merely because they are wicked.

God gives earthly benefits even to people who do not serve him.

These are not his best blessings.

He has far better gifts in store for his own children.

## God gives Israel the Amorites' land (verses 24–37)

God tested Israel by forbidding it to seize the wealthy lands of Moab and Ammon.

Then he gave the people the land of the Amorites.

When we refrain from what God forbids, obedience will not ultimately harm us.

The earth and everything in it belong to the Lord, who distributes its possessions as he wills.

But unless someone receives a clear divine command, that person cannot claim God's authority as an excuse for taking another person's land.

Although God promised the Amorites' territory to Israel, the people still had to fight to take possession of it.

We must exert ourselves to obtain what the Lord gives.

What a different world Israel entered as it approached Canaan!

Still greater will be the change experienced by holy souls when they leave this world's wilderness for the better, heavenly country—the city that has firm foundations.

As we remember Israel's history, let us also remember the years we wasted through our sins.

Happy are those whom Jesus has delivered from the coming judgment and who have received his Spirit as the pledge of their inheritance.

That heavenly inheritance cannot be altered by the rise and fall of kingdoms or by changes in earthly possessions.
