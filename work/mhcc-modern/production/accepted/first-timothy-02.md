# 1 Timothy 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Prayer to be made for all persons, since the grace of the gospel makes no difference of ranks or stations. | 1–7 |
| How men and women ought to behave, both in their religious and common life. | 8–15 |

## Pray for everyone through Christ the one Mediator (verses 1–7)

Disciples of Jesus should be **people who pray**.

Christians should pray without excluding anyone on account of **nation, religious group, social rank or party**.

Our Christian responsibilities can be summed up in two words:

**Godliness** means worshiping God rightly.

**Honesty** means behaving well toward **all people**.

These obligations belong together.

Someone who refuses to give God what is due to him cannot be truly honest.

And someone dishonest toward neighbors cannot be truly godly.

We should abundantly practice what **God our Savior** regards as good and acceptable.

There is **one Mediator**, Jesus Christ, who **gave himself as a ransom for all**.

This saving way has been appointed for **Jews and Gentiles from every nation**.

Everyone willing to come by this way may approach **God's mercy seat** to seek forgiveness and reconciliation.

Sin created hostility between humanity and God.

**Jesus Christ, our Mediator**, establishes peace.

God appointed that his ransom and its blessings would become known at the proper time.

Before Christ came, the Old Testament spoke about **his suffering and the glory that would follow**, still awaiting their full revelation.

Those who are saved must come to **knowledge of the truth**, because God has appointed truth as the way of salvation.

We cannot be ruled by truth **we do not know**.

## Prayer, modesty and the responsibilities of men and women (verses 8–15)

Under the gospel, prayer is not limited to a particular sacred building.

Believers should pray **privately, with families, at meals, while traveling and in gathered worship**, whether the meeting is large or small.

They must pray **in love**, without anger, hatred or hostility toward anyone.

They should pray **with faith**, rather than skepticism or contentious dispute.

Henry calls Christian women to **modesty in clothing**, avoiding pride in splendid, expensive or conspicuous appearance.

He says **good works are the best adornment**, precious in God's sight.

Cleanliness and a modest appearance should matter more than impressive fashion.

It would be good if professing Christians were free from vanity concerning clothes.

Instead of spending so much time and money adorning themselves and their children, they should give more to **the sick and distressed**.

Dress that contradicts a person's Christian profession or situation is not a harmless matter.

These are **divine commands**, not merely customs.

Henry follows Paul's restriction on women acting as **public teachers in church**, understanding that role as exercising authority.

He emphasizes, however, that believing women **may and should teach their children the principles of true religion at home**.

Women must also learn everything necessary for salvation.

Paul refers to the order of creation and to Eve's earlier participation in transgression.

But Henry also draws a word of comfort from the promise concerning **childbearing**.

He understands salvation **“in” or “with” childbearing** as coming **through the Messiah born of a woman**, with a word of comfort **for those who continue in sobriety**.

The particular suffering associated with childbearing should also move men to exercise authority **with tenderness, gentleness and loving affection**.
