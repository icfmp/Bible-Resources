# Psalm 125 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The security of the righteous. | 1–3 |
| Prayer for them, The ruin of the wicked. | 4, 5 |

## A security stronger than mountains (verses 1–3)

Minds firmly resting upon God find true stability.

They are like **Mount Zion**, strong through Providence and even stronger through God's promise.

Those who trust him cannot finally be driven away from their confidence.

His grace within them is a pledge of their eternal life in glory.

Because they entrust themselves to God, they are safe from their enemies in the ultimate sense.

Even mountains eventually crumble and rocks are moved.

But **God's covenant cannot be broken**, and his care for his people cannot cease.

Their suffering will not exceed what the Lord enables them to bear.

Wicked people may strike believers and affect their bodies, property, freedoms, families and reputations.

**But they cannot destroy the believer's soul**.

Their oppression may reach the righteous person's earthly circumstances, but it will not remain there forever.

God will work all these things together for their good.

Wicked people become **a rod of correction, not a sword of destruction**.

Even that rod will eventually be removed, so that believers will not be left thinking God has abandoned them.

## Prayer for the upright and warning to the crooked (verses 4 and 5)

God's promises should encourage our prayers.

**The path of holiness is straight**.

It does not twist and turn in dishonest compromises.

But the ways of sinners are crooked.

They change their purposes and use deceit to get what they want.

Disappointment and misery will eventually come upon them.

Those who remain on God's path may suffer along the way, but **their final destination is peace**.

Their Savior's intercession secures God's sustaining power and preserving grace for them.

Lord, count us among them **now and for eternity**.
