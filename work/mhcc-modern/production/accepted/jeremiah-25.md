# Jeremiah 25 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews rebuked for not obeying calls to repentance. | 1–7 |
| Their captivity during seventy years is expressly foretold. | 8–14 |
| Desolations upon the nations shown by the emblem of a cup of wrath. | 15–29 |
| The judgments again declared. | 30–38 |

## The people reject repeated calls to repent (verses 1–7)

God calls sinners everywhere to abandon their evil ways, return to his worship and service, **trust in Christ and share in his salvation**.

The Lord takes account of how long we have possessed **the means of grace**.

The longer we enjoy those opportunities without making good use of them, **the heavier our responsibility becomes**.

The prophets' practice of **rising early** to call the people reveals God's earnest desire that sinners **turn from their ways and live**.

A nation cannot truly be rescued while its inhabitants refuse **personal reform**.

Every person must turn from **their own evil way**.

Yet the people would not listen.

They refused the only right way to turn away God's anger.

## Seventy years of captivity and the judgment of Babylon (verses 8–14)

God announced exactly how long **the Jewish exile would last: seventy years**.

That definite period confirmed the prophecy and also **comforted believers**, encouraging them to pray and trust.

Babylon's ruin was also foretold.

When the instrument God used to correct his people had completed its work, **the rod itself would be thrown into the fire**.

When the appointed time came to favor Zion, Babylon would be judged for its own sins, just as other nations had been judged.

**Every warning in Scripture will be fulfilled**.

## The nations drink the cup of judgment (verses 15–29)

Scripture often represents life's blessings and calamities as **cups**.

The cup in this chapter pictures the coming destruction of many nations.

**Nebuchadnezzar** had just begun his reign and campaigns and would be the instrument.

But the destroying sword would ultimately act **under God's authority**.

The disorder the sword would bring upon these kingdoms is compared to the effects of **excessive drinking**.

This comparison should increase our hatred of drunkenness.

It deprives people of reason, can make them behave like mad people, and steals **the precious gift of health**.

Drunkenness is also a sin that **punishes itself**.

The image should also teach us to fear war.

War can quickly fill an entire country with confusion.

The nations would refuse to accept Jeremiah's warning.

But he was to tell them that **the Lord of Hosts had spoken**.

Resistance to almighty power would be useless.

If God's judgment begins with **people who have professed faith and then turned away**, unrepentant wicked people cannot expect to escape.

## God's controversy with all nations (verses 30–38)

The Lord has righteous grounds of complaint against **every nation and every human being**.

He will judge all the wicked.

Who could avoid trembling when he speaks in displeasure?

The appointed time had arrived—the time fixed in **God's own counsel** for bringing desolation upon the nations.

Even the gentle and delicate would share the common distress.

People who had lived peaceably and done nothing to provoke war would still suffer its effects.

But blessed be God: there is **a peaceful dwelling above** for everyone who belongs to the Lord's peace.

He will **preserve his church and every believer through all changes**, for **nothing can separate them from his love**.
