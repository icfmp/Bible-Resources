# Philippians 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortations to a kind, humble spirit and behaviour. | 1–4 |
| The example of Christ. | 5–11 |
| Diligence in the affairs of salvation, and to be examples to the world. | 12–18 |
| The apostle's purpose of visiting Philippi. | 19–30 |

## Practice Christlike humility and kindness (verses 1–4)

Paul continued calling Christians to **unity of mind and a humble attitude**, following the example of Jesus.

**Kindness** is the law of Christ's kingdom, the lesson of his school and the identifying clothing of his family.

The Lord's compassion toward us should move us to show compassion to **one another**.

Seeing believers live in loving agreement brings great joy to faithful ministers.

Christ humbled himself for our sake.

How inappropriate for **pride** to govern those who belong to him!

We should examine **our own faults strictly**, identifying our weaknesses quickly.

Toward others, however, we should be ready to make **generous allowances**.

Caring about other people does not mean becoming busybodies who interfere in every private matter.

Neither outward peace nor inward peace can endure without **humility of heart**.

## Jesus Christ humbled himself and was exalted (verses 5–11)

**Jesus Christ's example** is the standard for our attitudes and behavior.

If we would receive the blessings of his death, we should seek to resemble him in life.

Henry explicitly distinguishes **Christ's two natures**.

In his **divine nature**, Jesus is the eternal, only-begotten Son who shares God's nature (**John 1:1**).

Equality with God was his by right, and he could rightly receive divine worship.

In his **human nature**, he became like us **in every respect except sin**.

Of his own free will he stooped from the glory he shared with the Father **before the world existed**.

The passage describes two states of Christ: **humiliation and exaltation**.

He did not merely assume a human appearance.

He entered **a lowly human condition**, without worldly magnificence.

His life was marked by poverty and suffering.

His deepest humiliation was dying **on a cross**, the death assigned to criminals and slaves, bearing public contempt and hatred.

His exaltation involved his **human nature united to his divine nature**.

At Jesus' name, everyone should bow—not because of the mere sound of a word, but because of **the authority of Jesus himself**.

Confessing **Jesus Christ as Lord** glorifies God the Father.

The Father wills that the Son should receive the honor rightly given to the Father (**John 5:23**).

What greater reason could there be for **self-denying love**?

Do we love and obey the Son of God in this way?

## Work diligently because God is at work within you (verses 12–18)

Christians must diligently use **the appointed means that lead toward salvation**, persevering until the end.

We should be careful not to fall short despite our many privileges.

**Work out your salvation, because God works within you**.

God's action is not an excuse for laziness.

It encourages our greatest effort because we know our work, carried out by his grace, will not be wasted.

Yet in everything we must **depend upon God's grace**.

His gracious work within us awakens and strengthens our effort.

His favor is the reason he does such good within our souls.

Do your duties **without grumbling or endless quarrelling**.

Rather than constantly complaining about the work assigned, concentrate on performing it well.

Avoid giving others good reason to stumble.

God's children should be distinguishable from the world's prevailing conduct.

The more corrupt others become, the more careful we should be to remain **blameless and harmless**.

The teaching and example of consistent believers can **light the way for others**, like a lighthouse warning sailors away from rocks and guiding them safely into a harbor.

Let us shine in that way.

The gospel is **the word of life**, revealing eternal life through Jesus Christ.

Paul's language of **running** pictures energetic, determined effort.

His language of **laboring** pictures steadfast, careful work.

God wills that believers should **rejoice often**.

People blessed with faithful ministers have good reason to rejoice with them.

## Timothy and Epaphroditus serve sincerely (verses 19–30)

We are in a good spiritual condition when fulfilling duty becomes **natural to us**—meaning sincere, willing and free from pretense.

We commonly place our own reputation, comfort and safety above **truth, holiness and duty**.

**Timothy** did not.

Paul hoped for freedom from imprisonment not primarily to enjoy himself but **to do good for others**.

**Epaphroditus** wanted to return to Philippi to comfort the believers who had been distressed over his illness.

Henry thinks his illness **seems to have resulted from his service to God**.

Paul therefore urged the Philippians to hold him in even greater affection.

Mercies restored after we nearly lost them bring a special joy.

That is a reason to value recovered health, friendship or service more deeply.

And blessings received **in answer to prayer** should produce particularly sincere gratitude and rejoicing.
