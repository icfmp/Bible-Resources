## God reduces Gideon's army (verses 1–8)

God arranged matters so that all the praise for the coming victory would belong to him alone.

He chose to use just **three hundred men**.

Trusting God does not excuse us from active service and careful judgment in our lawful undertakings.

But when the Lord sees people tempted to forget him, to shrink from danger through unbelief or to boast proudly of their abilities, he may set them aside and accomplish his work through others.

Many people find excuses for abandoning God's cause or avoiding the cost of discipleship.

A company of believers may become smaller when such people depart, yet grow purer and receive greater blessing from the Lord.

He chooses servants who are not merely favorable toward his work but **eager and zealous** for it.

Those who stayed did not resent the liberty granted to those sent home.

When doing what God requires of us, we should not be governed by how willing or unwilling other people seem, or by what they choose to do.

The question is what God has asked **us** to do.

It is rare to accept without resentment that others may have greater gifts, blessings or freedom.

Only special grace enables us to heed God's command rather than keep comparing our responsibilities with those of others.

## God encourages Gideon's faith (verses 9–15)

The dream Gideon overheard seemed almost meaningless by itself.

But its interpretation showed plainly that God had arranged it.

Gideon's name had already filled the Midianites with fear.

He took the dream as a firm promise of success.

Without delay, he worshiped and praised God, then returned to his three hundred men with confidence.

We can speak to and worship God wherever we are.

When something strengthens our faith, he should receive the praise.

We should also recognize his providence in small events that seem accidental.

## The Midianites defeat themselves (verses 16–22)

The unusual defeat of Midian can picture the destruction of Satan's kingdom through the preaching of the everlasting gospel.

The **trumpet** is sounded and the **light** is displayed from fragile clay vessels—like gospel ministers (2 Corinthians 4:6–7).

God chooses what seems foolish in the world's eyes to humble the wise.

A simple **barley loaf** could overturn the tents of Midian, so that all would see that the surpassing power belonged to God.

The gospel is a sword not held in a hand but proclaimed from a mouth.

It is the sword of the Lord and Gideon, of God and Jesus Christ—of the One on the throne and the Lamb.

God often allows wicked people to turn against and destroy one another, driven by delusion and uncontrolled passion.

It is a shame when people who belong to the church behave in that same way.

## Ephraim captures Oreb and Zeeb (verses 23–25)

The people of Ephraim captured and killed two leading Midianite commanders.

Their cooperation teaches a valuable lesson.

When work remains to be done, we should be ready to help others finish it.

When another person begins something good and useful, we should welcome the chance to work together to complete it instead of hindering one another.
