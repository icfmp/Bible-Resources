# Isaiah 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Son that should be born, and his kingdom. | 1–7 |
| The judgments to come upon Israel, and on the enemies of the kingdom of Christ. | 8–21 |

## The promised Son and his everlasting kingdom (verses 1–7)

The Syrians and Assyrians had first ravaged the regions described here.

Yet those same regions were among the first to receive **Christ's preaching**.

Those without the gospel walk in darkness and extreme danger.

When the gospel reaches a place—or even one person's heart—**light comes**.

Let us pray earnestly that this light will shine in us and make us wise for salvation.

The gospel brings joy.

But joy may be preceded by **hard work**, as harvest follows the farmer's labor, and by **conflict**, as victory and spoils follow a soldier's struggle.

Israel had been delivered from various oppressors. Those deliverances pictured the greater release of believers from **Satan's yoke**.

The Holy Spirit cleanses souls from sin's power and pollution, like a purifying fire.

All these blessings come through **the Messiah, Emmanuel**.

The prophecy says that **a Child is born** as though it were already accomplished, because God's promise was certain.

Before Christ became human, the church already benefited from the work he undertook.

Those waiting for Israel's consolation treasured this promise of his person and kingdom.

The Child would be born **for sinners and believers throughout every age**.

He is rightly called **Wonderful**, being both God and man.

His love astonishes angels and glorified saints.

He is **Counselor**: he has known God's purposes from eternity and gives wise guidance for our good. No one teaches as he does.

He is **Mighty God**, for only divine power could accomplish the Mediator's saving work.

**He is God, one with the Father.**

He is **Prince of Peace**: he reconciles us with God, grants peace of heart and conscience, and will finally establish a kingdom in which people learn war no more.

He bears the responsibility for his government, and its peace and the happiness of its citizens will never end.

Henry sees the close agreement between this prophecy and New Testament teaching as showing that the Jewish prophets and Christian teachers proclaimed the same Messiah.

**No ordinary earthly king or kingdom can fulfill this description.**

Lord, grant your people the grace to know Christ through every glorious title and gracious attribute. Increase your grace in every redeemed heart.

## Israel's refusal to turn from sin (verses 8–21)

People are approaching ruin when God's humbling judgments fail to humble their hearts.

His purpose in striking them is to **turn them back to himself**.

When lighter judgments do not bring repentance, more severe ones may follow.

Israel's leaders misled the people.

We should be wary of people who flatter us **while we are doing wrong**.

Wickedness had spread everywhere.

Trouble would surround the people, and they would see no way of escape.

When a nation's conduct displeases God, even former friends may turn into enemies.

The supports in which the people trusted would be taken away.

The rulers were the **head**, while the false prophets were likened to **the tail and rushes**, displaying their worthlessness.

During civil conflict, people would attack even their own relatives.

Because they did not return to the One who struck them, the judgment continued.

When God judges, his purpose will prevail.

**The proudest sinner will either bend or break.**
