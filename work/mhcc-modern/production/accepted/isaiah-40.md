# Isaiah 40 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

## Comfort: the gospel and the coming of Christ (verses 1–11)

Every human life involves struggle, but the Christian life is especially a warfare.

Yet this struggle will not last forever.

When sin is pardoned, God lovingly removes the deepest cause of our trouble.

Through **Christ's death and great atonement**, God's mercy is displayed in a way that glorifies his justice.

The satisfaction Christ made through his death has **infinite value**.

A repentant sinner receives grace more than sufficient for the guilt once carried.

Isaiah's words do refer in part to **the Jews' return from Babylon**.

But that event is small compared with the fulfillment identified in the New Testament: **John the Baptist announcing the arrival of Jesus Christ**.

When eastern kings traveled through deserts, people cleared a passage for them and removed obstacles.

So let the Lord prepare **our hearts** through his word and Spirit.

May proud thoughts be humbled, good desires planted, crooked dispositions straightened, harsh tempers softened and every obstacle to obedience removed.

Everything that belongs to fallen humanity, including its apparent magnificence, withers like **grass and flowers**.

What good will wealth, status and possessions do for a dying sinner who remains condemned?

**God's word accomplishes what human strength cannot.**

The good news of Christ's coming is to be announced throughout the world.

Satan is strong and armed, but **Jesus Christ is stronger** and will accomplish everything he has resolved.

Christ is also **the good Shepherd**.

He cares tenderly for new converts, weak believers and sorrowful people.

He never demands service or permits suffering without providing grace sufficient to endure it.

May we hear his voice, follow him and prove ourselves to be his sheep.

## The Lord's immeasurable power and kindness (verses 12–17)

Compared with the Creator, every creature is small.

When the Lord made the world through his Spirit, nobody advised him or told him how to act.

All the nations are to him like **the remaining drop in a bucket** beside the ocean, or **a tiny amount of dust on a balance** compared with the earth.

Yet God's love is astonishing.

Though the world is so small in comparison with him, he gave his **only Son to redeem it** (**John 3:16**).

No service rendered by the church can add anything to God's greatness.

Our souls would have been eternally lost had the Father's only Son not given himself for us.

## Idols cannot be compared with God (verses 18–26)

Whatever created thing we esteem, love, fear or trust **more than God** becomes an idol to us, even when we do not make or worship a physical image.

Even people with little to offer may insist on having an idol of their own.

Some spend lavishly to support false gods while begrudging what is devoted to **the true God's service**.

Isaiah appealed to every generation and nation to recognize the Creator's greatness.

People who refuse this knowledge are often **willfully ignorant**.

God commands every creature and everything in creation.

The prophet appeals not only to our senses but also to **our reasoning**.

Consider the stars and the One who created them.

Every member of the heavenly host fulfills his will.

The Lord who governs them is also the One who has **spoken his promises and pledged to keep them**.

## Waiting upon the Lord instead of relying on ourselves (verses 27–31)

God rebuked his people for distrusting him.

They bore the names **Jacob and Israel**, inherited from a man who discovered that God was faithful through every trial.

Those names reminded them that they were a covenant people.

Many needless fears and complaints disappear when we take time to examine their causes.

It is wrong to entertain unfair thoughts about the Lord; it is worse to **turn those thoughts into harmful speech**.

The people already knew enough of God's goodness to silence their distrust.

**Where God has begun a work of grace, he will complete it**.

He helps those who depend humbly upon him and act in the strength he provides.

**As each day comes, so does the strength needed for it.**

By divine grace believers rise above the world's attractions.

They can run cheerfully in the way of God's commandments.

Guard against **unbelief, pride and self-confidence**.

If we proceed in our own power, we will faint and fall.

But when our hearts and hopes are set on heaven, God enables us to rise above difficulty and pursue **the prize of our calling in Jesus Christ**.
