# Ecclesiastes 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Commendations of wisdom. | 1–5 |
| To prepare for sudden evils and death. | 6–8 |
| It shall be well with the righteous, and ill with the wicked. | 9–13 |
| Mysteries of Providence. | 14–17 |

## The excellence of wisdom (verses 1–5)

No person distinguished by wealth, power, honor or worldly skill is as excellent, useful or truly happy as the person who possesses **wisdom**.

Who but the wise can interpret God's words rightly and teach others from his truth and providential dealings?

How senseless it is for weak, dependent creatures to rebel against the Almighty!

Many people judge wrongly and thereby bring misery upon themselves, **both in this life and the life to come**.

## Be ready for change and death (verses 6–8)

In his wisdom God has kept the future hidden from us so that we may always be ready for unexpected change.

We must all die.

No flight or hiding place will save us from death, and no weapon can ultimately defeat it.

Henry illustrates this with estimates of how many people die each day, minute and moment.

These figures express his own historical estimate; they are **not updated demographic statistics**.

How solemn the reality is!

May we become wise, understand our condition and consider our final end.

Only the person who believes in God is prepared to answer death's summons.

Wickedness sometimes enables people to escape **human justice**, but it can never exempt them from death.

## God will finally distinguish the righteous and wicked (verses 9–13)

Solomon observed that people sometimes gain power over others and use it to injure them.

Prosperity may harden sinners in their wickedness.

Those who take long delays of punishment as evidence that judgment will never come deceive themselves.

**Divine judgment may arrive slowly, but it will arrive certainly**.

A godly person's days have substance and a good purpose.

The wicked person's days pass **like a shadow**, empty of lasting worth.

May we see eternal realities as close, certain and supremely important.

## The mysteries of Providence (verses 14–17)

Only **faith in God** can steady the heart in a world where good people often suffer and wicked people sometimes prosper.

Solomon recommended a kind of joy arising from **holy confidence in God**.

In this world, people cannot do better than soberly and gratefully use the things of life according to their responsibilities and circumstances.

But faithful people have something infinitely better **above the sun**.

Solomon did not teach us to imagine we can explain all God's dealings.

Let us leave the unresolved problems of Providence for the Lord to clarify in his own time.

Meanwhile, we may gratefully enjoy what he provides and patiently endure our trials.

Through all outward changes, **peace of conscience and joy in the Holy Spirit** can remain ours.

They will continue even when bodily strength and earthly comfort fail.
