# Job 34 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elihu accuses Job of charging God with injustice. | 1–9 |
| God cannot be unjust. | 10–15 |
| God's power and providence. | 16–30 |
| Elihu reproves Job. | 31–37 |

## Elihu calls for judgment on Job's words (verses 1–9)

Elihu invited the people present to consider Job's words with him and determine what was right.

An ordinary Christian whose **mind has been enlightened**, whose **heart has been sanctified by God's Spirit**, and who knows Scripture can often judge whether a belief, statement or action agrees with true religion better than someone depending only on personal understanding.

Job had spoken as though he intended to justify himself completely.

Someone who says, **“I have kept my hands clean for nothing,”** offends God's children and speaks in agreement with God's enemies rather than his friends (**Psalm 73:13–15**).

## God cannot act unjustly (verses 10–15)

Elihu had already explained that God did not intend to harm Job through his suffering.

Rather, he intended Job's **spiritual good**.

Now Elihu argued that God did him **no wrong** in permitting the affliction.

If knowing God's good purpose did not satisfy Job, remembering God's justice should have silenced his complaints.

The Almighty never does evil or injustice.

Some faithful service remains unrewarded in this life, and some sins seem to go unpunished.

But the day of judgment is coming, when God will repay everyone according to their works.

A believer cannot be finally condemned, because **the Savior has paid the ransom**.

Even so, our sins deserve far worse than any outward suffering we experience, so no injustice is done when God tests us severely.

## God's rule is righteous and complete (verses 16–30)

Elihu appealed directly to Job.

Could he seriously imagine that God resembles unjust earthly rulers who hate righteousness and become scourges to those they govern?

It is presumptuous to condemn God's ways because we dislike our circumstances.

Elihu reminded Job of God's greatness and sovereignty so that he would submit.

Job had often wanted to bring his case personally before God.

What could he accomplish by doing so?

**Everything God does is right**, and eventually it will be seen to be so.

A soul resting securely in God cannot be made truly miserable by worldly troubles.

But the approval of the whole world cannot comfort a person who remains under God's displeasure.

## Elihu corrects Job's complaints (verses 31–37)

When we rebuke someone for doing wrong, we must also show the person **what would be right**.

Job's friends had demanded that he confess to being a wicked man.

Elihu instead urged Job to acknowledge that he had spoken **rashly and unwisely**.

We should not exaggerate another person's fault when correcting it.

Elihu taught Job to humble himself, accept God's discipline and pray to discover whatever sins needed to be addressed.

A godly person is willing to know even unpleasant truths about personal faults.

Especially in suffering, a believer wants to understand why God is contending with him.

It is not enough to regret sin: **we must turn away from it**.

Children who love their father are willing to speak honestly with him and open their minds to him.

We often want everything concerning us to happen exactly as we planned, but that expectation is unreasonable.

Job should ask whether his complaints revealed sin and folly.

**The Lord is righteous in all his ways and holy in all his works** (**Psalm 145:17**).

A believer may therefore say: *Let my Savior, my wise and loving Lord, choose everything for me. His choice will be the wisest, bringing the greatest glory to him and the most lasting good to me.*
