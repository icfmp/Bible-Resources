# Romans 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The doctrine of justification by faith is shown by the case of Abraham. | 1–12 |
| He received the promise through the righteousness of faith. | 13–22 |
| And we are justified in the same way of believing. | 23–25 |

## Abraham shows justification by faith (verses 1–12)

To answer Jewish objections, Paul first appealed to **Abraham**, the ancestor in whom the Jews took greatest pride.

Abraham had many remarkable qualities.

Yet he had **nothing to boast about before God**.

Like everyone else, he was saved **by grace through faith**.

Without even considering the years before Abraham's call or the times his obedience and faith failed, Scripture plainly states: **“He believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness” (Genesis 15:6)**.

If a person could perform everything demanded by the law, the reward would be owed as a **debt**.

That was clearly not Abraham's situation, because righteousness was counted to him **through faith**.

When Scripture says faith is counted as righteousness, it does not mean that faith justifies us **as a small or large piece of our personal righteousness**.

Rather, faith is the appointed **means of uniting us to the One called “The Lord our Righteousness.”**

Only forgiven people are truly blessed.

Abraham was justified **years before he was circumcised**, as Scripture makes clear.

Consequently, circumcision was **not necessary for his justification**.

Circumcision was a sign of humanity's original corruption and an outward seal.

It confirmed God's promises to Abraham and his descendants, as well as their duty to belong to the Lord.

It also assured Abraham that he **already shared in the righteousness received by faith**.

Thus Abraham became the spiritual father of all believers who follow his example of **obedient faith**.

For Christians, the Holy Spirit's **seal in sanctification**, making us new creations, provides inward evidence of righteousness through faith.

## Abraham believed the promise (verses 13–22)

The promise to Abraham was given **long before the law of Moses**.

It pointed to Christ and to the promise in **Genesis 12:3**: through Abraham, every family of the earth would be blessed.

The law produces wrath by exposing sinners to God's judgment for transgression.

Because God intended to grant people a true claim to the promised blessings, he established that they would be received **through faith**, so that the gift would be entirely by **grace**.

It would then be secure for all people who share Abraham's faith, Jews and Gentiles alike throughout every age.

Calling Gentiles who were not previously God's people, and justifying sinners, was an act of divine power **calling what did not exist as though it existed**.

Abraham's faith reveals what trusting God means.

He believed **God's testimony** and expected God to fulfill his promise, **hoping even when the circumstances seemed hopeless**.

Weak faith continually focuses on difficulties that stand in the way of a promise.

Abraham did not treat God's word as a proposition whose truth depended upon endless debate.

**Unbelief** lies beneath our wavering when we doubt divine promises.

Strong faith overcomes fear and thereby honors God.

God honors faith, and faith gives God particular glory.

But faith is only **the instrument by which we receive Christ's righteousness and redemption**.

The instrument receiving a gift cannot be identical with the gift itself.

Abraham's faith did not justify him through its own merit or value.

It justified because it brought him **into participation in Christ**.

## Believers are justified on the same basis (verses 23–25)

The account of Abraham's justification was recorded for later generations, especially people who would hear the gospel.

Like him, we are not justified because our works are meritorious.

We are justified **through faith in Jesus Christ and his righteousness**.

This truth is the great foundation of Christian comfort.

Christ **earned our justification and salvation through his suffering and death**.

Yet the accomplished power and perfection of his work for us are inseparable from **his resurrection**.

Through death **he paid the debt**.

Through resurrection **he received the acquittal** (Isaiah 53:8).

When he was declared discharged, we who belong to him were **also released from the guilt and penalty of our sins**.

The final verse of this chapter is therefore **a brief summary of the whole gospel**.
