# Ezekiel 16 — Modern English

## Original heading

**A parable showing the first low estate of the Jewish nation, its prosperity, idolatries, and punishment.**

## Jerusalem's history pictured as an unfaithful woman (verses 1–58)

This chapter recounts **God's dealings with the Jewish nation** and the way the people responded to him.

It also describes the punishment that came through the surrounding nations—even through **those in whom the Jews had trusted most**.

The account is told as an extended parable.

An **abandoned infant** is rescued from death, brought up and cared for, joined in marriage and generously provided for.

Later, despite such kindness, she commits **the most shameful acts of unfaithfulness** and is punished for them.

Yet in the end she is received into favor and becomes **ashamed of the conduct she had committed**.

We must understand these expressions with regard to **the customs, language and circumstances of their own time**, rather than judging their tone entirely by modern expectations.

Many phrases that now seem shocking would have sounded different to their original hearers.

The purpose of the imagery was to inspire **abhorrence of idolatry**.

Henry believes this kind of parable was especially well suited to that end.

## God remembers mercy and covenant (verses 59–63)

After a full warning of coming judgment, the Lord remembers **mercy**.

Mercy remains reserved for his people.

These closing verses contain a precious promise.

Part of it was fulfilled when **repentant, reformed Jews returned from Babylon**.

But Henry understands it to reach a **fuller fulfillment in the gospel age**.

God's gracious mercy should melt our hearts and awaken **genuine sorrow over sin**.

He will never leave a sinner to perish who **humbles himself for sin and trusts in divine mercy and grace through Jesus Christ**.

He will preserve that person by his power, **through faith unto salvation**.
