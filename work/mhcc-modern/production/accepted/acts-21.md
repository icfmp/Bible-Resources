# Acts 21 — Modern English

## Paul's journey toward Jerusalem (verses 1–7)

When our affairs go well, we ought to recognize **God's Providence**.

Whenever Paul arrived somewhere, he searched for disciples and found them.

His friends foresaw his troubles. Because they loved him and cared about the church, they mistakenly thought God's honor would be better served if he remained free.

Their earnest efforts to dissuade him made his godly determination even clearer.

Paul teaches us by both instruction and example **to pray continually**.

The disciples' last farewell was made sweeter by prayer.

## Agabus predicts trouble; Paul reaches Jerusalem (verses 8–18)

Paul received clear warning of the suffering ahead, so it would neither surprise nor terrify him.

Scripture gives us the same warning: **through many troubles we must enter God's kingdom**.

His friends' weeping began to weaken his resolution. Yet Christ had told his followers to take up their cross.

Paul was distressed that they so insistently pressed him toward something he could not do without wronging his conscience.

When trouble approaches, we should not merely say, “The Lord's will must be done; there is nothing we can do.”

Rather we should say, **“Let the Lord's will be done.”** His will expresses divine wisdom, and he orders all things according to it.

Once trouble comes, God's accomplished will should quiet our grief.

When trouble approaches, God's promised will should quiet our fears.

Our response ought to be **Amen; let it happen**.

It is an honor to become **an old disciple of Christ**, kept by God's grace in the path of duty, steady in faith, growing in experience into old age.

We should welcome fellowship with such believers, because their years can teach us wisdom.

Many believers in Jerusalem received Paul gladly.

We may imagine that we too would have welcomed him, but unless we welcome **his doctrine**, we would not truly welcome the apostle.

## Paul joins ceremonial observances (verses 19–26)

Paul credited God with all his success, and the Jerusalem believers gave God praise.

God had honored him greatly, yet the other apostles did not envy his fruitful service; they glorified the Lord.

James and the elders asked Paul to accommodate Jewish Christians through some compliance with ceremonial law.

They thought this was prudent.

But Henry judges that **clinging to ceremonial shadows after their substance, Christ, had come was a serious weakness**.

The religion Paul preached did not destroy the law but fulfilled it. He proclaimed **Christ as the law's goal for righteousness**, along with repentance and faith, in which the law retains its proper use.

Even disciples of Christ sometimes gave too little regard to Paul, despite his remarkable character and work, simply because his attachment to Jewish ceremonies differed from theirs.

This illustrates the danger of religious prejudice.

**The apostles were not beyond criticism in every action**.

Henry adds that it would be difficult to defend Paul from the charge of **yielding too much in this matter**.

Trying to win the approval of unyielding zealots and party-minded people is usually futile.

Paul's concession did not succeed: the very action intended to calm opposition inflamed it.

But God overruled both the elders' advice and Paul's compliance for a wiser purpose.

Nothing would satisfy people determined to destroy Christianity.

**Sincere integrity is safer than compliance against one's better judgment.**

This also warns us never to pressure people to act contrary to their conscientious convictions merely to please us.

## The Romans rescue Paul from his accusers (verses 27–40)

Paul was attacked in the temple, where he ought to have been safe.

His opponents falsely accused him of teaching and acting against Mosaic ordinances.

People who intend well and act lawfully often find themselves accused of actions they never contemplated.

Sometimes an attempt to oblige others becomes the very thing used against us.

God may protect his servants through people who have no affection for Christianity but who feel compassion for suffering people or value public peace.

Many are misled by false ideas about good people and faithful ministers.

Yet the Lord intervenes at the right time to protect his servants from wicked and unreasonable people, giving them opportunities to speak for themselves, plead **the Redeemer's cause** and spread his glorious gospel.
