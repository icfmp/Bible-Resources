# Ezekiel 39 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The destruction of Gog. | 1–10 |
| Its extent. | 11–22 |
| Israel again favoured. | 23–29 |

## Gog's army is destroyed (verses 1–10)

The Lord will make even the most careless and hardened sinners acknowledge his holy name, either through **his righteous judgment** or through **the abundance of his mercy and grace**.

Weapons formed against Zion will not succeed.

Henry regards this prophecy as **certain**, although its fulfillment belongs to **the latter days**.

From its language, he concludes that Gog's army **appears to be destroyed by a miracle**.

## The scale of the destruction and the work of cleansing (verses 11–22)

God will destroy a vast number of enemies to defend his people Israel.

Times of extraordinary deliverance should also be **times of reform**.

Everyone should do everything possible to remove what disgraces the land.

Sin is an enemy against which every person should struggle.

Those entrusted with public work, especially the work of cleansing and reforming a country, should be persistent and **finish what they begin**.

Whenever good work is needed, everyone should help it forward.

Receiving extraordinary favor from God should move us to **cleanse ourselves from all evil**.

This requires determined effort, including a careful search into **the hidden corners of sin**.

God's judgments against sin and sinners are a sacrifice to his justice and a feast to the faith and hope of his people.

Sin's consequences can pursue people even after their death.

After all the wealth and honor ambitious, greedy people pursue, **a burial place** may be all that remains to them on earth, while those who die guilty face misery in the world to come.

## Israel receives God's favor again (verses 23–29)

Henry looks forward to God's showing mercy to **the whole house of Israel** through their **conversion to Christianity**.

After Israel bears the shame of having been rejected because of sin, the nations will learn to know, worship and serve the Lord.

Israel too will know him as revealed **in and through Christ**.

Henry expressly says **past events do not fully answer this prediction**.

The outpouring of God's Spirit is a pledge that **his favor will continue**.

He will no longer hide his face from those upon whom he pours out his Spirit.

When we pray that God never cast us from his presence, let us pray just as earnestly that he **never take his Holy Spirit from us**.
