# Job 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Concerning wordly wealth. | 1–11 |
| Wisdom is of inestimable value. | 12–19 |
| Wisdom is the gift of God. | 20–28 |

## Human beings search for earthly riches (verses 1–11)

Job argued that God's Providence is governed by **perfect wisdom**.

To illustrate this, he described the extraordinary knowledge and wealth people can gain.

People can discover hidden caverns and minerals beneath the earth, yet they cannot uncover **God's hidden counsels in heaven**.

Look at miners, those who grow lazy in religion, and learn from their courage and diligence.

They labor intensely for treasures that will perish.

Should their example not shame our indifference and discouragement in seeking **true, eternal riches**?

Wisdom is better than gold—and safer and easier to obtain in the way God provides.

Yet people pursue gold while neglecting grace.

Why should the uncertain hope of earthly treasure motivate such effort while the certain promise of heavenly riches fails to move us?

## Wisdom is worth more than wealth (verses 12–19)

Job spoke of wisdom as **knowing God and ourselves, and enjoying fellowship with him**.

Its worth is infinitely greater than every earthly treasure.

It is the gift of **the Holy Spirit** and cannot be bought with money.

We should esteem most highly whatever God esteems most.

Job sought wisdom as someone who sincerely desired to find it, knowing that it was found **in God alone**, and by means of **his revelation**.

## God gives true wisdom (verses 20–28)

There are **two kinds of wisdom**.

One is hidden within God and is **his secret knowledge**, which does not belong to us.

The other is **revealed by him** so that human beings may understand and obey.

Even the events of a single day and a single person's affairs are connected in ways that only God can see fully.

Only the One who sees all at once can judge each part rightly.

But knowledge of God's **revealed will** is available to us and will do us good.

Let everyone regard this as true wisdom:

**Fear the Lord and turn away from evil.**

Someone who learns that has learned what matters most.

Where is such wisdom found?

Its treasures are **hidden in Jesus Christ**, revealed in Scripture, received through faith and communicated by the Holy Spirit.

It will not feed our pride, flatter vanity or satisfy empty curiosity.

Instead it teaches and encourages sinners toward reverence for God, repentance and faith.

It does this **without requiring us to solve all the mysteries of this life's events**.
