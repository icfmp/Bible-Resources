# Amos 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Israel is called to seek the Lord. | 1–6 |
| Earnest exhortations to repentance. | 7–17 |
| Threatenings respecting idolatries. | 18–27 |

## Seek the Lord and live (verses 1–6)

We must not listen only to words of **comfort and peace**.

We must also hear and take seriously the words of God that **convict and awaken us**.

Whether we listen or refuse, the word he has spoken will take effect.

The Lord still offers mercy to human beings.

But many expect salvation through **forms of worship they have invented**, even though those practices only strengthen their condemnation.

If people refuse to come to **Jesus Christ** and seek mercy in him, the fire of God's judgment will break out against them.

They may turn **the world itself into an idol**, only to discover it provides no protection.

## Repentance, justice and divine mercy (verses 7–17)

The same almighty power can easily turn the suffering of repentant sinners into **prosperity and joy**, or turn the wealth of defiant sinners into **complete darkness**.

People living in evil times often refuse **plain, honest correction**.

Good and wise people may conclude that speaking openly will be useless.

Those who choose to seek and love what is good can nevertheless help save a country from destruction.

We should plead **God's spiritual promises**, asking him to **create a clean heart and renew a right spirit in us**.

He remains ready to show grace to people who genuinely seek him.

Their devotion will then affect every other responsibility.

Israel, however, had already experienced judgments that **passed by** the people as warnings.

Now the judgments would **pass through** them in full force.

## The terrible day of the Lord (verses 18–27)

Woe to people who eagerly desire **the day of the Lord's judgment** or wish for war and national upheaval because they imagine they will profit from the destruction.

The promised devastation would be so great that **nobody would gain from it**.

For unrepentant sinners, the day of the Lord is **dark and dreadful**.

When God makes a day dark, **the whole world cannot make it light**.

Those whom one judgment fails to reform will be pursued by another.

Escaping one danger will only place them before another.

An outward appearance of godliness with no inward reality is **a double evil**.

Israel repeated its ancestors' sins.

True worship requires that **the Lord alone be served**.

People professing religion often progress very little because they have **little or no fellowship with God during their religious duties**.

Sin had already brought their hearts into **captivity to Satan and idolatry**.

God would therefore send them into **physical captivity among idol worshipers**.
