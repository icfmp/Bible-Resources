# Jeremiah 40 — Modern English

## Jeremiah is free to join Gedaliah (verses 1–6)

The commander of the guard seemed almost proud to have served as the **instrument through which God fulfilled** what Jeremiah had been sent to **announce**.

Many people readily recognize God's justice and truth in what happens to **others**, yet remain blind and careless about **their own sins**.

Sooner or later, everyone must see that sin is the cause of human misery.

Jeremiah was allowed to choose where to go.

He was advised to join **Gedaliah**, the governor appointed over the land under Babylon's king.

Henry expresses **uncertainty about whether Jeremiah made the best decision** in accepting this advice.

Yet people who sincerely seek sinners' salvation and the good of the church may look for signs of better days where others see very little hope.

They often prefer **an opportunity to do useful work** to a safer position where they cannot serve others.

## A plot against Gedaliah (verses 7–16)

Jeremiah had never promised that the Jews would enjoy good times **immediately following the captivity**.

Yet the circumstances briefly seemed to offer hope.

How quickly that prospect was destroyed!

When God begins a judgment, he will accomplish what he intends.

So long as **pride, ambition and revenge** rule people's hearts, they will continually devise new schemes and stir up trouble, usually bringing ruin upon themselves.

Who would have expected another rebellion **so soon after Jerusalem's destruction**?

Human nature does not become good simply because it has endured great suffering.

**Only God's grace can bring real change.**

Even if those imprisoned for final judgment were allowed to return to earth, their hearts would still be wicked unless their nature was renewed.

Lord, give us **new hearts and the new mind that comes with new birth**.

You have said that without this new birth we cannot enter your heavenly kingdom.
