# Revelation 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The church in heaven and that on earth triumph, and praise the Lord for his righteous judgments. | 1–10 |
| A vision of Christ going forth to destroy the beast and his armies. | 11–21 |

## The church praises God and the marriage of the Lamb is announced (verses 1–10)

When we praise God for mercies already received, we also express confidence and desire for the blessings yet to come.

Heavenly angels and redeemed people join in this triumphant song.

Christ is **the Bridegroom of the church he has redeemed**. Their glorious union will be complete in heaven.

Henry also suggests that the beginning of the **millennium**—which he describes as a thousand-year reign of Christ, or an era of happiness upon earth—can be understood as the celebration of their marriage here on earth.

In that happy condition, Christ's church will be purified from **errors, divisions and corruptions** in its doctrine, discipline, worship and conduct.

The Lord will then openly acknowledge her as his beloved and his delight.

Her clothing is not the bright and ostentatious display of the mother of prostitutes.

Instead, she wears clean, white linen: **Christ's righteousness credited to her for justification and given to her in sanctification**.

The marriage feast consists of God's trustworthy gospel promises, made plain, applied and sealed to believers by the Holy Spirit through the appointed ordinances.

Henry understands this as pointing to abundant grace and comfort for Christians in the happier days still to come.

When John tried to honor the angel religiously, **the angel refused** and directed him to worship God alone.

Henry applies that refusal against worship directed to **bread and wine, saints or angels**.

He also rejects worship given to Christ by those who deny that he is **truly God by nature**, because religious worship belongs to God alone.

The God who speaks truth here is the One worthy of worship, **Father, Son and Holy Spirit**.

## Christ defeats the beast and its armies (verses 11–21)

Christ appears as the glorious Head of his church, riding a white horse—a sign of **righteousness and holiness**.

He wears many crowns because he is **King of kings and Lord of lords**.

His garment is portrayed as dipped in blood.

Henry connects this both with **Christ's own blood**, through which he purchased his mediatorial authority, and with the blood of **the enemies over whom he triumphs**.

He is called **the Word of God**.

No creature can comprehend the full glory of this name.

Yet Scripture tells us what we need to know: **the Word is God revealed in human flesh**.

The angels and saints who follow him share his purity and righteousness.

He now carries out the judgments foretold in the written word against those who resist him.

His royal name declares his rightful authority and warns even the greatest earthly rulers to submit to him.

Otherwise they must fall.

Every power of earth and hell may join the struggle, but none can defeat him.

These verses concern weighty events announced by God's prophets.

Those who followed wicked leaders will not be excused simply because they obeyed orders.

What a useless defense it will be on the day of judgment to say, **“We merely followed our leaders and did what other people did.”**

God has given us **his word as the rule of life**.

Neither the conduct of the majority nor the orders of powerful people can justify disobeying it.

Those who blindly follow the multitude in sin will go where that multitude goes, Henry warns: **into the burning lake**.
