# Jeremiah 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews' profession of religion was hypocritical. | 1–9 |
| The cruel proceedings of their enemies. | 10–18 |
| Their apostacy and idolatry. | 19–31 |

## A profession of religion without integrity (verses 1–9)

Jerusalem could offer no one who behaved with the integrity and godliness God required. The Lord saw the people's true character **through every disguise**.

The poor were ignorant of God and his ways, and this ignorance led them into wickedness. Those who know nothing of God and true religion may be expected to produce the **works of darkness**.

But poverty itself does not make people ignorant or wicked. God has faithful people among the poor who **know his way, walk in it, and do their duty**.

The people Jeremiah condemned were **willfully ignorant**. They had chosen not to learn; their ignorance could not excuse their conduct.

The rich, meanwhile, were proud and insolent. By abusing the favors God had given them, **they made their guilt worse**.

## God places a limit upon approaching destruction (verses 10–18)

Multitudes are ruined because they believe that God will **not judge as strictly as his word declares**. Satan used that same deception to bring ruin upon humanity.

Sinners are unwilling to accept any teaching as God's word if it requires them to abandon sin or disturbs their pleasure in it.

By mocking and mistreating the Lord's messengers, Judah filled up the measure of its wickedness.

God can bring suffering upon us **from distant places and unexpected causes**.

Yet he still had mercy reserved for his people. Therefore he placed limits on this approaching devastation.

Do not overlook God's word **“nevertheless”** in verse 18.

Here the Lord remembers **his covenant with Israel**: he displays his holiness and complete opposition to sin **while still sparing the sinner** (**Psalm 89:30–35**).

## Sin explains the nation's suffering (verses 19–31)

Hearts that have never been humbled readily accuse God of treating them unjustly when suffering arrives.

But sinners may read the nature of their sin **in their punishment**. If we ask why the Lord permits such distress, we should examine our own wrongdoing.

The restless waves obey the boundary God has set for them. Even a **sandy shore** restrains the sea as effectively as a mountain, because the Lord has commanded it.

Yet human beings break through every restraint of **his law** and surrender themselves to wickedness.

They also disregard their own true interests.

Year after year the Lord gives the **appointed weeks of harvest**. People continually receive his generosity, yet rebel against the Giver.

Sin deprives us of blessings. It can make **the heavens like bronze and the earth like iron**.

The things of this world cannot be the best things. We must not conclude that God approves of wicked conduct simply because a wicked person enjoys prosperity.

The sentence against evil may be delayed, **but it will certainly be carried out**.

The question **“Shall I not punish them for these things?”** announces both the certainty and necessity of God's righteous judgments.

Those who follow sinful paths should remember that those paths have an end—and **their final outcome will be bitter**.
