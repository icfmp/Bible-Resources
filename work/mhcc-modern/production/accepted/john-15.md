## Christ is the true Vine (verses 1–8)

Jesus Christ is **the true Vine**.

The union of his human and divine natures, and the fullness of the Spirit in him, resemble a fruitful root sustained by rich soil.

Believers are branches growing from him.

The root remains hidden, as our life is hidden with Christ. It supports the whole vine and supplies every part of it with nourishment.

All spiritual strength and provision come from him.

The branches are many, yet joined in one root they form **one vine**. So genuine Christians, even when separated by geography or differing in opinions, are united **in Christ**.

They are also weak like unsupported branches. They cannot stand apart from the One who sustains them.

The Father is the **gardener**. No gardener ever tended a vineyard with more wisdom or attention than God gives his church. Under his care, it cannot finally fail.

We must **bear fruit**.

A vine should produce grapes, and Christians should display Christian character and a life that honors God and benefits others.

Branches that bear no fruit are removed. But even fruitful branches must be **pruned**.

The best believers retain mistaken ideas, passions and dispositions that need correcting.

Christ carries forward their sanctification through this cleansing work; they should receive it with gratitude.

His word has power to cleanse, producing grace and removing corruption.

The more fruitful his people become, the more the Lord is glorified.

Fruitfulness requires **abiding in Christ** through faith, continued dependence and fellowship with him.

Believers know from experience that whenever active trust in Christ weakens, holy affections fade, sinful tendencies return and spiritual comfort declines.

People who do not remain in him may flourish outwardly for a time in religious profession but finally achieve nothing.

A withered vine branch has no useful future except the fire.

Let us draw more simply from Christ's fullness and grow in every good word and work, so our joy in him and his salvation may become complete.

## Christ's love and the command to love (verses 9–17)

Those loved by God as Father need not fear the world's hatred.

The Father loved Christ, who is perfectly worthy, and Christ loved his disciples, **who were unworthy**.

Everyone who loves the Savior should remain in that love and use every opportunity to show it.

A hypocrite's joy lasts briefly. Those remaining in Christ's love enjoy a lasting feast.

They show love for him by **keeping his commandments**.

But unless the same divine power that first poured Christ's love into our hearts continued to preserve it, we could not remain in that love for long.

The love we receive from Christ must direct our love toward one another.

Although Jesus had many things to command, he concentrated here upon **brotherly love**.

That one command includes many other duties.

## The world's hatred of Christ's disciples (verses 18–25)

Many people fail to recognize that when they reject Christ as **Prophet, Priest and King**, they show ignorance of the true God whom they claim to worship.

The name into which Jesus' disciples are baptized becomes the name by which they live and die.

For those facing severe suffering, it is a great consolation to suffer **because of Christ's name**.

The world's ignorance of God lies at the root of its hatred toward Jesus' disciples.

The more clearly people have been shown Christ's grace and truth, the greater their guilt if they refuse to love or believe in him.

## The Comforter will testify (verses 26 and 27)

The blessed Holy Spirit will maintain Christ's cause in the world despite opposition.

Believers taught and encouraged by his influence will bear witness to Jesus and his salvation.
