# Philippians 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle exhorts the Philippians to stand fast in the Lord. | 1 |
| Gives directions to some, and to all in general. | 2–9 |
| Expresses contentment in every condition of life. | 10–19 |
| He concludes with prayer to God the Father, and his usual blessing. | 20–23 |

## Stand firm in the Lord (verse 1)

Our confident hope of eternal life should make us **steadfast and constant** in following Christ.

Believers have different gifts and enjoy different measures of grace. Yet all have been renewed by **the same Holy Spirit**, so we are **brothers and sisters in Christ**.

To stand firm in the Lord is to stand by **his strength and grace**, not our own.

## Agree, rejoice and bring every concern to God (verses 2–9)

Believers should seek **unity of mind** and be ready to help one another.

Paul had experienced the benefit of his friends' assistance and knew how much other gospel workers would value similar help.

Let us seek sound assurance that our names are **written in the book of life**.

**Rejoicing in the Lord** is deeply important to Christian living. Believers need to be called to it again and again, because that joy outweighs every reason for sorrow.

Let opponents observe our **moderation concerning outward circumstances** and the calmness with which we bear losses and suffering.

The day of judgment is coming. For believers it will bring **complete redemption**; for the ungodly, destruction.

Not every kind of concern is wrong.

There is **diligent care**, which is our duty and includes wise planning and an appropriate awareness of responsibilities.

But there is also **anxious, distrustful care**, which is sinful, foolish and destructive to inward peace.

The appointed remedy for oppressive anxiety is **constant prayer**.

We should not pray only at fixed times, but turn to the Lord **in everything**.

Combine **thanksgiving** with requests. Do not merely ask for what you lack; recognize the mercies you have already received.

God knows our desires and needs better than we do. He requires prayer so that we acknowledge **our dependence upon him** and learn to value his gifts.

The peace of God is the comforting knowledge of **reconciliation with him**, a share in his favor and hope of heavenly happiness.

No words can fully express its value.

Through Jesus Christ this peace protects hearts and minds. It keeps believers **from sinning under troubles or being overwhelmed by them**, granting calmness and inward contentment.

Christians should seek and preserve **a good reputation for virtuous conduct** before God and upright people.

We should remain faithful to every kind of virtue whether or not other people praise us.

Paul's teaching and conduct agreed. He offered his own life as an example.

The way to enjoy **the God of peace** is to remain close to our duty.

Every privilege and the whole of our salvation come from **God's free mercy**, yet the enjoyment of those blessings is associated with **sincere, holy conduct**.

Even that obedience is **God's own work within us**. All praise belongs to him—not to human beings, words or deeds.

## Learn contentment in poverty and prosperity (verses 10–19)

Helping a faithful minister who is suffering is a good work.

Genuine Christian sympathy does not stop at **feeling sorry** for people. It moves us to **do what we can to help them**.

Paul had often known imprisonment, poverty, hardship and physical need.

Yet through every circumstance he learned to be content—to bring his desires into agreement with the situation God appointed and to use it as well as possible.

Discontent can arise even when circumstances are favorable.

**Pride, unbelief, restless desire for what we lack and sudden dissatisfaction with what we possess** all contribute to it.

When brought low, we should pray for **patient submission and confidence in God**.

When raised to prosperity, pray for **humility and a heavenly mind**.

A steady, contented spirit in every circumstance is a special gift of grace.

In poverty, believers should not abandon their comfort in God, distrust his Providence or pursue unlawful means of meeting needs.

In prosperity, they must not become **proud, careless or worldly**.

In fact, **prosperity is a harder spiritual lesson than poverty**, because plenty brings greater temptations than scarcity.

Paul was not writing to pressure the Philippians for **another gift**.

He wanted to encourage the charitable love they had already shown, assuring them that it would receive God's reward.

Through Christ we receive grace **to do what is good**, and through Christ we expect the reward.

Everything we possess comes through him. Therefore we should use everything **for him and his glory**.

## Praise the Father and receive Christ's grace (verses 20–23)

Paul closed his letter with **praise to God**.

Even when weakness and fear trouble us, we should look to the Lord **not as an enemy but as our Father**, ready to pity and help us.

Glory therefore belongs to him as Father.

God's favorable acceptance of reconciled souls, together with every gracious quality produced within them, is **purchased through Christ's merit** and applied **through his intercession**.

For this reason those blessings are rightly called **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ**.
