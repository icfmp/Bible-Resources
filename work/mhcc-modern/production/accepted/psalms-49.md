# Psalm 49 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A call for attention. | 1–5 |
| Folly of worldlings. | 6–14 |
| Against fear of death. | 15–20 |

## A warning for everyone (verses 1–5)

Few psalms begin more solemnly, because few truths are more important.

Everyone should listen and apply this teaching personally.

Poor people may be harmed by **excessive longing for wealth**, just as wealthy people may be harmed by **excessive enjoyment of it**.

The psalmist first applied the warning to himself, which is the right way to approach divine truth.

Before he described the foolishness of worldly self-confidence, he explained the security that comes through **trusting God instead of possessions**.

At judgment, the sins of our past lives will confront us.

Worldly sinners will rightly fear.

But why should someone fear death if **God is with that person**?

## The folly of trusting wealth (verses 6–14)

A person may possess riches and still have a heart enlarged by love, gratitude and obedience, using wealth for good.

It is not merely **having possessions** that makes someone worldly, but **treating them as the highest good**.

Worldly people may think occasionally about God, yet their deepest, most lasting thoughts center on earthly things.

Even the richest person cannot buy back the life of a beloved friend.

The psalm points beyond that limit toward **the eternal redemption accomplished by the Messiah**.

Redeeming a soul costs more than any earthly fortune.

But once Christ has accomplished that redemption, **it never needs to be repeated**.

The Redeemer rose before seeing corruption and lives forever (**Revelation 1:18**).

How foolish to sell one's soul for possessions that can never purchase its salvation!

No wealth can protect its owner from death.

Yet one generation after another praises the wisdom of making money the goal of life.

Even professing Christians imitate the rich fool in **Luke 12:16–21**.

Death will confront the proud sinner with the question: **Where are all your possessions and splendor now?**

At resurrection, the upright will be raised to honor while unrepentant sinners face everlasting shame (**Daniel 12:2**).

We should assess riches and holiness now as they will appear then.

**The beauty of holiness alone cannot be damaged by the grave**.

## Hope beyond death (verses 15–20)

Believers need not fear death.

The enormous inequalities of wealth and rank we see during life disappear at the grave.

But differences in people's **spiritual condition** become enormously significant at and after death.

God, who created human life, is also able to redeem it from eternal ruin.

Believers are tempted to envy worldly prosperity.

Other people praise someone who has built a large fortune and secured an influential family.

Yet what does human approval matter if **God condemns us**?

People rich in the Spirit's grace and comfort possess something death cannot take away. Indeed, death will bring them into a fuller enjoyment of it.

By contrast, worldly possessions must all be left behind.

We brought nothing into the world and can take nothing away.

**Gaining the whole world is worthless if it means losing one's soul**.

Without true heavenly wisdom, a person fails to live and die as a being made in God's image.

Yet some people still prefer the lot of the wealthy sinner to that of **poor Lazarus**, even when they consider the life to come.

How deeply we need the teaching of the Holy Spirit, since our supposed wisdom can still make us foolish about what matters most!
