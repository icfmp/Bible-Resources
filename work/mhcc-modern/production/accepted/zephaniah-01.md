# Zephaniah 1 — Modern English

## Judgment against divided worship (verses 1–6)

Complete destruction was approaching, brought by the Almighty.

The prophets consistently warned that **there is no peace for the wicked**.

Their language pictured the devastation of the land in vivid images, as though it would be left without inhabitants.

Those facing judgment included people openly practicing idolatry.

They also included people who claimed to worship **Jehovah while worshiping idols**, swearing by the Lord and by **Malcham**.

God will not accept hearts and worship divided between himself and false gods.

**What fellowship can there be between light and darkness?**

If Satan receives half of our allegiance, he will demand all of it.

If the Lord receives only half, he will accept none of it.

Neglecting God is itself an expression of irreverence and contempt.

May we not belong to those who turn away and perish, but to those who believe and receive the salvation of their souls.

## Pride, oppression and indifference judged (verses 7–13)

The day appointed by the Lord was near.

The punishment of presumptuous sinners would be like a sacrifice offered to divine justice.

The royal household of Judah would have to answer for pride and vanity.

Others would answer for **leaping over the threshold to invade their neighbors' rights and seize their belongings**.

Traders and rich merchants would also be called to account.

The Lord would confront the comfortable and indifferent.

They believed themselves secure and said in their hearts that **the Lord would neither do good nor do harm**.

In effect they denied that God rewards righteousness or punishes evil.

But the day of his judgment would show that people perish for breaking his law and because they lack, through faith, any share in **the Redeemer's atoning sacrifice**.

## The terrifying day of the Lord (verses 14–18)

The warning of approaching destruction was enough to make even sinners in Zion tremble.

The day of the Lord would reveal his anger through the punishment of evil.

It was near—a day of wrath, trouble and distress for sinners.

We must not allow God's patience to lull us into false security.

What does a person gain by obtaining the whole world if that person loses **their soul**?

And what can anyone give in exchange for the soul?

Let us flee from the wrath to come and choose the good portion that can never be taken away.

Then we will be ready for whatever happens.

Nothing will separate us from **God's love in Christ Jesus our Lord**.
