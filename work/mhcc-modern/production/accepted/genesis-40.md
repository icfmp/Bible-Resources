## The cupbearer's and baker's dreams (verses 1–19)

The chief cupbearer and the chief baker were troubled less by their imprisonment than by their dreams.

God has more than one way of bringing sorrow to a person's spirit.

Joseph showed compassion for them. We should care when we see sadness in the faces of our brothers and sisters.

Those who are in distress often find relief simply in being noticed.

We should also examine the causes of our own sorrow.

Is there a good reason for it? Is there no comfort sufficient to outweigh it, whatever the cause may be?

“Why are you cast down, O my soul?”

Joseph was careful to give all the glory to God.

The cupbearer's dream foretold his restoration to his position. The baker's dream foretold his death.

It was not Joseph's fault that he could bring the baker no happier news.

Ministers of God's word are likewise interpreters. They cannot change the message they have been given.

When they faithfully declare it, they are not to blame if people dislike what they hear.

Joseph did not bring up his brothers' cruelty in selling him. Nor did he dwell on the wrong done to him by his master's wife and his master.

He simply stated his own innocence.

When we must defend ourselves, we should avoid speaking badly of others as far as we can.

Let us be content to establish our innocence without reproaching others for their guilt.

## The cupbearer forgets Joseph (verses 20–23)

Joseph's interpretation of both dreams came true on the exact day he had announced.

It was Pharaoh's birthday. His servants gathered before him, and the two prisoners' cases were considered.

We can all benefit from taking note of our own birthdays.

They give us an opportunity to thank God for the mercy of our birth, to grieve over the sins of our lives, and to look toward the day of our death as better than the day of our birth.

Yet it is strange that worldly people, so attached to life here, celebrate the passing of another year in their short time on earth.

Christians have reason to rejoice both that they were born and that they are drawing nearer to the end of sin and sorrow, and to everlasting happiness.

But the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph. He forgot him, even though Joseph had done him good.

We should not be surprised if love is repaid with hatred or kindness with neglect.

How quickly people at ease forget those who are still suffering!

Through this disappointment, Joseph learned to trust in God alone.

We cannot expect too little from people or too much from God.

Let us not forget our Redeemer's sufferings, promises, and love.

We blame the cupbearer for his ingratitude toward Joseph, but we are far more ungrateful toward the Lord Jesus.

Joseph only foretold the cupbearer's release. Christ actually accomplished ours, pleading for us before the King of kings.

Yet we forget him, despite frequent reminders and despite our own promises never to do so.

How poorly we repay him—like a foolish, unwise people.
