# Song of Solomon 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The mutual love of Christ and his church. | 1–7 |
| The hope and calling of the church. | 8–13 |
| Christ's care of the church, Her faith and hope. | 14–17 |

## Christ and his church love one another (verses 1–7)

Believers are lovely because they are clothed in **Christ's righteousness** and fragrant with **the graces of his Spirit**.

They flourish under the refreshing rays of **the Sun of Righteousness**.

The lily of the East may grow tall while possessing a fragile stem.

So the church is weak in itself but strong in **Christ who supports it**.

The wicked, who do not love Christ, are like thorns that injure and destroy.

Sinful corruption is itself like **thorns in the flesh**.

But the lily now surrounded by thorns will one day be transplanted into a paradise **without any thorn or briar**.

The world is a barren tree for the soul.

Christ is a **fruit-bearing tree**.

People dried out by conviction of sin, fear of the law or earthly troubles may find rest under his shade.

But merely passing by this shade is insufficient.

**We must sit beneath it**, receiving and enjoying his protection.

Believers have tasted the goodness of the Lord Jesus.

His fruit consists of **the privileges of the new covenant**, bought by his blood and given through his Spirit.

His promises are sweet, but so are **his commands**.

Forgiveness is sweet, as is peace of conscience.

When our taste for sinful pleasures has been lost, divine consolation becomes especially precious.

Christ leads souls to find comfort through **his appointed ordinances**, like a banqueting house where believers feast with him.

The banner over them is **Christ's love**, shown in his death and his word.

How much better to be overwhelmed by love for Christ than by love for the world!

Even when Christ seems absent, he remains a very present help.

His people are held in his hands with extraordinary tenderness.

The soul, recognizing Christ nearby, fears losing communion with him.

We can grieve the Spirit through sinful attitudes.

Let those who enjoy his consolation be careful not to **sin away their comfort**.

## Christ calls the church out of winter (verses 8–13)

The church delights in the prospect of deeper fellowship with Christ.

He alone can speak directly to the heart.

The believer sees him approaching.

Henry says this image may refer to the Old Testament believers' expectation of **Christ's coming in human flesh**.

Christ comes gladly to fulfill his saving mission, and he comes quickly.

Even if he seems to leave his people, the absence is brief; he returns with enduring loving-kindness.

The saints of earlier times saw him through **sacrifices and ceremonial ordinances**.

We also see him imperfectly, as through a lattice.

Christ calls newly converted people to rise from idleness and discouragement and leave behind sin and worldly vanity in order to enjoy fellowship with him.

Winter may represent **years of ignorance and unfruitfulness**, or the storms of conviction concerning guilt and danger.

Even the earliest, unripe fruits of holy living are pleasing to the One whose grace produced them.

These tokens of divine kindness encourage the soul to follow Christ more wholeheartedly.

Rise and leave **the world and the flesh** for fellowship with Christ.

All this change comes from **the Sun of Righteousness** and his gracious influence.

## Christ protects and guides his church (verses 14–17)

The church is Christ's **dove**, returning to him as to **Noah**.

Christ is the **Rock** where the dove finds refuge from birds of prey.

He invites her to come boldly before the throne of grace.

There she has **a great High Priest** to hear and present her requests.

Speak freely.

Do not fear contempt or rejection.

Prayer is sweet and acceptable to God.

Those whom he sanctifies possess their true beauty.

The **little foxes** picture the first beginnings of sinful thoughts and desires, frivolous distractions, wasted visits, small departures from the truth and the desire to conform to the world.

Believers must put such things away, mortifying passions that would destroy new spiritual growth.

Whatever prevents progress in what is good should be removed.

Christ **feeds among the lilies**: he is graciously present with his people.

Even when believers feel abandoned, they should remember his kindness and resist temptation.

The shadows of Jewish ceremonial worship disappeared as the gospel day dawned.

And after a night of spiritual absence, a new day of consolation will come.

Christ will cross **the mountains of Bether**, the mountains that divide, and ultimately cross every obstacle to bring his people home to himself.
