# Zechariah 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## The Lord surrounds his growing city (verses 1–5)

The man with the measuring line is, in Henry's reading, **the Son of David, Jesus Christ**.

He is the Master Builder of his church.

God knows the extent of his church and will ensure that every guest invited to the wedding supper has a place.

Jerusalem's enlargement is a gracious promise.

City walls normally protect the inhabitants but also limit the room available to them.

This Jerusalem would be as spacious **as a city without walls**, yet as secure **as a city with the strongest walls**.

There is room for multitudes no person can number.

No one who trusts in Christ will be refused.

The Lord never shuts out of heaven a genuine member of his church on earth.

**God himself will be a wall of fire** around his people.

No enemy can break through, undermine or attack such a wall without facing danger.

Henry sees this vision finding its fuller accomplishment in **the gospel church**, enlarged by the admission of the Gentiles and protected by the Son of God, especially during **the glorious days still to come**.

## The people are called to leave Babylon (verses 6–9)

If the Lord builds Jerusalem for his people, they should inhabit it for his glory.

The privileges and promises of life among God's people should persuade us to join them whatever the cost.

When Zion is being enlarged to receive all God's Israel, remaining in Babylon is foolish.

We must not remain willingly captive to sin, even when worldly circumstances are comfortable.

**Escape for your life; do not look back.**

Jesus Christ has announced freedom for captives and has accomplished that deliverance himself.

Everyone should resolve that **sin will no longer rule** over them.

Those who wish to belong to God's children should separate from this corrupt world (**Acts 2:40**).

Christ's care for his church displays God's tender love.

Whoever touches the church touches **the apple of God's eye**—its most sensitive and precious part.

The slightest attack is a serious offense to him.

**Christ has been sent as the Protector of his church.**

## God promises to dwell among his people (verses 10–13)

Henry understands the promise as announcing **Christ's coming in human nature**.

Many nations would turn away from idols.

God would acknowledge as his own everyone who sincerely joined himself to the Lord.

The passage announces glorious days for **Christ's kingdom**.

God is about to do something extraordinary and unexpected.

He will take up the cause of a people who seemed long neglected.

Meanwhile we are to **submit quietly to his holy will and wait patiently** for the outcome.

We may be certain that God will complete all the work he has begun.

In due time he will come to judge, finish saving his people and punish the inhabitants of the world for their sins.
