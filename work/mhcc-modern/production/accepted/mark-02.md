# Mark 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ heals one sick of the palsy. | 1–12 |

| Why Christ's disciples did not fast. | 18–22 |
| He justifies his disciples for plucking corn on the sabbath. | 23–28 |

## Jesus forgives and heals a paralysed man (verses 1–12)

The man's need to be carried revealed how much suffering human life contains.

The friends who carried him showed real **compassion for someone in distress**.

Genuine and strong faith may be expressed in different ways, but Jesus receives and approves it.

Sin is the origin of all human suffering and sickness.

To remove the effects decisively, the cause must be dealt with.

**Forgiving sin reaches the root of every disease.**

Jesus demonstrated his authority to pardon sin through his power to heal the paralysed man.

Physical cures picture the even greater miracle of spiritual healing.

Sin is **the soul's disease**; when forgiven, it is healed.

When we understand Christ's power to transform sinful souls, we may truly say we have never seen anything like it.

Most people think they are spiritually healthy.

Believing they have no need of a physician, they disregard Christ and the gospel.

But people who have been convicted and humbled, and know there is no hope apart from the Savior, reveal faith by **coming to him without delay**.

## Levi is called and welcomes Jesus (verses 13–17)

Levi, also known as Matthew, was a **tax collector serving Rome**.

Henry judges that this position was especially discreditable for a Jew of the time, and remarks that honest and fair-dealing publicans were rare.

The occupation reminded Jewish people of their subjection to Rome, leading them to despise its officials.

Yet Jesus called this man to follow him.

Through Christ, God has mercy to pardon the greatest sins and grace to change even those regarded as the greatest sinners.

There is nothing new about good and wise actions being misrepresented and condemned.

Jesus freely ate with tax collectors and people considered sinful, even when Pharisees took offense.

He had come because the world was not righteous.

Without sin, there would have been no occasion for him either to preach **repentance** or to purchase **forgiveness**.

We must not form intimate friendships with ungodly people because we love their empty and corrupt conversation.

But we should love **their souls** and seek their salvation.

Our great Physician has healing power within himself and could not catch the spiritual disease of those he helped.

We, however, can be harmed by bad company.

Therefore, while seeking to help others, we must guard against being led into sin.

## The question of fasting (verses 18–22)

Strictly religious people often criticize others merely for failing to conform to all their personal opinions.

Jesus himself was slandered in this way.

We should be willing to endure unjust criticism while carefully avoiding conduct that truly deserves blame.

We must also fulfill every duty **at its proper time and in the proper way**.

## The disciples gather grain on the Sabbath (verses 23–28)

The Sabbath is a **holy institution given by God**.

It is a **privilege and benefit**, not forced labor or a burden.

God never intended it to injure human beings.

We must not interpret his command in ways that undermine its own gracious purpose.

The Sabbath was established for humanity living in communities, bearing needs and troubles, and preparing for an everlasting condition of happiness or misery.

Human beings were **not created in order to serve the Sabbath**, as though keeping it added something to God's own happiness.

They were not commanded to observe it outwardly at the cost of actual harm to themselves.

Every rule connected with its observance must be understood **according to mercy**.
