# 1 Samuel 31 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Saul's defeat and death. | 1–7 |
| Saul's body rescued by the men of Jabesh-gilead. | 8–13 |

## Saul is defeated and dies (verses 1–7)

We cannot determine anyone's spiritual or eternal state merely from the way that person dies.

Death comes to the righteous and the wicked.

Saul was badly wounded and could neither resist nor flee.

Yet he showed no concern for his immortal soul.

His only stated concern was that the Philistines might insult him or cause him pain.

He then took his own life.

Henry condemns that final action as sinful.

He also warns that the devil tempts people in great distress to believe such a desperate act is an answer to suffering.

We should guard against that temptation by understanding its moral seriousness before God and the grievous consequences it can bring to others.

But our real security does not come from our own strength.

It comes from the Lord who keeps Israel.

Let us **watch and pray**, and put on the **whole armor of God**, so that we may stand firm in the evil day and remain standing after the conflict.

## The men of Jabesh-gilead retrieve Saul's body (verses 8–13)

Scripture records what became of the **bodies** of Saul and his sons.

It does **not** say what became of their **souls** after death.

Hidden things do not belong to us.

The way we die, or what happens afterward to our physical remains, matters far less than our eternal relation to God.

If our souls are saved, our bodies will be raised **incorruptible and glorious**.

But refusing to fear the One who can destroy both body and soul in hell is extreme folly.

The honor people give the dead cannot help those suffering under God's judgment.

Impressive funerals, magnificent monuments and generous praise may preserve a person's public reputation.

Yet those things cannot change the soul's condition.

We should therefore seek **the honor that comes from God alone**.
