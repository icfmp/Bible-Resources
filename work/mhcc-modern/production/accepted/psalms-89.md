## God's unbreakable covenant (verses 1–4)

Our expectations may disappoint us, but **God's promises remain established in heaven**.

They belong to his eternal purposes and cannot be defeated by opponents on earth or in hell.

Trust in God's boundless mercy and everlasting truth can comfort us even through our deepest trials.

## God's majesty, power and righteousness (verses 5–14)

The more we learn of God's works, the more reason we have to admire them.

True praise confesses that no one is like him.

Therefore our worship should be marked by **reverence**.

How little reverence is sometimes present in our congregations! We have reason to humble ourselves for that neglect.

The almighty power that struck Egypt will also defeat the church's enemies.

Those who trust God's mercy may rejoice in his name.

**Mercy and truth direct all God's works**. From his eternal counsel to the eternal results of his actions, everything rests upon justice and righteous judgment.

## The joy of knowing God (verses 15–18)

Happy are those who truly know the **joyful message of the gospel**: they obey it, experience its power within their hearts and show its fruit in their lives.

Believers possess nothing in themselves, yet **have everything in Christ Jesus**, so they may rejoice in his name.

May the Lord enable us to do so.

**Joy in the Lord strengthens his people**.

Unbelief, by contrast, weakens our courage and discourages others. Though it can disguise itself as humility, **it is actually a form of pride**.

**Christ is the Holy One of Israel**. In him God's people are more blessed than through any other gift.

## God's covenant with David points to Christ (verses 19–37)

The Lord anointed David with holy oil.

The anointing represented the grace and gifts David received, but it also pointed forward to **Christ, the King, Priest and Prophet**, who received the Holy Spirit without measure.

David was persecuted after being anointed, but his enemies were unable finally to overcome him.

Even this was only a faint picture of **the Redeemer's suffering, rescue, glory and authority**.

Only in Christ are the promises fully fulfilled.

He is **the mighty God**, the appointed Redeemer who alone can finish the work of our salvation.

We should seek assurance of our part in those blessings through **the Holy Spirit's witness in our hearts**.

David's descendants were corrected for their sins, and so are God's people.

But their correction is **a rod, not a sword**: it is intended **to correct, not to destroy**.

It rests in the hands of a wise and gracious Father who knows when discipline is necessary and what is best.

As the sun and moon remain in the heavens even when their appearance changes, God's **covenant of grace in Christ** remains secure despite changes in how his people experience it.

## Lament, the cross, and continuing hope (verses 38–52)

At times God's providential dealings are difficult to reconcile with his promises.

Yet we know his works always fulfill his word.

When **Christ himself hung upon the cross**, it seemed as though God had rejected his Anointed One.

But the eternal covenant was not broken.

The honor of David's royal family faded. Crowns and thrones are easily brought into the dust.

Yet **an unfading crown of glory** awaits Christ's spiritual descendants.

Sin has brought devastation to many households, even distinguished families where true faith was once found.

Still, the Lord's unchanging truth and faithfulness reassure us that he will not finally reject the people he has chosen and brought into covenant with himself.

People mocked the faithful for serving God.

Likewise, the scoffers of the last days mock the Messiah's promised return (**2 Peter 3:3–4**).

God's dealings with David's household help us understand his dealings with believers and the church.

Their suffering may be terrible, but God will not finally abandon them.

Those who merely deceive themselves may misuse this promise, while other believers bring darkness and trouble upon themselves by careless living.

Yet **true believers** may rely on God's faithfulness as they perform their duties and bear the cross.

The psalm ends in praise despite its grief.

Those who thank God for his past actions may also thank him for what he will yet do.

The Lord will continue showing mercy to those who continue praising him.
