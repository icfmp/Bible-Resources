# Job 15 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| Eliphaz reproves Job. | 1–16 |
| The unquietness of wicked men. | 17–35 |

## Eliphaz criticizes Job again (verses 1–16)

Eliphaz began his second attack upon Job.

Instead of becoming gentler after hearing Job's suffering, he became harsher.

He **wrongly accused Job** of abandoning reverence for God, neglecting prayer and losing all regard for the Lord.

Henry summarizes much of religion in two duties: **fearing God** as a vital inward principle, and **praying to God** as a vital practice.

Eliphaz also charged Job with conceit and with despising the advice and comfort his friends offered.

We often imagine our own words are of great importance when others, sometimes with good reason, find little value in them.

Eliphaz accused Job of resisting God himself.

Yet he should not have placed such harsh interpretations upon the words of someone long known for godliness and now enduring temptation.

These speakers clearly recognized **original sin and the deep corruption of fallen human nature**.

But that true doctrine did not make their harsh accusations against Job just.

Let us marvel at God's patience in enduring sinners.

Let us marvel even more at his love in redeeming us through his beloved Son, **Jesus Christ**.

## Eliphaz describes the unrest of wicked people (verses 17–35)

Eliphaz correctly taught that wicked people are in a miserable condition.

But from that truth he drew a false conclusion: **because Job was miserable, Job must be wicked**.

The fact that some of God's people prosper in the world does not mean those who experience poverty or loss cannot also belong to him.

Eliphaz described the anxiety of wicked people, especially oppressors.

Such people often live in fear and die in misery.

If the prosperity of unrepentant sinners ends in ruin, we should regard other people's disasters as warnings.

God's correction does not feel pleasant while we undergo it.

Nevertheless, it can produce **the peaceful fruit of righteousness** in those who learn from it.

No hardship, however severe, can rob a true follower of the Lord of his favor.

**What can separate us from Christ's love?**
