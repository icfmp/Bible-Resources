# Isaiah 35 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The flourishing state of Christ's kingdom. | 1–4 |
| The privileges of his people. | 5–10 |

## The wilderness blossoms through grace (verses 1–4)

Judah prospered under **Hezekiah**, but the main subject of this prophecy, according to Henry, is **Christ's kingdom**.

Converting grace makes a soul that was once a wasteland **rejoice and sing**, and grow abundantly like a garden filled with flowers.

The gospel encourages the weak and fearful.

Fear weakens us; resisting fear strengthens us both for the tasks we must do and for the suffering we may have to endure.

The One who calls us to be strong has made help available through **One who is mighty**.

The Messiah would come to confront the powers of darkness and bring generous consolation to those who mourn in Zion.

**He will come and save**.

He will also **come again at the end of time** to judge those who oppressed his people and give the afflicted a rest that fully compensates their suffering.

## The highway of holiness and eternal joy (verses 5–10)

When Christ establishes his kingdom, he works great wonders **within human souls**.

Through his **word and Spirit**, those spiritually blind begin to see.

Those who were deaf to God's calls become willing listeners.

People unable to do good receive grace to act faithfully.

Those who did not know how to speak **about God or to God** receive opened lips that praise him.

When the Holy Spirit came upon **Gentiles hearing the gospel**, the fountains of spiritual life were opened.

Much of the earth still resembles a desert, lacking the means of grace, faithful worshipers and the fruits of holiness.

But **the path of godliness will be opened**.

The highway of holiness is **the way of God's commandments**, the good and ancient way.

It is a clear road toward heaven.

Even uneducated people who know little may be kept from missing it.

It is also a safe road, for nothing can ultimately harm those traveling on it.

**Jesus Christ is the way to God**.

He is clearly revealed, and the duties of faithful people are plainly marked out.

Let us therefore go forward cheerfully, assured that our destination is **everlasting joy and rest for the soul**.

Those who enter gospel Zion by faith rejoice in Jesus Christ.

Divine consolation causes sorrow and sighing to flee.

Such hopes and expectations of eternity should overshadow **both the sorrows and the fleeting delights of this present life**.

Yet merely admiring Scripture's beauty is not enough.

Do we personally possess its promises through faith?

Do we love God not only as Creator but because **he gave his only Son to die for us**?

Are we truly **walking in holiness**?

These practical questions are far more useful than spending our time on curious, amusing but fruitless speculation.
