# 1 Corinthians 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle shows his authority, and asserts his right to be maintained. | 1–14 |
| He waved this part of his Christian liberty, for the good of others. | 15–23 |
| He did all this, with care and diligence, in view of an unfading crown. | 24–27 |

## Paul has a right to material support (verses 1–14)

It is nothing new for ministers to receive unkind treatment in return for **good intentions, diligent work and fruitful service**.

Paul answered his critics and made himself an example of **self-denial for other people's good**.

He possessed the right to marry, like other apostles.

He could also claim adequate support for a wife, and for children if he had any, from the congregations he served.

He was not required to earn all this through his own manual labor.

**Those who care for our souls should receive provision for their bodily needs**.

Yet Paul deliberately surrendered this right rather than allow demanding his support to hinder the gospel's work.

The duty of supporting gospel ministers nevertheless remains with the people they serve.

A minister may **waive a right**, as Paul did.

But congregations who deny or withhold the support due to ministers **violate Christ's command**.

## Paul gives up liberty to win people for Christ (verses 15–23)

It is a minister's honor to deny personal advantage in order to serve Christ and lead people to salvation.

When a minister voluntarily gives up lawful support for the gospel's sake, that person goes beyond the minimum required by the office.

By preaching without payment, Paul showed his zeal and love.

This gave him encouragement and hope in his own soul.

He understood that the ceremonial law had become **a yoke removed by Christ**.

Yet he was willing to comply with its observances where this could help Jewish hearers overcome prejudice, listen to the gospel and be brought to Christ.

He never broke **Christ's commandments** merely to please people.

But wherever lawful, he adapted his conduct to those he served, seeking to win at least some.

**Doing good was the great work of his life**.

He refused to let insistence upon personal privileges obstruct that goal.

We must guard against extremes and trust **Christ alone**, rather than our own flexible conduct, for salvation.

And we must never tolerate error or misconduct that injures others or brings disgrace upon the gospel.

## Run the race for an unfading crown (verses 24–27)

Paul drew upon **the races and athletic contests held at the Isthmian games**, familiar to the Corinthians.

In the Christian race, **everyone may run in such a way as to obtain the prize**.

This is powerful encouragement to persevere with all our strength.

The contestants followed strict diets, trained their bodies, became accustomed to hardship and practiced their skills.

Similarly, those concerned for their eternal welfare must fight **against sinful bodily desires**.

The body must not be allowed to govern the soul.

Paul urged believers to beware of indulging appetite or giving in to temptation.

He himself recognized the danger of yielding to the desires of the flesh.

If even an apostle needed **holy fear and watchfulness** to remain faithful, how much more do we need them!

Let us learn humility and caution and remain alert to the dangers facing us while we live in mortal bodies.
