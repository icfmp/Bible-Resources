# Psalm 118 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| It is good to trust in the Lord. | 1–18 |
| The coming of Christ in his kingdom. | 19–29 |

## Trusting God's mercy in every trial (verses 1–18)

The psalmist's account of his suffering is especially applicable to **Jesus Christ**.

Many hated Christ without cause.

The Lord also subjected him to severe suffering, bruising and grieving him so that **through his wounds we might be healed**.

At times God strengthens his people when he is not yet their delight.

They may receive **spiritual support without the immediate pleasure of spiritual comfort**.

Believers can find abundant reasons for praise whether they look back to the Lord's everlasting kindness and mercy or forward to his promised blessings.

Every answer to prayer reminds us that **the Lord is on our side**.

Therefore we need not be ruled by fear of what other people can do.

We must faithfully perform our duties toward everyone and trust God alone to accept and bless our service.

Seek to live so that you can **declare God's works** and encourage others to serve and trust him.

This confidence also characterized the triumphs of **the Son of David**, who knew the Lord's purpose would prosper in his hands.

## Christ, the rejected stone who became the cornerstone (verses 19–29)

Even ancient believers who saw Christ's coming from a great distance had reason to praise God in anticipation.

The prophecy about **the stone rejected by the builders** (verses **22 and 23**) may refer in part to David's promotion, but it points principally toward **Christ**.

**First, his humiliation:** he is the stone the builders rejected.

They attempted to continue their work without him, and their rejection brought their own ruin.

Those who reject Christ are themselves rejected by God.

**Second, his exaltation:** Christ became the **chief cornerstone** of the foundation.

He is also the final topstone that completes the building and therefore deserves preeminence in everything.

His name is **Wonderful**, and the redemption he accomplished is the greatest of God's marvelous works.

We should rejoice in **the Lord's day**, not only because he has appointed it, but because it celebrates Christ becoming the Head.

**Sabbaths ought to be joyful days**, giving us a foretaste of heaven.

Let this Savior be **my Savior and Ruler**.

May my soul prosper and be in health through the peace and righteousness of his reign.

May he give me victory over desires that fight against my soul and, through grace, bring my heart under his rule.

The duties he commands come with **true light**.

Our response to the privilege of redemption must include sacrifices of gratitude.

These are **not bodies killed upon an altar**, but **ourselves as living sacrifices**, bound to the altar in grateful dedication to God.

They also include spiritual sacrifices of **prayer and praise** in which our hearts sincerely participate.

The psalmist praises the Lord and invites everyone nearby to give thanks for the good news of great joy: **there is a Redeemer, Christ the Lord**.

In him **the covenant of grace is secure and everlasting**.
