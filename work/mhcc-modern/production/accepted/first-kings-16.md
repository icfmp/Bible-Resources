# 1 Kings 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| The reigns of Baasha and Elah in Israel. | 1–14 |

| Ahab's wickedness, Hiel rebuilds Jericho. | 29–34 |

## Baasha and Elah (verses 1–14)

This chapter concerns only the northern kingdom of Israel and the repeated upheavals in its government.

God still called the Israelites **his people**, though they had become terribly corrupt.

Through Jehu, God announced that Baasha's family would suffer the same destruction that Baasha had been used to bring upon Jeroboam's family.

Those who imitate another person's sins may expect to share the consequences, particularly when they condemn wrongdoing in others while tolerating it in themselves.

Baasha himself died peacefully and received an honorable burial.

That fact reminds us that the judgments we should fear most may come **after death**.

Elah's end warns people who drink excessively.

Death can surprise them while they are drunk.

Beyond the diseases caused by overdrinking, intoxicated people are easy prey for enemies and vulnerable to accidents.

It is dreadful to be overtaken by death while committing sin and unprepared for prayer or any act of devotion.

The Lord's word came true, and the sins of Baasha and Elah were brought to account.

Their idols are called **vanities**, because idols cannot help or save anyone.

How miserable are those who depend on empty gods!

## Zimri and Omri (verses 15–28)

People who forsake God may be left to destroy one another.

Ambitious, proud people bring each other to ruin.

Omri fought Tibni for several years.

We do not always understand the principles by which God governs individuals and nations in his providence.

Nevertheless, this history offers clear warnings.

When tyrants follow one another, and massacres, conspiracies and civil wars multiply, the people have every reason to recognize God's displeasure against sin.

They are urgently called to repentance and reform.

Omri became notorious for his wickedness.

Many evil people have also been powerful and famous.

They have built cities and had their names preserved in history, yet possess **no name in the book of life**.

## Ahab's sin and the rebuilding of Jericho (verses 29–34)

Ahab did more evil than the kings who ruled before him.

He showed particular hostility toward both Jehovah and Israel.

He was not content with breaking the **second commandment** by worshiping images.

He broke the **first commandment** by worshiping other gods.

Treating smaller sins lightly opens the way to greater sins.

Marriage to people who are bold in wrongdoing also encourages sin and may hurry someone toward terrible extremes.

One of Ahab's subjects, **Hiel**, followed that example of presumption when he began rebuilding Jericho.

Like Achan, he took for his own purposes what had been placed under God's judgment.

He defied the curse that was well known in Israel.

No one who hardens his heart against the Lord can ultimately prosper.

Let the chapter make us consider the dreadful end of all who persist in evil.

Across every rank and circumstance of human life, the record of ungodly people provides similar sorrowful warnings.
