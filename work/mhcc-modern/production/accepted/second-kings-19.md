# 2 Kings 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Hezekiah receives an answer of peace. | 1–7 |
| Sennacherib's letter. | 8–19 |
| His fall is prophesied. | 20–34 |
| The Assyrian army destroyed, Sennacherib slain. | 35–37 |

## Hezekiah hears a message of reassurance (verses 1–7)

Hezekiah was deeply troubled by the dishonor Rabshakeh's blasphemy brought upon God.

When we receive God's word through his messengers, we should especially desire that they pray to God for us.

The **great Prophet** is also our **great Intercessor**.

People who lift their hearts to the Lord in prayer may expect his help.

Human desperation provides a special occasion for God to show his power.

While the Lord's servants must announce warnings to the irreverent, proud and hypocritical, they also bring words of comfort to discouraged believers.

## Sennacherib's letter and Hezekiah's prayer (verses 8–19)

Prayer is an unfailing refuge for Christians facing temptation, whether the struggle comes from outward troubles or inward enemies.

At the mercy seat of our all-powerful Friend, we may open our hearts and lay our problems before him, as Hezekiah spread out the letter.

We may appeal confidently to his mercy.

When we recognize that **God's glory** is involved in our cause, faith receives fresh strength and rejoices that it will not be shaken.

Our strongest reasons for asking God's help are those drawn from **his own honor**.

## God foretells Sennacherib's defeat (verses 20–34)

Every movement of Sennacherib was known to God.

The Lord himself promised to defend the city.

Any person or place he has undertaken to protect is secure.

The Assyrian invasion probably prevented Judah's fields from being sown that year.

Henry supposes that the following year was a **Sabbath year**.

Yet God promised enough food from the land to sustain the people through those two years.

This provision would follow the destruction of Sennacherib's army.

The future harvest therefore served as a sign to strengthen Hezekiah's present faith, assuring him that the promised deliverance would come and that the Lord would continue caring for Judah.

God promised these things **not because the people deserved them but for his own glory**.

May our hearts be good soil in which his word takes root and bears fruit through our lives.

## The Assyrian army is destroyed (verses 35–37)

That very night, after God's message reached Hezekiah, the greater part of the Assyrian army was killed.

See how weak even the strongest people are before Almighty God!

Who has hardened himself against the Lord and prospered?

Sennacherib's own sons later murdered him.

Parents who suffer from disobedient children should ask whether they themselves have disobeyed their Father in heaven.

This history powerfully confirms the value of steadfast trust in God.

He may afflict his people, but he will not abandon them.

It is good when troubles drive us to prayer.

Yet our dependence on emergencies may also expose our unbelief.

How reluctant we are to rest entirely on God's promises!

How eagerly we want to know **how** he will save us!

How impatient we become when relief is delayed!

We must wait for his word to be fulfilled.

Lord, help our unbelief!
