# Isaiah 63 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ's victory over his enemies. | 1–6 |
| His mercy toward his church. | 7–14 |
| The prayer of the church. | 15–19 |

## The victorious Messiah brings judgment (verses 1–6)

In a vision, the prophet sees **the Messiah returning in victory** from the defeat of his enemies.

**Edom** serves as a picture of those enemies.

He does not travel as someone worn out by battle.

Rather, he moves in the greatness of his strength, ready to overcome every opposing power.

The Messiah declares that he has been treading **the winepress of God's judgment** (**Revelation 14:19; 19:13**).

By his own power, without human assistance, he defeats those stubbornly opposing him.

The appointed day of judgment has come as **the time for rescuing his church**.

When Christ first appeared on earth, he seemed weak as he poured out **his own precious blood in atonement for our sins**.

But at the appointed time he will appear in the full display of his strength.

The harvest is rapidly ripening, and the day determined for judgment approaches.

Let sinners seek reconciliation with **their righteous Judge** before he brings their strength to nothing.

Does Christ say, **“I am coming soon”**?

Then let our hearts respond, **“Come, Lord Jesus; let the time of the redeemed arrive.”**

## God's mercy remembered amid Israel's rebellion (verses 7–14)

Henry understands the latter part of this chapter and all of the next as expressing, or anticipating, **the prayers of Jews at their conversion**.

The people remember God's great mercy and favor toward their nation.

They confess their wickedness and stubbornness, ask for pardon and grieve over the long misery they have experienced.

The **Father's only Son** became the **Angel or Messenger of God's love**.

He redeemed his people and carried them tenderly.

Yet they complained, resisted the Holy Spirit, despised and persecuted God's prophets, and **rejected and crucified the promised Messiah**.

Every true comfort and hope we have comes from **God's loving-kindness**.

Our misery and fears arise from **our sins**.

Nevertheless, he is the Savior.

When sinners seek the God who in earlier times glorified himself by rescuing, feeding and guiding his purchased flock through danger, they have reason to hope.

He has also given **the Holy Spirit** to bless his ministers' labors.

Seeking this Savior is a hopeful sign that people are discovering **the way of peace**.

## The church pleads with its everlasting Redeemer (verses 15–19)

The people ask the Lord to look down upon the desolate condition of the nation he once specially favored.

Would it not glorify his name to remove **the veil from their hearts** and restore the tribes of his inheritance?

The Babylonian captivity and the return that followed were **shadows of the greater events** predicted here.

God looks upon his people with tenderness and mercy.

**Spiritual judgments** are more frightening than any outward disaster.

We should avoid the sins that justly cause the Lord to leave people to themselves and to the deceiver.

**“Our Redeemer from everlasting”** is his name.

His people have always recognized him as the God to whom they could appeal.

The Lord will hear those who belong to him.

He will deliver them from oppression by those who do not bear his name.

## Editorial review — Isaiah 63

**Whole original-English secondary witness read:** [CCEL, Isaiah 63](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xxiv.lxiii.html). Published `MHCC 0.1.1` remains authoritative for native-SWORD key/reference and independent comparison.

- **1–6:** Messiah victorious over **Edom as type**, no weariness, **Revelation 14:19; 19:13** winepress, **first coming in atoning weakness vs second in might**, judgment as church's rescue, call to reconciliation — retained.
- **7–14:** Henry's **interpretation of prayers at Jewish conversion**, Son as Angel/Messenger of love, redeemed people **resisting Spirit/prophets and rejecting Christ**, Spirit blessing ministers, renewed hope — retained.
- **15–19:** prayer for veil removed and tribes restored, Babylon events **as shadows**, **spiritual judgment worse than temporal**, appeal to everlasting Redeemer — retained.

**Secondary source textual anomaly:** CCEL's phrase **“prayers of the Jews on their conversation”** probably means *conversion*, but this is not silently corrected in original MHCC. The modern manuscript expressly attributes the reading to Henry's interpretation; primary-native collation remains pending.
