## Zedekiah's final defeat (verses 1–11)

Of all the consequences of sin, we should pray most urgently against being **cast away from God's presence** (**Psalm 51:11**).

No one is finally removed from his favor without first turning away through sin.

**Zedekiah** fled, but his escape failed.

There is no escape from divine judgment.

Judgment catches up with the sinner wherever that person runs.

## Jerusalem and its temple are destroyed (verses 12–23)

The **Chaldean army** devastated Jerusalem.

The chapter pays particular attention to **the removal of the temple's sacred articles**.

Remembering their beauty and value helps us understand **how serious the people's sin was**, since that sin brought about such devastation.

## Judah's leaders are carried away (verses 24–30)

The leaders of the Jews had led the people astray.

Now those leaders themselves became especially visible examples of **the Lord's righteous judgment**.

The narrative also records **two earlier deportations**.

This people repeatedly became a remarkable example of **both divine judgment and divine mercy**.

## Jehoiachin is raised from prison (verses 31–34)

For the fuller historical account of **Jehoiachin's release and promotion**, see **2 Kings 25:27–30**.

Those living under oppression may take encouragement that **waiting quietly for the Lord's salvation is not in vain**.

Our times are in God's hand.

So are the hearts of everyone with whom we have dealings.

May we grow increasingly able to **rest upon the Rock of Ages**.

May we look forward in holy faith to the hour when God restores Zion and overthrows every enemy of his church.
