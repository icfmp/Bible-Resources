# 1 Timothy 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The duty of Christians towards believing, as well as other masters. | 1–5 |
| The advantage of godliness with contentment. | 6–10 |
| A solemn charge to Timothy to be faithful. | 11–16 |
| The apostle repeats his warning to the rich, and closes with a blessing. | 17–21 |

## Respect lawful authority and reject teaching driven by greed (verses 1–5)

Christians should not imagine that greater religious knowledge or privilege gives them permission to **despise unbelieving masters**, disregard lawful duties or expose employers' faults maliciously.

Those whose masters also believe in Christ must not withhold proper respect merely because they share equal spiritual privileges.

Instead, their common faith should move them to **serve with greater diligence and goodwill**.

The only teaching to which we must give unconditional agreement is **the sound teaching of our Lord Jesus Christ**.

People who know very little are often the proudest, precisely because **they do not know themselves well**.

Pride leads to envy, quarrels, insults, hostile suspicions and useless disputes.

These arguments may sound clever but contain no solid spiritual truth.

They arise in minds corrupted by sinful desires, ignorant of the gospel's transforming power and motivated by **worldly advantage**.

## Godliness joined with contentment is great gain (verses 6–10)

Those who turn Christianity into a business for their own worldly advancement will eventually be disappointed.

But those who make godliness their true calling can rely upon God's promises **for this life and the life to come**.

Genuine believers can know their final happiness is secure.

When they are also **content with their present circumstances**, they already have enough.

Even in the greatest poverty, we cannot own less than we possessed **when we were born**.

At death, even the richest person can finally carry away little more than **a burial shroud, coffin and grave**.

Nature can be satisfied with relatively little.

If our natural needs can be content with a little, **grace should be content with even less**.

The necessary things of life should limit the Christian's ambitions.

With food and clothing, we should seek to remain thankful.

Consider the danger of greed.

Paul does not condemn everyone who **is rich**.

He warns those who **are determined to become rich**—people who place their happiness in wealth and pursue it as their highest goal.

Such people expose themselves to the devil's temptations.

They may turn to **dishonest methods** to increase their possessions.

They may become so overwhelmed by business that they have no time or desire left for spiritual duties.

They may enter relationships that draw them into wrongdoing and folly.

**What sin will love of money not draw people toward?**

Someone may possess a great deal of money **without loving it**.

But where money is loved as an idol, it drives the person toward every kind of evil.

Different sins and vices grow out of this root in different ways.

We can see examples everywhere, especially in periods of wealth, luxury and careless religious profession.

## Fight the good fight and live for eternal things (verses 11–16)

No one should set the heart upon earthly possessions, but such attachment is particularly inappropriate in a **servant of God**.

Those who belong to the Lord should concentrate upon **his purposes and commands**.

Christian life involves genuine conflict with **corruption, temptation and the powers of darkness**.

**Eternal life** is the crown offered to strengthen our determination.

We are called to **take hold of it**.

Those who possess much wealth especially need clear instruction about **its dangers and proper uses**.

But a minister cannot warn them sincerely while secretly craving the very pleasures their money can purchase.

Christ's return is certain.

**Its exact time is not ours to know**.

The radiance of God's glory is more than mortal eyes can bear.

We can approach him only as he has made himself known to sinners **in and through Jesus Christ**.

Henry explains that this passage adores **the divine nature without distinguishing the Persons**, because its statements are true of **Father, Son and Holy Spirit**.

God has made himself known to us **through the human nature of Jesus**, the Father's only-begotten Son.

## Be rich in good works, not merely possessions (verses 17–21)

Being wealthy in this world is entirely different from being **rich toward God**.

Few things are more uncertain than earthly wealth.

Rich people should recognize that **God is the One who gives wealth**.

Only he can also give them the heart to **enjoy it rightly**.

Many people own much but obtain little true comfort from it because they cannot use it wisely or generously.

What good is a large estate except as an opportunity **to do more good**?

Let genuine faith in Christ show itself through **loving actions and generosity**.

Take hold of eternal life rather than following the self-indulgent, greedy and ungodly, who will eventually **lift up their eyes in torment**.

Knowledge that contradicts **the truth of Christ's gospel** is not genuine wisdom.

If it were true knowledge, it would recognize and welcome gospel truth.

People who elevate **human speculation above faith** risk abandoning the faith altogether.

Grace contains every spiritual good.

**Grace is the beginning and pledge of glory**.

Where God genuinely gives his grace, he will also bring the person into **glory**.
