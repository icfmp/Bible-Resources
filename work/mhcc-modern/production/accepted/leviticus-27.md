# Leviticus 27 — Modern English

## Vows concerning persons and animals (verses 1–13)

Zeal for God's service sometimes led Israelites to dedicate themselves or their children to lifelong work in the Lord's house.

Some people could actually serve as assistants there.

In general, however, those dedicated were to be redeemed by paying a stated value.

It is good to be eager and generous in serving God.

Yet we should carefully consider the obligations we take on.

Wise judgment must guide our decisions.

Rash promises followed by reluctance to fulfill them dishonor God and disturb our own peace of mind.

## Vows concerning houses and land (verses 14–25)

Our homes, land, animals and all our possessions should be used to bring glory to God.

He welcomes gifts that support his worship and advance his cause.

But the Lord does **not** approve of reckless generosity that leaves a person's family ruined.

Our zeal must be guided by care for those entrusted to us.

## What has been fully devoted to the Lord (verses 26–33)

The law distinguishes things that were **devoted** to God from those that were merely **consecrated**.

Fully devoted things were most holy and could not be taken back or used for another purpose.

Israel was also commanded to honor God with a tenth of the produce from which it benefited, wherever that produce could be given.

In doing this, the people acknowledged him as **the owner of their land** and **the giver of its fruit**.

They confessed that they were his tenants, depending on him for everything.

They expressed gratitude for abundance already received and asked for his continuing favor.

We too are taught to honor the Lord through our possessions.

## The closing word of Leviticus (verse 34)

The final verse appears to refer to the entire book.

Many of these instructions are moral laws that remain binding.

Others are ceremonial regulations given specifically to Israel.

Yet even those ceremonies have a spiritual meaning from which Christians can learn.

Through them, the gospel was proclaimed to Israel and, in this sense, to us as well (Hebrews 4:2).

The teaching that sinners are reconciled to God through a Mediator is not made clearer by the smoke of burning sacrifices.

It is made clear through knowing Jesus Christ and him crucified.

We now live under the joyful and gentle ordinances of the gospel.

True worshipers approach the Father **in spirit and truth**, through Christ alone and in his name.

Nevertheless, freedom from ceremonial offerings does not mean that honoring God calls for only a little thought, time or expense.

Because Jesus' blood gives us boldness to enter the Most Holy Place, let us draw near with sincere hearts and the full assurance of faith.

May we worship with greater joy and humble confidence, continually saying:

**Blessed be God for Jesus Christ!**
