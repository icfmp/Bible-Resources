# Jeremiah 32 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jeremiah buys a field. | 1–15 |
| The prophet's prayer. | 16–25 |
| God declares that he will give up his people, but promises to restore them. | 26–44 |

## Jeremiah buys land during the siege (verses 1–15)

Jeremiah was imprisoned because of his prophecies, yet he **purchased a field**.

That purchase was a sign.

Although Jerusalem was under siege and the land seemed about to be ruined, a time would return when people again **bought and possessed houses, fields and vineyards**.

Those who preach God's promises should demonstrate **that they themselves believe what they teach**.

Even our ordinary financial dealings and everyday business should be handled **in faith**, with attention to **God's Providence and his promises**.

## Jeremiah prays to the sovereign and just God (verses 16–25)

The prophet worshiped the Lord for **his infinite perfection**.

Whenever we struggle to understand a particular way Providence is working, we should return to **the truths that are most certain**.

God is **the source of all existence, power and life**.

Nothing presents a difficulty too great for him to overcome.

He is **a God of unlimited mercy**.

He is also **a God of uncompromising justice**.

He directs everything according to what is best.

Jeremiah acknowledged God's righteousness in bringing judgment upon his people.

Whatever our situation, whether a **personal trial or a public calamity**, the Lord sees it and knows how to remedy it.

We should never quarrel with **God's right to act**.

But we may rightly ask to understand **what his actions mean**.

## God's judgment and restoration both display his purposes (verses 26–44)

The Lord's answer revealed both **his purposes of judgment against that generation** and **his purposes of mercy toward generations still to come**.

**Nothing but sin was responsible for the people's ruin**.

Yet God promised the restoration of Judah and Jerusalem.

The people had finally fallen into despair.

The Lord answered by announcing **mercy reserved for their future**.

His promises are equally sure for everyone who truly believes.

He will acknowledge them as **his people** and show himself to be **their God**.

He will give them **a heart that fears him**.

All true Christians will possess a disposition to **love one another**.

They may differ in their views about lesser questions.

But they will be united in the great truths of God: **the evil of sin; the low condition of fallen human beings; salvation through Jesus Christ; true holiness; the world's emptiness; and the importance of eternal things**.

Those whom God loves, **he loves to the end**.

We have no reason to distrust his faithfulness or constancy.

We have every reason to be cautious about **our own hearts**.

He would settle the people again in Canaan.

Jeremiah's purchase was a pledge that many future purchases would follow the exile.

But those restored fields only faintly picture **the inheritance reserved in the heavenly Canaan** for people who fear God and persevere with him.

Let us therefore endure our trials, confident that he will accomplish **all the good he has promised**.
