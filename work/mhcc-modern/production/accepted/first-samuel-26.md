# 1 Samuel 26 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Saul goes after David, who again spares Saul's life. | 1–12 |
| David exhorts Saul. | 13–20 |
| Saul acknowledges his sin. | 21–25 |

## David spares Saul again (verses 1–12)

How quickly an ungodly heart loses the good impressions once made by conviction!

Saul and all his men were completely helpless.

They might as well have been disarmed and chained, yet their only limitation was that God had put them into a deep sleep.

How easily the Lord can weaken the strongest, confuse the wisest and frustrate the most watchful!

David remained determined to wait until God himself thought it right to avenge him.

He would never seize the promised kingdom through sinful methods.

The temptation to kill Saul was powerful.

But David knew that giving in would be a sin against God.

He therefore refused it and entrusted the outcome to the Lord.

## David speaks with Saul (verses 13–20)

David reasoned earnestly and affectionately with the king.

People who prevent us from attending God's appointed worship are doing everything they can to estrange us from God and make us live as heathens.

Anything that exposes us to sin should be counted among the greatest wrongs another person can do us.

David suggested that if the Lord had stirred Saul against him as a judgment on either of them, they should both seek reconciliation.

If an evil spirit permitted by God was troubling Saul, the answer was still to seek peace with God.

David therefore urged a sacrifice and reconciliation.

## Saul acknowledges wrong but does not repent (verses 21–25)

Saul again spoke pleasant words and expressed good wishes.

Yet he gave no evidence of genuine repentance toward God.

David and Saul parted for the last time.

No peace between human beings is secure unless it rests upon **peace with God through Jesus Christ**.

People who sin against God are acting foolishly and making serious mistakes.

Many glimpse this truth for a moment, then deliberately close their eyes to it.

Fair promises do not make someone trustworthy after a long pattern of knowingly disobeying God's light.

Nevertheless, the admission of wrongdoing by a stubborn sinner can assure believers that they are acting rightly and encourage them to keep going.

Their true reward must be expected from the Lord alone.
