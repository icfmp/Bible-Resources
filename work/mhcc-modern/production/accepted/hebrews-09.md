# Hebrews 9 — Modern English

## The tabernacle points to Christ (verses 1–5)

The writer explains how Israel's ceremonies **prefigured Christ**.

The tabernacle was a movable temple. It pictured both the church's temporary condition on earth and **Jesus Christ's human nature**, in which the fullness of deity truly dwelt.

Its furnishings and ordinances pointed to Jesus as the **Light and Bread of life**, and to his divine person, holy priesthood, perfect righteousness and prevailing intercession.

From the beginning, **Christ was all in all**.

Read in the light of the gospel, the ceremonial objects reveal God's wisdom and strengthen faith in the One they represented.

## The limitations of the old sanctuary (verses 6–10)

Christ, our High Priest, first shed his blood before entering heaven.

Likewise, no believer can enter **God's gracious presence now or his glorious presence hereafter** except through Jesus' blood.

Sins are deep errors of heart, understanding and conduct. Who can recognize all their own wrongs?

Their guilt cannot be washed away except through Christ's atonement.

We plead his blood here on earth while **he pleads for us in heaven**.

Some Israelites, instructed by God, understood something of communion with him through the promised Redeemer. But many saw no further than the external rites.

The ceremonial forms could neither remove the pollution and dominion of sin nor settle the worshiper's **guilt and inward doubts**.

The gospel age should therefore be an age of **reformation**, clearer knowledge and greater love, with ill will toward none and goodwill toward all.

Greater freedom in spiritual life and expression brings **greater responsibility for holy conduct**.

## Christ enters heaven through his own blood (verses 11–14)

Every true spiritual blessing—past, present or future—rests upon **Christ's priestly work**.

Our High Priest entered heaven once for all, having obtained **eternal redemption**.

The Holy Spirit showed that older sacrifices could remove **ceremonial uncleanness from the outward person**, granting certain outward privileges.

Their power did not extend to the conscience.

Christ's blood has an infinitely greater effect because **he offered himself without any sinful stain in his nature or life**.

His sacrifice cleanses even deeply guilty consciences from dead and sinful works, so that we may **serve the living God**.

Just as contact with a corpse made an Israelite outwardly unclean, sinful works defile the soul.

The same grace that seals forgiveness **recreates and renews the defiled person**.

Nothing weakens gospel faith more than undermining **the direct saving power of Christ's blood**.

We cannot measure the depth of his sacrifice or fully comprehend its wisdom, love and grace. But faith finds **life, food and refreshment** in contemplating it.

## The testament and the death that makes it effective (verses 15–22)

Scripture uses both **covenant** and **testament** for God's solemn dealings with humanity.

A testament is the willing disposition of an inheritance to those specified, taking effect through the testator's death.

Christ therefore died not only **to purchase salvation's blessings** but also **to establish their distribution** to his people.

Through sin, all became guilty before God and forfeited every good thing.

God nevertheless proclaimed a **covenant of grace** to display the greatness of his mercy.

Not even a sinner's religious duties can be clean unless guilt is removed through an **adequate sacrifice**, upon which the worshiper continually depends.

All truly good works owe their existence to this all-sufficient cause.

We should present our spiritual offerings as **sprinkled and purified by Christ's blood**.

## His one sacrifice and promised return (verses 23–28)

The sacrifices of Christ are infinitely superior to those prescribed by the law.

The older sacrifices could neither secure pardon from sin nor give power to overcome it.

By **one sacrifice**, Jesus has destroyed the devil's works, so that believers may become **righteous, holy and happy**.

No wisdom, education, virtue, wealth or power can protect human beings from death.

Likewise, only Christ's **atoning sacrifice** can rescue sinners from condemnation on the day of judgment.

Those who deliberately neglect or despise this salvation cannot expect to escape eternal judgment.

The believer knows **the Redeemer lives** and trusts that they will see him.

This is the patient hope of the church, expressed in the prayer: **“Come, Lord Jesus.”**
