# Judges 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Praise and glory ascribed to God. | 1–5 |
| The distress and deliverance of Israel. | 6–11 |
| Some commended, others censured. | 12–23 |
| Sisera's mother disappointed. | 24–31 |

## Deborah and Barak praise the Lord (verses 1–5)

We should waste no time thanking the Lord for his mercies.

Praise is most acceptable to him and most helpful to us when it rises from a full heart.

Thanksgiving strengthens and deepens believers' love and gratitude.

It also makes God's wonderful acts more widely known and remembered for longer.

Whatever Deborah, Barak or the army had accomplished, **the Lord must receive all the praise**.

The will to act, the power to perform and the success achieved all came from him.

## Israel's suffering and deliverance (verses 6–11)

Deborah described Israel's misery under Jabin so that its deliverance would appear all the more gracious.

She identified the cause of the suffering: idolatry.

Israel had chosen new gods with new names.

But behind those images, Henry says, the people were in fact serving Satan.

Deborah had been a mother to Israel by diligently seeking the people's spiritual welfare.

She called everyone who benefited from their great deliverance to thank the Lord.

Those who regained their freedom and position should praise God for that restoration.

The Lord himself accomplished it.

In judging his enemies, he also displayed his righteousness.

During persecution, attending the worship God has appointed may place a believer's life in danger.

Satan always tries to keep believers from approaching the throne of grace, the fountain of life's water.

Notice also God's tenderness toward frightened people.

His glory includes protecting those most exposed to danger and helping the weakest.

Let us value public peace, especially the safety enjoyed by those living in villages, and thank God for it.

## Courage commended; indifference condemned (verses 12–23)

Deborah called upon her own soul to awaken and praise God.

Anyone who hopes to kindle love for Christ in others must first be burning with that love.

Praise is a task to which we should awaken both ourselves and our affections.

She described three groups: those who fought against Israel, those who fought for Israel, and those who kept away.

The enemies were stubborn and dangerous opponents of God's people.

The tribes that fought for Israel deserved honor.

God must receive the highest praise, but the people who serve him well should also be acknowledged, so others will be encouraged.

Indeed, the whole creation is against those who have made God their enemy.

The River Kishon fought against Sisera's forces.

Usually it was shallow, but Henry considers it likely that heavy rain had made it deep and powerful, drowning those who attempted to cross.

Deborah herself was earnestly engaged in the struggle.

When our souls become deeply involved in worship and holy duties, God's grace helps us overcome spiritual enemies.

Other tribes failed to help when their aid was needed.

Many people are held back from duty by fear of trouble, love of ease, and excessive concern for business or wealth.

Selfish people scarcely care what happens to the church so long as they can earn, keep and save money.

All seek their own interests (Philippians 2:21).

Those reluctant to serve can always find excuses for staying at home when an undertaking is dangerous or difficult.

But we cannot remain neutral in the conflict between the Lord and his enemies.

Even though God needs no human help, he accepts the service of those who employ their gifts in his cause, and he calls everyone to do so.

## Jael is praised; Sisera's mother waits in vain (verses 24–31)

Jael received a special blessing.

People whose lives are spent in a small or humble setting will not lose their reward if they faithfully serve God with the gifts and opportunities they have.

Sisera's mother waited confidently for her son's return.

She had no thought that his campaign could have failed.

Her anxious hopes concerned worldly triumph and the display of greatness.

We should beware of setting our hearts on temporal advantages, especially those that feed pride and empty glory.

What a picture her wishes give of a heart ruled by worldly appetite!

The old mother's hopes and the attendants' speculations were foolish and shameful.

God can bring ruin upon his enemies precisely when they feel most confident and proud.

Deborah ended by praying for the defeat of all God's enemies and the well-being of everyone who loves him.

Those who sincerely love the Lord will finally shine like the sun in all its brightness.
