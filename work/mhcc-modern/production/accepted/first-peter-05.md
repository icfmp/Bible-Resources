# 1 Peter 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elders exhorted and encouraged. | 1–4 |
| Younger Christians are to submit to their elders, and to yield with humility and patience to God, and to be sober, watchful, and stedfast in faith. | 5–9 |
| Prayers for their growth and establishment. | 10–14 |

## Shepherd God's flock under Christ the Chief Shepherd (verses 1–4)

Peter **exhorted rather than commanded** the elders.

He did not claim the right to rule all pastors and churches.

Peter and a small number of others uniquely witnessed **Christ's sufferings**. But all true believers share the promised glory when Christ appears.

The scattered, poor and afflicted Christians were **God's own flock**.

Their redemption came through **the great Shepherd** and they were called to live in holy love and fellowship according to God's will.

They are also called **God's heritage or clergy**, meaning God's chosen and special possession, set apart for his service and favor.

**Christ is the Chief Shepherd of the entire flock**.

Faithful ministers will receive a crown of **unfading glory**, worth infinitely more than earthly influence, possessions or pleasure.

## Humble yourselves, cast your cares on God and resist the devil (verses 5–9)

Humility preserves peace and good order in churches and societies. Pride disturbs them.

When God grants humility he also supplies wisdom, faith and holiness.

Submitting to our reconciled God brings greater comfort than selfish ambition.

His help comes **in his own wise time**, not always according to the timetable we imagine.

He waits patiently. Can we not wait for him?

Confidence in his wisdom, power and goodness helps us endure every difficulty.

Cast upon him **every concern**: personal and family matters, troubles of the present and anxiety about the future, concern for ourselves, others and the whole church.

These cares become deeply harmful when they arise from unbelief, torment the mind, prevent us from doing our duty and steal the joy of serving God.

The remedy is to leave outcomes to God's wise and gracious ordering.

**Trust in his good purposes calms the soul**.

All lasting spiritual consolation comes from God and the Holy Spirit.

Satan continually seeks **to destroy souls**, laying snares to lead them toward eternal ruin.

We must be **sober** in outward and inward life, governing ourselves by temperance.

We must be **alert**, aware of spiritual danger and watching against his schemes.

Be steadfast through faith.

No one can fight securely on swampy ground. Faith places the soul upon **the solid ground of God's promises**.

Remember that believers throughout the world experience the same kinds of suffering. Their trials should strengthen our patience in our own.

## Seek the God of all grace and stand firm in his peace (verses 10–14)

Peter prays to **the God of all grace**.

His four petitions describe growth:

**Perfect** asks that believers advance toward spiritual maturity.

**Establish** asks that their instability be healed.

**Strengthen** asks that the graces, especially the weakest, increase.

**Settle** asks that they rest upon **a secure foundation**, Christ himself.

Perseverance and growth in grace should therefore be among every Christian's first concerns.

Christian teaching proves its reality through **the change it makes in the heart and the fruit it produces in life**.

Genuine brotherly love is not merely polite words. It is **Christ's recognizable mark** upon his disciples.

Wicked people sometimes enjoy a temporary false peace, but their hope is empty.

All lasting peace is **founded on Jesus Christ and flows from him**.
