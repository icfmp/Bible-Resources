# Proverbs 30 — Modern English

## Verses 1–6 — Agur's humility and God's sufficient word

Agur describes himself as lacking righteousness and having acted foolishly. We should likewise think humbly about ourselves.

He says he needs **revelation to guide him** into truth and wisdom. The more understanding people gain, the more clearly they see how much they do not know. They become eager to discover still more of God and the riches of **his grace in Jesus Christ**.

Henry understands **verse 4** as a prophetic reference to the One who descended from heaven to become our **Teacher and Savior**, then ascended to heaven as our **Advocate**.

The Messiah is presented here, in Henry's reading, as a **Person distinct from the Father**, although his name remained hidden at that time.

Even the Redeemer's wonderful works of providence and grace cannot be completely searched out. Without Christ, Henry says, the foundations of the earth would have sunk beneath the curse brought upon creation through human sin.

Who is the mighty One accomplishing all this?

God's word lacks nothing necessary to fulfill its purpose. **Adding to it opens the way for error and corruption**.

## Verses 7–9 — Neither poverty nor riches

Agur wisely asks to be kept in a middle condition, away from the temptations associated with both poverty and wealth.

He prays for **daily bread** appropriate to his circumstances, family and genuine welfare. His prayer strikingly resembles parts of **the Lord's Prayer**.

If God removes vanity and lies from us, grants us a share in Christ's pardoning love, becomes our portion and teaches us to walk with him, we have far more than everything we need spiritually.

People who possess abundance are tempted to abuse it. Poverty also brings distress and temptation.

That is why Agur's prayer is wise, although seldom offered sincerely.

What is appropriate provision for one person or family may not be appropriate for another. We can nevertheless trust our heavenly Father to provide **whatever is genuinely good for us**.

Why should we desire more than that?

## Verse 10 — Do not slander a servant

Do not maliciously accuse a servant to an employer, even in relatively small matters, in order to cause trouble.

## Verses 11–14 — Four kinds of deeply corrupt people

Every age contains people who repay their parents' care with monstrous ingratitude.

Others persuade themselves they are holy while secretly practicing wickedness and cherishing sinful hearts.

Still others display outrageous **pride**.

And in every age there have been people of exceptional **cruelty**.

## Verses 15–17 — Greed, cruelty and contempt for parents

Cruelty and covetousness are like **the two daughters of the leech**, endlessly demanding, **“Give, give!”**

Such passions never give their owners peace. They resemble four things that can never be satisfied.

People who continually crave more wealth are never truly rich.

Many who came to a terrible end later acknowledged that their wicked course began when they **despised their parents' authority**.

## Verses 18–20 — Wonders of nature and the hidden ways of sin

Four things lie beyond complete human understanding.

Nature itself is full of astonishing mysteries.

But Henry describes the fourth example as **a mystery of iniquity**: the tactics through which a corrupt seducer wins a woman's affection and those through which an immoral woman conceals her misconduct.

His comments here concern the hidden tactics of wrongdoing, not a claim that every relationship or woman resembles these examples.

## Verses 21–23 — Four kinds of troublesome people

Henry describes four types of people whose behavior causes considerable disturbance.

The first is someone who rises from humble beginnings to authority but possesses **a base and tyrannical spirit**. The problem is not poverty or low birth, but the abuse of power.

The second is a foolish, violent person given over to excess.

The third is a woman with a contentious spirit and corrupt habits.

The fourth is a servant who gains improper influence and abuses it.

Those whom Providence raises from humble circumstances should particularly guard against temptations that could corrupt their new position.

## Verses 24–28 — Wisdom learned from small creatures

Four small creatures deserve our admiration for their wisdom.

They remind us that people who are poor, obscure and considered of little importance in the present world may nevertheless be **wise concerning their own souls and the life to come**.

## Verses 29–33 — Self-government and peace

We can learn appropriate conduct even from animals.

We should learn particularly **to govern our temper** when others provoke us.

An evil thought must not be allowed to become evil speech. Nor should we stir up anger in other people.

Let us act and speak without violence, showing gentleness and calmness.

How often we have acted foolishly in rebellion against **the Lord our King**!

Let us humble ourselves before him.

Having found peace with God, let us pursue **peace with everyone**.
