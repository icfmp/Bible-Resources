# Ecclesiastes 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A description of the infirmities of age. | 1–7 |
| All is vanity: also a warning of the judgment to come. | 8–14 |

## Remember your Creator before old age (verses 1–7)

Remember the sins you have committed against your Creator.

Repent and seek his forgiveness.

Remember also the duties you owe him and begin fulfilling them, looking to him for grace and strength.

Do this **early in life**, when the body is strong and the mind active.

Someone who reaches old age with the painful memory of a wasted life may finally abandon sinful pleasure only because it has become impossible to enjoy.

When such a person says, **“I no longer find pleasure in these things,”** sincerity in renouncing sin can reasonably be questioned.

Solomon then gives a **figurative description** of old age and its infirmities.

Some details of the figures are difficult to interpret.

But their central meaning is clear: the ordinary disabilities of old age often bring great discomfort.

Henry reads **verses 2–5** as images of weaknesses commonly accompanying advancing age, and **verse 6** as an image of what happens at the hour of death.

If sin had not entered the world, these weaknesses would not have appeared.

People who are old therefore have special reason to reflect upon **the evil of sin**.

## Vanity, true religion and final judgment (verses 8–14)

Solomon repeats the central statement of his preaching:

**“Vanity of vanities! All is vanity!”**

He speaks as someone who has paid dearly to learn how empty the world is.

Earthly possessions and accomplishments cannot free people from **the burden of sin**.

Because Solomon understood the worth of souls, he considered carefully the words he spoke and wrote.

Truthful words will always be fitting and valuable.

God's truths resemble **goads**, urging on those who grow lazy and shrink back, and **nails**, holding steady those who wander or become unstable.

They give the heart firmness so that we neither neglect our responsibilities nor abandon them under pressure.

**The Shepherd of Israel** gives inspired wisdom.

Every genuine teacher and guide receives instruction from him.

Scripture also applies the title **Shepherd** to **the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God**.

The prophets searched diligently to understand the time and circumstances of **the sufferings of Christ and the glory that would follow**, as the Spirit of Christ testified through them.

Writing many books is tiring, and human life is short.

In Solomon's time producing and reading books required still greater effort than today.

But all our studies would end in vanity if they did not bring us to this conclusion:

**Fear God and keep his commandments: this is the whole duty of humanity.**

The fear of God includes all the affections directed toward him through **the Holy Spirit's work**.

Terror may exist without love, and may even be accompanied by hatred.

But **the gracious fear of God** resembles the reverence of a loving child.

It can stand for the whole of true religion within the heart and for the obedience that flows from it.

Let us pursue **the one necessary thing**.

Come to Christ now as **a merciful Savior**, for he will soon appear as **the almighty Judge**, bringing to light everything hidden and exposing the intentions of every heart.

Why has God told us so plainly that all is vanity?

So that we will not deceive ourselves and destroy our souls.

God has made our duty our true good.

May his command be written within every heart:

**Fear God and keep his commandments, for this concerns every human being.**
