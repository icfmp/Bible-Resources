# Exodus 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God directs the Israelites to Pihahiroth, Pharaoh pursues after them. | 1–9 |
| The Israelites murmur, Moses comforts them. | 10–14 |
| God instructs Moses, The cloud between the Israelites and the Egyptians. | 15–20 |
| The Israelites pass through the Red sea, which drowns the Egyptians. | 21–31 |

## Pharaoh pursues Israel (verses 1–9)

Pharaoh expected the Israelites to become trapped in the wilderness, where they would be easy prey.

But God declared that he would gain honor through what happened to Pharaoh.

Everyone was made to honor the Creator. Those who refuse to honor him by willing obedience will nevertheless become occasions for the display of his justice and power.

What appears to threaten the church with destruction is often overruled by God to bring ruin upon the church's enemies.

Pharaoh pursued his malice and revenge, but in doing so he advanced the very purpose God had declared for him.

Although he had every reason to release Israel, he now regretted having done so.

God can turn the envy and rage people direct against his people into torment for those very enemies.

Those who turn toward heaven and seek to live godly lives in Christ Jesus must expect Satan to attack them with temptation and fear.

He does not willingly let anyone leave his service.

## Israel fears; Moses calls the people to trust (verses 10–14)

No way was open for Israel except upward—to God. Their deliverance came from him.

We may be walking in obedience, following God toward heaven, and still find ourselves surrounded by trouble.

Some Israelites cried out to the Lord. Fear drove them to pray, and that was good.

God sometimes brings us into a desperate situation so that we will fall on our knees.

Others shouted against Moses. Their fear led them to complain, as though God could no longer perform miracles.

They blamed Moses for bringing them out of Egypt. In effect, they were angry at God for the greatest kindness he had ever shown them.

Unbelief can be utterly unreasonable.

Moses told them not to fear.

When we cannot immediately escape trouble, it is still our duty and in our interest to rise above fear.

Let trouble strengthen our prayers and our efforts, not silence our faith and hope.

“Stand still” did not mean they should save themselves by fighting or fleeing. They were to wait for God's instructions and obey them.

They could quiet their minds through confidence in him and consider calmly the great salvation he was about to accomplish.

If God brings his people into a place where they can see no way out, he can still make a way for them.

## The cloud protects Israel (verses 15–20)

Moses' silent prayers of faith accomplished more before God than Israel's loud cries of fear.

The pillar of cloud and fire moved behind the Israelites, where they needed protection, and became a wall between them and their enemies.

God's word and providence have a dark and threatening side toward sin and sinners, but a bright and gracious side toward his own people.

The God who first separated light from darkness (Genesis 1:4) now assigned darkness to Egypt and light to Israel.

There will be a still greater separation between the inheritance of God's people in light and the complete darkness reserved forever for hypocrites.

## Israel crosses the sea; Egypt's army is destroyed (verses 21–31)

The division of the Red Sea terrified the Canaanites (Joshua 2:9).

For Israel it became a source of praise and triumph (Psalms 114:3; 106:9; 136:13).

It also foreshadowed baptism (1 Corinthians 10:1–2).

Israel's passage through the water pictured the conversion of souls (Isaiah 11:15), while the drowning of the Egyptians pictured the final destruction of all unrepentant sinners.

God displayed his almighty power by opening a path through waters extending for miles.

He can lead his people through the greatest difficulties and create a way where none exists.

Israel walked across on dry ground in the middle of the sea.

God did this to encourage his people in every age to trust him in their greatest distress.

What could be impossible for the One who accomplished this?

What will he not do for those who fear and love him, since he did so much even for these complaining, unbelieving Israelites?

Then God's just anger fell upon his enemies and the enemies of his people.

The sinners' own presumption brought them to ruin.

They could have left Israel alone but would not. When they finally wished to flee, they could not.

People often refuse to believe that attacking God's people harms themselves until it is too late.

Moses stretched out his hand at God's command, and the waters returned.

Pharaoh and his servants, who had encouraged one another to harden their hearts, fell together. None of the pursuing force escaped.

The Israelites saw the Egyptians lying dead on the shore, and the sight deeply affected them.

When people see God's works and experience his goodness, they fear and trust him.

How much better it would be if we always lived with the same faith we sometimes show!

Consider the end toward which a Christian may look.

Our spiritual enemies may rage with great strength. Yet while we hold fast to God, we will pass safely through the waves under the protection of the same Savior whose power will defeat those enemies.

The enemies of our souls whom we see today, we will never see again.
