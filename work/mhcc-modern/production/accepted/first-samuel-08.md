# 1 Samuel 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The evil government of Samuel's sons. | 1–3 |
| The Israelites ask for a king. | 4–9 |
| The manner of a king. | 10–22 |

## Samuel's sons govern corruptly (verses 1–3)

Samuel's sons do not seem to have been as openly profane and immoral as Eli's sons.

Yet they were corrupt judges.

They pursued dishonest gain and accepted bribes.

Samuel himself had not done so.

His sons, however, allowed bribes to distort their judgments.

The people's suffering was worsened by the threat of invasion from **Nahash, king of the Ammonites**.

## Israel asks Samuel for a king (verses 4–9)

Samuel was displeased by the demand for a king.

He could patiently bear criticism directed at himself or his family.

But the people's words, **“Give us a king to judge us,”** reflected upon God, and that greatly troubled him.

Samuel brought his distress before the Lord in prayer.

When something upsets us, it is both our duty and our benefit to tell God about it.

The Lord instructed Samuel to grant the people's request.

That did not mean God approved of it.

Sometimes God opposes our desires **out of love**.

At other times, he gives us what we demand **as an act of judgment**.

That is what happened here.

He knows how to bring glory to himself and accomplish his wise plans even through human foolishness.

## Samuel warns Israel what a king may demand (verses 10–22)

If the Israelites wanted a king who ruled like the rulers of surrounding eastern nations, they would discover how heavy that form of government could become.

Those who place themselves under the rule of **the world and sinful desires** also need to know how severe those masters can be.

Sin is a tyrant.

God's law and ordinary human conduct differ greatly.

**God's law should govern how we treat others.**

But **what people usually do** may teach us what disappointments to expect from them.

Israel would complain under the new system, but God would not immediately answer those complaints.

When we create suffering for ourselves through sinful wishes and schemes, we may forfeit the comfort of prayer and the benefit of God's help.

The people were stubborn and insistent.

Rash wishes and sudden decisions may create problems requiring long, painful repentance.

It is wise to be thankful for the advantages of whatever government we live under and patient with its disadvantages.

We should pray continually for our rulers, asking that they govern with reverence for God and that we may live peaceful, godly and honest lives.

It is a hopeful sign when our desires for worldly things can tolerate delay and when we are willing to entrust both the **timing and manner** of their fulfillment to God's providence.
