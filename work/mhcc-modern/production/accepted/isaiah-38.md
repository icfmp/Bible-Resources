# Isaiah 38 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Hezekiah's sickness and recovery. | 1–8 |
| His thanksgiving. | 9–22 |

## Hezekiah's illness and restoration (verses 1–8)

When we pray during sickness, God may not give us the same answer that he gave Hezekiah.

But if, through **his Spirit**, he strengthens our hearts, assures us that our sins are forgiven and teaches us that **whether we live or die we belong to him**, then our prayers have not been offered in vain.

For the earlier account, see **2 Kings 20:1–11**.

## Hezekiah thanks the Lord (verses 9–22)

Hezekiah recorded the condition in which he found himself before receiving mercy.

It is good to remember divine kindness, especially kindness received during sickness.

He feared he would no longer see the Lord in the land of the living.

A truly godly person does not seek life merely for its own pleasures, but so that **he may serve God and enjoy communion with him**.

Our present dwelling resembles **a shepherd's temporary hut**.

It is poor, cold and insecure; moreover, while we occupy it, we are responsible for work entrusted to us, like a shepherd tending a flock.

Our days resemble **a weaver's shuttle** (**Job 7:6**), passing quickly back and forth while each movement leaves a thread behind.

When the woven piece is complete, the Master cuts it from the loom and examines the finished work.

When a faithful person's life ends, burdens and toil end too, and rest begins.

But God determines **the length of the cloth**—the duration of each life.

During sickness, people often try to calculate how much time remains, but they cannot know.

Their main concern should be **reaching the next world in safety**.

The more deeply we experience God's kindness, the more we should love him and live for him.

It was through love for perishing sinners that **Christ delivered our souls**.

Forgiveness does not mean that wrongdoing becomes innocent. It means that sin is **not punished as it deserves**, because the Lord has provided mercy.

We may remember healing with special gratitude when we see it alongside God's pardon.

Hezekiah regarded every opportunity to glorify God as the business, delight and purpose of life.

After recovering, he resolved to increase his praise and service.

**God's promises never remove the obligation to use appropriate means**.

Instead, they encourage and strengthen us to employ them.

Life and health are given so that we may **glorify God and do good**.
