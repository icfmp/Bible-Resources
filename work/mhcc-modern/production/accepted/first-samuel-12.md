# 1 Samuel 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Samuel testifies his integrity. | 1–5 |
| Samuel reproves the people. | 6–15 |
| Thunder sent in harvest time. | 16–25 |

## Samuel bears witness to his integrity (verses 1–5)

Samuel not only cleared his own character; he also gave Saul an example and reminded the people how ungrateful they had been both to God and to Samuel.

Everyone owes a proper duty to a good reputation, particularly those serving in public offices.

They should protect it from unjust accusations and suspicions, so that they may complete their work with honor as well as joy.

When we know we have acted honestly in our responsibilities, that knowledge can comfort us even if others treat us with contempt or ingratitude.

## Samuel reminds Israel of God's justice (verses 6–15)

Ministers must reason with those they teach.

It is not enough simply to exhort people or give instructions.

They should seek to persuade, convince the mind and thus win both **the will and the affections**.

Samuel reminded Israel of the righteous works God had done for the people.

The Lord will enable those who continue to follow him faithfully to go on following him.

But disobedience would bring certain ruin upon Israel.

We are deeply mistaken if we suppose we can escape God's justice merely by refusing his authority.

Even if we declare that he will not rule us, **he will still judge us**.

## God sends thunder during the wheat harvest (verses 16–25)

At Samuel's word the Lord sent thunder and rain during the **wheat harvest**, a season when such weather was highly unusual in that country.

The storm occurred on a clear day.

Samuel also announced it in advance.

The unusual timing and prophecy together convinced Israel of the wrong it had done in demanding a king.

The people had expected more from human strength than from God's power or Samuel's prayers.

Could their new king command a storm as the prophet could through prayer?

They were deeply shaken.

Some sinners will not recognize their guilt until God confronts them through the storm and thunder of severe providence.

Israel begged Samuel to pray for the people.

Now they recognized their need of the man they had recently disregarded.

Likewise, people who refuse Christ's rule over their lives may still wish to have him intercede for them to turn away God's wrath.

Samuel's goal was to strengthen Israel's faith.

Anything we make into a god will eventually disappoint us.

Created things are good in their proper place, but they become empty substitutes when we put them in God's place.

It is sinful to abandon prayer, and especially to stop praying for God's church.

The people asked Samuel to pray, but he promised something more: **he would also teach them**.

He gave two reasons for faithful service.

**Gratitude**, because God had already done such great things for them.

And **their own welfare**, because they must consider what would happen if they continued in wrongdoing.

As a faithful watchman, Samuel warned them and thereby fulfilled his own responsibility before God.

When we remember God's great acts—above all **the work of redemption**—we need never lack reasons, encouragement or help for serving him.
