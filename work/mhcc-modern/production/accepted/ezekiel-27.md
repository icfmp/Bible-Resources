# Ezekiel 27 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The merchandise of Tyre. | 1–25 |
| Its fall and ruin. | 26–36 |

## Tyre's trade and riches (verses 1–25)

People who live comfortably are still to be pitied if they are **unprepared for trouble**.

No one should consider themselves truly beautiful or adorned except to the extent that they have been **made holy**.

The description of Tyre's trade reminds us that God sees what people do while conducting ordinary business.

He watches us not only when we are at church, praying and listening, but also **at markets and fairs, while buying and selling**.

We should preserve a conscience free of wrongdoing in all our dealings.

As the **common Father of humanity**, God gives different countries different resources.

One nation produces an abundance of something another lacks, and their goods can serve the needs, comfort and even enjoyment of human life.

**Trade is a blessing when conducted in reverence for God**.

Besides essentials, many goods become valuable chiefly through custom. Yet God permits us to enjoy them.

But when riches increase, we easily set our hearts on them and forget **the Lord who gives us the ability to gain wealth**.

## Tyre's downfall warns those who trust worldly power (verses 26–36)

Even the strongest and most impressive kingdoms eventually fall.

Those who make created things their **ultimate confidence** will fall along with what they trust.

Happy are those whose help is **the God of Jacob**, and whose hope rests in the Lord, who lives forever.

People engaged in trade should conduct their business **according to God's word**.

Those who possess wealth must remember that they are **stewards of God's property** and should use it to **do good to everyone**.

Let us seek first **God's kingdom and his righteousness**.
