# Job 37 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elihu observes the power of God. | 1–13 |
| Job required to explain the works of nature. | 14–20 |
| God is great, and is to be feared. | 21–24 |

## God's power displayed in weather (verses 1–13)

Changes in the weather occupy much of our thinking and conversation.

Yet how seldom do we speak about them as **Elihu did—with reverence toward God, who directs them**!

We should recognize his glory not only in frightening **thunder and lightning**, but also in ordinary, quieter changes such as **snow and rain**.

Animals instinctively seek refuge when a storm comes.

Should humans alone remain without a shelter?

God warns people in many ways to flee from coming judgment.

He also invites them to receive his salvation and be happy.

People reveal how little they trust divine Providence by the way they complain about the weather.

The outcome of an entire year often shows how foolish those complaints were.

Believers should avoid this pattern.

No day is made evil by the Lord.

We make many days miserable through **our own sin**.

## Job cannot fully explain God's works (verses 14–20)

Thinking carefully about God's works in nature can reconcile our hearts to the events of his Providence.

God sends both the **freezing north wind** and the **warm, thawing south wind**.

The Holy Spirit is compared with both because he **convicts** and **comforts** (**Song of Songs 4:16**).

Even the best human beings understand little about God's glorious character and government.

People who through grace know much about God still know almost nothing compared with everything that remains to be known.

How much greater will our knowledge be when **what is perfect** has come!

## God's greatness and the clouds over human understanding (verses 21–24)

Elihu concluded with statements about God's majesty.

Sunlight continues shining even when clouds hide it.

In the same way, the light of God's favor continues toward his faithful servants even when they cannot perceive it.

**Sin can come between our hearts and that comforting light**.

Sorrow can also darken our minds.

But God has a wind that can scatter the clouds: **the Holy Spirit**.

As a wind sweeps clouds across the sky, God's Spirit removes the ignorance, unbelief, sin and harmful desires clouding the soul through **regeneration**.

The Spirit also removes the clouds that trouble a believer's conscience through **consolation**.

Now that the Lord was about to speak for himself, Elihu summed up his discourse.

With God is **majestic and awe-inspiring power**.

Sooner or later, every human being will fear him.
