# Psalm 79 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The deplorable condition of the people of God. | 1–5 |
| A petition for relief. | 6–13 |

## A devastated holy city (verses 1–5)

God's people brought their distress before him.

Where else could children go but to **their Father, who is able and willing to help**?

Sin had brought a dreadful change upon the holy city.

The people themselves defiled it through wrongdoing, and God permitted their enemies to defile it through violence and contempt.

They longed for reconciliation with him.

People who regard **God's favor as better than life** naturally dread his displeasure as **worse than death**.

Whenever we suffer, we should first beg the Lord to cleanse away **the guilt of sin**.

Then we can look for the comfort of his tender mercy.

## A plea grounded in God's mercy (verses 6–13)

Those who continually ignore God and refuse to pray show that they do not know him rightly.

However unrighteous the invaders' actions were, the Lord was righteous in permitting those events within his Providence.

Deliverance from trouble becomes a genuine mercy when it rests upon **forgiveness of sin**.

We should therefore pray even more earnestly for **sin to be removed** than for suffering to end.

The people claimed no merit before God.

Their only hope was his mercy.

They appealed to the glory of his name: **Help and pardon us for your name's sake**.

Christians should remember that they too may be bound by **chains of sin**.

The world can resemble a prison, and every person lives under a sentence of death whose execution may come suddenly.

How earnestly should we pray that the sighing of prisoners reaches God, and that he preserves those appointed to die!

What a glorious day it will be when the church, victorious over **sin and sorrow**, sees every spiritual enemy finally disarmed!

Then, generation after generation, the redeemed will praise **their great Shepherd, Bishop, King and God**.
