# Revelation 21 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A new heaven, and new earth: the new Jerusalem where God dwells, and banishes all sorrow from his people. | 1–8 |
| Its heavenly origin, glory, and secure defence. | 9–21 |
| Its perfect happiness, as enlightened with the presence of God and the Lamb, and in the free access of multitudes, made holy. | 22–27 |

## The new heaven, new earth and new Jerusalem (verses 1–8)

The new heaven and the new earth will not be separated from one another.

The glorified bodies of the saints will belong to a heavenly condition.

The former world, with all its unrest and conflict, will have passed away.

The declaration that **there will be no sea** fittingly represents freedom from conflicting passions, temptation, distress, uncertainty and fear—everything that divides believers or interrupts their fellowship.

The new Jerusalem is **God's church in its renewed and perfected condition**, the church triumphant.

Her blessedness comes entirely from God and rests completely upon him.

God's presence with his people will no longer be interrupted as it is here.

He will dwell among them continually.

Every effect of former suffering will disappear.

His children have often wept because of their sin, their troubles and the distress of the church.

But in that state there will be neither the signs nor the memory of former sorrows.

**Christ makes everything new**.

When we genuinely desire the Redeemer to renew **our hearts and nature**, we can trust him to bring our circumstances also into the fullness of happiness he promises.

The certainty of this future rests on the character of God himself.

He calls himself **Alpha and Omega, the Beginning and the End**, pledging that he will fulfill what he has said.

Worldly and sinful pleasures resemble **poisoned, muddy water**.

Even the best earthly comforts are like a small quantity of water stored in a cistern.

When we make idols of them, they become broken cisterns and yield only disappointment.

But Christ's gifts are like water flowing from an abundant spring: **pure, refreshing, inexhaustible and everlasting**.

The sanctifying comforts of the Holy Spirit prepare God's people for heavenly joy, like streams providing water in the wilderness.

Those described as **cowardly** shrink from the hardships of true religion because they do not believe.

They may refuse the cross of Christ while still rushing into severe wickedness.

The terrors of the first death are followed by something incomparably worse for the unrepentant: everlasting death.

## The heavenly city's glory and security (verses 9–21)

God gives his holy angels many different tasks.

Sometimes they sound trumpets announcing divine judgments and warnings to an unthinking world.

At other times they reveal heavenly realities to those who will inherit salvation.

Those seeking a clear view of heaven should ascend, as it were, **the mountain of meditation and faith**.

The vision portrays the perfected, triumphant church in all her glory.

Her glory rests on **her relationship with Christ**.

Heaven's happiness consists in fellowship with God and becoming like him.

The image changes from **bride to city**, reminding us to draw the broad spiritual meaning rather than try to interpret every architectural detail literally.

The city's wall signifies **complete security**.

No evil or enemy can harm anyone who dwells there.

Its enormous size assures us that there is room for **all God's people**.

The foundation of its safety is **God's promise and power, and what Christ has purchased**.

The twelve kinds of precious stone may represent the varied beauties of **gospel doctrine, the Spirit's graces, or the perfections of Christ himself**.

Henry offers these as possible meanings, not as an identification of each individual stone.

The city has gates because everyone whom God has made holy is freely admitted.

Its gates are pearls, pointing toward **Christ, the Pearl of great price, who is our way to the Father**.

Its street is pure gold, clear as glass.

What people prize most highly on earth is beneath the feet of the saints in heaven.

The redeemed enjoy rest, but **not sleep, inactivity or idleness**.

They have joyful fellowship both with God and with one another.

Even these magnificent images are only faint representations of the happiness to come.

## The Lord God and the Lamb illuminate the city (verses 22–27)

Direct and perfect fellowship with God will more than replace the religious ordinances believers now need on earth.

The absence of a separate temple reveals **the unity and equal deity of the Son with the Father**.

Without the sun's light, this world would be dreadful.

But in heaven **God's glory gives light, and the Lamb is its lamp**.

Through Christ, God becomes an everlasting source of understanding and joy to the redeemed.

There is no night and no need to close the city's gates.

All is peaceful, secure and full of divine light.

No sinful, unclean, idolatrous, false or deceitful thing can enter.

Everyone there has been made **perfectly holy**.

For now, even the saints struggle with remaining corruption, which interrupts worship and communion with God.

But on entering heaven's true sanctuary, they are **washed through Christ's blood** and presented to the Father without stain.

No hypocrite or person devoted to falsehood can enter.

Because nothing impure enters God's heavenly city, these glimpses of its glory should move us to work diligently toward **holiness in the fear of God**.
