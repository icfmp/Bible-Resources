# Deuteronomy 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A provision respecting Levites. | 1–8 |
| The abominations of the Canaanites to be avoided. | 9–14 |
| Christ the great Prophet. | 15–22 |

## Provision for the priests and Levites (verses 1–8)

God took care that the priests would not become entangled in ordinary worldly business or seek to enrich themselves with the world's wealth.

They had more important work to do.

But the Lord also made provision so that they would not lack the ordinary comforts and necessities of life.

The people were responsible for supporting them.

Those who benefit from gathered worship ought to contribute to the adequate support of the ministers who serve and teach them.

## Israel must avoid occult practices (verses 9–14)

Israel had received extraordinary blessings from God.

Yet the people remained at risk of turning to false teachers among the nations God had placed under judgment.

Moses therefore repeated the warning not to imitate the abominations of the Canaanites.

Henry explicitly condemns **belief in lucky or unlucky days, charms for curing illness, amulets or spells intended to prevent harm, and fortune-telling**.

These practices contributed to the judgment that fell upon Canaan.

Henry expresses astonishment that people still claimed occult powers in an age with such broad access to divine truth.

He regards them as impostors who deceive and exploit their followers.

## Jesus Christ, the promised great Prophet (verses 15–22)

God promised to raise up a Prophet greater than all the prophets who came before.

Through him, God would reveal himself and his will to humanity more fully and clearly than ever before.

Henry identifies this Prophet as **Jesus Christ**.

He is the Light of the world (John 8:12).

He is the **Word through whom God speaks to us** (John 1:1; Hebrews 1:2).

Through his birth, he belonged to Israel.

In his resurrection, he rose in Jerusalem, and his teaching went out from there to the whole world.

God raised up his Son Jesus to bless us.

Christ is like Moses, but infinitely greater.

The promised Prophet has already come.

We are not to look for another.

His revelation of God is meant not to crush us with terror but to give us encouragement.

He speaks with both a father's tenderness and God's own authority.

Anyone who refuses to listen to Christ does so at great risk.

The One appointed as Prophet will also be Judge (John 12:48).

Those who reject his voice, salvation and rule face judgment.

But people who trust and obey him are blessed.

He guides them along safe and peaceful paths until he brings them into the land of perfect light, purity and joy.

Moses also warned against false prophets.

We urgently need a reliable test for every message we hear, so that we can distinguish what God has spoken from what he has not.

Anything contradicting the clear meaning of the written word, or encouraging people to sin, cannot be a message from the Lord.
