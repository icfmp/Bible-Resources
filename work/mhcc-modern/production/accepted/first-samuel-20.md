# 1 Samuel 20 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## David seeks Jonathan's help (verses 1–10)

The troubles David suffered were preparing him for future honor.

God often deals this way with those he is preparing for glory.

He does not immediately place them in possession of his kingdom.

Instead, he leads them through many afflictions and uses those afflictions to prepare them to receive it.

They should not complain about his gracious plan or distrust his care.

They should look forward joyfully to the crown reserved for them.

At times we feel that only a single step separates us from death.

In reality, this is always possible, so we should be prepared.

Yet no danger can end our lives until God's purpose concerning us has been accomplished.

If we are believers, we will not die until we have served our generation according to his will.

Jonathan generously offered David his help.

That is genuine friendship.

Christ similarly assures his people of his love: **ask, and it will be done for you**.

We should demonstrate our own love for him by keeping his commandments.

## Jonathan renews his covenant with David (verses 11–23)

Jonathan faithfully promised to tell David how his father felt about him.

We do well to become friends with people whom God favors and to make his friends our friends.

Such a relationship benefits not only us but our families.

True friendship rests on a firm foundation.

It can overcome ambition, selfishness and excessive concern for what others think.

But who can fully understand the love of Jesus?

He offered himself as a sacrifice for rebellious and polluted sinners.

How powerfully should his love move us to love him, support his cause and care for his people!

## Saul turns his anger against Jonathan (verses 24–34)

David was exceptionally faithful in attending God's appointed worship.

He would not have missed it except because staying away was necessary for his safety.

When great danger threatens, an opportunity for public worship may reasonably be passed by.

But unless there is real necessity, it harms us to neglect regular opportunities to worship God.

Jonathan acted wisely for himself and his family by remaining faithful to David, though Saul attacked him for it.

It is always good to make **God's people our people**.

That decision will finally benefit us, even when the world considers it contrary to our interests.

Saul's rage was savage.

How anger turns people into beasts—or makes them behave even worse than beasts!

## David and Jonathan part (verses 35–42)

The separation grieved both faithful friends.

David's position was even more painful because he was leaving behind all his comforts, including the blessings of God's sanctuary.

Yet Christians need not mourn like people without hope.

United with Christ, they are also united with one another.

Before long they will meet again in his presence, never to part.

There God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.
