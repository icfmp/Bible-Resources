# Numbers 25 — Modern English

## Israel is drawn into sin (verses 1–5)

The friendship of wicked people can be more dangerous than their open hostility.

Israel could not be overcome by enemies unless the people first yielded to the sinful desires within their own hearts.

No magical curse could defeat them in the way that worldly ambition, pleasure and temptation could.

The women of Moab and Midian enticed Israel into wrongdoing.

Those who draw others into sin may be our worst enemies, because nothing can harm us more seriously.

Israel's own sin accomplished what all Balaam's attempted curses had failed to do.

It brought God's judgment against the people.

Henry understands the outbreak of disease as a righteous consequence of serious prevailing sins.

Those who lead other people into such wrongdoing were to be publicly held accountable.

## Phinehas acts against Zimri and Cozbi (verses 6–15)

Phinehas acted with courage, zeal and faith when he put Zimri and Cozbi to death in this historical judgment.

But Henry adds a decisive limitation.

**This action must never be treated as a precedent for private revenge, religious persecution or unauthorized public punishment.**

The exceptional action recorded in Israel's history gives no license to carry out violence against others today.

## The judgment against Midian (verses 16–18)

The account does not say that any Midianites died from the plague that struck Israel.

God instead judged them through the sword of an enemy, not through the corrective rod used against his own children.

We must oppose whatever draws us toward sin (Matthew 5:29–30).

Anything that tempts us to wrongdoing should become painful and unwelcome to us, like a thorn in the flesh.

Those who follow Satan's pattern by cleverly enticing others into sin will face especially serious judgment.
