## The bronze altar and basin (verses 1–8)

In every age of the church, some people have been especially devoted to God.

They attend faithfully to his appointed worship and are more willing than others to surrender even things they have a perfect right to keep for his sake.

Some women of Israel were deeply committed to the Lord and zealous for worship in the tabernacle.

They showed that zeal by giving up their mirrors.

These were polished bronze plates, which served the purpose of looking glasses before glass mirrors had been invented.

The women chose to give up something valuable and useful so that it could serve the Lord.

## The court and its curtains (verses 9–20)

The walls around the tabernacle's court were only curtains.

Henry understands this as a reminder that the Jewish church's outward form was temporary and could change.

In time, that arrangement would be taken down and folded away.

The space occupied by the tent would then be enlarged, and its ropes extended, so that the Gentile world could be welcomed in.

## The offerings and the meaning of the sanctuary (verses 21–31)

The foundation was made from heavy pieces of silver.

They pointed to the strength and purity of the truth upon which the church is founded.

As we read about the tabernacle's furnishings, let us keep our eyes on the Lord Jesus Christ.

When we look at the altar for burnt offerings, let us see Jesus.

In his righteousness and salvation there is a full and sufficient offering for sin.

Let our souls be washed in the basin of regeneration by his Holy Spirit, so that we may be clean.

And just as the Israelites gave willingly, may our own hearts be made willing.

Let us be ready to surrender anything for Christ and count everything else as loss in order to gain him.
