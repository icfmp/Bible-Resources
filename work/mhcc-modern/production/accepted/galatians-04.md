# Galatians 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The folly of returning to legal observances for justification. | 1–7 |
| The happy change made in the Gentile believers. | 8–11 |
| The apostle reasons against following false teachers. | 12–18 |
| He expresses his earnest concern for them. | 19, 20 |
| And then explains the difference between what is to be expected from the law, and from the gospel. | 21–31 |

## From bondage to the adoption of God's children (verses 1–7)

Paul spoke plainly against those trying to combine the Mosaic law with the gospel in a way that brought believers back into bondage.

The old covenant contained many **demanding ceremonies and restrictions**.

Like a child placed under tutors and guardians, God's people lived under a form of instruction that did not yet reveal fully what those observances meant.

The gospel brings Christians into **a happier and freer condition**.

These verses display the wonder of God's love.

**The Father sent his Son into the world** for our redemption.

**The Son humbled himself and suffered** for us.

**The Holy Spirit graciously comes to dwell in believers' hearts**.

We were by nature children of disobedience and exposed to divine wrath.

Through grace we become **children whom God loves**, and the Spirit begins shaping us into the family likeness.

In human families only the eldest son commonly received the special inheritance.

But **every one of God's children shares the inheritance of an eldest son**.

May our attitudes and actions demonstrate our adoption!

May the Holy Spirit bear witness with our spirits that we are God's children and heirs.

## Do not return to the slavery of false worship (verses 8–11)

The Gentile Christians had turned from idols to the living God and had received adoption through Christ.

This change came entirely through **God's free and generous grace**.

How serious, then, to abandon the freedom he had given!

All genuine knowledge of God begins **with him**.

We know him because **he first knew us**.

Although Christianity forbids worshiping idols, many still practice **idolatry in their hearts**.

Whatever a person loves and values most becomes that person's god.

Some make wealth their god; others enthrone pleasure or sinful desire.

Some worship an imaginary god who possesses **only mercy and no justice**.

They assume God will pardon them even as they refuse repentance and continue in sin.

Even people who once professed faith confidently can be drawn away from the simplicity and purity of the gospel.

The more mercy God has shown us in giving us gospel knowledge and freedom, the greater our foolishness if we surrender those blessings.

Members of the visible church should therefore examine themselves carefully rather than resting in a few outward signs of religious life.

Paul feared that his labor might have accomplished little, yet **he continued laboring**.

Perseverance in duty despite uncertain results is wisdom and reverence toward God.

Each person should follow this rule in the place and calling assigned to them.

## Paul appeals to the Galatians as their friend (verses 12–18)

Paul wanted the Galatians to agree with him about the Mosaic law, but also to remain united with him in love.

Whenever we correct someone, we should make clear that the correction comes from **concern for God's honor and that person's genuine welfare**.

Paul reminded them of the physical difficulty he had experienced when he first preached among them.

At that time they had welcomed him gladly as God's messenger.

But human admiration and respect are sadly unstable.

We should labor above all to **be approved by God**.

The Galatians once regarded themselves as happy to receive Christ's gospel.

Why should they now act as though it had been a mistake?

Christians must not stop telling the truth merely because some people might feel offended by it.

The false teachers pretended affection for the Galatians, but their motives were **self-serving rather than sincere**.

Paul gave an excellent rule: **be zealous in a good cause at all times**.

Zeal should not appear briefly and then vanish. It should remain steady.

How blessed the church would be if believers held to that kind of faithful eagerness!

## Paul's concern until Christ is formed in them (verses 19 and 20)

The Galatians had begun to regard Paul almost as an enemy.

Yet he assured them of his genuine friendship and **parental affection**.

He was deeply troubled about their spiritual condition and wanted to learn whether they would recover from their present deception.

The strongest evidence that a sinner has received **justification** is that **Christ is being formed within that person** through the renewing work of the Holy Spirit.

But this inward transformation cannot be expected while someone continues seeking acceptance with God **through the law instead of Christ**.

## Hagar and Sarah picture two covenant arrangements (verses 21–27)

Paul illustrated the difference between relying upon Christ and relying upon the law through **Isaac and Ishmael**.

The historical account of **Sarah and Hagar** has its ordinary literal meaning, but Paul explained that the Holy Spirit also intended it to point toward **a deeper spiritual truth**.

The two women form suitable pictures of **two different covenant arrangements**.

Sarah represents **the heavenly Jerusalem**, God's true church, living in freedom and becoming the spiritual mother of believers.

Those born of the Holy Spirit and united to Christ through genuine faith belong to **Abraham's promised spiritual descendants**.

## Freedom in Christ, not a new bondage (verses 28–31)

Paul applied this interpretation to his readers.

Believers are **children of the free woman, not the slave woman**.

Since their privileges under the gospel are so great, it would be absurd for Gentile Christians to return to a legal arrangement that could not rescue even unbelieving Jews from spiritual bondage or condemnation.

Henry makes an important qualification.

We would not have identified this allegorical meaning in Sarah and Hagar **if the apostle had not revealed it**, but because the Holy Spirit disclosed it through Paul, its divine intention is certain.

The allegory **explains the teaching rather than serving as an independent proof of it**.

It illustrates the **covenant of works and covenant of grace**, and the attitudes of legalistic and gospel-minded religion.

Good works attempted in a person's own strength belong to the legalistic spirit.

Works arising **from faith in Christ** reflect the gospel.

The first spirit produces **bondage to sin and death**.

The second produces freedom—not **freedom to sin**, but freedom **to fulfill duty**.

The first spirit leads to persecution, while the second is marked by **love**.

People who treat God's servants with a violent, harsh or controlling spirit should examine themselves.

Even a genuine believer may temporarily fall back into an approach based upon **personal merit or self-strength**, especially through unbelief or neglect of God's promises.

Such a person may also treat other believers harshly instead of lovingly.

Yet this is not the believer's settled way of life.

A genuine believer will find no rest until returning to **dependence on Christ**.

Let us ground our hope upon Scripture and demonstrate through cheerful obedience that our hearts and treasure truly belong in heaven.
