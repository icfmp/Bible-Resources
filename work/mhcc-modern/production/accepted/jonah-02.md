# Jonah 2 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

| The prayer of Jonah. | 1–9 |

## Jonah prays in the fish (verses 1–9)

Consider **when Jonah prayed**.

He was in distress, experiencing God's displeasure because of his disobedience.

We should pray whenever we suffer.

He had been preserved alive by a miracle and now turned to God.

Knowing that God still regards us with kindness, despite our wrongdoing, gives us courage to pray when fear of judgment has kept us silent.

Consider **where he prayed**: **inside the great fish**.

No place is unsuitable for prayer.

People may separate us from one another, but **they cannot separate us from fellowship with God**.

Consider **whom he addressed**: **the Lord his God**.

That encourages even people who have wandered away from their faith to return.

Consider **the nature of his prayer**.

The words recorded here seem to recount **Jonah's experience and later reflections**, rather than reproduce the exact words he said at every moment inside the fish.

He remembered how earnestly he had cried out, and how readily God had heard and answered him.

If we are to benefit from suffering, we must **recognize God's hand in it**.

Jonah had fled wickedly from the Lord's presence.

God could justly have withdrawn his Holy Spirit from him and never returned to him.

Only those from whom God withdraws his favor completely are truly miserable.

Jonah was perplexed but **not without hope**.

When he sought and trusted God in trouble, he experienced mercy.

He urged others to remain close to the Lord.

People who abandon their duty also abandon **the comforts associated with fulfilling it**.

As far as believers copy people who worship false things, they turn away from their own mercy and live beneath the privileges God has given them.

But Jonah's experience encourages believers **in every age** to trust the Lord as **the God of salvation**.

## Jonah is released (verse 10)

Jonah's deliverance demonstrates God's **power over every created thing**.

It also displays his **mercy toward a repentant sinner** who prayed in trouble.

And it serves as **a type of Christ's resurrection**.

Through every change in circumstances, let us fix our faith upon **our Redeemer who suffered and died, but has now risen and ascended**.

Confess our sins.

Regard Christ's resurrection as **a pledge of our own**.

Receive every earthly and spiritual deliverance with thanks as **a foretaste of eternal redemption**.
