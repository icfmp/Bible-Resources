# 1 Kings 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David's declining age. | 1–4 |
| Adonijah aspires to the throne. | 5–10 |
| David makes Solomon king. | 11–31 |
| Solomon is anointed king, and Adonijah's usurpation stopped. | 32–53 |

## David grows old (verses 1–4)

David had become weak in his old age.

He was experiencing the correction of his recent sins and the lasting effects of his earlier labors and hardships.

## Adonijah tries to take the throne (verses 5–10)

Parents who indulge their children instead of correcting them often suffer afterward through those children's disobedience and impatience to possess the family inheritance.

Neither worldly wisdom, long experience nor an outwardly holy reputation can guarantee that someone ruled by selfishness will remain on a good path.

We may especially wonder how **Joab and Abiathar** allowed themselves to be drawn into Adonijah's rebellion.

## David confirms Solomon as king (verses 11–31)

Consider Nathan's words to **Bathsheba**.

He offered her advice intended to save both her life and her son's.

Ministers of Christ give us similar counsel in his name.

They urge us to be diligent so that no one takes our crown (**Revelation 3:11**) and so that our own souls are saved.

David made a solemn declaration that he would keep his earlier decision to appoint Solomon as his successor.

As the weakness of old age increased and death approached, David remembered the many troubles from which the Lord had rescued him.

These memories increased his comfort, strengthened his hope and encouraged him to perform his remaining duty.

## Solomon is anointed; Adonijah's plan collapses (verses 32–53)

The people rejoiced greatly when Solomon became king.

Every true Israelite likewise rejoices at the exaltation of the **Son of David**.

Conspiracies founded upon evil principles collapse quickly when personal interests shift.

How can people who do evil expect joyful news?

Adonijah had despised Solomon, but he soon feared him.

This scene helps us understand the exaltation of **Jesus Christ**, Son of David and Son of God, to his glorious throne despite all opposition.

His kingdom is much greater than David's, and all God's people rejoice sincerely in its advancement.

But the prosperity of Christ's kingdom brings grief and fear to its enemies.

Clinging to **an altar's horns**, observing outward religious forms, or claiming to be devout will not help people who refuse his authority and salvation.

If their submission is merely hypocritical, they face destruction without remedy.
