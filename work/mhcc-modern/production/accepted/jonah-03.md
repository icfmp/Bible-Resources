# Jonah 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jonah sent again to Nineveh, preaches there. | 1–4 |
| Nineveh is spared upon the repentance of the inhabitants. | 5–10 |

## Jonah returns to his calling (verses 1–4)

God employed Jonah again.

The Lord's willingness to use us in his work is evidence of **peace restored between us and him**.

Jonah was no longer rebellious.

He did not avoid hearing the command or refuse to carry it out.

This is what genuine repentance looks like: **a changed mind and conduct, a return to assigned work and duty**.

Suffering can benefit us by bringing us back to the service we abandoned.

But that change displays **divine grace**, because suffering by itself is more likely to drive sinners **away from God** than to draw them to him.

The Lord's servants must go where he sends them, come when he calls, and do what he commands.

Jonah faithfully and boldly delivered his message.

Henry says it is **uncertain whether Jonah added more explanation about God's anger or simply repeated the announcement**.

Its central meaning is clear.

**Forty days** is a long delay for a righteous God to allow before judgment, but it is little time for sinful people to repent and reform.

The announcement should make us think seriously about preparation for death.

We cannot be as confident that **we will live forty days** as Nineveh was that it would endure for forty days.

We would be frightened if told with certainty we had less than a month to live, yet often remain indifferent though **we cannot guarantee another day**.

## The Ninevites repent and are spared (verses 5–10)

The repentance and reform of Nineveh were **a marvel of God's grace**.

The people condemned later generations that heard the gospel and did not repent (**Matthew 12:41**).

Even limited knowledge about God can convince people that **humility, confession, prayer and turning from sin** are necessary in seeking mercy.

The people followed their king's example.

The repentance became **national**, as was necessary when the announced judgment concerned the whole nation.

Even the cries of animals deprived of food should remind their owners to cry to God.

When praying, we must cry **earnestly**, with fixed attention, strong faith and sincere affection.

Every part of the heart should be involved.

It is not enough to **fast because of sin**.

We must **fast from sin**.

If we cherish evil inwardly, our prayers cannot be sincere (**Psalm 66:18**).

A day's appointed fast does not complete the work of repentance when the day is over.

The Ninevites hoped that God would turn from his anger.

They had less certainty of finding mercy than Christians now possess through **the death and merits of Jesus Christ**, the foundation of pardon for those who repent.

They dared not presume upon grace, but **they did not despair**.

The hope of mercy is a powerful encouragement to sincere reform.

Let us humbly cast ourselves upon **God's free grace**.

He sees who actually turn from evil and who continue in it.

That is why he spared Nineveh.

The account records **no sacrifices offered to provide atonement**.

But God did not despise **broken and contrite hearts**.
