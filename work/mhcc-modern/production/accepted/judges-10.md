# Judges 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Tola and Jair judge Israel. | 1–5 |
| The Philistines and Ammonites oppress Israel. | 6–9 |
| Israel's repentance. | 10–18 |

## Tola and Jair govern Israel (verses 1–5)

Peaceful periods of government give historians less to write about, even though they are often the best times in which to live.

Such were the days of **Tola and Jair**.

They were humble, energetic and useful men, appointed by God to govern.

## Philistines and Ammonites oppress Israel (verses 6–9)

God's warning now came true: Israel would be unable to stand against its enemies (Leviticus 26:17, 37).

Through their own sinful ways and actions, the people had brought this misery upon themselves.

## Israel turns toward God (verses 10–18)

God can multiply punishments to correspond with the number of people's sins and idols.

But hope remains when sinners cry to the Lord for help and grieve over both their open wrongdoing and their deeper ungodliness.

True repentance requires recognizing that whatever we have placed alongside God as an object of trust **cannot save us**.

Israel acknowledged what the people deserved but begged God not to treat them according to that desert.

We must submit to God's justice while hoping in his mercy.

**Repentance means not merely being sorry for sin, but turning away from it.**

Just as a loving father is grieved by the disobedience and suffering of a child, God is grieved by the wrongdoing of his people.

No one seeks his mercy in vain.

The trembling sinner and the believer who has nearly despaired after falling away should stop debating God's hidden purposes.

Nor should they demand encouragement from their earlier religious experiences before daring to hope.

Instead, let them throw themselves upon the mercy of God our Savior.

Let them humble themselves before him, seek freedom from the powers of darkness, separate from sin and its temptations, diligently use the means of grace and wait for the Lord's appointed time.

Then they will surely rejoice in his mercy.
