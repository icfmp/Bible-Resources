# Numbers 20 — Modern English

## Israel complains again; Moses and Aaron fail (verses 1–13)

After thirty-eight weary years in the wilderness, Israel began moving toward Canaan once more.

There was no water for the congregation.

We live in a world full of needs.

Wherever we go, we should expect something to disturb our comfort.

Having abundant water is a great mercy. If we lacked it, we would value it more.

The people complained against Moses and Aaron.

They spoke the same unreasonable and bitter words their parents had spoken.

Their conduct was especially blameworthy because their families had suffered for years as a result of the earlier generation's complaining and unbelief.

Yet they repeated the very same sins.

God directed Moses to bring water from a rock again.

The Lord was no less able to supply his people than before.

But Moses and Aaron sinned in the way they handled the miracle.

They spoke as though the power belonged partly to themselves.

“Must **we** bring you water?”

That took some of the glory that belonged to God alone.

They were commanded to **speak** to the rock.

Instead, Moses **struck** it.

In this way they failed to honor the Lord as holy and failed to give him the complete glory he deserved.

Provoked by the people, Moses also spoke rashly.

Henry sees in this the continuing sinful desire of people to take over the work of the one appointed Mediator.

We try to become our own wisdom, righteousness, holiness and redemption.

But the gospel condemns every attempt to live independently of the Savior.

## Edom refuses Israel passage (verses 14–21)

The shortest route to Canaan passed through Edom.

Moses sent messengers asking permission to travel through the country.

The Edomites refused.

They feared that Israel's large army might cause them harm.

Such fears might have been understandable if Israel were not under the discipline of the righteous God himself.

But Edom's hostility also continued the hatred that Esau had shown Jacob because of the blessing.

Now the old resentment reappeared as Jacob's descendants approached their inheritance.

We should not be surprised when unreasonable people reject fair requests or when people favored by God are treated with contempt.

## Aaron dies and Eleazar becomes priest (verses 22–29)

God told Aaron to prepare for death.

The command expressed both displeasure and mercy.

There was displeasure because Aaron could not enter Canaan after his failure at the waters of Meribah.

But there was mercy because he was allowed to die peacefully, with honor, as someone received into the care of divine grace.

His exclusion from Canaan also had spiritual meaning.

It showed that the Levitical priesthood could not make anything perfect.

A better hope had to be introduced.

Aaron submitted to the appointed manner and time of his death.

As far as the record shows, he did so as peacefully as someone preparing to sleep.

He could see his beloved son Eleazar succeed him.

The priesthood would continue.

Even more importantly, that succession pointed toward the eternal priesthood of Christ.

A good person should want to remain useful for as long as God wills.

Why should we desire to stay in this world beyond the time when we can serve the Lord and our generation?
