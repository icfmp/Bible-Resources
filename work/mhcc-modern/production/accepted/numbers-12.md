# Numbers 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God rebukes the murmuring of Aaron and Miriam. | 1–9 |
| Miriam struck with leprosy, and healed at the prayer of Moses. | 10–16 |

## Miriam and Aaron criticize Moses (verses 1–9)

Moses' patience was tested by his own family as well as by the people he led.

Miriam and Aaron objected to his marriage to a foreign woman.

But Henry considers it probable that they were really motivated by wounded pride and envy of his greater authority.

Opposition from relatives and from friends who share our faith can hurt especially deeply.

We should expect such difficulties and seek to keep Moses' gentle and humble spirit.

These trials helped prepare Moses for his calling.

God not only defended Moses but praised him.

Moses was entrusted with prophetic revelation in a way that placed him above the other prophets.

Yet the least person in the kingdom of heaven enjoys a greater privilege, and Jesus Christ is infinitely greater than Moses (Hebrews 3:1).

Miriam and Aaron should have considered whom they were insulting.

We should be cautious about speaking against God's servants.

It is presumptuous to speak contemptuously about those in positions of dignity (2 Peter 2:10).

Nothing shows God's displeasure more sadly than his presence being withdrawn.

Woe to us if he leaves!

He does not withdraw until our sins and foolishness have driven him away.

## Miriam is afflicted; Moses intercedes (verses 10–16)

The cloud departed, and Miriam was struck with leprosy.

When God's presence goes, distress follows.

Bishop Hall, whom Henry quotes, observed that her offensive tongue was punished by an afflicted appearance.

Aaron, as a priest, had to judge the condition.

He could not pronounce Miriam unclean without trembling, since he knew he was equally guilty.

If speaking against Moses was so serious, what will happen to those who sin against Christ?

Aaron, who had just criticized Moses with Miriam, now had to plead with him on behalf of both.

He was forced to speak respectfully of the very person he had blamed.

People who despise God's servants may one day need their help.

Correction has a good effect when it leads to confession and repentance.

Even offenders who are disciplined and publicly humbled may be forgiven.

Moses showed that he had forgiven the personal wrong done to him.

We should imitate his mercy and the Savior's prayer, “Father, forgive them.”

Miriam had to remain outside the camp for seven days, accepting the disgrace that followed her sin.

Whenever God corrects us, we should humbly acknowledge our wrongdoing.

Israel's journey was delayed by this event.

Many things can slow our progress, but nothing hinders our journey toward heaven more seriously than sin.
