# Revelation 22 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A description of the heavenly state, under the figures of the water and the tree of life, and of the throne of God and the Lamb. | 1–5 |
| The truth and certain fulfilling of all the prophetic visions, The Holy Spirit, and the bride, the church, invite, and say, Come. | 6–19 |
| The closing blessing. | 20, 21 |

## The water and tree of life (verses 1–5)

The streams of comfort we find on earth are often muddy and mixed with trouble.

But the water in this vision is clear and refreshing.

It gives life and preserves the lives of all who drink, flowing forever.

The river pictures **the life-giving and sanctifying work of the Holy Spirit**, given to sinners through Jesus Christ.

**The Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son**, applying salvation to our souls through his renewing love and power.

The trees of life draw nourishment from the pure river flowing from the throne of God.

God's presence is the health and happiness of the saints in heaven.

The tree represents **Christ and all the blessings of his salvation**.

The leaves that heal the nations show that God's favor and presence supply every good thing needed in that blessed world.

The devil has no power there.

He cannot draw the saints away from serving God, nor disturb them while they worship.

**God and the Lamb are spoken of as one**.

Service in heaven brings not only freedom but **honor and a share in the kingdom**.

There is no night, pain or discouragement.

Worship and enjoyment of God will never be interrupted.

No humanly invented distractions, amusements or pleasures will be needed.

How different this is from merely earthly ideas of heaven—even those that imagine only refined intellectual pleasures!

## The sure prophecy and the invitation to come (verses 6–19)

Jesus Christ, speaking through his angel, solemnly confirms the truth of the book and especially the final vision.

He is **the faithful and true Lord God**.

Holy angels delivered God's message to his holy servants.

The visions concern things that must come to pass, and **Christ will come quickly** and make their truth evident.

John also received a confirmation from the integrity of the angel who had explained the vision.

When John offered him religious worship, the angel **refused and reproved him**.

Henry takes this as another testimony against worship directed toward **saints and angels**, which belongs to God alone.

The Lord calls all people to heed these declarations.

The book is left **open**, and its words will have an effect.

People devoted to wickedness may grow more hardened, while sincere believers will be strengthened and further sanctified.

Never suppose that **lifeless or disobedient faith** will save.

The First and the Last pronounces blessing on people who **keep his commandments**.

The book excludes from heaven the wicked, unjust and especially those who **love and practice falsehood**.

It cannot itself be dismissed as false without contradicting the solemn testimony it gives.

There is no secure middle state between fidelity to Christ and continuing rebellion against him.

Jesus, the One who gives prophecy, has provided the churches with this **light before dawn**, assuring them that the brightness of the full day is coming.

The prophecy closes with **an open invitation to all humanity** to receive freely the gospel's promises and blessings.

The Holy Spirit, speaking through Scripture and working within the conscience of sinners, says, **“Come to Christ for salvation.”**

The bride, the whole church on earth and in heaven, likewise says, **“Come and share our joy.”**

No one should hesitate.

Whoever is willing may come and receive **the water of life without payment**.

May everyone hearing or reading this invitation receive it at once.

The solemn warning at the end condemns anyone who would **corrupt God's word by adding to it or taking anything away**.

## The final promise and blessing (verses 20 and 21)

After making these things known to his people on earth, Christ seems, in Henry's description, to take his leave and return to heaven.

But he assures them that his return is near.

While we carry out our different duties, regardless of the labor, difficulties or grief we face, we can hear our Lord's promise with joy:

**“I am coming quickly.”**

He will bring an end to the toil and suffering of his faithful servants.

He comes with a **reward of grace**, generously recompensing every work of faith and every labor of love.

He comes to receive his persevering people into his presence, where they will live with him forever.

**Amen. Come, Lord Jesus.**

The book ends with a blessing.

Only by **Christ's grace** can we continue expecting his glory with joy, be prepared for it and be preserved until we receive it.

His appearing will bring joy to those who already share his grace and favor.

May everyone add a sincere **Amen**.

Let us thirst more earnestly for Jesus' gracious influence in our souls and his presence with us until his grace reaches perfection in glory.

**Glory to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit**, as in the beginning, now and forever.

Amen.
