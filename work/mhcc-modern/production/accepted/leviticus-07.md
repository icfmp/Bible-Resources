## The guilt offering (verses 1–10)

In the sin offering and guilt offering, the sacrifice was divided between the altar and the priest.

The person bringing it received no portion, unlike the person who brought a fellowship offering.

The sin and guilt offerings expressed repentance and sorrow for wrongdoing. Fasting therefore suited them better than feasting.

Fellowship offerings, on the other hand, expressed communion with God through Christ after reconciliation, the joy and thankfulness of a forgiven sinner, and the privileges of a true believer.

## The fellowship offering (verses 11–27)

God allowed more freedom in expressing gratitude for mercy than in acknowledging guilt.

Fellowship offerings could therefore be freely given, making the offerer's willing gift especially pleasing.

Yet God required sacrifices of atonement.

That requirement taught the necessity of the great sacrifice that would reconcile sinners to him.

The chief reason blood was prohibited as food was that God had appointed it for atonement.

This symbolic purpose reached its fulfillment in Christ, who offered himself and shed his blood, bringing the earlier sacrifices to an end.

**For that reason, Henry says this prohibition is no longer binding upon believers.**

## The lifted and waved portions (verses 28–34)

The officiating priest received the breast and the right shoulder.

After the animal was killed, the person making the offering was to present God's portion personally.

Doing so expressed a willing surrender to him.

The offerer raised it with both hands to acknowledge God as Lord of heaven.

Then it was waved from side to side to acknowledge him as Lord of all the earth.

Let us be encouraged to receive and enjoy Christ, our fellowship offering.

This blessed gift is not only for priests or exceptionally mature believers; it is also for ordinary people.

Do not delay in turning to Christ.

Some people plan to repent only when they are dying. But they should receive this fellowship offering now, before the time of God's patience has ended.

The provision that a fellowship offering could not be eaten on the third day underlines the urgency.

It will be too late to seek Christ after death and judgment.

## The conclusion of the sacrificial regulations (verses 35–38)

Public acts of worship are not optional matters that we may accept or ignore according to personal preference.

Neglecting them puts us in danger.

Obedience to the laws of Christ cannot be any less necessary than obedience to the laws Moses delivered.
