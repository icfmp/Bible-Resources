# Isaiah 41 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's care of his people. | 1–9 |
| they are encouraged not to fear. | 10–20 |
| The vanity and folly of idolatry. | 21–29 |

## God's care for his people (verses 1–9)

Could any pagan god raise up a person in righteousness, direct that person as he chose, and grant victories over nations?

The Lord did this with **Abraham**—or, as Henry considers more likely in this prophecy, would do so with **Cyrus**.

Sinners encourage one another along paths of wrongdoing. Should not those serving the living God encourage one another still more actively in his service?

God's people are descendants of **Abraham, his friend**. Henry calls this the highest title ever given to a human being. Through divine grace Abraham became like God in character and was admitted to fellowship with him.

Happy are the Lord's servants who have been called his friends, to walk with him in faith and holy obedience.

Such favored people need not give way to fear. The conflict may be intense, but **the victory is certain**.

## The Lord strengthens those who must not fear (verses 10–20)

God speaks tenderly: **Do not fear, for I am with you**.

He is not merely near enough to be called; he is present.

Are you weak? He will strengthen you. Do you lack friends? He will help when you need help. Are you about to fall? His righteous right hand will uphold you, rewarding good and judging evil.

Some seek the ruin of God's people. But believers should **not repay evil with evil**. They must wait for the Lord's time.

Jacob is called **a worm**: weak, small, despised and readily trampled upon. God's people are worms in their humble opinion of themselves and in their enemies' proud opinion of them—but **not vipers, belonging to the serpent's offspring**.

God's word humbles human pride. Yet the Lord helps his people because **he is their Redeemer**.

He promises to turn Jacob into a new and sharp threshing instrument, prepared for useful work. Henry sees its fulfillment in the triumph of Christ's gospel and of faithful Christians over the powers of darkness.

God has prepared enough comfort to meet his people's needs and answer their prayers.

Our journey to heaven passes through the wilderness of this world. Human souls thirst for satisfaction, and grow weary of seeking in the world what the world cannot give.

Yet God promises refreshment even where no one expects it: **rivers of grace and living water**. Christ used living water as a figure of the **Holy Spirit** (**John 7:38–39**).

When God establishes his church in the wilderness of the Gentile world, the change will be like thornbushes turning into cedars, firs and myrtles.

These gifts are for the poor in spirit who hunger for **divine light, forgiveness and holiness**. God's Spirit makes barren hearts fruitful so others can recognize his work.

## Idols cannot foretell or save (verses 21–29)

Often the quickest way to expose the folly of sin is to examine the reasons people give for defending it.

There is nothing in idols deserving our trust. They are worse than worthless.

Let those who offer doctrines other than **salvation through Christ** present their case. Can they provide a cure for the corruption of human nature?

Only Jehovah possesses irresistible power and certain knowledge of what lies ahead. He reveals his plans and fulfills them.

Henry contrasts biblical prophecy with all other predictions, which he regards as uncertain.

The return of captives from Babylon displayed God's power. But in **the redemption accomplished by Christ**, he revealed something far greater.

The gospel's good news—long hidden from previous ages—announces a Deliverer more glorious and powerful than the one who rescued the captive Jews.

May we belong among his **obedient servants and faithful friends**.
