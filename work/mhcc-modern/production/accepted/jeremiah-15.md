# Jeremiah 15 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The destruction of the wicked described. | 1–9 |
| The prophet laments such messages, and is reproved. | 10–14 |
| He supplicates pardon, and is promised protection. | 15–21 |

## Even great intercessors could not avert judgment (verses 1–9)

Even if **Moses and Samuel** stood before God to plead for the people, they would not prevail.

Henry emphasizes that this is **a hypothetical example**, not a statement that the prophets actually stand praying in heaven.

He argues on that basis against the belief that **saints in heaven pray for those on earth**.

The Jews would suffer different kinds of misery under the Lord's righteous judgment.

Survivors would be driven into captivity **like chaff scattered from grain**.

The city once filled with people would become desolate.

Bad examples and the misuse of authority can have **deadly consequences even after people die or repent of their crimes**.

We should fear causing another person to fall into sin.

## The prophet is despised, but God's plan stands (verses 10–14)

Jeremiah received reproach when people should have **blessed him and thanked God for giving them his ministry**.

Whatever difficulties God's people face, they may find sufficient strength in knowing **their latter end will be good**.

The Lord then confronted the nation.

Could all its strength resist **his decree** or stop **the army of the Chaldeans**?

The enemy would treat **Jeremiah** kindly while those with large estates would suffer severely.

Every part of the country had contributed to national guilt, and **each must take its share of shame**.

## The Lord calls Jeremiah away from distrust (verses 15–21)

It is comforting to have a God whose knowledge embraces **everything**.

Jeremiah appealed to him for protection against his enemies, persecutors and slanderers.

When a minister is despised, **a clear conscience** can still provide encouragement.

Yet Jeremiah complained that he had gained little pleasure from his service.

Even good people can lose much of religion's joy when they indulge **a fretful and restless natural disposition**.

God called the prophet to abandon distrust and **return to his task**.

If he remained faithful, the Lord would deliver him from his enemies.

Those who stay close to God and serve him sincerely will be **rescued from trouble or carried safely through it**.

Many events appear frightening though they can do **no final harm to a true believer in Christ**.
