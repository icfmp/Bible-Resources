# Acts 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Paul and Barnabas at Iconium. | 1–7 |
| A cripple healed at Lystra, The people would have sacrificed to Paul and Barnabas. | 8–18 |
| Paul stoned at Lystra, The churches visited again. | 19–28 |

## Paul and Barnabas preach in Iconium (verses 1–7)

The apostles spoke plainly and persuasively, with evidence of the Holy Spirit's presence and with such warmth and concern for people's souls that their listeners could recognize God's hand in their work.

Yet their success was not the achievement of their manner of preaching. **It belonged to the Spirit of God**, who worked through that preaching.

Perseverance in doing good despite danger and hardship is a blessed sign of grace.

Wherever the Lord's servants are driven, they should look for ways to proclaim the truth.

As Paul and Barnabas continued in Christ's name and strength, the Lord confirmed **the word of his grace**.

He has assured us that his word is true. We may confidently entrust our souls to it.

Jews and Gentiles were divided from one another, yet their hostility to Christians united them.

If the church's enemies combine to destroy it, **should not its friends unite to protect and strengthen it?**

God provides shelter for his people amid storms. He is their refuge and will continue to be their hiding place.

During persecution, believers may rightly **leave one location without abandoning their Master's work**.

## A man is healed at Lystra; the crowd offers worship (verses 8–18)

All things are possible to those who believe.

Faith is God's precious gift. Through it, we are freed from the spiritual helplessness into which we were born and from the sinful habits later formed.

We become able to stand upright and walk joyfully in the Lord's ways.

When **Jesus, the Son of God**, appeared in human likeness and performed many miracles, people did not respond by offering sacrifices to him.

Instead, they made him **the victim of their pride and hatred**.

Yet after Paul and Barnabas performed one miracle, the crowd wanted to worship them as gods!

The same influence of the ruler of this world that closes sinful minds against truth makes errors and false beliefs seem easy to accept.

Notice that the apostles did not tear their clothes when people talked about **stoning** them.

They did so when people talked about **worshiping** them.

**God's honor mattered more to them than their own safety.**

God's truth does not need the service of human falsehood.

His servants might easily gain improper honors by quietly tolerating other people's mistakes and sins.

But they should fear and reject that kind of respect more than criticism or disgrace.

When the apostles spoke to Jews, who already rejected idols, they principally proclaimed God's grace through Christ.

When they addressed Gentiles, they also needed to correct mistaken ideas about **natural religion and false worship**.

Compare their words with the view that worship offered to a god under any name, or in any manner, is equally acceptable to the Almighty.

Even the strongest reasoning, warmest appeals and clearest miracles barely restrain people from dreadful errors and practices.

Without **God's special grace**, none of these can turn sinners' hearts toward him and holiness.

## Paul is stoned and the churches strengthened (verses 19–28)

See how relentlessly some opponents pursued the gospel.

In a public riot they stoned Paul.

The sinful human heart is difficult to restrain from evil in one direction, yet easily persuaded toward evil in another.

If Paul had allowed himself to be called **Mercury**, the people would have worshiped him.

When he insisted on being **a faithful servant of Christ**, they stoned him and threw him outside the town.

People who readily accept powerful delusions can stubbornly refuse to love the truth.

**Converts need to be strengthened in faith; plants need to take root.**

A minister's work is not only awakening sinners, but also establishing believers.

Only the **grace of God** can establish disciples' souls securely.

We should expect considerable suffering, yet take courage: it does not mean we will be destroyed or lost.

The One to whose power and grace the new believers and churches were commended was clearly **the Lord Jesus, in whom they had believed**.

Committing them to him was itself **an act of worship**.

Whatever little good we do, all praise belongs to God.

He works within us both to desire and to do his will, and he works alongside our efforts to make them fruitful.

Everyone who loves Jesus should rejoice when he opens **the door of faith** to people who had once been strangers to his salvation.

Like the apostles, let us remain in fellowship with those who know and love the Lord.
