# Genesis 34 — Modern English

## Dinah and Shechem (verses 1–19)

Young people, especially young women, are safest and best cared for under the protection of godly parents.

Their lack of experience exposes them to danger, as do the flattery and schemes of wicked people who deliberately set traps for them.

They place themselves at risk when they are eager to go out, especially alone, among people who do not share true faith.

Parents are seriously at fault when they fail to prevent their children from exposing themselves to needless danger.

Children who have been allowed to do whatever they please, as Henry describes Dinah, often bring grief and shame to their families.

Dinah's stated purpose was to visit the daughters of the land—to see how they dressed and danced, and what was fashionable among them.

But Henry argues that she wanted not only to see others, but also to be seen herself.

She sought the acquaintance of the Canaanites and wanted to learn their ways.

Consider what followed from Dinah's restless wandering.

The beginning of sin is like water breaking through a barrier.

How great a blaze can begin with a small flame!

We should therefore carefully avoid opportunities for sin and even the paths that lead toward it.

## Simeon and Levi's revenge against the Shechemites (verses 20–31)

The men of Shechem submitted to a sacred religious rite only because it served their immediate interests.

They wished to please their ruler and enrich themselves.

God was therefore just in bringing punishment upon them.

Nothing offers greater security than genuine religion.

But few things leave people more exposed than a mere pretense of religion.

Yet Simeon and Levi acted with the greatest injustice.

Those who do wicked things under the appearance of serving God are among the worst enemies of the truth.

They harden the hearts of many people, with destructive consequences.

Other people's sins never excuse our own.

How sadly one sin leads to another, spreading devastation like a fire!

Sinful pleasure leads to seduction.

Seduction gives rise to anger; anger develops into a thirst for revenge.

Revenge turns to deceit; deceit ends in murder; and murder is followed by further acts of lawlessness.

Henry concludes that the history of unlawful sexual relationships shows how often such wrongdoing ends in bloodshed—more often, he argues, than other kinds of sin.

## Editorial verification — Genesis 34

A separate source-meaning review was conducted against [STEP's complete rendering of CrossWire MHCC for Genesis 34](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version%3DMHCC@reference%3DGen.34). This chapter has no documented wording changes in the published `MHCC 0.1.1` source edition.

- **1–19:** the author's historical pastoral warning about young people (specifically young women), godly parental protection and responsibility, dangers of inexperience and deliberate flattery, religious outsiders, indulgence, Dinah's visit to the daughters of the land, their fashions and dancing, Henry's **inference** about her wish to be seen, and the images of released water and a small fire — all retained. The claim about Dinah's motives is explicitly attributed to the commentator rather than silently turned into an established biblical fact.
- **20–31:** the Shechemites' selfish exploitation of the rite, pleasing the ruler and gaining wealth, Henry's account of just divine judgment, genuine versus pretended religion, **Simeon and Levi's grave injustice**, abuse of religion to excuse wickedness, the fact that another person's crimes are no excuse, the fire image, every link in the escalation **pleasure → seduction → wrath → revenge → treachery → murder → further lawlessness**, and the author's concluding historical generalization — all retained and appropriately attributed.

The two original exposition sections, their verse scopes and both original outline rows remain unchanged. The manuscript records the author's interpretation rather than introducing a new editorial judgment of Dinah or rewriting the narrative. The editorial review was a distinct same-assistant pass, not external human review.
