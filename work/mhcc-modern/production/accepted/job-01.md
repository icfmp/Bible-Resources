# Job 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The piety and prosperity of Job. | 1–5 |
| Satan obtains leave to try Job. | 6–12 |
| The loss of Job's property, and the death of his children. | 13–19 |
| Job's patience and piety. | 20–22 |

## Job's godliness and prosperity (verses 1–5)

Job was wealthy, yet he was also godly.

Although it is difficult and rare for a rich person to enter God's kingdom, it is **not impossible**.

By God's grace, people can overcome the temptations that accompany wealth.

The record of Job's faith and prosperity comes before the account of his great suffering.

This shows that **neither wealth nor godliness keeps a person free from trouble**.

Job rejoiced in his children's companionship and happiness, yet his understanding of the human heart made him concerned about their spiritual condition.

He called them to be consecrated, examine their conduct, confess sin and seek forgiveness.

He offered a burnt offering for each child, trusting in acceptance through **the promised Savior**.

This reveals his concern for their souls, his understanding of human sin and his full dependence upon divine mercy in the way God had appointed.

## Satan receives permission to test Job (verses 6–12)

Job's trials began through **Satan's hostility**, under **God's permission** and for purposes that were wise and holy.

An evil spirit opposes God and all righteousness.

He continually tries to trouble, mislead and, if possible, destroy those who love God.

We do not know how far his influence extends, but Henry judges it probable that much instability and unhappiness among Christians can be attributed to his attacks.

While we remain in this world, we are within his reach.

We must therefore be sober and alert (**1 Peter 5:8**).

Satan slandered Job by suggesting something he had no reason to believe.

This is the ordinary way of people who defame others.

We should fear **actually being hypocrites** more than almost anything else.

But we need fear very little when people falsely call us hypocrites without evidence.

It is not wrong to be encouraged by the hope of eternal reward.

It is wrong, however, to practice religion merely to gain **worldly advantages**.

God specially protects his people and what belongs to them.

Even Satan recognized that the Lord's blessing had made Job prosper.

God permitted the testing of Job, just as he later allowed Peter to be sifted.

Our comfort is that God holds the devil **on a chain** (**Revelation 20:1**).

Satan cannot lead people into sin without their consenting to it.

Nor can he afflict them beyond what divine permission allows.

The conversation in this passage is described **in human terms** to teach us that the Lord rules all affairs of the world.

## Job loses his possessions and children (verses 13–19)

Satan brought these disasters upon Job when his children were gathered for one of their feasts.

The troubles arrived in rapid succession.

Before one messenger had finished reporting a disaster, another came.

Then Job learned that his **ten children** had died.

They were his dearest possessions and would have been the greatest comfort to him amid the loss of everything else.

They were taken precisely when he most needed them.

**Only in God do we have help available at every moment.**

## Job responds in humble faith (verses 20–22)

Job humbled himself before the Lord.

He considered the condition shared by every human being.

We bring none of this world's possessions into life.

Everything we have is received from others, and we must leave it to others when we die.

Job had only been returned to the condition in which he entered the world—and to which he must eventually have come.

He had been stripped of his earthly burdens **earlier than he expected**.

Taking off clothing before bedtime may be inconvenient, but it is easier to bear when the hour of rest is already close.

The One who gave these gifts had also taken them away.

Job looked **beyond the human agents and instruments** of his suffering to the First Cause.

Suffering should drive us toward God, not away from him.

If we turn to him in every difficulty, he will sustain us.

The Lord is righteous.

Everything we possess is his gift. Through sin we have forfeited any claim upon it, so we cannot accuse God of injustice if he takes some of it away.

Discontent and impatience effectively accuse God of foolishness.

Job resisted that temptation, as we must.

God has acted both justly and wisely, even though we have often acted wickedly and foolishly.

May Satan's hostility make the Savior all the more precious to us.

Christ came to destroy the devil's works and, for our salvation, suffered far more from that enemy than Job did or we can imagine.
