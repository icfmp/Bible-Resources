# Deuteronomy 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A persuasive to obedience. | 1–3 |
| An exhortation to obedience. | 4, 5 |
| Obedience taught. | 6–16 |
| General precepts, Instructions to be given to their children. | 17–25 |

## Obedience and the fear of God (verses 1–3)

In this passage, **commandments** appear to refer chiefly to the moral law, **statutes** to ceremonial regulations, and **judgments** to the law used by judges.

Moses taught the people everything God commanded him to teach—and nothing beyond it.

Ministers of Jesus Christ must likewise teach churches everything he commanded, **neither more nor less** (Matthew 28:20).

Reverence for God within the heart is the strongest motive for obedience.

We should earnestly desire that our children and grandchildren fear the Lord as we do.

Faith and righteousness strengthen the well-being of every community.

## The Lord is one; love him with everything (verses 4 and 5)

This short confession summarizes the first principles of faith and obedience.

Jehovah our God is the only living and true God.

He alone is God, and he is **one God**.

We should desire no other.

Henry sees the threefold mention of divine names and the plural form of the word for God as hints of **three divine Persons** even within this proclamation of divine unity.

This is his Trinitarian interpretation of the text, not a change to its original words.

Happy are those who have this one Lord as their God!

One inexhaustible fountain is better than a thousand empty reservoirs.

One all-sufficient God is better than a thousand insufficient friends.

The first and great commandment is to **love God** and let love motivate every duty we perform toward him.

“Give me your heart,” says the Lord.

We must love him with all our heart, soul and strength.

Henry identifies **five qualities** of this love.

**First, it is sincere.** We love God inwardly and truly, not merely with our words.

**Second, it is strong.** We give him our whole affection, because the One who is everything to us deserves everything from us.

**Third, it is supreme.** We love God above every creature and love other things in relation to him.

**Fourth, it is intelligent.** We recognize good reasons to love him with both heart and understanding.

**Fifth, it is undivided.** Because he is one, our hearts should be united in loving him.

May God's love be poured out into our hearts!

## Keeping God's word within the family (verses 6–16)

Four practices help preserve living faith in our hearts and homes.

**First, meditate.** Store God's words within your heart, so that they shape your thoughts every day.

**Second, teach children.** Repeat the words faithfully and carefully, teaching children and others under your responsibility.

**Third, speak about God reverently.** Discuss his truth with children, servants, friends and companions.

Focus on the plain teaching of Scripture and the truths that lead to peace, not endless arguments about doubtful questions.

**Fourth, read Scripture frequently.** God instructed Israelites to write words from the law on their walls and on parchment scrolls worn near their wrists.

Henry regards that literal command as binding upon Israel, while its **purpose** remains binding upon Christians.

We should use every good means to become familiar with God's word, ready to call it to mind when temptation comes or duty requires guidance.

We should never be ashamed to confess our faith or accept its authority over us.

We must be especially careful not to forget the Lord during times of comfort and prosperity.

When people receive gifts easily, they often forget the Giver.

A comfortable and secure life can tempt us to seek happiness in the world instead of in God, our only true rest.

Be watchful while life seems pleasant.

We must not test the Lord—either by despairing of his goodness while walking in obedience, **or** by presuming upon his help while deliberately departing from duty.

## Faithful obedience and the instruction of children (verses 17–25)

Moses commanded the people to keep God's laws diligently.

Neglect leads toward ruin.

But without care and diligence, we will not enjoy salvation.

Faith is both our duty and our benefit: it is our life.

Godliness promises blessings in the present life insofar as those blessings serve God's glory.

It is also our righteousness—but only **through the Mediator** can we be righteous before God.

Knowing the spiritual reach and excellence of God's law reveals the sinner's need for a Savior.

It prepares the heart to welcome salvation as a free gift.

The gospel honors the law not only because the Son of God obeyed it perfectly.

It also brings rebels and enemies back to God through **repentance, faith, forgiveness and renewing grace**, so that they may begin to love him above everything even now.

In heaven, they will love him perfectly, as the angels do.
