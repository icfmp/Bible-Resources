# 1 Corinthians 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Corinthians reproved for their contentions. | 1–4 |
| The true servants of Christ can do nothing without him. | 5–9 |
| He is the only foundation, and every one should take heed what he builds thereon. | 10–15 |
| The churches of Christ ought to be kept pure, and to be humble. | 16, 17 |
| And they should not glory in men, because ministers and all things else are theirs through Christ. | 18–23 |

## Quarreling reveals spiritual immaturity (verses 1–4)

Simple gospel truths about human sin, God's mercy, **repentance toward God** and **faith in Jesus Christ** benefit many believers more than difficult mysteries.

A person may know a great deal about Christian doctrine yet remain a beginner in **living faith and spiritual experience**.

Arguments and quarrels over religion reveal remaining worldliness and immaturity.

True religion makes people **peaceable rather than contentious**.

Sadly, many professing Christians continue behaving too much like the surrounding world.

Some believers—and even ministers—show carnality through boastful rivalry, eagerness to dispute and willingness to despise or insult others.

## Ministers are servants; only God produces growth (verses 5–9)

The ministers over whom the Corinthians fought were only **instruments God had chosen to use**.

We must never put ministers in God's place.

The person planting and the person watering belong to **one work**.

Both serve the same Lord, receive the same revelation and pursue the same purpose.

Their different gifts come from **the same Holy Spirit** and should be used in sincere cooperation.

Those who work diligently and faithfully will receive God's reward.

They cooperate in God's work to advance **his glory and the salvation of souls**.

The Lord knows their labor and will ensure it has not been wasted.

They are workers in **God's field and God's building**, both of which remain under his careful supervision.

## Christ is the only foundation (verses 10–15)

Paul was a wise builder, but **God's grace made him one**.

Spiritual pride is especially shameful because it turns the Lord's greatest gifts into fuel for our vanity and self-idolatry.

Every Christian worker must be careful.

A person can build **poorly upon a good foundation**.

Only things consistent with the foundation should be placed upon it.

We must not combine a merely worldly life with divine faith or join sinful practices to a claim of Christianity.

**Jesus Christ is the firm, unchanging Rock of Ages**.

He can bear every burden God places upon him and every burden the sinner brings to him.

**There is salvation in no one else**.

Remove **Christ's atonement**, and the foundation of Christian hope disappears.

Among those who profess to build on Christ, Henry describes **two kinds of workers**.

Some proclaim the truth as it is in Jesus and build only what agrees with that truth.

Others build things that cannot survive the coming test, though they profess the same good foundation.

We may misjudge ourselves or others.

But a day is coming when every work will be seen in its true light, without disguise.

Those whose work spreads true and pure religion will receive a reward, far greater than they deserve.

Other workers' corrupt teachings, human inventions and unauthorized religious practices will be exposed and rejected.

The **fire** that tests these works is **figurative, not literal fire**.

No ordinary flame can consume a doctrine or a religious ceremony.

The test applies to everyone's work, including **Paul and Apollos**.

Let us consider the effects of our undertakings, compare them with Scripture and judge ourselves, so that God need not condemn our work.

## God's church must remain holy (verses 16 and 17)

Elsewhere in the letter we learn that some Corinthian false teachers promoted unholy ideas.

Such instruction threatened to corrupt and destroy **God's building**, which should be preserved pure.

People spreading doctrines that encourage an unholy church bring judgment upon themselves.

**Christ dwells by his Spirit within genuine believers**.

Christians profess to be holy and should therefore seek purity in heart and conduct.

Anyone who believes themselves to be **a temple of the Holy Spirit** while remaining indifferent to personal holiness and the church's peace and purity is deceived.

## Do not boast in human wisdom or ministers (verses 18–23)

Having an exaggerated opinion of our own wisdom is **self-flattery**, and self-flattery leads quickly to self-deception.

The wisdom admired by the world is foolishness before God.

He can easily confound its supposed experts.

Even the thoughts of the most intelligent humans contain weakness, error and emptiness.

This should humble us and make us eager to learn from God.

Do not let claims of human expertise turn you from **the plain truth revealed by Christ**.

Human beings often resist the very purposes for which God blesses them.

Consider the spiritual wealth of true believers: **“All things are yours.”**

Ministers and appointed means of grace serve their good.

Even the world belongs to them in the sense that infinite divine wisdom gives them **as much of it as is good for them, together with God's blessing**.

**Life** belongs to believers as time to prepare for eternity.

**Death** belongs to them as a messenger bringing them from sin and sorrow to their Father's house.

**Present things** sustain them during the journey.

**Future things** will delight them forever at its end.

If we belong to Christ, all truly good things are secure for us.

Believers are subjects of his kingdom.

He is our Lord, and we ought to accept his authority gladly.

**God in Christ reconciling sinful people to himself and pouring out grace upon them** is the heart of the gospel.
