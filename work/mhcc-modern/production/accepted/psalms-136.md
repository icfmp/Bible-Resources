# Psalm 136 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God to be praised as the Creator of the world. | 1–9 |
| As Israel's God and Saviour. | 10–22 |
| For his blessings to all. | 23–26 |

## God's everlasting mercy in creation (verses 1–9)

We are so forgetful that we need to hear essential truths repeatedly. Here every refrain reminds us that **God's mercy endures forever**.

Mercy means more than a general disposition to kindness. It is **the Lord's willingness to rescue people made miserable and vile through sin** and includes everything he has provided to redeem sinners through **Jesus Christ**.

The purposes of that mercy are eternal. Its saving effects endure forever for those who receive it.

God remains as ready to show mercy to those who seek him as he ever was. This is the foundation of our comfort and hope.

## God's saving mercy to Israel (verses 10–22)

The Lord's extraordinary works in bringing Israel out of Egypt were mercies whose benefits continued for generations.

The greater redemption accomplished by **Christ**, which those events pictured, endures forever.

We should examine the history of God's favors carefully, remembering in each event that **his mercy lasts forever**.

He brought Israel into a fruitful land. This too served as a picture of the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ.

## Mercy in every gift and all our praise (verses 23–26)

God's everlasting mercy is praised here in the deliverance of his church, in all his glorious works and in all the gifts he provides.

Blessed be God, who has made known and provided salvation through his Son.

May we know and experience **his redeeming power**, so that we serve him in righteousness throughout our lives.

The Lord gives food to every living creature. Let us ask him to **feed our souls with eternal life** and stir up our affections through grace, so that we thank and praise his holy name.

Trace every blessing to its true source, and keep offering him praise, for **his mercy endures forever**.
