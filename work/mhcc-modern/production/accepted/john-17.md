# John 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Christ prays concerning his own glorification (verses 1–5)

Jesus prayed as **a man and the Mediator of his people**, yet spoke with the majesty and authority of One who is **equal to the Father**.

Believers could not receive eternal life unless Christ, their **Surety**, had glorified the Father and been glorified by him.

The sinner's way to everlasting life is found in this Mediator. When our knowledge of God is perfected in heaven, holiness and happiness will be perfectly enjoyed.

Christ and the Father are especially glorified by **the holiness and happiness of the redeemed**.

For the joy set before him—his people brought to salvation—Jesus endured the cross and despised its shame.

When he obtained this, the purpose of his soul's suffering was fulfilled and he was satisfied.

We learn that glorifying God is a necessary **evidence** that we belong to Christ, through whom eternal life is **God's free gift**.

## Christ prays for the disciples given to him (verses 6–10)

Jesus prays for those who belong to him.

The Father has given them to Christ like **sheep to a shepherd for protection**, **a patient to a physician for healing** and **children to a teacher for instruction**.

Jesus will faithfully return the charge entrusted to him.

Our confidence in Christ rests on this: everything he is, has, says and does, and everything he will yet accomplish, comes from God.

In this prayer he particularly intercedes for **his believing people**, rather than the world considered as a whole.

But no person who desires to come to God, recognizing their own unworthiness, should be discouraged by that distinction.

Jesus is **willing and able to save completely everyone who comes to God through him**.

Earnest conviction of sin and desire for his salvation are hopeful signs that grace is already working—evidence of God's choice through **the Spirit's sanctification and faith in the truth**.

Christ's disciples belong to the Father. He will care for his own.

The ground of Jesus' request is **“All mine are yours, and yours are mine.”** This expresses the Father and Son's unity.

The Son claims no one as his own who is not also devoted to his Father's service.

## Christ prays for his disciples and all believers (verses 11–26)

### Protection from the evil of this world (verses 11–16)

Jesus does not ask that his followers become **wealthy and powerful**.

He asks that they be protected from sin, strengthened for duty and brought safely to heaven.

**The soul's prosperity is the best prosperity.**

Christ appeals to the holy Father to preserve the disciples by his power and for his glory, keeping them united in love and work according to the Father and Son's union.

Nor does he ask that they be taken out of the world to escape human hostility.

They have work to accomplish for God's glory and other people's good.

Instead, he asks that they be kept from **the evil one**, from corruption through the world, from remaining sin within themselves and from Satan's cunning and power.

They must pass through this world like travelers through hostile territory, as Jesus himself did.

They were not left on earth to pursue the same ends as those around them, but to glorify God and serve their generation.

The Spirit present in genuine Christians is opposed to the spirit of this world.

### Sanctification through truth (verses 17–19)

Jesus prays not only that his people be kept from evil but **that they be made holy**.

Even disciples must seek sanctifying grace.

The instrument is **God's truth: his word is truth**.

To be sanctified is to be set apart for God and his service.

Christ asks the Father to accept their work and accompany them in fulfilling it.

Jesus devoted himself entirely to the task the Father gave him, especially offering himself without blemish to God through the eternal Spirit.

The holiness of genuine believers comes through Christ's death, which purchased **the gift of the Holy Spirit**.

He gave himself for his church **to sanctify it**.

If our understanding of divine truth never produces holiness, either our understanding is mistaken or we have received truths as **mere ideas rather than living and active faith**.

### Unity among all who will believe (verses 20–23)

Jesus prays especially that believers be **one body under one Head, animated by one Spirit**, through their union with Christ and with the Father in him.

The more Christians quarrel about lesser matters, the more doubt they cast upon Christianity.

Let us seek the **unity of the Spirit in peace**, praying that believers grow increasingly united in understanding and judgment.

Such unity will testify to the truth and excellence of the gospel and deepen our fellowship with God and other believers.

### The redeemed behold Christ's glory (verses 24–26)

As One with the Father, Jesus asks that everyone given to him, including people who would believe later, be brought to heaven.

There the whole redeemed company will behold his glory as their beloved **Friend and Brother** and find happiness in him.

Jesus has made known and will continue making known God's name and character through **his teaching and his Spirit**.

The result is that the Father's love for the Son may abide with those joined to Christ.

United to him by one Spirit, they will be filled with all God's fullness and enjoy **blessedness far beyond what we can now imagine**.
