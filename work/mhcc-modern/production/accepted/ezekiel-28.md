# Ezekiel 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The sentence against the prince or king of Tyre. | 1–19 |
| The fall of Zidon. | 20–23 |
| The restoration of Israel. | 24–26 |

## The king of Tyre is humbled for pride (verses 1–19)

Henry identifies the prince or king of Tyre as **Ethbaal, or Ithobal**.

Lifted up by excessive pride, this ruler claimed honors belonging to God.

Pride is especially characteristic of **fallen human nature**.

No wisdom except that which God gives can bring lasting happiness, whether in this life or the life to come.

The proud ruler imagined that he could protect his people through his own power and regarded himself as equal to the inhabitants of heaven.

Yet even if we could live in **the garden of Eden** or enter **heaven itself**, we would find no true happiness without **a humble, holy and spiritual heart**.

Spiritual pride in particular comes from the devil.

Those who cherish it must expect destruction.

## Sidon is judged and Israel promised restoration (verses 20–26)

The people of **Sidon** lived near Israel.

They could have learned to glorify the Lord.

Instead, they led Israelites into the worship of their own idols.

**War and disease** can be instruments of divine judgment.

But God will also reveal his glory by restoring his people to **security and well-being**.

He will heal them of their sins and relieve their suffering.

This promise will find its complete fulfillment in **the heavenly Canaan**, where all the saints are gathered, everything offensive is removed, and every grief and fear ends forever.

Blessed are God's church and all who truly belong to it, even while they are **poor, afflicted or despised**.

The Lord will reveal **his truth, power and mercy** through the salvation and happiness of his redeemed people.
