## John the Baptist preaches repentance (verses 1–6)

After Malachi, no prophet appeared until **John the Baptist**.

He began his public ministry in **the wilderness of Judea**, an area not densely populated or extensively settled, rather than an entirely uninhabited desert.

No place is so remote that divine grace cannot reach it.

John preached **repentance**.

The word means a comprehensive change of mind: a new understanding, disposition, set of affections and direction for the soul.

Consider your ways.

You have thought wrongly; think again, and think rightly.

True repentance changes how we think about **God and Christ**, about **sin and holiness**, and about **this life and eternity**.

A change of mind produces **a changed way of living**.

Gospel repentance springs from seeing Christ, recognizing his love and hoping for pardon through him.

We are invited to repent because God promises forgiveness to those who do.

Turn toward him through obedience, and **through Christ he receives you in mercy**.

Repentance and humility are still necessary if a person is to welcome the Lord.

To prepare a soul for Christ, much spiritual work is needed.

We must recognize sin and be convinced that **our own righteousness cannot save us**.

The ways of Satan and sin are crooked.

The path prepared for Christ must be made straight.

## John warns the Pharisees and Sadducees (verses 7–12)

A sermon comes alive when its truths are applied directly to the consciences of the listeners.

John did this.

The **Pharisees** chiefly relied upon outward forms of religion, neglecting the weightier requirements of the moral law and the spiritual meaning of ceremonies.

Some were outright hypocrites who used a pretense of holiness as a covering for wrongdoing.

The **Sadducees** took the opposite extreme: they denied spirits and a future state, showing contempt for the truths others professed.

**There is a coming wrath**, and everyone needs to flee from it.

God does not delight in human ruin.

He warns through **the written word, ministers and conscience**.

People who say they regret their sins but continue deliberately committing them do not possess genuine repentance.

True penitents are humble, grateful for small mercies, patient under great suffering, watchful against every appearance of sin, faithful in their duties and charitable toward others.

We must not trust in **external religious privileges**.

The human heart can invent many excuses to evade the convincing and commanding power of God's word.

Many people rest on church membership and outward honors but never enter heaven.

The warning is meant to awaken the careless.

Only **the regenerating Spirit of Christ** can make a corrupt heart produce good fruit.

Every tree without **fruit appropriate to repentance** will eventually be cut down and cast into judgment, regardless of its outward gifts, reputation or impressive appearance.

If a tree does not produce fruit, it is fit only for fuel.

John's preaching also announced the purpose of the Messiah's arrival.

**No outward ordinance, no matter who administers it or by what method, can replace the baptism of the Holy Spirit and fire.**

Only the Spirit's cleansing power can make a heart pure and produce the holy affections that accompany salvation.

**Christ alone baptizes with the Holy Spirit**.

He did so through the remarkable gifts bestowed upon the apostles.

The secondary witness prints a reference to **Isaiah 21:10** near this statement; its original source context should be checked separately, without altering the pinned module.

Believers are like **wheat**—substantial, useful and precious.

Hypocrites are like **chaff**—empty and easily blown about.

They are presently mixed within the same outward religious community.

But the final judgment will separate them completely.

In heaven, believers will be gathered safely, free from corrupt neighbors and from the remaining corruption within their own hearts.

There will be no chaff among them.

Hell is the unquenchable fire that awaits impenitent unbelief and hypocrisy.

Life and death, good and evil, are set before us.

How we stand before God now determines which side of the final separation we will enter.

## Jesus is baptized by John (verses 13–17)

The humility and kindness of Christ are so extraordinary that even mature believers may initially struggle to understand them.

Even people who know his will may raise objections to his way of acting.

Those who have received much of God's Spirit should recognize their continued need to seek **more grace from Christ**.

Jesus did not deny that John needed the baptism Christ could give.

Yet Jesus insisted that **John should now baptize him**, because the Savior had entered a state of humiliation.

It was appropriate for him to **fulfill all righteousness**.

He acknowledged every divine institution and willingly obeyed God's righteous commands.

Through Christ, heaven is opened to sinful humanity.

The Holy Spirit descended upon him, showing that he received the Spirit's holy influences **without measure**.

The Spirit produces **love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control**.

At Jesus' baptism, **the three Persons of the Holy Trinity** were made known.

The **Father** affirmed the Son as Mediator.

The **Son** solemnly entered upon the work of redemption.

The **Holy Spirit** descended upon him, through whose mediation the Spirit would be given to his people.

Our spiritual sacrifices are acceptable through Christ, because he is the altar that sanctifies every gift (**1 Peter 2:5**).

Apart from Christ, God is a consuming fire.

**In Christ, God is a reconciled Father.**

This is the gospel's message, which we must receive cheerfully through faith.
