# Proverbs 31 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An exhortation to king Lemuel to take heed of sin, and to do duties. | 1–9 |
| The description of a virtuous woman. | 10–31 |

## Lemuel's mother's instructions (verses 1–9)

While children are under a mother's care, she has an important opportunity to shape their minds in the right way.

Even after growing up, people should often remember the faithful lessons they received during childhood.

Many young people who once appeared promising have been ruined through immoral relationships or love of wine. Their example warns us against these dangers.

Wine, Henry says, can properly be used **in need or as medicine**. Every creature of God is good, and wine has legitimate purposes, although people often abuse it.

The same principle applies figuratively to **praise and consolation**.

Encouragement is like a restorative medicine when offered to people who are downcast, struggling or tempted. It is not appropriate to flatter someone already self-satisfied and overconfident.

People in positions of authority should practice **even greater temperance than others**.

They should also defend people who cannot or dare not speak for themselves.

Our blessed Lord did not refuse even **the most bitter suffering** in the cup his Father appointed him.

Yet he places **the cup of consolation** in the hands of his people and brings joy to those experiencing the deepest grief.

## The virtuous woman (verses 10–31)

This passage describes **a virtuous woman living in its own historical setting**. Henry says that its broad principles are appropriate for **every age and nation**.

She carefully seeks her husband's respect and affection, understands his wishes and willingly accepts his authority. This reflects Henry's original account of marital relationships, rather than a new editorial judgment about social expectations.

Henry then describes **eight qualities**.

**1. Trustworthiness and devotion to her husband's good.** Her husband can confidently entrust his household to her care. Her constant intention is to do him good and bring happiness to their home.

**2. Industry and joy in duty.** She works diligently and takes pleasure in her responsibilities. She makes good use of time, rises early, does her work energetically and refuses to waste her days.

**3. Prudent household management.** She makes wise use of what her work produces, does not spend beyond the family's means and provides thoughtfully for current and future needs.

**4. Responsible leadership of the household.** She watches over the affairs of her household so that everyone can fulfill duties toward God, one another and herself.

**5. Cheerful generosity.** She is as ready to give as to receive, helping others freely and gladly.

**6. Wise and gracious speech.** She acts with discretion, governs her words by wisdom and advises others carefully. **The law of kindness and love is written upon her heart**, and it is evident in the way she speaks.

Even while her hands are occupied with earthly responsibilities, **her heart is filled with the world to come**.

**7. Reverence for the Lord.** More important than every other quality is her fear of God. Beauty alone proves neither wisdom nor goodness and can mislead people who choose a spouse on that basis. But reverence for God is **the beauty of the soul**, a beauty that endures forever.

**8. Strength to endure hardship.** She can bear disappointments and trials with courage. When she grows old, she can look back with comfort upon a youth devoted to useful work, and she can rejoice in the hope of the coming life.

She is a great blessing to her family.

Good fruit deserves a good reputation, but she does not need to praise herself: **her work speaks on her behalf**.

Everyone should seek **the honor that comes from God** and evaluate others by that standard.

Henry urges women who wish to be respected, loved and useful to study this picture regularly.

He also invites a **spiritual application to the church**.

Scripture describes God's church as **a virtuous bride**.

Through grace, God gathers a church of true believers from among sinful human beings and forms in it the excellent qualities described here.
