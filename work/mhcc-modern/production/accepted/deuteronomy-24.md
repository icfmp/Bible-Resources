# Deuteronomy 24 — Modern English

## Divorce and the responsibility to bear hardship (verses 1–4)

Sometimes God's providence, or a person's own unwise choice in marriage, brings a Christian into a painful relationship rather than the companionship hoped for.

Such a person should sincerely prefer to bear the cross rather than seek a form of relief that leads toward sin, disorder and suffering.

God's grace can make that burden holy, sustain the person under it and teach conduct that gradually makes the situation more bearable.

## Marriage, kidnapping and consideration for debtors (verses 5–13)

It is of great importance that love between a husband and wife be preserved.

Both should carefully avoid whatever might cause them to become distant or estranged from each other.

Kidnapping people and enslaving them was a **capital crime**, not an ordinary theft whose wrong could be settled merely by restitution.

The laws concerning leprosy also had to be followed carefully.

When we recognize guilt and fear God's judgment, we should not conceal the truth or try to shake off our convictions.

Instead, through repentance, prayer and humble confession, we should seek the path of forgiveness and peace.

God also gave regulations about the possessions a lender could hold as security for a loan.

We should consider another person's necessities and comfort as seriously as our own financial advantage.

Let a poor debtor keep the garment needed for sleeping and thank God for your kindness.

Debtors should recognize the generosity of creditors who refuse to take every legal advantage available to them.

Such restraint should not be regarded as weakness.

## Justice and generosity toward those in need (verses 14–22)

Purity, reverence for God, justice, mercy, fair dealing, kindness to poor and vulnerable people, consideration for their needs and generosity of spirit are plainly pleasing to God.

These virtues are appropriate for everyone he has redeemed.

The difficulty lies not in proving their value, but in putting them consistently into practice in our everyday conduct.
