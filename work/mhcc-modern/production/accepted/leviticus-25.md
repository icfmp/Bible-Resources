## The seventh-year rest for the land (verses 1–7)

Just as ordinary work stopped on the seventh day, the ordinary cultivation of the land was to cease during the seventh year.

These regulations warned Israel against greed.

A person's life does not consist in owning many possessions.

We should willingly depend on God's providence for our support.

We are his tenants and stewards; our possessions should be used as he directs.

The year of rest pictured the spiritual rest all believers enter through Christ.

He lifts the burden of worldly anxiety and weariness.

Our ordinary work and concerns become more bearable and more useful when they are sanctified by him.

Through Christ we are enabled and encouraged to live by faith.

## The Jubilee and the prohibition of oppression (verses 8–22)

The word *jubilee* refers to an especially joyful sound of silver trumpets.

That sound was heard on the evening of the great Day of Atonement.

The connection showed that the proclamation of freedom and salvation through the gospel flows from the Redeemer's sacrifice.

Israelite land could not be permanently sold away from the family to which it belonged.

A sale was more like a lease lasting until the next Jubilee, when the land returned to its family.

This protected the separate identity of tribes and families until the Messiah's coming.

Likewise, anyone who had lost personal liberty could regain it at the Jubilee.

The arrangement pictured Christ's redemption from slavery to sin and Satan, and the renewed freedom of God's children.

The principle governing every sale was that no one should oppress or exploit another.

We must not take advantage of a neighbor's ignorance or desperate need.

Fear of God should restrain injustice in speech and action.

The people were promised that observing the appointed years of rest would not leave them poorer.

If we are faithful to our duty, we can entrust our needs and comfort to God.

His provision for Israel during the fallow year was a miracle intended to encourage his people in every age to trust him.

We lose nothing by faith and self-denial when we obey.

Some Israelites asked what they would eat during the seventh year.

Christians also worry about future difficulties and hesitate to follow duty because they anticipate suffering.

But we should not torment ourselves with imagined future troubles.

Our obedience may appear foolish to people concerned only with this world's interests.

Even so, the path of duty remains the path of safety.

## Redemption of land and houses (verses 23–34)

If land had not been redeemed before the Jubilee, it then returned to the original seller or that person's family.

This was a picture of God's free grace in Christ.

Through that grace—not through a payment or merit of our own—we are restored to God's favor.

Houses inside walled cities were more directly the result of human labor than farmland, which was a gift of God's generosity.

A person who sold a house within such a city therefore had only one year to buy it back.

These provisions also encouraged foreigners and converts to settle among Israel.

## Caring for people who have become poor (verses 35–38)

Poverty and financial decline are common sources of deep suffering.

The poor will always be among us.

We should help them by **sympathy**, showing genuine compassion; by **service**, doing practical work for them; and by **material help**, giving according to their needs and our ability.

We must not exploit poor people who owe us money.

The law gives two reasons to restrain oppressive lending: **fear God**, and **help the poor remain able to live among you**.

Those who struggle financially can still contribute to the community.

Rich and poor need one another.

People who have received mercy should show mercy.

## Laws for debt servants (verses 39–55)

An Israelite sold into service because of debt or wrongdoing was, in the situation Henry describes, to serve six years and be released in the seventh.

Someone who sold his own labor because of poverty had to be treated in a way appropriate to a descendant of Abraham.

Masters are required to treat their servants fairly and justly (Colossians 4:1).

At the Jubilee, the servant and the servant's children were to go free and return to the family.

This pictured our redemption from slavery to sin and Satan through God's grace in Christ.

His truth makes us free (John 8:32).

We cannot buy the freedom of another sinner's soul.

But we can point people to Christ.

Through his grace, our lives can honor his gospel, express love, show thankfulness and glorify his holy name.
