# Matthew 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The transfiguration of Christ. | 1–13 |
| Jesus casts out a dumb and deaf spirit. | 14–21 |
| He again foretells his sufferings. | 22, 23 |
| He works a miracle to pay the tribute money. | 24–27 |

## Jesus is transfigured before his disciples (verses 1–13)

The disciples were permitted to see something of **Christ's glory as the Father's only Son**.

This would strengthen their faith when they later witnessed his crucifixion.

It also gave them a glimpse of the glory prepared for believers when Christ changes them and makes them like himself.

The apostles were overwhelmed by the sight.

Peter wanted to remain on the mountain rather than descend to face the suffering he had been so reluctant to hear about.

But he did not understand what he was asking.

We are mistaken when we expect **heaven's enjoyment here on earth**.

Whatever temporary dwelling places or comforts we propose to build in this world, we should seek Christ's direction.

The great sacrifice by which sinful human beings could be saved **had not yet been offered**.

Peter and the other disciples also had important work still to do.

A bright cloud overshadowed them as Peter spoke, representing **God's presence and glory**.

Ever since humanity sinned and heard God's voice in Eden, extraordinary displays of God's presence have caused sinful people to tremble.

The disciples fell to the ground.

Jesus comforted them, and when they looked up again they saw their familiar Lord.

Our way to glory includes many different experiences, both extraordinary and ordinary.

When we return to daily life after sacred worship or an unusually encouraging experience, we must **take Christ with us**.

Then we may find comfort knowing that he remains with us.

## Jesus delivers a suffering child (verses 14–21)

Children's suffering should move us to present their needs to God in **earnest and believing prayer**.

Jesus healed the child.

Although the surrounding people were unbelieving and perverse and gave Christ reason for displeasure, **the child was not neglected**.

When every other form of help fails, we may still come to Christ and trust his power and goodness.

This healing also illustrates Jesus' saving work as **Redeemer**.

Parents should bring to him children whose souls are held captive through Satan's influence.

He is both able and willing to heal them.

Bring them not merely through personal prayer but also through **Christ's word**, the appointed means by which Satan's spiritual strongholds are broken down.

It is good to distrust **our own power**.

But it displeases Christ when we distrust **power he has promised or supplied**.

The child's condition presented unusual difficulty.

Satan's strength should not discourage our faith but should move us toward deeper prayer for stronger faith.

If we are astonished by bodily demonic oppression beginning in childhood, should we not also consider the **spiritual bondage** of fallen humanity ever since Adam's sin?

## Jesus predicts his death and resurrection again (verses 22 and 23)

Christ knew perfectly everything he would suffer.

Yet he willingly undertook our redemption.

How great was his love!

His earthly life combined **outward humiliation** with **divine glory**.

His humiliation led finally to exaltation.

Let us learn to endure the cross, regard wealth and earthly status as insignificant beside Christ, and remain content with his will.

## Jesus supplies the temple tax (verses 24–27)

Peter was confident that his Master would do what was right.

Jesus spoke before Peter could explain the situation, showing that **no thought was hidden from him**.

We must never neglect duty simply because obeying might offend someone.

But there are times when we should **give up a legitimate personal advantage** to avoid causing unnecessary offense.

Only the One who knows all things could know that the required coin was in the fish.

Only almighty power could bring the fish to Peter's hook.

We should remember **Christ's power and his poverty together**.

If Providence appoints poverty for us, as it did for our Lord, we may trust his power.

God will supply our true needs according to his glorious riches in Jesus Christ.

Peter received help while obeying, in the course of his ordinary occupation.

We too may expect divine help along the way of faithful work.

If an unexpected need arises for which we have no provision, let us **seek Christ first** rather than immediately depend on other people.
