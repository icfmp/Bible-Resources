# Ezekiel 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Threatenings against hypocrites. | 1–11 |
| God's purpose to punish the guilty Jews, but a few should be saved. | 12–23 |

## Idols in the heart make outward worship empty (verses 1–11)

No outward religious ceremony or reform can be acceptable to God while **an idol rules the heart**.

Yet many people prefer their own inventions and supposed righteousness to **God's appointed way of salvation**.

Sinful desires and corrupt habits become **idols people set up within themselves**.

God may let them continue down that path.

Sin makes a person hateful in the sight of the **pure and holy God**.

It also becomes hateful to the sinner when **conscience finally awakens**.

Let us seek to be cleansed from both **the guilt and the pollution of sin** in the fountain God has opened.

## Judgment and the limits of others' righteousness (verses 12–23)

**National sins bring national judgments.**

A sinner who escapes one judgment may still encounter another.

When people who publicly profess God's name rebel against him, they have reason to expect his judgment.

**Noah's** faith, obedience and prayer helped save his household, but did not save **the whole world**.

**Job's** offerings and prayers for his friends were accepted.

**Daniel's** intercession helped preserve his companions and the wise men of Babylon.

Yet a people who have reached the full measure of wickedness cannot expect to escape God's announced judgment simply because **righteous people live among them**.

Even the holiest believers are accepted personally only **through Christ's suffering and righteousness**.

Nevertheless, even amid the most widespread devastation, God preserves some people as **living examples of his mercy**.

We may trust that we will ultimately recognize **the wisdom and justice of all his dealings**, both with ourselves and with humanity.

Let this confidence quiet our rebellious murmuring and objections.
