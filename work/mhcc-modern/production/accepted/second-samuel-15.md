# 2 Samuel 15 — Modern English

## Absalom's ambition (verses 1–6)

David allowed Absalom to display his wealth and rank.

Parents who encourage pride in their children do not understand the harm they may be causing.

Many young people are ruined by pride.

And people most eager to obtain authority often understand its duties least.

## Absalom conspires against David (verses 7–12)

Loving parents naturally want to believe the best about their children.

How wicked it is for children to exploit that trust and deceive them through an **appearance of religion**!

Some prominent men of Jerusalem attended Absalom's celebration connected with a sacrifice.

Godly people are pleased to see others displaying devotion.

That very willingness to encourage religion can make them vulnerable to deception.

The schemes of wicked people and the cunning of Satan can draw good people into supporting evil plans.

## David leaves Jerusalem (verses 13–23)

David decided to leave Jerusalem.

He accepted the flight as a **penitent submitting to God's discipline**.

Before an unrighteous opponent like Absalom, David could have defended himself and stood his ground.

But before the righteous God, he had to condemn his own sin and submit to the judgment following it.

He therefore accepted its consequence.

Godly people also worry about other people's suffering when trouble comes upon themselves.

David did not force anyone to go with him.

Those who preferred Absalom could join him, and they would share in his fate.

In the same way, Christ does not force people to become his followers.

David especially wanted to protect **Ittai**, a foreign exile and recent convert, from hardship.

He thought Ittai should receive encouragement and kindness, not danger.

But Ittai valued David's wisdom and goodness so highly that he refused to leave him.

A true friend loves at all times and remains loyal through adversity.

Let us cling wholeheartedly to the Son of David.

Neither life nor death can separate us from his love.

## David sends the ark back (verses 24–30)

David cared deeply about the ark's safety.

It is right to care more about the **prosperity of Christ's church** than our own comfort.

We should prefer the success of the gospel over our wealth, reputation, ease and even safety.

Notice how willingly David placed his circumstances into God's hands.

Accepting the Lord's will is both our duty and our best interest.

Recognize his hand in every event.

Then, because you know that every event rests under his authority, you need not be afraid of the future.

David's sin was always before him (**Psalm 51:3**).

Yet it had never seemed as evil to him as during this humiliation.

He had never wept like this when Saul pursued him.

**A wounded conscience makes suffering heavier** (**Psalm 38:4**).

## David prays about Ahithophel's counsel (verses 31–37)

David prayed **against Ahithophel's counsel, not against the man himself**.

He trusted that God controls the hearts and words of human beings.

Yet he also worked to oppose that counsel.

**Prayer must be joined with lawful effort**.

Otherwise we would be testing God.

No ordinary human being combines wisdom and simplicity so perfectly that nothing remains needing forgiveness.

But when the **Son of David** experienced every possible form of betrayal and cruelty, his wisdom, gentleness, honesty and patience remained perfect.

Let us follow him, hold to him and serve him in life and in death.
