# Exodus 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God gives Moses power to work miracles. | 1–9 |
| Moses is loth to be sent, Aaron is to assist him. | 10–17 |
| Moses leaves Midian, God's message to Pharaoh. | 18–23 |
| God's displeasure against Moses, Aaron meets him, The people believe them. | 24–31 |

## God gives Moses miraculous signs (verses 1–9)

Moses feared that the people would not believe him unless he showed them a sign.

God therefore gave him power to perform miracles.

But those now called to deliver God's messages to others do not need miraculous powers to prove their mission.

Their character and teaching must be tested by the word of God to which they appeal.

These signs also pointed especially toward the miracles of Jesus Christ.

Only Christ could drive the devil's power out of the soul and heal the soul from the leprosy of sin.

It was therefore fitting that he should first cast the devil out of a person's body and heal bodily leprosy.

## Moses hesitates, and Aaron is appointed to help (verses 10–17)

Moses continued to shrink from the work God had appointed for him.

His reluctance included cowardice, laziness, and unbelief.

But we should never judge people's worth by how easily they speak.

A slow tongue may accompany great wisdom and genuine goodness.

God sometimes chooses people with few natural advantages or speaking skills to deliver his message.

Then the glory of his grace becomes more visible.

Christ's disciples were not gifted public speakers until the Holy Spirit enabled them.

God graciously answered Moses' excuse.

Yet a lack of confidence in ourselves can be very displeasing to God when it keeps us from doing our duty or hinders us while doing it.

As we criticize Moses for drawing back from such dangerous service, let us ask whether we are neglecting duties that are much easier and less risky.

Aaron's speaking ability, joined with Moses' understanding and devotion, would make them fully equipped for the task.

God promised, “I will be with your mouth and with his mouth.”

Even Aaron, who spoke well, could say nothing truly effective unless God continually taught and helped him.

Without the ongoing help of divine grace, even our finest natural gifts will fail.

## Moses leaves Midian; God warns Pharaoh (verses 18–23)

After appearing to Moses at the burning bush, God often spoke to him.

Pharaoh had already hardened his own heart against the cries and groans of the oppressed Israelites.

Now God, in righteous judgment, would harden Pharaoh's heart against the evidence of miracles and the terror of the plagues.

But whether Pharaoh listened or refused, Moses had to declare, “This is what the Lord says.”

He had to demand Israel's release.

“Let my son go.”

Israel was not merely God's servant—whom Pharaoh had no right to hold captive—but God's son.

This was God's son who served him and must therefore be released and protected.

If Pharaoh refused, the Lord would strike down Pharaoh's own son, even his firstborn.

People should expect to be treated as they treat God's people.

## Moses obeys, and Israel receives the message (verses 24–31)

God met Moses in anger.

The Lord threatened him with death—or sent illness upon him—as punishment for failing to circumcise his son.

Whenever God shows us something wrong in our lives, we should be diligent to correct it without delay.

Every painful correction carries this call: return to the One who disciplines you.

God sent Aaron to meet Moses.

The more clearly they recognized God's hand in bringing them together, the happier their meeting became.

Israel's elders met them with faith and were ready to obey.

Work done according to God's will and for his glory often turns out to have fewer difficulties than we feared.

Let us rise and begin the work God has given us.

The Lord will be with us and will make our efforts prosper.

If the Israelites welcomed the good news of their deliverance and worshiped the Lord, how much more should we welcome the joyful news of redemption, receive it by faith, and adore our Redeemer!
