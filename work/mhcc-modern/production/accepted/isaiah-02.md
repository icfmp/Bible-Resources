# Isaiah 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The conversion of the Gentiles, Description of the sinfulness of Israel. | 1–9 |
| The awful punishment of unbelievers. | 10–22 |

## The nations called to God's light (verses 1–9)

Isaiah foretells **the calling of the Gentiles**, the spread of the gospel and a still wider proclamation yet to come.

Christians should encourage and strengthen one another.

God teaches his people through **his word and Spirit**.

Christ advances **peace as well as holiness**. If every human being were a genuine Christian, war would no longer exist.

But the complete fulfillment of this vision has not yet appeared in the world.

Whatever others do, let us walk in **the light of that peace**.

When true faith flourishes, people delight in gathering for worship and urge others to join them.

We should be cautious about making those who do not know God our closest guides and companions; we easily imitate the habits of people with whom we spend time.

God does not condemn people simply for owning silver or gold, horses or chariots.

The wrong lies in **depending upon possessions and military strength**, as though they guaranteed safety, comfort and happiness or as though we could have none without them.

Sin disgraces people of every rank.

Countries that describe themselves as Christian may no longer be filled with literal idols. Yet they can be full of **wealth treated as a god**.

When people are consumed with their gains and pleasures, they forget or despise God's truth and commandments.

## Human pride humbled before the Lord (verses 10–22)

Henry regards the **Chaldean conquest of Jerusalem** as the first historical judgment in view, during which Jewish idolatry was brought low.

Yet the passage also turns our attention toward **the final destruction of Christ's enemies**.

People who face God's judgment cannot hide or find safe shelter from it.

The shaking of the earth will terrify those who have attached their hearts to earthly things.

Human arrogance will be brought low, either **through God's grace convincing people of pride's evil** or **through Providence removing the things in which they took pride**.

The Lord's day of judgment comes against everything in which sinners place their trust.

Those unwilling to listen to warning and reason may eventually be forced to recognize their danger through terror.

People who make money their god will discover it has become a burden.

Henry also applies this passage to an awakened sinner willing to surrender everything to receive salvation.

The Jews had been inclined to depend on neighboring nations instead of the Lord.

We face the same temptation to depend upon mortal human beings.

**Let no person become your ultimate fear or your ultimate hope. Place your hope in the Lord your God.**
