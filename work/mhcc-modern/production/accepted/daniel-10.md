# Daniel 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Daniel's vision near the river Hiddekel. | 1–9 |
| He is to expect a discovery of future events. | 10–21 |

## Daniel sees a glorious figure by the Hiddekel (verses 1–9)

This chapter begins **Daniel's final vision**, which continues to the end of the book.

A long period would pass before all its events were fulfilled, and Henry says that **much remained unfulfilled in his own day**.

Henry identifies the glorious figure appearing to Daniel as **Christ**.

That appearance should lead us to think of him with **great honor and reverence**.

We should marvel that he humbled himself **for our salvation**.

Daniel lost all strength before the vision.

Even the greatest and most godly human beings cannot bear **an unrestricted revelation of divine glory**.

No one can see it and live.

But glorified believers will see **Christ as he is** and will be able to endure and enjoy that sight.

Even when Christ seems terrifying to people convicted of sin, his word contains enough comfort **to calm their hearts**.

## The messenger explains future events (verses 10–21)

Whenever we draw near to God in fellowship, we should remember **the immeasurable distance between sinful creatures and the holy Lord**.

We are dust and ashes. How can we speak before the Lord of glory?

Nothing more effectively revives discouraged believers than the assurance that **God loves them**.

From the first day we turn toward him in duty, he is ready to meet us **in mercy**.

He is eager to hear prayer.

After describing coming events, the angel was to return and oppose measures by Persian kings that would harm **the Jewish people**.

Angels serve God as his messengers and helpers (**Hebrews 1:14**).

The rulers of Persia would do much evil against the Jews with God's permission.

Yet they would have done far greater harm if **God had not prevented it**.

The messenger would explain God's purposes more fully, though the prophecies give only **their outline**.

We should study what is written in **the Scriptures of truth**, because they concern our eternal peace.

**Satan, his angels and evil advisers** may incite rulers to harm the church.

We may rejoice that **Christ our Prince and his powerful angels** act against our enemies.

But we should not expect this hostile world to provide us with many supporters.

Nevertheless, **all God's purposes will stand**.

Let each of us pray:

**Lord Jesus, be our righteousness now, and you will be our everlasting confidence—in life, in death, at the day of judgment and forever.**
