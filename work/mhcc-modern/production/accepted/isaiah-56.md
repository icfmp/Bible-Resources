# Isaiah 56 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A charge to keep the Divine precepts. | 1, 2 |
| Blessings promised. | 3–8 |
| Reproof to the careless watchmen, the teachers and rulers of the Jews. | 9–12 |

## Justice, obedience and the Sabbath (verses 1 and 2)

The Lord teaches us what he expects from us.

Be **honest and just in every transaction**.

Observe the Sabbath carefully and keep it holy.

If we desire God's blessing upon the work of the other days of the week, we should make it a matter of conscience to honor his Sabbath.

Keep away from sin.

Blessed is the person who restrains the hands from everything that displeases God or harms the soul.

Those who, **through the Holy Spirit**, wait for the hope of **righteousness by faith** will also be found living in **holy obedience**.

## Blessings promised to those who come to God (verses 3–8)

Unbelief often suggests discouraging fears to believers.

But God has expressly provided promises to answer those fears.

**Spiritual blessings are immeasurably better than the gift of sons and daughters.**

Children require care and may become a cause of grief or shame.

But the blessings received in God's house are lasting comforts that cannot finally become bitter.

People who genuinely love the Lord will serve him faithfully.

To them, his commandments are not burdensome.

Henry identifies **three blessings promised**.

**Help:** God does not merely permit people to come; he also **inclines their hearts** to approach him.

**Acceptance:** those who come are welcomed by him.

**Comfort:** people who enter his house of prayer grieving may leave rejoicing.

They find relief by laying their burdens before God.

Many sorrowful hearts have become glad in the house of prayer.

**The Gentiles will become one body with the Jews**.

As Jesus said, there will be **one flock and one Shepherd** (**John 10:16**).

Thanks be to God: no one is separated from him except through **willful sin and unbelief**.

Those who come to him are accepted through **the sacrifice of our great High Priest**.

## Careless spiritual leaders are rebuked (verses 9–12)

The prophet summons judgments that will bring desolation.

He then rebukes Israel's careless rulers and teachers.

The rebuke also applies to churches, societies and leaders in other ages.

A people are in grave danger when their shepherds **sleep instead of guarding the flock** and pursue worldly advantage.

Let us ask **the Great Shepherd** to give us pastors after his own heart.

May they feed his people with **knowledge**.

May believers rejoice in his holy name, and may the church continually receive people newly added to it.
