## Trespasses against one's neighbor (verses 1–7)

Every offense described here harms another person, but Scripture also calls it a trespass against the Lord.

The person injured may be poor or despised. Even so, wrongdoing against that person dishonors the God who commands us to love our neighbors as ourselves.

Human law may assign different penalties to different offenses. But every dishonest way of harming another person breaks God's law.

Even keeping something we have found is wrong if we are able to identify its owner.

Fraud is commonly accompanied by lies and sometimes by false oaths.

Anyone seeking to escape God's judgment must make full restitution, so far as it is possible, and seek forgiveness through faith in the one Offering that takes away the world's sin.

The offenses named in this law also violate the law of Christ. He insists upon justice and truth no less than the law of nature or the law of Moses.

## Keeping the altar fire burning (verses 8–13)

This instruction particularly concerns the daily lamb offered in sacrifice.

The priests were to keep the fire on the altar burning continuously.

The first fire had come from heaven (Leviticus 9:24).

By preserving that same fire, the priests showed that every sacrifice was consumed by a flame originally sent from heaven, a sign of God's acceptance.

Our faith and love, and our habits of prayer and praise, should likewise remain alive continually.

## The grain offering for the priests (verses 14–23)

The burnt offering required demanding service from the priests, yet they received no portion of its meat; only the hide belonged to them.

By contrast, most of a grain offering was for the priests' use.

God intends that those who serve him in ministry should receive the support they need.

## The sin offering (verses 24–30)

The priests had to wash any garment on which the blood of a sin offering was splashed.

This pointed to the special reverence due to Christ's blood, which must never be treated as something ordinary.

If the meat was cooked in a clay vessel, that vessel had to be broken. A bronze vessel could instead be washed carefully.

These rules showed that the ceremonial sacrifice did not remove every impurity in itself.

But Christ's blood truly cleanses us from all sin.

The instructions also displayed sin's polluting power and pictured guilt passing from the sinner to the sacrifice.

Consider the love of Christ, who willingly became the sin offering for us so that our repeated sins and failures could be forgiven.

He knew no sin, yet was made sin for us—that is, a sin offering (2 Corinthians 5:21).

Through him we receive not only forgiveness, but also power over sin (Romans 8:3).
