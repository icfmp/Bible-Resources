# Isaiah 58 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Hypocrisy reproved. | 1, 2 |
| A counterfeit and a true fast, with promises to real godliness, and, | 3–12 |
| to the keeping the sabbath. | 13, 14 |

## Hypocrisy must be confronted (verses 1 and 2)

The Holy Spirit intended this warning for hypocrites **in every age**.

Self-love and timid Christians may urge the messenger, **“Spare yourself.”**

Fear of suffering and other motives may also suggest, **“Spare the rich and powerful.”**

But God says, **“Do not hold back.”**

We must obey **God rather than human beings**.

We all need to pray earnestly for God's help in examining ourselves.

People may advance a long way toward heaven and **still fall short**.

They may even go toward eternal destruction **while enjoying an excellent reputation**.

## A true fast rejects oppression and shows mercy (verses 3–12)

Fasting is intended to humble the soul before God.

If a fast expresses no genuine sorrow for sin and does not lead us to put sin away, **it is not a true fast**.

The people described here had shown outward signs of sorrow at regular or specially appointed fasts.

Yet they continued indulging **pride, greed and hostile passions**.

Generosity and mercy are more pleasing to God than empty fasting.

Without them, fasting is vain and hypocritical.

Some people look very humble in God's house while being **harsh at home** and causing suffering to their own families.

No person's faith can justify that person if it **does not work through love**.

Still, individuals, families, neighborhoods, churches and whole nations may express genuine repentance by fasting sincerely **from right motives** and then **turning from sin and doing good works**.

We must remove the heavy yoke of **sin and oppression**.

Sin and sorrow can weaken the body and dry up its strength.

By contrast, duties of **kindness and charity** strengthen and refresh both body and mind.

People who **act justly and love mercy** will experience God's comfort even during earthly life.

Good works receive his blessing **when they arise from love for God and neighbor and are produced in the heart by the Holy Spirit**.

## Honor the Sabbath as the Lord's day (verses 13 and 14)

The Sabbath is a sign between God and the people professing to belong to him.

His appointment of it shows **his favor toward them**.

Their faithful observance of it demonstrates **obedience toward him**.

Henry calls believers to turn from unnecessary travel and from pursuing their own pleasures on the holy day without the control of conscience.

They must not surrender to sensual pleasures.

On the Sabbath, they should turn from their ordinary businesses and amusements.

In both word and deed, they should mark this day as distinct from the other days of the week.

Even during Old Testament times it was called **the Lord's day**.

Henry says it is fitting to continue using that name, with the further significance that it is **the day of the Lord Jesus Christ** (**Revelation 1:10**).

Remembering the Sabbath to keep it holy brings spiritual comfort and benefit.

Then we may gladly say, **“It is good to draw near to God.”**
