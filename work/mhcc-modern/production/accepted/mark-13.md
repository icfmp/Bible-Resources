# Mark 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The destruction of the temple foretold. | 1–4 |
| Christ's prophetic declaration. | 5–13 |
| Christ's prophecy. | 14–23 |
| His prophetic declarations. | 24–27 |
| Watchfulness urged. | 28–37 |

## Jesus foretells the temple's destruction (verses 1–4)

Christ places little value on impressive outward splendor where **purity of heart** is absent.

He looked with compassion upon ruined souls and wept over them.

We do not read that he mourned in the same way for the destruction of even a magnificent building.

Let this teach us the importance of having **a lasting home in heaven**, prepared for us through the Holy Spirit's work.

We should seek that preparation earnestly through every appointed **means of grace**.

## Jesus warns against deception and persecution (verses 5–13)

Jesus answered the disciples not chiefly to satisfy curiosity but to **direct their consciences and conduct**.

When many people are deceived, believers should become more attentive to guarding themselves.

Christ's disciples may enjoy holy peace of mind even when everything around them is confused, provided they do not turn from their duty.

Suffering for Christ should never be allowed to draw them away from him.

Being hated by everyone is a serious trial.

Yet the work God assigned them would continue and prosper.

The servants may be crushed, **but the gospel cannot be crushed**.

The promised salvation is more than temporary rescue from danger.

It includes **everlasting blessedness**.

## The approaching destruction of Jerusalem (verses 14–23)

Henry understands these warnings as pointing toward Jerusalem's destruction.

He says that rebellion against Rome and persecution of Christians accelerated the disaster, which came **less than forty years** after Jesus spoke.

The resulting devastation was extraordinary.

Promises that believers will receive strength to persevere fit perfectly alongside warnings not to fall away.

The more seriously we think about these events, the more reason we have to seek refuge in Christ without delay.

For the salvation of our souls, we should be prepared to relinquish **every earthly attachment**.

## The future appearance of Christ (verses 24–27)

The disciples had confused the destruction of Jerusalem with the end of the world.

Jesus corrected that misunderstanding.

Henry distinguishes Jerusalem's tribulation from **Christ's later coming and the last judgment**.

Jesus foretold the final dissolution of the present arrangement of the world.

He also described **his visible appearance in the clouds** and the gathering of all the elect to him.

## Remain watchful (verses 28–37)

This is the practical application of Jesus' prophetic teaching.

With regard to Jerusalem's destruction, Henry says the disciples should expect it soon.

With regard to the end of the world, they should **not try to calculate the time**.

No human being knows its day or hour.

Henry says that Christ, **in his divinity, could not be ignorant of anything**, while divine wisdom was communicated to his **human soul** according to God's pleasure.

That is his theological explanation of this passage, not a wording added to the biblical text.

The duty in either case is the same: **watch and pray**.

When Jesus ascended to heaven, he left meaningful work for every servant.

We should continually expect his return and remain at our assigned tasks.

The warning applies both to **our individual death** and to the general judgment.

We do not know whether our Master will summon us during youth, middle age or old age.

As soon as we are born, we begin moving toward death.

Our chief concern should be that Christ never finds us **careless, idle, absorbed in pleasure or neglectful of duty**.

His command to all is **“Watch!”**, so that we may be found in peace, without spot and blameless.
