## Judah faces danger and prays (verses 1–13)

In every danger, whether public or personal, our first response should be to seek God's help.

This is one benefit of days set aside for national fasting and prayer.

Throughout our approach to God, we must humble ourselves over our sins and rely upon **his mercy and power alone**.

Jehoshaphat acknowledged God's sovereign rule over all events and asked him to exercise it on Judah's behalf.

Whom else should they seek or trust for relief but the God they had chosen and served?

Those who use their possessions for God may hope confidently in his protection.

Every true believer is a child of Abraham and a friend of God, included in his eternal covenant and entitled to its promises.

God's love is assured to us because he has dwelt in human nature in **the person of the Savior**.

Jehoshaphat appealed to the temple as a sign of God's gracious presence.

He also described the injustice of his enemies.

We may appeal to God when people repay good with evil.

Though Jehoshaphat commanded a large army, he acknowledged, **“We have no strength without you; we depend entirely on you.”**

## Jahaziel announces victory (verses 14–19)

The Spirit of prophecy came upon a **Levite** while the people were gathered together.

Like the wind, the Spirit acts where and upon whom he wills.

The message encouraged them to trust the Lord.

When a Christian soldier goes out against spiritual enemies, the God of peace will make that believer more than a conqueror.

Our trials can become our gain.

We receive the benefit, but all the glory belongs to God.

## Judah celebrates deliverance (verses 20–30)

Jehoshaphat encouraged the troops to maintain firm faith in God.

Faith gives genuine courage.

Nothing more firmly establishes a heart during unstable and frightening times than confidence in **God's power, mercy and promise**.

In every act of trust and praise, we should especially remember his everlasting mercy toward sinners through **Jesus Christ**.

The invading forces destroyed one another in an extraordinary defeat.

God often allows wicked people to destroy one another.

Few victories have ever been celebrated with thanksgiving as solemn as Judah's was.

## Jehoshaphat joins Ahaziah (verses 31–37)

Jehoshaphat remained devoted to God's appointed worship and worked to keep his people faithful.

But after God had granted him such remarkable gifts—not only victory, but wealth—his alliance with a wicked king was deeply ungrateful.

He should have expected the Lord's displeasure.

It appears, however, that he accepted the later warning.

When Ahaziah again pressed him to join in a project, Jehoshaphat refused (**1 Kings 22:49**).

The alliance ended, and God's rebuke produced a change, **at least for a time**.

Let us be thankful for losses that may have prevented **the far greater loss of our immortal souls**.

Praise the Lord, who sought us and did not leave us to perish in our sins.
