# Job 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job reproves his friends. | 1–5 |
| The wicked often prosper. | 6–11 |
| Job speaks of the wisdom and power of God. | 12–25 |

## Job rebukes his friends (verses 1–5)

Job criticized his friends for thinking their wisdom was so much greater than his.

We often mistake correction for insult and imagine we are being mocked when someone offers advice or warning.

That is our own folly.

But here Job had some reason to object.

He suspected his friends treated him this way because poverty and suffering had lowered his social standing.

This is how the world often behaves.

Even upright people may be despised when troubles overshadow their lives.

## Wicked people often prosper (verses 6–11)

Job appealed to observable facts.

Bold robbers, oppressive people and those who openly despise God often enjoy prosperity.

Yet this is not simply chance.

The Lord governs these things.

Worldly prosperity has little value in his sight.

He has **better gifts for his children**.

Job referred everything to God's complete ownership of every created thing.

He asked his friends to let him consider and assess their claims, appealing to any fair judgment.

## Job celebrates God's wisdom and power (verses 12–25)

Here Job gave a noble account of God's wisdom, power and sovereignty.

The Lord governs all human affairs according to the counsel of his will.

No one can resist him.

Wise and godly people who disagree about lesser matters would do well to dwell chiefly on the **great truths they share**.

Doing so brings honor, comfort and benefit to themselves and to others.

Here Job offered neither accusations nor complaints.

He described numerous ways God rules human affairs, overruling every scheme and overcoming every opposition.

Possessing all wisdom and power, God knows how to use even foolish and wicked people.

If he did not govern the world, so much foolishness and dishonesty would have brought it to ruin long ago.

These truths should have made Job and his friends recognize that they were **out of their depth when claiming to know why God had afflicted Job**.

God's ways are beyond searching, and his judgments cannot be fully traced.

Scripture contains many wonderful examples of divine wisdom in sovereignty.

But the greatest and most important is **the crucifixion of Jesus Christ through human malice**.

Only God could have known that this terrible act would become **the salvation of the world**.
