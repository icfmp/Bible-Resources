# Psalm 57 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David begins with prayer and complaint. | 1–6 |
| He concludes with joy and praise. | 7–11 |

## Prayer during danger (verses 1–6)

David depended entirely upon God.

Even the strongest believers need to repeat the tax collector's prayer: **“God, be merciful to me, a sinner.”**

When we entrust our souls to the Lord, we can be certain even in great danger that our troubles will eventually pass.

Until they do, **faith and prayer** must make God our refuge.

Though God is exalted above everything, he stoops to arrange all things for the good of his people.

This encourages us to pray earnestly.

Wherever we look on earth, help may fail, but **heaven remains open to prayer**.

Those who have fled from coming judgment to **Jesus Christ** may trust that the Savior who has done everything needed to purchase salvation will also accomplish everything necessary **for them and within them** so that they may enjoy it.

David grieved that people hated him so much.

Yet their harmful designs eventually returned upon themselves.

Even at the lowest point of shame and suffering, David did not say, **“Lord, exalt me,”** but **“Lord, exalt your own name.”**

The glory of God offers the strongest encouragement in prayer.

We should care for it even more than for our own comfort when asking for mercy.

## A heart established in praise (verses 7–11)

Through living faith, David's complaints and prayers turned immediately into songs of praise.

His heart was fixed upon God and therefore prepared to face whatever might happen.

When God's grace gives us such a calm and steady frame of mind, we have great reason for gratitude.

Nothing worthwhile is accomplished in religion unless it comes **from the heart**.

The heart must be prepared for worship and remain attentive during it.

Our tongue is our glory, and never more so than when praising God.

Dull, half-asleep religious exercises are not acceptable worship.

Let us rise early and begin our day with God.

When his mercies first approach us, let us meet them **with thankful praise**.

David desired other people to join him.

Through his psalms he continues to praise God among his people and **sing among the nations**.

May our hearts remain fixed upon his **boundless mercy and unfailing faithfulness**.

Let us glorify him with body, soul and spirit, which belong to him, and pray that **the gospel's blessing reaches every land**.
