# Psalm 131 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The psalmist's humility. Believers encouraged to trust in God.**

## A quiet heart before the Lord

The psalmist did not aim at worldly greatness.

He wanted to be content in whatever circumstances God assigned him.

Humble believers think less highly of themselves than other people think of them.

When **love for God rules the heart**, it weakens selfish pride.

A proud heart often shows itself through a proud expression.

Knowing God and understanding our duties is already learning enough to occupy our lives well.

Wisdom keeps us from meddling with things that do not belong to us.

The psalmist was willing to accept any condition in which the Lord placed him.

He had become **like a young child being weaned**, content to remain entirely under God's direction just as a child depends upon a mother or nurse.

**We must become like little children** (**Matthew 18:3**).

Our hearts naturally cry out for worldly possessions and pleasures.

But as grace makes the soul holy, it gradually **weans us from those desires**.

A child being weaned may complain and grow restless.

Soon, however, the longing for milk fades, and the child can receive more substantial food.

In a similar way, a renewed soul learns to be quiet when deprived of favorite comforts or disappointed in cherished hopes.

When circumstances do not match our wishes, we should learn to bring **our wishes into submission to our circumstances**.

Then we find rest within ourselves and live more peaceably with others.

The psalmist used his experience to encourage all God's people to trust him.

It is good to **hope in the Lord and quietly wait for his salvation** throughout every trial.
