## The priest examines suspected skin disease (verses 1–17)

The affliction called *leprosy* was treated primarily as a condition of ceremonial uncleanness rather than an ordinary illness.

Henry notes that Christ is said to **cleanse** those afflicted, not simply to cure them.

The condition was widespread among the Hebrews during and after their time in Egypt. Henry considers it likely that their harsh lives and work in Egypt made them especially vulnerable to illness, and he sees no reason to think this particular affliction was familiar among them earlier.

Yet some cases were evidently direct judgments from God.

Miriam, Gehazi and King Uzziah were struck with leprosy in connection with particular sins. It was therefore important to distinguish this affliction from an ordinary illness.

The priests were appointed to examine suspected cases.

Leprosy pictured the moral pollution of the soul by sin, which defiles the conscience and from which only Christ can cleanse us.

A priest could declare someone unclean, just as the law reveals knowledge of sin.

But the priest could not remove that uncleanness. Christ can truly take sin away.

Determining our spiritual condition is very important, yet difficult.

We are all aware of faults and stains in ourselves. The question is whether our spiritual condition is clean or unclean.

Just as marks helped the priest identify the physical affliction, spiritual signs can reveal whether a person remains trapped in bitterness and corruption.

The priest was to take time before pronouncing judgment.

This teaches ministers and ordinary believers not to condemn people hastily or judge before the proper time.

Some people's sins become apparent before judgment, while the sins of others—and likewise their good works—become visible only later.

Even when the person proved to be clean, the clothes still had to be washed because there had been reason for suspicion.

We all need the blood of Christ to wash us from our stains, even when they are not the severe marks of leprosy. Who among us can claim to be entirely free from sin?

## Further signs of the affliction (verses 18–44)

The priest received instructions for cases where suspected leprosy appeared in an old sore.

Henry compares this to the spiritual danger faced by people who escape the world's corruption but later become entangled in it again.

He also considers cases where the disease appears in a burn (verse 24).

He sees in this a warning that strife and angry disputes can expose and intensify the corruption of the heart.

Human life is surrounded by many afflictions.

We face countless diseases, all of which entered the world because of sin.

When we enjoy health, strength and bodily comfort, we should use our bodies to glorify God.

Special attention was required when leprosy appeared on the head.

If the *leprosy of sin* corrupts a person's judgment, leading that person to accept false principles that support wicked behavior, the danger is especially grave.

Few people recover easily from deeply corrupt convictions.

Soundness in the faith helps protect the mind from that spiritual disease.

## The person declared unclean (verses 45–46)

When a priest declared a person unclean, ordinary work stopped.

The person was separated from friends and family and deprived of many worldly comforts.

Such a person was to humble himself beneath God's mighty hand rather than insisting on a cleanness the priest had denied.

The spiritual lesson is that we should acknowledge the shame of our own sin.

With sorrowful hearts we must confess that both our hearts and lives have been unclean: through the corruption inherited from Adam and through our own acts of disobedience.

Because of these sins, we deserve to be excluded forever from fellowship with God unless his infinite mercy intervenes.

The leper had to warn others not to approach too closely.

The person was also required to live outside the camp. Later, in Canaan, this meant outside the town or village, apart from everyone except others suffering the same affliction.

Henry sees this as a picture of the purity that ought to characterize the Christian church.

## Suspected contamination in clothing (verses 47–59)

Clothing suspected of leprosy was not to be burned immediately.

First it had to be examined.

If a persistent affected patch was found, the whole garment—or at least the affected part—was to be destroyed.

If the garment proved free of the affliction, it could be washed and used.

This, too, showed the serious evil of sin.

Sin stains not only the conscience but also a person's possessions and activities.

Those who use their clothes to display pride or indulge lust may see them spiritually stained.

But the robes of righteousness never decay or become moth-eaten.
