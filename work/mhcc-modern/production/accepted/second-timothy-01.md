# 2 Timothy 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Paul expresses great affection for Timothy. | 1–5 |
| Exhorts him to improve his spiritual gifts. | 6–14 |
| Tells of many who basely deserted him; but speaks with affection of Onesiphorus. | 15–18 |

## Paul thanks God for Timothy's faith (verses 1–5)

The promise of **eternal life in Christ Jesus** is the central message of ministers appointed according to God's will.

The blessings Paul asked for Timothy are the finest we can desire for those we love, including peace with **God the Father and Jesus Christ our Lord**.

All the credit for the good we do belongs to God.

True believers in every age share **the same essential faith**. Their faith is sincere and enduring, because it lives in them as an active principle.

The example of **Lois and Eunice** should encourage faithful women who teach children.

Some of the most helpful ministers Christ's church has known owe deep gratitude to God for the earliest impressions of faith made upon them **by their mothers and other female relatives**.

## Stir up God's gift and guard the gospel (verses 6–14)

God has not given believers a spirit of cowardice or slavery to fear. He gives **power, courage, resolution, love for him and a sound, peaceful mind**.

When his strength supports us, we can bear affliction faithfully.

Whenever Paul spoke of Christ and redemption, he loved to dwell on them at length. Christ was all his salvation and should be all our desire.

The gospel's calling is **holy** and makes people holy. Salvation comes entirely by **free grace**, given in God's eternal purpose **in Christ Jesus before the world began**.

All the gifts God grants to fallen humans reach us through Christ alone.

The prospect of eternal happiness through him, **the Resurrection and the Life**, should move us to make our calling sure.

Those who remain faithful to the gospel need not be ashamed, though its opponents will eventually be ashamed.

Paul had entrusted **his life, soul and eternal future** to Jesus. Only Christ can preserve a soul through life, death and the last judgment.

A day is coming when everyone will be asked what they did with the soul entrusted to them. Did they serve sin or Christ?

The humblest genuine Christian stands upon **the same foundation of hope as Paul**: believing in Jesus and recognizing the saving change he has brought to the heart.

Timothy must hold firmly to **the Holy Scriptures and the trustworthy pattern of gospel truth**. Mere intellectual agreement is insufficient; we must **love** that truth.

The gospel is a precious deposit entrusted to believers. We must preserve it **whole and pure**, not through confidence in our own intelligence or heart but by **the Holy Spirit living within us**.

## Remember faithful Onesiphorus (verses 15–18)

Many had shamefully deserted Paul. In contrast, **Onesiphorus** often encouraged and refreshed him through **letters, counsel and comfort**, without being ashamed of his imprisonment.

A good person seeks opportunities to do good.

Death and judgment are momentous realities. If we wish to receive mercy then, we must seek it from the Lord now.

For ourselves and our friends, the greatest request we can make is **that we find mercy from God when we pass from time into eternity and stand before Christ's judgment seat**.
