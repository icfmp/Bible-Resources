# Deuteronomy 33 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The glorious majesty of God. | 1–5 |
| The blessings of the twelve tribes. | 6–23 |
| Strength to believers. | 24, 25 |
| The excellency of Israel. | 26–29 |

## Moses blesses the tribes and describes God's majesty (verses 1–5)

Moses added a solemn blessing to all his previous instructions, warnings and prophecies.

He began by recalling the Lord's glorious appearance when he gave the law.

God's law works like fire.

When accepted, it softens hard hearts, warms and purifies them, burning away corrupt desires.

When rejected, it hardens, burns and destroys.

The Holy Spirit descended upon the church in divided tongues like fire.

The gospel is also a fiery law.

When God's law is written on a person's heart, it is evidence that divine love has been poured into that heart.

We should regard God's law as one of the gifts of his grace.

## Moses blesses the tribes (verses 6–23)

The tribes were not blessed in the usual order.

The blessing pronounced on Judah may refer to the tribe as a whole or to David, who foreshadowed Christ.

Moses gave a particularly full blessing to Levi.

In our religious service, the greatest goal should be **acceptance with God**, whether other people approve of us or not (2 Corinthians 5:9).

Henry understands this blessing as a prophecy that the Lord will continue supplying ministers to his church until the end of time.

Benjamin's inheritance was close to Mount Zion.

Living near the appointed means of worship was a precious gift that should not be exchanged for any worldly benefit or pleasure.

We should gratefully receive the earthly gifts God sends through the seasons.

But his spiritual gifts are immeasurably greater.

They come from the Father of lights, through Christ the Sun of Righteousness, and through the Holy Spirit's influence like the rain that makes the earth fruitful.

These are the signs of God's special love: the Spirit's gifts, grace and comfort.

When Moses prayed for the favor of the One who dwelt in the burning bush, he recalled the covenant, which is the foundation of every hope we have of God's favor.

God assigns people their places to live and wisely directs them toward different occupations for the common good.

Whatever our situation or work, it is both wise and right to apply ourselves to it and be content.

We should not merely invite other people into God's service.

We should also abound in it ourselves.

The blessing on Naphtali leads Henry to a final observation: **God's favor alone can satisfy the soul**.

Those who have it are truly happy, and they recognize that in possessing it they need nothing more.

## Strength for each day (verses 24 and 25)

For true believers, all their circumstances can be made holy.

When they travel along a difficult road, God equips them with the readiness that comes from the gospel of peace.

“As your days, so shall your strength be.”

In Scripture, a *day* can mean the events and troubles that take place during it.

The promise is that God will continually and graciously sustain his people through whatever burdens or trials he appoints.

It belongs to all Abraham's spiritual descendants.

If they have work to do, they will be given strength for it.

If burdens are laid upon them, God will provide what they need to carry them.

He will never allow them to be tested beyond the strength he supplies.

## The incomparable blessing of belonging to God (verses 26–29)

No people had a God like Israel's God.

The blessings proclaimed over Israel can also be applied to the spiritual church.

**No people have a better dwelling place.** Those who make God their home receive the comfort and protection of his presence (Psalm 91:1).

**No people receive firmer support.** God's everlasting arms hold up his people when they are brought very low.

His grace prevents their spirits from collapsing and their faith from failing (2 Corinthians 12:9).

**No people are better governed.** Through the Christ who loves them, believers become more than conquerors over their spiritual enemies.

**No people enjoy better security.** Israel can dwell safely, and those who remain close to God are protected by him.

**No people receive a better inheritance.** Every true Israelite looks by faith toward the heavenly Canaan, which contains better things than grain and wine.

**No people have better help.** In every danger or need, the eternal God is available to them.

Those whom he helps cannot finally be destroyed.

**No people are better armed.** Those who possess holiness in their hearts are defended by the complete armor of God (Ephesians 6).

**No people have a surer promise of victory.** The God of peace will soon crush Satan under believers' feet (Romans 16:20).

May the Lord help us set our hearts on things above, turning away from earthly possessions that will soon perish.

May we not share the hopelessness of Israel's enemies, but enter the realm of everlasting love and joy with God's true Israel.
