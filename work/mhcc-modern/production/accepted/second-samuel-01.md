# 2 Samuel 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Tidings brought to David of the death of Saul. | 1–10 |
| The Amalekite is put to death. | 11–16 |
| David's lamentation for Saul and Jonathan. | 17–27 |

## David receives news of Saul's death (verses 1–10)

The event that opened David's way to the throne happened at about the same time he was suffering severe distress.

People who have entrusted their concerns to the Lord can wait quietly for his will to be done.

David's response showed that he had not desired Saul's death and had not grown impatient to receive the kingdom.

## The Amalekite is put to death (verses 11–16)

David genuinely mourned Saul.

All those with him humbled themselves beneath God's hand, which had fallen heavily upon Israel in its defeat.

David ordered the man who brought the news to be executed because the man had **confessed to murdering the king**.

Henry argues that David did not act unjustly in this case.

If the Amalekite's account was true, the man had committed treason and deserved death under the law of that time.

But if he had lied to David, the lie had brought about his own death.

So lies will sooner or later turn against the people who tell them.

David showed zeal for public justice without allowing his own personal advantage to influence the matter.

## David laments Saul and Jonathan (verses 17–27)

Henry thinks **Kasheth**, meaning **“the bow,”** was probably the title of David's funeral song.

David did not praise Saul for virtues he had not possessed.

In particular, he said nothing about Saul's religious devotion or personal goodness.

Yet Jonathan had been a dutiful son and Saul an affectionate father, and they loved one another.

David had good reason to say that Jonathan's love for him had been extraordinary.

Next to the love shared between Christ and his people, the affection that flows from such love produces the strongest friendships.

The suffering of God's people and the victories of his enemies always grieve true believers, even when those events may bring the believers personal advantage.
