# Jeremiah 22 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Justice is recommended, and destruction threatened in case of disobedience. | 1–9 |
| The captivity of Jehoiakim, and the end of Jeconiah. | 10–19 |
| The doom of the royal family. | 20–30 |

## Royal authority must be exercised justly (verses 1–9)

The king of Judah sat on **David's throne**, and David had been **a man after God's heart**.

The king should have followed David's example so that he could share the benefit of the promises made to that family.

The surest way to preserve a government is for its rulers to **perform the duties of government**.

But sin destroys the households of princes as readily as those of ordinary people.

Who can resist destroyers whom **God has appointed**?

God destroys neither people, cities nor nations without the cause of **sin**.

Often even in this life he makes clear the wrongs for which he sends punishment.

At the final judgment, those reasons will be completely evident.

## The sons of Josiah reject their father's example (verses 10–19)

The passage announces a sentence of death over two kings, **wicked sons of a deeply godly father**.

Josiah was spared the sight of the approaching calamity.

He was taken from this life to behold **the good of the world to come**.

Therefore the people had greater reason to weep for **Shallum**, who would live and die as a miserable captive, than for their father.

The death of a faithful believer may be cause for hope, while the continued life of an unrepentant person may rightly call forth pity.

The prophecy also announces the fate of **Jehoiakim**.

It is lawful for rulers and great people to build, improve and furnish their houses.

But those who make their homes grander must guard against **vanity and pride**.

Jehoiakim built through **injustice**.

His money had been acquired wrongfully, and he **refused to pay his workers their wages**.

God sees the wrong powerful people do to **servants and laborers**, and will repay in justice those who have refused to pay others what justice demands.

Even the most powerful person must regard the poorest person as **a neighbor owed fairness**.

Jehoiakim also shed innocent blood.

**Greed**, the root of every kind of evil, lay beneath his wickedness.

Children who dismiss the old ways of their parents often miss the **true excellence** beneath those customs.

Jehoiakim knew his father had found obedience to be a path of comfort, yet refused to follow him.

He would die without mourning, having become hated for **oppression and cruelty**.

## The royal family's confidence collapses (verses 20–30)

Judah's condition had **three distinct stages**.

First, the people were **arrogant during peace and security**.

Then they became **fearful when trouble was announced**.

Finally, they were **utterly crushed under the weight of disaster**.

Many people feel no real shame over sin until it has brought them into the most desperate circumstances.

The king would end his days in **captivity**.

Those imagining themselves to be like **signet rings upon God's right hand** should not presume they can never be removed.

The king and his family would be transported to **Babylon**.

We know where we were born, but we do not know where we will die.

**God knows both**.

Our responsibility is to make sure that we **die in Christ**.

Then it will be well with us wherever death comes, even far from home.

The king who had once been admired would become despised.

People in whom God takes no pleasure may eventually become a disappointment to everyone else too.

If someone has no children, God has permitted that condition; a person who spends the available years without doing good cannot expect to prosper.

How little we should depend on **royal splendor or flourishing families**!

But those who **hear Christ's voice and follow him** possess eternal life.

They will never perish, and no enemy can tear them from **his almighty hands**.
