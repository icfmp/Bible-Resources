# Zechariah 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Punishment of the enemies of Judah. | 1–8 |
| Repentance and sorrow of the Jews. | 9–14 |

## God protects his people (verses 1–8)

God's prophecy announces a heavy burden for the enemies of his church, but comfort and blessing for Israel.

The Lord will confuse their plans and weaken their courage.

**Henry admits the precise meaning is unclear.**

Yet God often begins by calling the poor and despised, and even the weakest may become like **David**, courageous and excellent in all that is good.

May the labor and example of Christians be like **fire among dry wood or a torch in a sheaf**, spreading divine love and true religion on every side.

## The Spirit leads people to mourn over Christ (verses 9–14)

The prophet describes a day when God defends and delivers Jerusalem.

When Christ first came, he wounded the serpent's head and broke the dark powers opposing his kingdom.

At his second coming, **he will complete their defeat** and bring down all hostile powers.

Death itself will be swallowed up in victory.

The Holy Spirit is gracious and merciful, the source of holiness and every spiritual grace.

He is also **the Spirit of supplication**, showing people their ignorance, need, guilt, misery and danger, and teaching them to pray.

Henry interprets this prophecy as anticipating a time when **the Jews would recognize Jesus as the crucified Messiah**.

They would look toward him with faith and mourn for him, in public and privately, with each person grieving personally.

There is a holy sorrow over sin that flows from the Spirit's work.

Such sorrow strengthens **faith in Christ** and prepares people to rejoice in God.

It is evidence of genuine grace and prayer.

That pattern applies to everyone who mourns over sin with godly sorrow.

Looking to **Christ crucified** moves the heart to repent of the sin for which he suffered.
