## God renews the stone tablets (verses 1–4)

When God created humanity in his own image, he wrote the moral law directly upon the human heart through his own power.

No outward instrument was needed.

But humanity broke the covenant.

Since then, the Lord has used human servants to write the law in Scripture and has also worked through them as he writes it anew upon people's hearts.

When he was reconciled to Israel, he ordered the stone tablets to be replaced and wrote his law on them.

Even under the gospel of peace through Christ, the moral law remains binding upon believers.

Christ has redeemed us from the law's **curse**, but not from its **commands**.

The earliest and surest sign that sin has been forgiven and peace made with God is that his law has been written upon the heart.

## God proclaims his name and character (verses 5–9)

The Lord descended in a cloud, openly showing his presence and a glimpse of his glory.

He proclaimed his **name**—the divine qualities and character signified by *Jehovah*.

**The Lord is merciful.** He is ready to forgive sinners and help those in need.

**He is gracious.** He is kind and freely gives benefits that no one deserves.

**He is patient.** He is slow to anger, allowing time for repentance and punishing only when punishment is necessary.

**He abounds in goodness and truth.**

Even sinners receive his generous gifts, though they often misuse them.

Everything he reveals is unfailing truth, and everything he promises he faithfully performs.

**He keeps mercy for thousands.**

He continually shows kindness to sinners.

His store of mercy will never be exhausted, even to the end of time.

**He forgives iniquity, transgression and sin.**

His goodness includes the full and free forgiveness of wrongdoing.

**Yet he will never treat the guilty as innocent.**

God's holiness and justice are part of his goodness and love toward all his creatures.

In Christ's suffering, divine holiness and justice are displayed fully, and the true evil of sin is revealed.

God's forgiving mercy is always joined to grace that changes us and makes us holy.

None are pardoned without repentance and turning away from the deliberate practice of sin.

Those who abuse, neglect or despise this great salvation will not escape judgment.

Moses bowed before God and worshiped reverently.

Every aspect of God's revealed character gives believers reason to plead for forgiveness, holiness of heart and the growth of the Redeemer's kingdom.

## Israel is warned against idolatry (verses 10–17)

The Israelites were ordered to destroy every object and monument connected with idolatry, however beautiful or expensive.

They were to refuse alliances, friendships and marriages that would draw them into the worship of false gods.

They were also to avoid idolatrous feasts.

God reminded them not to repeat their sin of making a metal image for worship.

Human jealousy is described as a fierce passion (Proverbs 6:34).

But God's jealousy is holy and righteous displeasure.

No one can worship God rightly without worshiping him **alone**.

## The festivals and renewed covenant (verses 18–27)

Israel must rest one day every week.

Even plowing and harvesting had to stop.

Ordinary work must give way to holy rest, even when the harvest was ready.

Henry argues that observing the Sabbath during harvest would leave their work better blessed.

They were to show that they valued fellowship with God and their duty to him above the labor and joy of gathering crops.

Three times each year the people were to appear before the Lord God of Israel.

Canaan was a desirable land, and neighboring nations wanted its riches.

Yet God promised that those nations would not desire to seize Israel's land while the people gathered to worship.

Let us restrain sinful desires in our own hearts—especially anything that opposes God's glory.

Then let us trust him to restrain the evil desires of others against us.

The way of duty is the way of safety.

Those who risk things for God's sake never finally lose through serving him.

Three feasts are named.

**First**, Passover remembered the deliverance from Egypt.

**Second**, the Feast of Weeks, or Pentecost, included the offering of firstfruits.

**Third**, the Feast of Ingathering, or Tabernacles, marked the gathering of the harvest.

Moses was commanded to write God's words so the people would know and understand them better.

We can never be thankful enough for the written word of God.

The Lord made his covenant with Israel through Moses as mediator.

In the same way, the covenant of grace is made with believers through Christ.

## Moses' shining face and the veil (verses 28–35)

Deep spiritual fellowship with God strengthens and beautifies a renewed, holy character.

Sincere godliness can give a person's face a brightness that wins respect and love.

The veil Moses wore showed how much less clearly God's revelation was understood under the old covenant than it would be under the gospel.

It also pictured the natural veil over human hearts that prevents people from understanding spiritual realities.

Henry further applies the image to Israel's continuing difficulty in recognizing Christ.

He says this veil can be removed only through the Lord's Spirit, who reveals Christ as the goal of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes.

Fear and unbelief can also place a veil before **us**, hindering our free approach to the mercy seat above.

We should tell our heavenly Father about all our needs, both earthly and spiritual.

Let us speak openly of our obstacles, struggles, trials and temptations.

Let us confess our sins.
