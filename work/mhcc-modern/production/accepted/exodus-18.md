# Exodus 18 — Modern English

## Jethro brings Moses' family (verses 1–6)

Jethro came to rejoice with Moses over Israel's deliverance.

He also brought Moses' wife and two sons back to him.

Moses needed his family with him.

While governing God's people, he was to set a good example in caring for and leading his own household (1 Timothy 3:5).

## Moses welcomes Jethro and praises God (verses 7–12)

Talking together about God's wonderful deeds is good for us and builds us up.

Jethro rejoiced not only because his son-in-law had received honor but because God had been so good to all Israel.

Those watching from outside were sometimes more deeply moved by Israel's blessings than those who had received them.

Jethro gave glory to Israel's God.

Whatever gives us joy should also move us to praise the Lord.

They offered a sacrifice of thanksgiving together.

Shared worship sanctifies friendship.

It is especially good for family members and friends to unite in the spiritual sacrifice of prayer and praise as people who meet in Christ.

The meal was modest. They ate bread—manna.

Jethro could see and taste the bread from heaven.

Though he was a Gentile, he was welcome at their table.

In the same way, Gentiles are welcomed to Christ, the Bread of Life.

## Jethro advises Moses about judging the people (verses 13–27)

Moses worked tirelessly and zealously as Israel's judge.

He had already been used to redeem Israel from slavery.

Now he also foreshadowed Christ by giving laws and settling disputes among the people.

If the Israelites quarreled with one another as often as they quarreled with God, Moses must have heard many disputes.

Yet this was the work God had given him, and he carried it out with care and kindness.

Even the humblest Israelite could bring a case to him.

Moses worked from morning until evening.

Jethro saw that doing everything alone was too heavy a burden.

It also made justice painfully slow for the people who needed it.

We can try to do too much even when we are doing good.

Wisdom shows us how to avoid doing less than our duty while also avoiding responsibilities beyond our strength.

Jethro proposed a better arrangement.

People in important positions should not merely try to be useful themselves. They should also plan ways for others to become useful.

Those chosen to carry responsibility must be carefully selected.

They should understand people and affairs, show sound judgment, and not be intimidated by angry looks or loud complaints.

They must utterly reject bribes.

Above all, they should fear God.

True reverence for him keeps people from doing wrong even when they could conceal it and escape human punishment.

The fear of God is the strongest protection against the temptation to act unjustly.

Moses did not despise Jethro's advice.

People who think themselves too wise to receive counsel are not wise at all.
