# Ecclesiastes 10 — Modern English

## Preserve a reputation for wisdom (verses 1–3)

People who profess true religion have special reason to avoid even **the appearance of evil**.

The wise person has important advantages over the fool, who is constantly uncertain when anything needs doing.

Wherever sinners go, sin brings them disgrace and exposes their folly.

## Patience, government and mutual care (verses 4–10)

Solomon seems to warn against seeking redress for injury too hastily or allowing pride and revenge to govern our decisions.

Do not abandon the duties of your position simply because someone has offended you.

Wait, remain steady and be ready to yield where you can.

**Gentleness can settle even serious offenses**.

People are not always promoted according to their merit.

Those most eager to advise or interfere are sometimes the least aware of the difficulty and consequences of what they propose.

Henry applies the same lesson to **the church, the body of Christ**: all its members should show equal concern for one another.

## The danger of foolish speech (verses 11–15)

Henry refers to an eastern practice of charming serpents through music.

The person who constantly talks foolishly or maliciously has a tongue that is **unruly and full of deadly poison**.

Direct contradiction may make such a person angrier and more violent.

We need wisdom to keep the peace.

Through rash, immoral or slanderous speech, people bring vengeance upon themselves, either openly or secretly.

If we remembered how little we know of what the future holds, we would stop saying many of the pointless things we now multiply.

Foolish people labor to no good purpose.

They may not even understand something as simple as **the road into a great city**.

By contrast, Henry speaks of the **road to the heavenly city** as a clearly marked highway on which even inexperienced travelers need not lose their way.

The CCEL witness attaches the reference **“Is. 25:8”** to that comparison; the cited verse requires primary-source and biblical-reference checking before any editorial correction.

Sinful folly can nevertheless cause people to miss the one way leading to true happiness.

## The duties of public leaders and citizens (verses 16–20)

A country's happiness depends in part upon the character of its leaders.

People suffer when their rulers behave immaturely and devote themselves chiefly to pleasure.

Laziness harms **public as well as private responsibilities**.

Money itself cannot feed or clothe anyone, but in ordinary economic life it can be exchanged for necessities and comforts.

The human soul, however, can neither be redeemed nor sustained through **perishable things like silver and gold**.

God knows what human beings do and hears what they say in secret.

Whenever he chooses, he can bring hidden things to light through surprising means.

If people can endanger themselves through secret insults against earthly rulers, how much more serious are words, acts and thoughts of rebellion against **the King of kings and Lord of lords**!

He sees what is hidden.

His ear is always open.

Sinner, do not curse **this King** even in your most private thought.

Your curse cannot injure him.

But his judgment can bring you down to eternal destruction.
