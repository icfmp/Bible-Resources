# Jeremiah 36 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Baruch is to write the prophecies of Jeremiah. | 1–8 |

| The king having heard a part, burns the roll. | 20–32 |

## Baruch writes Jeremiah's prophecies (verses 1–8)

**God himself directed that the Scriptures should be written down.**

His wisdom appointed writing as an appropriate means of warning the people.

If the warning failed to change them, Judah would have still less excuse for refusing to listen.

The Lord announces judgment against sinners so that they may **hear, fear and turn away from their evil ways**.

Whenever anyone responds to his warnings in this way, trusting in his promised mercy, the Lord is **ready to forgive** their sins.

Those who refuse are left without excuse.

The seriousness of God's anger against our sin ought to stir us both to **prayer** and to **earnest efforts to obey him**.

## The officials hear the scroll (verses 9–19)

Outward displays of reverence and devotion may be found among people who keep up the **forms of religion** but remain strangers—even enemies—to its **living power**.

The officials listened patiently as the whole scroll was read.

They became deeply afraid.

Yet even people convinced that a message is true and important, and inclined to help those who preach it, may still hesitate because of concerns about **their safety, their personal interests or their prospects for advancement**.

Such reservations can keep them from acting according to what their consciences know.

They may even try to dismiss the disturbing truth rather than obey it.

## The king burns the scroll (verses 20–32)

Those who despise God's word may soon show that they **hate** it.

Like the king who burned the scroll, they would like to see the word itself destroyed.

This reveals the deep hostility of the sinful human mind toward God, and should make us marvel at his patience.

The officials had shown some concern about the warning.

Yet when they saw how lightly the king treated it, their own concern weakened.

**Do not treat God's word as something trivial.**
