## The miraculous catch and the calling of fishermen (verses 1–11)

After Jesus had finished preaching, he instructed Peter to return to the work of his ordinary occupation.

Time spent in public worship or religious instruction during working days need not make us less diligent in our employment.

Indeed, it can improve our conduct and frame of mind in ordinary work.

How cheerfully can we fulfill our daily responsibilities when we have first spent time with God and sought to have our work sanctified through **his word and prayer**!

Peter and his companions had caught nothing. Yet Jesus told them to lower the nets again.

We should not abandon our lawful occupations simply because success does not come as quickly or fully as we hoped.

It is good to act according to **Christ's word**, whatever recent experience may suggest.

The extraordinary catch was a miracle.

Peter acknowledged himself to be a sinner.

All of us must confess that Jesus would be justified in leaving us because of our guilt.

Yet we should also beg him **not to leave**, because what hope could sinners have if their Savior departed?

Rather, ask him to dwell within our hearts by faith and make them clean and new.

These fishermen left everything to follow Jesus **when their fishing had become profitable**.

It is especially commendable to leave riches for Christ when they are increasing and tempting us to set our hearts on them.

## Jesus cleanses a man full of leprosy (verses 12–16)

The man was described as **full of leprosy**.

His disease pictured the corruption of our fallen nature through sin.

From head to foot we have no soundness apart from divine healing.

His words combined **great confidence in Christ's power with profound humility**.

A sinner who knows personal unworthiness may ask: I know Christ can make me clean, but will he receive someone like me? Will he apply his blood to cleanse my sin?

Henry encourages such a person to come to Jesus confidently, yet humbly submitting the matter to his mercy.

When we have received deliverance from the guilt and power of sin, we should tell others of Christ and help them come to him to hear his word and receive healing.

## Jesus forgives and heals a paralyzed man (verses 17–26)

Many attend gatherings where the gospel is preached but do not truly listen.

They sit near God's word without receiving it as **a message addressed to them**.

The friends bringing the paralyzed man teach us the importance of pressing forward earnestly toward Christ.

Such persistence shows faith, pleases Jesus and leads us to him.

Lord, grant us faith in your willingness and power **to heal our souls**.

Make us desire **forgiveness of sins more than health, earthly prosperity or even life itself**.

Enable us to trust your authority to forgive.

Then we will gladly rise and go wherever you command.

## Jesus calls Levi and answers the Pharisees (verses 27–39)

How wonderful is the grace of Christ!

He called **a tax collector** to become his disciple and follower.

His call worked powerfully and effectively.

He came to call **sinners to repentance**, assuring them that forgiveness is available.

He endured with patience the hostility and contradictions directed against himself and his disciples.

He also arranged his disciples' duties according to **their present strength and level of growth**.

The Lord prepares his people gradually for the trials appointed to them.

We should imitate that patience when helping someone weak in faith or undergoing temptation, rather than placing burdens on them that they are not yet ready to bear.
