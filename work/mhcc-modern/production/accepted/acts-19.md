## Paul instructs disciples of John at Ephesus (verses 1–7)

In Ephesus Paul found religious people who looked toward Jesus as the promised Messiah.

They had not yet been taught to expect the **Holy Spirit's extraordinary miraculous powers** or to understand that the gospel was, in a particular way, **the ministry of the Spirit**.

But they were ready to welcome the teaching.

Paul explained that John the Baptist never intended people whom he baptized to **stop with John**.

He directed them to believe in the One coming after him, **Jesus Christ**.

They gladly received this fuller knowledge and were baptized in Jesus' name.

The Holy Spirit then came upon them in a remarkable and overwhelming way.

They spoke in different languages and prophesied, as the apostles and the first Gentile converts had done.

We do not now expect **the same extraordinary miraculous powers**.

Nevertheless, every person professing to be Christ's disciple should ask whether they have received the Holy Spirit's **seal in his sanctifying work**, as evidence of genuine faith.

Many people seem scarcely to have heard of the Holy Spirit.

Others dismiss every account of his grace and comfort as mere illusion.

Such people may rightly be asked what their baptism means to them, because they evidently do not understand **the inward spiritual reality pictured by the outward sign** in which they place so much confidence.

## Paul teaches publicly; miracles confirm the message (verses 8–12)

When persistent argument and persuasion only make people more hardened in unbelief and blasphemy, we must withdraw from their fellowship and, when appropriate, lead others away from it.

God confirmed the preaching of his early servants through miracles.

Hearers unwilling to accept their spoken testimony could recognize the power of God through the works he performed.

## False exorcists are exposed; evil books destroyed (verses 13–20)

People, especially among the Jews, sometimes claimed or tried to drive out evil spirits.

If we resist the devil **through faith in Christ**, he will flee from us.

But if we imagine Jesus' name or his miracles operate like **a magical formula**, Satan will prevail against us.

True sorrow for sin includes **honest confession to God in prayer**, and confession to people we have wronged whenever the circumstances require it.

If God's word gained greater influence among us, many owners of corrupt, irreligious and immoral books would themselves destroy them.

Will these Ephesians not stand as witnesses against people who claim Christ's name while trading in such writings for profit or allowing themselves to possess them for pleasure?

If we are serious about salvation, we must give up every pursuit and enjoyment that **weakens the gospel's hold upon our minds and hearts**.

## A riot erupts over the worship of Artemis (verses 21–31)

Travelers to Ephesus frequently bought little silver shrines or models of the famous temple to take home after visiting.

The craftsmen profited from **other people's superstition** and used it to advance their own financial interests.

People cling fiercely to what produces their wealth.

Many turn against Christ's gospel because it calls them to abandon **profitable but sinful occupations**.

Some will defend ideas obviously absurd and false, such as gods made by human hands, so long as their worldly interests depend on those ideas.

The city became full of confusion—a common and natural effect of zeal for false religion.

The desire to defend Christ's honor, together with love for fellow believers, can lead courageous servants to risk danger.

God sometimes raises up friends of Christianity among people who do not share true faith but have witnessed the **honest and consistent conduct** of its followers.

## The riot is quieted (verses 32–41)

Some Jews attempted to distinguish themselves from Christ's servants during the uproar.

Those who now are ashamed of being associated with him will face consequences on the day of final judgment.

At last a public official used his authority to calm the crowd.

Here is a valuable rule for both private and public affairs: **do not act rashly**.

Take time to consider.

Keep your emotions under control.

Do not do in haste what you will later regret.

**Ordinary legal procedures should restrain popular riots**; in a well-governed society they generally do.

Many people fear human courts far more than **God's judgment**.

How valuable it would be to calm our own unruly passions by remembering that soon we must answer before the Judge of heaven and earth!

God's overruling Providence keeps a measure of public peace through his otherwise mysterious rule over human minds.

Without it, people would destroy one another.

It is difficult to look around without seeing people behave like Demetrius and his fellow craftsmen.

Arguing with those enraged by party loyalties and disappointed greed may be as dangerous as confronting wild animals.

They imagine every objection answered as soon as they show that the practice being questioned **makes them wealthy**.

Whatever group, party or religious label embraces this attitude, it is **worldly** and should be rejected by all who value truth and godliness.

Do not lose heart.

**The Lord on high is mightier than the roar of many waters**.

He can also quiet the rage of a crowd.
