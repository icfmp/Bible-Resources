# Romans 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle recommends Phebe to the church at Rome, and greets several friends there. | 1–16 |
| Cautions the church against such as made divisions. | 17–20 |
| Christian salutations. | 21–24 |
| The epistle concludes with ascribing glory to God. | 25–27 |

## Paul commends Phoebe and greets the believers (verses 1–16)

Paul recommended **Phoebe** to the Roman Christians.

Christians ought to help one another in practical affairs, **especially when people are strangers in a new place**.

We do not know how much help we ourselves may need in the future.

Paul asked the believers to assist a woman who had already helped many people.

Those who water others will themselves receive refreshment.

Although Paul carried the daily concern of many churches, he still remembered **individual people**.

He sent personal greetings, describing their character and showing affection for them.

He also greeted believers he could not name individually, so no one should feel forgotten.

He regarded all of them as **brothers, sisters and saints**.

At the end he sent the united greetings of **Christ's churches**.

## Beware teachers who create division through deception (verses 17–20)

How earnest and affectionate Paul's warnings are!

Teaching that contradicts the sound doctrine of Scripture opens the door to offenses and divisions.

If truth is abandoned, **unity and peace cannot survive for long**.

Many call Jesus their Lord and Master while failing to serve him.

Instead they pursue **worldly, bodily and selfish interests**.

False teachers corrupt people's understanding by first gaining power over **their affections**.

They distort judgment by exploiting trust.

Therefore we must guard our hearts diligently.

Deceivers commonly target people who have become sensitive or uncertain through conviction.

A receptive disposition is valuable under wise guidance, but can also leave someone open to deception.

Be **wise enough not to be deceived**, yet **simple and sincere enough never to become a deceiver**.

Paul looks for God's promise of **victory over Satan**.

This victory includes the defeat of Satan's plans to corrupt, disturb and destroy souls and to keep them from peace with God and final enjoyment of heaven.

When Satan appears to be winning and everything seems lost, **the God of peace will intervene for his people**.

Persevere a little longer in faith and patience.

If Christ's grace remains with us, who can prevail against us?

## Greetings from Paul's companions (verses 21–24)

Paul added warm greetings from those with him who were known to Roman believers.

Seeing relatives become **holy and useful** is a wonderful comfort.

Not many powerful or noble people are called, but **some are**.

Believers may lawfully serve in civil office.

Henry hopes that public offices in Christian communities, as well as offices in the church, will be entrusted to **prudent and steadfast Christians**.

## Glory to God through Jesus Christ (verses 25–27)

What truly establishes souls is **the plain preaching of Jesus Christ**.

Our redemption and salvation through him form a great mystery of godliness.

Yet thanks be to God, enough of that mystery has been made clear to lead us into heaven if we do not deliberately neglect salvation.

Life and immortality have been revealed through the gospel.

**The Sun of Righteousness has risen upon the world**.

The writings of the prophets not only became clearer through Christ.

They also make known God's saving mystery to **all nations**.

Christ is the Savior offered to every nation.

The gospel is revealed **not merely to be discussed or argued about, but to be obeyed**.

**The obedience of faith** is obedience to the word of faith, brought about through the grace of faith.

Every act of praise that fallen human beings offer to God and that he accepts must come **through Jesus Christ**.

Only through Christ are our persons and works acceptable to the Father.

We must speak of **his righteousness alone**.

As Christ mediates our prayers, so he mediates **our praise now and forever**.

We are called to obedient faith.

Every degree of true wisdom comes from **the only wise God**.

Therefore, through both words and deeds, let us bring glory to him **through Jesus Christ**, praying that the Lord's grace will be with us forever.
