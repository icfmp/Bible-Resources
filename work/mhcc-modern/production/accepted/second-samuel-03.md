# 2 Samuel 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David's power increases His family. | 1–6 |
| Abner revolts to David. | 7–21 |
| Joab kills Abner David mourns for him. | 22–39 |

## David grows stronger (verses 1–6)

The long conflict tested David's faith and patience.

When he finally established his kingdom, the blessing was all the more welcome because he had waited for it.

This struggle pictures the war between **grace and corruption** in the hearts of believers.

It is a long conflict.

The flesh desires what opposes the Spirit, and the Spirit opposes the flesh.

As God's work of holiness progresses, corruption gradually grows weaker—like the household of Saul—while grace grows stronger, like the household of David.

## Abner leaves Ish-bosheth for David (verses 7–21)

Many people commit disgraceful offenses but are too proud to bear correction or even the suspicion that they may be guilty.

Those who continue in sin while pretending not to care often know within themselves that they are fighting against God.

Some pursue only their own advantage.

They will betray the people who trust them when they see something to gain.

Even through people driven by revenge, ambition or sinful desire, the Lord can carry out his own purposes.

But because such people do not intend to honor him, they may ultimately be cast aside with contempt.

David's willingness to receive **Michal** showed generosity both toward her and toward the memory of Saul.

He probably recalled that he had once owed his life to her love.

He also knew her separation from him had occurred partly through her father's authority.

No one should set his heart upon someone or something to which he has no rightful claim.

If disagreement has separated husband and wife, those who desire God's blessing should seek reconciliation and live together in love.

## Joab murders Abner; David mourns (verses 22–39)

Abner had acted scornfully, and judgment awaited him.

Yet **Joab's killing of him was wicked**.

David was deeply distressed by the murder and publicly demonstrated his hatred of the crime.

The guilt of murder can bring a curse upon families.

If human courts fail to hold murderers accountable, God will.

People act foolishly when they shorten their own days or make no preparation for the world to come.

How desirable is human authority, really?

A ruler can hold the title and bear responsibility, yet find his ability to do right severely limited.

David should have performed his duty and then trusted God with the outcome.

But worldly calculation led him to spare Joab.

The Son of David may delay judgment for a long time.

**He will never fail to judge unrepentant sinners**.

The One who now reigns on David's throne possesses a much greater kingdom.

Everything he does is observed with joy by those who willingly belong to him.
