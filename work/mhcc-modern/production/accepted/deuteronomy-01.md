# Deuteronomy 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

| Of the sending the spies—God's anger for their unbelief and disobedience. | 19–46 |

## Moses reminds Israel of the command to enter Canaan (verses 1–8)

Moses told the people everything the Lord had commanded him to say.

Horeb was only **eleven days' journey** from Kadesh-barnea.

Remembering that distance would help Israel understand how much suffering their own disobedience had brought upon them.

They had wandered for years because they had refused to believe God.

This memory should encourage them to recognize the benefits of obedience.

Now the Lord commanded them to move forward.

Although he brings his people through hardship and testing, he also knows when they have suffered long enough.

When he commands us to advance in the Christian life, he places the heavenly Canaan before us to encourage us.

## The appointment of judges (verses 9–18)

Moses reminded Israel of its well-ordered system of government.

If the people used it properly, the system could make life peaceful and secure.

He acknowledged that God had fulfilled the promise to multiply Abraham's descendants.

He also prayed that the Lord would continue fulfilling it.

God's goodness and power are not limited. Why should our faith and hope be limited?

The Lord had given Israel good laws and provided good people to administer them.

This showed both his care for Israel and the care Moses had taken for the people.

## The spies and Israel's unbelief (verses 19–46)

Moses recalled the difficult journey from Horeb to Kadesh-barnea through the great and terrifying wilderness.

By then the people were very close to Canaan.

The final ruin of hypocrites will be made more terrible by the realization that they had once been **not far from God's kingdom**.

Israel had every assurance that its God would go ahead.

But the people still wanted to send men ahead to investigate the land.

Everyone who saw Canaan had to admit that it was good.

What reason, then, did Israel have to distrust the Lord who promised to give it to them?

Unbelief lay beneath their whole rebellion.

Every refusal to obey God's law and every suspicion of his power and goodness ultimately springs from disbelief of his word.

True obedience, by contrast, flows from faith.

We would benefit from dividing our past lives into distinct periods.

For each period, we should thank God for the mercies we received, confess the sins we remember and seek forgiveness.

In this way we can renew our acceptance of God's salvation and our dedication to his service.

Our independent plans rarely lead us to the best outcome.

But courage born of faith and expressed through duty enables believers to follow the Lord fully, stand firm against obstacles, triumph over opposition and take hold of the blessings he promises.
