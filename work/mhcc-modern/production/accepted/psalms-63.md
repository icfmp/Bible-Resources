# Psalm 63 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David's desire toward God. | 1, 2 |
| His satisfaction in God. | 3–6 |
| His dependence upon God, and assurance of safety. | 7–11 |

## David thirsts for God (verses 1 and 2)

**“Early will I seek you.”**

A true Christian devotes the morning to God. As the eyes of the body open, the eyes of understanding should awaken to righteousness.

Such a believer wakes with a thirst for comfort the world cannot provide, and immediately turns in prayer to **the Fountain of the water of life**.

The immortal soul cannot be satisfied by anything this sinful world offers. It seeks happiness in God as its portion.

The more actively faith and hope work, the more the world resembles a **dry and weary desert**.

Then the heart longs for the joys of heaven, of which believers receive some foretastes through God's **appointed ordinances** here on earth.

## God's love is better than life (verses 3–6)

Even during affliction there are reasons to praise God.

A believer rightly values **God's loving-kindness above earthly life**. That kindness gives spiritual life, which is better than bodily existence.

Our praise should therefore arise from **holy joy**. Lips offering praise should be joyful lips.

David was continually in danger. Anxiety and fear kept him awake through exhausting nights.

Yet he found consolation in remembering God.

When the Lord's mercies come to mind during the night watches, they sustain the soul and make even the darkness cheerful.

How happy will be **the final morning**, when believers awaken satisfied with God's likeness and enjoy his fullness!

There will be no night, grief or sighing there, only joyful praise.

## Following God because his hand upholds us (verses 7–11)

Christians may sometimes speak with the confidence David expresses here, though too often their souls cling to the dust.

Having entrusted ourselves to the Lord, we ought to rest in his care without surrendering to fear.

Those who earnestly follow God would soon fail if **his right hand did not uphold them**.

He is the One who strengthens and comforts.

The psalmist expected that, although he was **sowing in tears**, he would eventually **reap in joy**.

The **Messiah, our Prince**, rejoices in God. He has entered the joy set before him, and his glory will be complete at **his second coming**.

Blessed Lord, let our desire for you increase every hour. May our love remain fixed upon you, and may all our joy and satisfaction be found in you.

Be everything to us during our present wilderness journey and bring us home to enjoy you forever.
