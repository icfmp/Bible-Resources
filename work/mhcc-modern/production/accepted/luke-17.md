# Luke 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| To avoid offences, To pray for increase of faith, Humility taught. | 11–19 |
| Ten lepers cleansed. | 1–10 |
| Christ's kingdom. | 20–37 |

## Avoid causing offense; forgive and serve humbly (verses 1–10)

The fact that offenses will occur does **not** lessen the responsibility or punishment of anyone who causes them.

Faith in God's forgiving mercy helps us overcome even great difficulties in **forgiving our brothers and sisters**.

Nothing is impossible for God, and remarkable things are possible to those who truly trust him.

Jesus taught the disciples that they needed **deep humility**.

The Lord owns and rules his creatures in a way no human master owns another person.

God **cannot become indebted to us** for our service.

Even our obedience does not earn a claim upon him, and our duties do not deserve a reward as though he owed us one.

## Jesus cleanses ten people with leprosy (verses 11–19)

Awareness of **our spiritual leprosy** should humble us as we approach Christ.

It is enough to appeal to his compassion, because his mercies never fail.

We may hope to find divine mercy when we are walking in the path of obedience.

Only **one of the ten healed people returned to thank Jesus**.

Like him, we should bring deep humility into **thanksgiving** as well as prayer.

Jesus particularly noticed the grateful man: he was **a Samaritan**.

The other nine received **physical healing**.

Henry distinguishes the Samaritan as the one who also received **a spiritual blessing**.

## The coming of God's kingdom and judgment (verses 20–37)

The kingdom of God was already among the Jewish people—or, more precisely, **within some of them**.

It is a spiritual kingdom established in the heart through the power of divine grace.

Jesus reminded people of earlier times when sinners continued their usual routines despite warnings of coming judgment.

Those who are careless and absorbed in pleasure will be terribly surprised when judgment overtakes them.

Henry applies this both to **the Roman destruction of the Jewish nation** and to **Christ's future return to judge the world**.

The same pattern appears in every generation: sinners live securely in wrongdoing, forgetting the end toward which they are moving.

Wherever people marked for final ruin may be, divine judgment will find them.

Let us therefore be ready for the revelation of the Son of Man.
