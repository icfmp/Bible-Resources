## The king's wedding feast (verses 1–14)

Jesus pictured God's provision for perishing souls as **a royal feast celebrating the marriage of a king's son**.

Our merciful God provides not merely basic food but an abundant banquet for rebellious creatures.

In the salvation given through Jesus Christ there is everything needed for present comfort and eternal happiness.

The **first guests invited were the Jews**.

The prophets, John the Baptist and Christ himself announced the coming kingdom.

After Jesus rose from the dead, the apostles and other gospel ministers were sent to proclaim that it had arrived and to invite people to receive salvation.

The reason sinners refuse Christ is **not a lack of ability for which they are innocent**, but their unwillingness to come.

Treating the Savior and his great salvation lightly is a deadly sin.

Many perish not through open hatred but through **carelessness about their souls**.

Farmers and merchants should attend diligently to their work.

But people occupied with worldly business and profit must keep **the world in their hands without letting it rule their hearts**.

The parable includes a warning of the destruction coming upon **the Jewish nation and its religious institutions in Henry's reading**.

Persecution of the gospel's faithful messengers completes the guilt of a people.

The unexpected invitation to Gentiles was like travelers suddenly being invited to a royal banquet.

The gospel gathers souls to Christ from across the world (**John 10:16; 11:52**).

The guest without a wedding garment represents **hypocrisy**.

Everyone should prepare to be examined by the King.

The garment belongs only to those who **put on the Lord Jesus**, have a Christ-shaped heart, live through faith in him and regard him as everything.

**Christ's imputed righteousness and the Spirit's sanctification are both necessary**.

No person possesses this garment by nature or can produce it through personal strength.

A day is coming when false professors will be judged for presumptuously claiming Christian privileges without genuine faith.

Such people use even the light of the gospel to descend into darkness.

Many are called, but fewer possess the garment of **Christ's righteousness and the Spirit's holiness**.

Let us examine whether we are in the faith and seek the King's approval.

## Paying taxes to Caesar (verses 15–22)

The Pharisees sent disciples to join with **the Herodians**, who favored submission to the Roman emperor.

Though the groups held opposed political views, they cooperated against Jesus.

Their words about Christ's integrity as a teacher were true, even though their motives were false.

He is indeed a faithful Teacher and bold corrector.

Jesus perceived their hypocrisy.

No disguise can hide a person's heart from him.

He did not take the role of civil judge in their dispute, since **his kingdom is not of this world**.

He taught peaceful submission to lawful governing authority.

The Christian faith is not an enemy of civil government.

Jesus astonished his opponents, who admired his wisdom without following its teaching and recognized his power without submitting to it.

## Jesus answers the Sadducees about resurrection (verses 23–33)

The Sadducees opposed Jesus' teaching no less than the Pharisees and Herodians.

Christ explained resurrection and the future state more fully than these truths had previously been revealed.

We cannot simply infer the condition of the life to come from the patterns of this present world.

When truth is displayed clearly, it has great power.

Jesus then demonstrated resurrection teaching **from Moses' writings**.

God declared himself the God of patriarchs who had already died.

Their continuing relation to him showed that they **still existed and could enjoy his favor**.

The Old Testament therefore taught resurrection as well as the New Testament, although it would be revealed more fully after Christ himself rose as **the firstfruits of those who sleep**.

Errors arise from ignorance of **Scripture and God's power**.

Death brings an end to our earthly connections, joys, griefs and expectations.

How miserable are those who hope for nothing better beyond it!

## The greatest commandments (verses 34–40)

An expert in the law questioned Jesus to test his judgment rather than merely his factual knowledge.

**Love for God** is the first and greatest commandment and summarizes the commands concerning our duty toward him.

Such love must be sincere, not merely spoken.

All the love we can offer is too small for his worth; therefore all our mind, affections and abilities should be directed toward him.

**Love for our neighbor as ourselves** is the second great commandment.

There is a corrupt form of self-love that causes serious sin and must be resisted and put to death.

But there is also a proper concern for the well-being of our **bodies and souls**.

That rightful concern becomes the measure of our duty to others.

We should sincerely seek our neighbors' welfare as we seek our own and often deny ourselves for their good.

Let these two commandments **shape our hearts like a mold**.

## How can David's son be David's Lord? (verses 41–46)

After silencing his opponents, Jesus asked about the identity of the promised Messiah.

How could the Messiah be **David's descendant** and yet **David's Lord**?

He quoted **Psalm 110:1**.

If Christ were only a human being born centuries after David died, why would David call his descendant **Lord**?

The Pharisees could not answer.

Henry maintains that the answer requires acknowledging that the Messiah is **the Son of God**, truly David's Lord along with the Father.

He took on human nature and thus became **God manifested in flesh**, the Son of Man and the Son of David.

Above all other questions we should seriously ask, **What do I think of Christ?**

Is he glorious to our eyes and precious to our hearts?

May he become our joy, confidence and everything.

May we grow daily in his likeness and service.
