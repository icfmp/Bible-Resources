## The altar of incense (verses 1–10)

The incense altar represented the Son of God in his human nature.

The incense burning upon it pictured his pleading on behalf of his people.

Its daily offering, morning and evening, represented Christ's continual intercession.

Once each year, the blood of atonement was applied to the altar.

This taught that Christ's intercession draws all its power from the suffering he endured on earth.

We need no sacrifice or intercessor apart from Christ himself.

## The ransom payment (verses 11–16)

The required contribution was half a shekel.

Henry compares this with about fifteen pence in the money of his own time.

The rich must not give more, and the poor must not give less.

The souls of rich and poor are equally precious.

God shows no favoritism (Acts 10:34; Job 34:19).

In other offerings, people gave in proportion to their financial means.

But this contribution represented the ransom of the soul and therefore had to be the same for everyone.

Every soul is of equal value.

All face the same danger, and all need a ransom.

The money collected supported the service of the tabernacle.

Those who benefit from public worship should not resent the expense of maintaining it.

Money cannot atone for anyone's soul.

But it can be used to honor the One who has made atonement and to support the proclamation of the gospel through which its benefits are applied.

## The bronze basin for washing (verses 17–21)

A large bronze basin filled with water stood near the tabernacle entrance.

Aaron and his sons were required to wash their hands and feet there each time they entered to minister.

This taught them to serve God in purity and to dread the defilement of sin.

They were not merely to be washed once, when they first became priests.

They also had to wash repeatedly and remain clean whenever they came to serve.

We likewise need to come to God every day.

We must continually renew our repentance and look to Christ's blood for forgiveness.

We offend against God in many ways each day.

## The holy oil and incense (verses 22–38)

God gave exact instructions for preparing the oil for anointing and the incense used in the tabernacle.

The fragrant oil, pleasing to the eye and to the sense of smell, pictured the excellence of holiness.

Christ's name is like perfume poured out (Song of Songs 1:3).

And the good name of a Christian is like precious ointment (Ecclesiastes 7:1).

The incense offered on the golden altar was made from sweet spices.

Before use it had to be crushed very fine.

Henry sees this as a picture of the Redeemer's bruising in sacrifice, an offering with a sweet fragrance to God.

The same oil and incense must not be manufactured for ordinary private use.

God thereby taught the people to revere the worship appointed for him.

We must not treat as common or misuse anything through which God reveals himself.

It is a great insult to God to make jokes about sacred things or amuse ourselves at the expense of his word and ordinances.

It is especially dangerous—and may prove fatal—to use an outward profession of Christ's gospel as a means of advancing worldly interests.
