# Matthew 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostles called. | 1–4 |
| The apostles instructed and sent forth. | 5–15 |
| Directions to the apostles. | 16–42 |

## Jesus appoints the twelve apostles (verses 1–4)

The word **apostle** means **messenger**. These men were Christ's messengers, sent to proclaim his kingdom.

Jesus gave them authority to heal every kind of sickness.

In the grace of the gospel there is a remedy for every spiritual wound and disease.

There is no spiritual illness for which Christ lacks the power to give healing.

The names of the apostles were recorded, and that was an honor.

Yet their greater reason to rejoice was that **their names were written in heaven**, while the names of powerful earthly rulers eventually pass into obscurity.

## The apostles receive their first mission (verses 5–15)

The apostles were initially directed to Israel.

Henry says that the gospel was not to be carried to the Gentiles **until the Jews had first been offered it and refused it**.

This restriction applied **only to their first mission**, not permanently.

Wherever they went, they were to announce: **The kingdom of heaven is near**.

Each word of that message matters.

They preached so that faith might be established.

They announced a **kingdom** to encourage hope.

They announced the kingdom **of heaven** to inspire love for heavenly things and a willingness to give up earthly attachments.

They said it was **near**, urging immediate preparation.

Jesus gave miraculous powers to confirm the truth of their teaching.

Henry judges that **such signs are not now necessary**, since God's kingdom has come.

Their meaning was that Christ's gospel heals spiritually sick souls and raises those dead in sin.

When proclaiming the **freely given grace of God**, those serving Christ should be especially careful to avoid even **the appearance of working merely for pay**.

The apostles received directions about entering unfamiliar towns and homes.

Christ's servant arrives as **an ambassador of peace**.

The message is intended even for the worst sinners, but the messenger should seek out people of upright character for hospitality and assistance.

We must pray earnestly for everyone and treat everyone courteously.

Those who reject the message must still hear **the whole counsel of God**, including the danger of refusing his gracious invitation.

People who hear the gospel should reflect seriously on their responsibility.

Otherwise, greater privileges may only increase the seriousness of their condemnation.

## Jesus prepares his messengers for persecution (verses 16–42)

Jesus warned his disciples that persecution would come.

They should avoid conduct that provides unnecessary advantages to their enemies: **political entanglements, worldly interference, the appearance of wrongdoing or self-interest, and deceitful methods**.

He foretold their suffering not simply to prepare them but also so that the fulfillment of his warning would strengthen their faith.

Christ told them what difficulties would come and who might cause them.

He did not conceal the cost of serving him.

We too should **count that cost**.

Persecutors may behave worse than wild animals because they attack people of their own kind.

Even strong family relationships can be torn apart by hostility to Jesus.

Suffering caused by close relatives or friends is especially painful.

All who seek to live godly lives in Christ must expect opposition and many troubles along the way to God's kingdom.

But Christ coupled his warnings with counsel and comfort.

**Be wise as serpents**. His servants must be careful when enemies try to destroy them.

**Be harmless as doves**. This means not merely refusing to injure anyone but also refusing to cherish ill will.

Prudence is necessary, but not anxious and tormenting worry. Cast those cares upon God.

Christians should be more concerned about **doing what is right** than about **speaking impressively**.

When danger becomes severe, followers of Christ may move out of danger, but they must never move **out of their duty**.

They must not use sinful or unlawful methods to escape; a way opened by disobedience is not a door opened by God.

Fear of people is a trap. It disorders peace of mind and entangles us in sin.

We should resist it and pray against it.

Trouble, distress and persecution cannot sever God's love for his people or their love for him.

We must fear **the One who can judge both soul and body**, rather than be ruled by fear of human beings.

The gospel must be announced publicly because everyone has a deep interest in its truth.

**The whole counsel of God must be declared** (**Acts 20:27**).

Christ supplied reasons for courage.

The persecution his disciples would suffer would become testimony against those opposing his gospel.

When God calls them to speak for him, they may depend on his help in knowing what to say.

Knowing that trials have an appointed end helps believers endure them.

They can persevere to the end because the Lord will continue sustaining them.

He provides strength suited to the day's need.

Those who work for Christ can be encouraged that **his work will certainly be accomplished**.

God's Providence extends to all his creatures, even the smallest sparrows.

His people are worth more than many sparrows.

**Even the hairs on their heads are numbered.** The Lord knows and cares for each person who belongs to him.

We must not merely believe in Christ in private.

When called to do so, we must **confess him openly**, even under persecution.

Henry qualifies the warning about denial: it concerns **a denial persisted in**.

The promised reward of confession likewise belongs to confession that truly and consistently arises from **faith and love**.

Religion is worth more than everything else.

Everyone who really knows its value will be willing to surrender competing interests for it.

Christ leads his people through suffering into glory.

Those least attached to earthly life are best prepared for the life to come.

Even a small kindness shown to one of Christ's disciples is accepted when it is all a person has the opportunity and ability to offer.

But such kindness **does not earn a claim against God**.

We can merit nothing from his hand.

Any reward is a **free gift of grace**.

Let us confess Christ courageously and demonstrate love for him in everything.

## Editorial review — Matthew 10

**Whole original-English secondary witness read:** [CCEL, Matthew 10](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xxxii.x.html). Exact published `MHCC 0.1.1` remains primary for source-native SWORD keys, annotation placement and independent editorial acceptance.

- **1–4:** apostles as **messengers** with healing signs, gospel salve for spiritual diseases, heavenly register of names surpassing worldly fame — retained.
- **5–15:** **Jewish-only initial mission explicitly temporary**, all parts of **kingdom-of-heaven-at-hand** message, miracles **not necessary now in Henry's view**, freely given gospel not hireling work, Christ's peaceful messenger and warning against refusal — retained.
- **16–42:** avoid **political/underhand entanglement**, advance warning of suffering, **family persecution**, serpent wisdom and dove harmlessness including **absence of malice**, flight from danger but **not from duty**, no unlawful escape, fear God, **Acts 20:27**, God teaching speech and sustaining perseverance, **sparrows and numbered hairs**, open profession, **persisted-in denial** distinguished from transient weakness, reward **gracious not earned** — retained.

**CCEL textual defects:** the secondary witness prints “**slave** for every sore” where coherent context indicates **salve**, and “**There names** are recorded” where “their” is required. The modern reading states the intended sense without altering or claiming a correction to the published original `MHCC 0.1.1`; primary-native collation remains necessary.
