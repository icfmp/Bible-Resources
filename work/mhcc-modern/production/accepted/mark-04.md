# Mark 4 — Modern English

## The sower and the soils (verses 1–20)

This parable contains teaching so important that everyone capable of hearing should pay attention.

If we do not understand the **plain truths of the gospel**, how will we understand its deeper teachings?

Considering the unhappy condition of people without gospel privileges should make Christ's disciples value those privileges more highly.

In the church's great field, God's word is announced widely.

But relatively few hearers receive it in a way that produces **lasting fruit**.

Many feel moved or enthusiastic for a time yet receive no enduring benefit.

The reason the word leaves so little permanent impression is that their hearts have not been prepared to receive it.

The devil is especially active among careless hearers, like birds eating seed left exposed on a path.

Many continue in a barren religious profession until they perish.

Shallow religious feelings cannot endure.

Many neglect the **work of the heart**, without which religion has no true substance.

Others are hindered by **an abundance of worldly concerns and possessions**.

But people who own little can also be ruined by giving themselves over to **bodily indulgence**.

God rightly expects fruit from everyone privileged to hear his gospel.

This includes a renewed disposition, Christian graces exercised each day and duties faithfully performed.

Ask the Lord, through his **new-creating grace**, to make our hearts good soil.

May his word produce **good words and works through Jesus Christ**, bringing glory to God the Father.

## The lamp and the growing seed (verses 21–34)

Jesus taught these parables to call his disciples' full attention to **his word**.

Through learning from him, they would become able to teach others.

A lamp is lit to illuminate a room, not to be hidden beneath a container.

Likewise, disciples receive light in order to help others.

The growing seed pictures how God's kingdom advances.

When Christ's word occupies its rightful place in a soul, the change becomes visible through **good conduct**.

Growth is gradual: **first the blade, then the ear, then the full grain in the ear**.

Once genuine spiritual life has begun, it advances.

The first work of grace may seem like **a very small beginning**.

Even while it is still growing it produces significant blessings.

How much greater will its glory be when that work is **perfected in heaven**!

## Jesus calms the storm (verses 35–41)

Jesus slept during the storm to test his disciples' faith and move them to pray.

Their faith was weak, but their prayers were urgent.

When our sinful hearts resemble a restless sea and our passions become unruly, let us hear Christ's command: **“Be silent! Be still!”**

When we experience outward conflict and inward fear, and our spirits are overwhelmed, one word from him can bring **an immediate, great calm**.

Jesus asked why they were so frightened.

Some fear may be understandable, but not fear so great that people imagine **Jesus does not care whether his followers perish**.

Such thoughts expose weakness in faith.

Even the best believers remain imperfect in this world.

Fear and faith alternate while we are here.

But soon **fear will be overcome and faith will give way to sight**.
