# Revelation 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Those faithful to Christ celebrate the praises of God. | 1–5 |
| Three angels; one proclaiming the everlasting gospel; another, the downfal of Babylon; and a third, the dreadful wrath of God on the worshippers of the beast. The blessedness of those who die in the Lord. | 6–13 |
| A vision of Christ with a sickle, and of a harvest ripe for cutting down. | 14–16 |
| The emblem of a vintage fully ripe, trodden in the wine-press of God's wrath. | 17–20 |

## The Lamb and his faithful people on Mount Zion (verses 1–5)

**Mount Zion** represents Christ's gospel church.

Jesus remains with her even amid all her troubles.

His presence protects the church from destruction and secures the perseverance of his people.

They appear with honor.

God's name written upon their foreheads shows that they openly and boldly confess **faith in God and Jesus Christ**, and then live in a way consistent with that confession.

Even in the darkest periods, some believers have been willing to suffer and die for the truth and worship of Christ's gospel.

They kept themselves from the wicked practices of those Henry identifies with Antichrist.

Their hearts were sincerely devoted to God.

They were **freely forgiven in Christ**, bringing glory to him and receiving glory in him.

Let our prayer, our labor and our ambition be to belong to that company.

The passage speaks about people **truly justified and sanctified**.

No hypocrite, however convincing an outward appearance may be, can be judged without fault before God.

## The gospel proclaimed, Babylon warned and the faithful blessed (verses 6–13)

Henry suggests that this scene **appears to describe the progress of the Protestant Reformation**.

He sees the four proclamations or declarations in this passage as encouragement for Christians to remain faithful during testing.

This is his historical reading, not a prophetic date or identification independently established here.

The **everlasting gospel** is God's great means of bringing people to fear him and give him glory.

Its proclamation shakes the foundations of powers hostile to Christ and hastens their downfall.

Those who continue willingly serving the beast and promoting its cause, Henry warns, face **misery of both soul and body forever**.

Believers, by contrast, should be willing to endure hardship and loss while obeying God's commands and confessing their faith in Jesus.

May God grant us that patience.

Consider the blessedness of **those who die in the Lord**.

They die united to Christ and in the service of his cause; they belong to him when death comes.

They rest from **sin, temptation, grief and persecution**, where the wicked cease troubling them and the weary find repose.

Their works follow them.

Those works do **not** go before them as **a price paid for salvation or a title by which to claim it**.

They follow as **evidence** that these believers lived and died in Christ.

Remembering their service will bring joy, while their reward will far exceed any labor or suffering they endured.

The Holy Spirit assures them of this by his inward testimony and through the written word.

## The harvest and the winepress (verses 14–20)

The warnings and earlier judgments have failed to bring universal repentance.

The wickedness of the nations reaches its full measure, so they become ripe for judgment.

The **harvest** can represent God's merciful gathering of righteous people into heaven when they are ready.

Grain is harvested when it has ripened.

In the same way, God's faithful people will be gathered into **Christ's storehouse** at the proper time.

The **grape harvest and winepress**, by contrast, represent judgment on Christ's enemies.

The Lord does not destroy them until their sins have ripened into complete guilt.

The winepress expresses **the wrath of God** in some dreadful calamity—Henry suggests **probably the sword and bloodshed of the wicked**.

God's patience toward sinners is an astonishing miracle.

But although his patience lasts a long time, it will not last forever.

When sin has reached its full measure, judgment is near.
