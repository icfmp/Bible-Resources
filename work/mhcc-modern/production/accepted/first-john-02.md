# 1 John 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle directs to the atonement of Christ for help against sinful infirmities. | 1, 2 |
| The effects of saving knowledge in producing obedience, and love to the brethren. | 3–11 |
| Christians addressed as little children, young men, and fathers. | 12–14 |
| All are cautioned against the love of this world, and against errors. | 15–23 |
| They are encouraged to stand fast in faith and holiness. | 24–29 |

## Jesus is our righteous Advocate (verses 1 and 2)

We have **an Advocate before the Father**, who willingly and completely pleads for everyone seeking forgiveness and salvation through him.

He is **Jesus, the Savior**, and **Christ, the anointed Messiah**.

He alone is **the Righteous One**: free from sin in his nature and, as our Surety, perfectly obedient to God's law.

People of every land and generation are invited to seek God through his **all-sufficient atonement** and this new and living way.

When properly understood and received, the gospel turns the heart against **every sin** and ends its deliberate practice, while also giving wonderful relief to guilty consciences.

## Knowing Jesus produces obedience and brotherly love (verses 3–11)

What sort of knowledge of Christ could fail to recognize his worthiness of our obedience?

Disobedience in conduct exposes both dishonesty and the absence of genuine religion.

God's love accomplishes its purpose in those who keep his commands.

Through grace it progressively renews them, although no believer becomes absolutely perfect during this life.

If Christ's commands were kept universally, the earth would begin to resemble heaven.

The command **to love one another** has existed since creation, yet is **new for Christians** because of the new motives, rules and obligations arising from their relationship with Christ.

Those who hate other believers remain in spiritual darkness.

Christian love values another person's **soul**, guarding against anything harmful to that person's purity or peace.

Where spiritual darkness rules the mind, judgment and conscience become confused about the way to eternal life.

We should therefore examine ourselves seriously and pray for God to show us **what we are and where we are going**.

## Children, young people and parents in faith (verses 12–14)

Christians at different stages of life have particular responsibilities, but some commands—including mutual love and refusing worldliness—belong to everyone.

Even the youngest genuine disciple is **forgiven**.

Long-standing Christians still need teaching. No one becomes too old to hear Scripture or receive helpful correction.

Young Christians may have gained spiritual strength, resisted early temptations, abandoned corrupt habits and associations and entered through the narrow gate of conversion.

Children in Christ know **God as their Father**.

Mature believers know the One who existed before the world's creation and therefore should be ready to let the world go.

Young people are honored when they become **strong in Christ through grace**.

Through God's word they overcome the wicked one.

## Do not love the passing world (verses 15–17)

The things of this world may be desired, possessed and used for **the purposes God intended**, according to his grace and for his glory.

But believers should not value them for the sinful purposes to which people often turn them.

As love for the world increases, **love for God declines**.

Henry groups worldly desire into three forms.

**First, the desire of the flesh:** pursuing sensual gratification and bodily pleasures.

**Second, the desire of the eyes:** delighting in wealth and possessions through greed.

**Third, the pride of life:** craving prestige, luxury, honor and applause.

Worldly objects and even the desires directed toward them will pass away. But **love for God will not fail**.

People have tried to escape these warnings through clever distinctions about how far they may love the world and still claim to be Christians.

But the passage's meaning is plain.

Unless victory over worldliness **begins inwardly**, a profession of faith has no lasting root or fruit.

Because worldly temptations are so attractive to our corrupt nature, we must remain watchful and prayerful, seeking victory over the ruler of this world.

## Reject lies about the Son and the Father (verses 18–23)

Henry calls anyone **antichristian** who rejects Christ's true person or any essential aspect of his work.

Rejecting the Son also denies the Father, and rejects God's appointed salvation.

The prophecy that deceivers would appear should make Christians vigilant.

The visible church cannot know with certainty who among its professed members truly belongs to Christ.

False teachings expose some who seemed to belong and warn others to remain humble.

True Christians are **anointed by the Holy Spirit**, receiving his grace, gifts and privileges.

The most dangerous lies of Satan often concern **the person of Jesus Christ**.

Only the Spirit's anointing can preserve believers from such deception.

We should judge kindly all who truly trust **Jesus as the divine Savior**, obey his word and seek fellowship with them.

At the same time, Henry urges Christians to **pray with compassion for those denying Christ's deity, atonement or the Holy Spirit's renewing work**, while clearly resisting those teachings.

## Remain in the truth and practice righteousness (verses 24–29)

The truth of Christ dwelling within a person provides a means of separation from sin and unites that person to God's Son (**John 15:3–4**).

How highly we should value gospel truth!

Through it God assures believers of **eternal life**.

The Holy Spirit, the Spirit of truth, teaches everything needed to know God in Christ and to recognize his glory in the gospel.

John's affectionate words **“little children”** show that he seeks to persuade through love.

Gospel privileges carry matching duties.

Those anointed by Jesus should remain in him and live by the new spiritual nature he grants.

Faithful perseverance through times of testing is evidence of **new birth from Christ**.

Beware of knowing the truth while living unrighteously.

Those genuinely born of God bear his holy likeness and follow his righteous ways.

## Editorial review — 1 John 2

**Full CCEL English secondary witness read:** [CCEL, 1 John 2](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.liv.ii.html). The exact released `MHCC 0.1.1` remains source authority, native SWORD and independent QA pending.

- **1 and 2:** two separately cited verses; **Christ as sinless Advocate and all-sufficient sacrifice**, gospel ending allowed sin while comforting wounded conscience — retained.
- **3–11:** obedience proving knowledge and loving fellow believers, **new-old command** and prayerful self-examination — retained.
- **12–14:** children's pardon, mature believers **still needing teaching**, young believers' strength through the Word — retained.
- **15–17:** lawful possessions vs idolatrous use, **three lusts** flesh/eyes/pride, worldly love displacing divine love — retained.
- **18–23:** anti-Christ denial of Son/person/work, spiritual anointing, compassion and prayer for those in error while resisting error — retained.
- **24–29:** **John 15:3–4** source references, Spirit teaching, loving exhortation, holiness and perseverance as signs of new birth — retained.

**Structural difference:** the original printed **outline has five groups**, including **15–23** as one, but **source prose has six groups**, dividing that scope into **15–17** and **18–23**. Both are preserved exactly rather than inventing one original-native merged SWORD unit.
