# Hosea 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's judgments against the sins of the people. | 1–5 |
| and of the priests. | 6–11 |
| Idolatry is reproved, and Judah is admonished. | 12–19 |

## The sins of the people (verses 1–5)

Hosea condemned **immorality as well as idolatry**.

Truth, mercy and knowledge of God had disappeared from the land.

Violence and murder filled it, as Henry also recalls in **2 Kings 21:16**.

Disaster was approaching and would leave the country desolate.

Our sins—whether committed as individuals, families, neighbors or nations—give the Lord cause to bring charges against us.

We should therefore submit to him and humble ourselves, lest his judgments continue toward destruction.

## Priests and people reject God's knowledge (verses 6–11)

Both priests and people had rejected **the knowledge of God**.

God would therefore justly reject them.

They forgot his law.

They neither desired nor tried to keep it in mind or teach it to their descendants.

Therefore God would forget them and their children.

When we use for dishonor the gifts that ought to bring honor to God, those very gifts may become **our shame**.

The sacrifices should have warned people how seriously God regards sin: **atonement was needed**.

Instead, the priests encouraged sin by treating its remedy as **cheap and easy**.

It is wicked to be pleased with other people's wrongdoing because we expect to profit from it.

Gains acquired unlawfully cannot be enjoyed with a peaceful conscience.

Priests and people **strengthened one another in wickedness**, so they would share the consequences.

Those who share in sin should expect to share **its ruin**.

A secret sinful desire allowed to remain in the heart can gradually destroy **its spiritual strength**.

That explains why many professing believers become **dull, lifeless and sluggish in serving God**.

Some hidden desire has taken hold of their hearts.

## Idolatry and God's warning to Judah (verses 12–19)

The people consulted **idols instead of God's word**.

That led to disorder and sin.

In doing so, they prepared punishments for themselves and helped wrongdoing spread through the nation.

**Judah must not imitate Israel's idolatry**.

Israel had surrendered itself to false worship and now faced the dreadful command **“Leave them alone.”**

When people cast off the **gentle yoke of Christ**, they may continue sinning until God abandons them to their chosen path.

They then receive no further warnings and feel no conviction.

Satan takes hold of them, and they move toward destruction.

Being **left undisturbed in sin** is a severe divine judgment.

Those who face no disturbance over their sin may ultimately be **destroyed because of it**.

May God keep us from that dreadful state.

His judgment, like a powerful storm, will soon carry hardened sinners toward ruin.
