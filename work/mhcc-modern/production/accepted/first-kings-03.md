# 1 Kings 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Solomon's marriage. | 1–4 |
| His vision, His prayer for wisdom. | 5–15 |
| The judgment of Solomon. | 16–28 |

## Solomon loves the Lord (verses 1–4)

Someone who loves the Lord should, for his sake, seek a spouse among the Lord's own people.

Solomon was wise, wealthy and powerful.

Yet his brightest commendation was one shared by every genuine believer, even the poorest: **he loved the Lord**.

Where God gives much, he rightly expects much in return.

Those who genuinely love him and his worship will not resent the cost of serving him.

We should never regard what we spend in God's service as wasted.

## Solomon asks for wisdom (verses 5–15)

Solomon's dream was no ordinary dream.

Although his bodily powers were asleep, God strengthened his mind and soul so that he could receive a divine vision and make a fitting choice.

The Lord likewise puts us on the way toward true happiness by assuring us that he will provide what we genuinely need and ask for.

Solomon's decision, made while his ordinary reasoning faculties were least active, showed that the choice arose from **God's grace**.

He felt humbly how little wisdom and strength he possessed.

He pleaded that he was like a small child.

The wiser and more thoughtful people become, the more clearly they recognize their weakness and the more cautious they become about trusting their own judgment.

Solomon asked God for wisdom.

We must do the same (**James 1:5**), so that we can fulfill our own calling and respond well to the many circumstances we face.

The Lord accepts people who prize spiritual blessings above earthly advantages.

Solomon's prayer was effective—and God gave him **more than he asked**.

He received wisdom greater than that of any other ruler, together with wealth and honor.

If we secure wisdom and grace, they may bring outward benefits, or they will sweeten the lack of those benefits.

To receive spiritual blessings, we must seek them earnestly in prayer.

Concerning earthly benefits, we must entrust ourselves to God's decision.

**Solomon received wisdom because he asked for it, and wealth because he did not ask for it.**

## Solomon identifies the true mother (verses 16–28)

This judgment demonstrates Solomon's God-given wisdom.

The situation was difficult.

To discover which woman was the child's mother, he could not test which woman the child **loved most**.

He therefore tested **which woman loved the child most**.

When a child appeared to be in danger, the true mother's love became clear.

Parents should show love to their children chiefly by caring for their **souls**, seeking to rescue them like brands from a fire.

Solomon's wisdom in this and other judgments made him greatly respected throughout Israel.

Such wisdom did him more good than weapons of war.

Through it he became **both feared and loved**.
