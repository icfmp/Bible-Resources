# Luke 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The resurrection of Christ. | 1–12 |
| He appears to two disciples on the way to Emmaus. | 13–27 |
| And makes himself known to them. | 28–35 |
| Christ appears to the other disciples. | 36–49 |
| His ascension. | 50–53 |

## Jesus' resurrection is announced (verses 1–12)

The women showed deep love and respect for Jesus even after his burial.

They were surprised to discover the stone rolled away and the grave empty.

Believers often turn something that should bring **comfort and encouragement** into a source of needless fear.

The women expected to find their Master's body in burial cloths; instead, they saw angels in brilliant clothing.

The angels assured them that **Jesus had risen from the dead by his own power**.

They did not bring an entirely new gospel.

They reminded the women of **Jesus' own words** and taught them to apply what they had already heard.

Why were disciples who believed Jesus was God's Son and had heard repeated predictions of his death and resurrection so slow to believe it?

They had also watched him raise other people from the dead.

Our religious errors often come through **ignorance or forgetfulness of Christ's words**.

Peter now ran toward Jesus' tomb—the same Peter who had recently run from Jesus.

He remained astonished.

Many difficulties that confuse us would become clear and useful if we understood Christ's teaching correctly.

## Jesus joins two travelers on the way to Emmaus (verses 13–27)

Jesus appeared to the two disciples **on the day of his resurrection**.

It is fitting for his followers to discuss his death and resurrection.

Such conversation can deepen knowledge, refresh memory and awaken holy affections.

Where even two believers are helpfully discussing Christ, **he may come among them as a third companion**.

Those who sincerely seek him will find him, and he grants understanding to those who make use of the means of instruction already available.

The disciples were prevented from recognizing Jesus at first, enabling them to speak more freely with him.

His followers can remain sorrowful even when they have great reason for joy, because weak faith keeps them from receiving comfort.

Though Jesus had entered his glorified state, he still noticed the sorrows of his disciples.

Everyone should know about his suffering and death.

Those who know Christ crucified ought to seek opportunities to make him known.

Jesus rebuked the disciples because they had failed to believe **the Old Testament Scriptures**.

If we better understood what God has already revealed, we would escape many avoidable confusions.

He showed that **suffering was the appointed road to his glory**.

But the disciples could not reconcile themselves to the cross.

Beginning with **Moses**, Jesus explained everything written concerning himself.

Throughout the Old Testament are prophecies, promises, prayers and types pointing toward Christ.

**A golden thread of gospel grace runs through the whole fabric of Scripture**.

Christ is the best interpreter of God's word.

Even after rising from death, he did not teach novel doctrines unrelated to Scripture.

He showed **how the Scriptures had been fulfilled** and urged his people to study them earnestly.

## Jesus is recognized at the breaking of bread (verses 28–35)

If we wish Christ to remain with us, we should earnestly ask for his presence.

Those who have enjoyed fellowship with him naturally long for more.

Jesus took bread, blessed it, broke it and gave it to the disciples.

He probably acted with his familiar affection and authority, perhaps even using the same words he had used before.

We learn from him to **ask God's blessing on our ordinary meals**.

Christ makes himself known spiritually to his people.

He opens the Scriptures.

He meets believers at his table in **the Lord's Supper**, where he is known in the breaking of bread.

Yet full understanding comes through **the opening of the mind's eyes**.

Here we see only brief glimpses of Jesus; in heaven we will see him continually.

The disciples had felt the power of his exposition even before recognizing their teacher.

Scripture passages that speak of Christ warm the hearts of genuine disciples.

Teaching that awakens love for **Jesus who died for us** is especially fruitful.

Those to whom he reveals himself should tell others what he has done for their souls.

Sharing and comparing the experiences of grace can greatly benefit Christians.

## Jesus appears to the gathered disciples (verses 36–49)

Jesus appeared suddenly and miraculously.

He offered the disciples **his peace**, even though they had recently abandoned him.

He promised all the spiritual blessings included in that peace.

Many of our troubled thoughts arise from mistaken views of Christ.

He knows every thought that disturbs our hearts.

He challenged the disciples' unreasonable unbelief.

Everything that happened had been foretold by the prophets and was necessary for sinners' salvation.

The world must now hear **the need for repentance and the promise of forgiveness**, sought through **faith in Jesus' name**.

Christ works upon people's minds through his Spirit.

Even good people need the Lord to **open their understanding**.

To think rightly about Jesus, we need not invent new explanations beyond God's revelation: we need to **understand the Scriptures**.

## Jesus ascends into heaven (verses 50–53)

Jesus ascended near **Bethany and the Mount of Olives**, where his agony had begun.

The road to heaven passes through a world of sorrow and trial.

The disciples had not watched him physically rise from the grave.

They knew he had risen because they had seen him alive afterward.

But they **saw his ascent into heaven**, the necessary evidence that he had truly ascended.

He raised his hands and **blessed them**.

He did not leave in anger but in love, leaving a blessing behind.

As with his resurrection, Jesus ascended **by his own power**.

The disciples worshiped him.

The fresh display of Christ's glory led to renewed confession and praise.

They returned to Jerusalem **with great joy**.

His glory brings happiness to believers even while they remain in this world.

As we wait for God's promises, we should meet them with thanksgiving and praise.

Nothing better prepares the heart to receive the Holy Spirit.

Praise silences fear, softens sorrow and sustains hope.

We can come boldly before the Father's throne because it is also the throne of **our Mediator, Jesus Christ**.

Let us rely on his promises and plead them in prayer.

Let us honor his appointed worship, thank God for mercy, set our affections on heavenly things and long for **the Redeemer's return** to complete our blessedness.

**Amen. Even so, Lord Jesus, come quickly.**
