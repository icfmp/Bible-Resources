## The high priest's sin offering (verses 1–12)

Burnt offerings, grain offerings and peace offerings were known before the law was given at Sinai.

Through them, the patriarchs already sought atonement for sin.

Now Israel received more detailed instructions for bringing sacrifices to make atonement.

These offerings were shadows of greater blessings yet to come.

Their reality is Christ and the single offering of himself through which he put away sin.

The sins addressed by these offerings were understood to be **outward acts**.

They were **things done** that ought not to have been done.

Failure to do our duty is also sin and will come under God's judgment.

Yet something neglected earlier may sometimes still be done later, whereas a wrong act already committed cannot be undone.

These offerings particularly addressed sins committed through **ignorance**.

The instructions begin with the anointed priest.

This showed that God never established an infallible priest in his earthly church.

Even the high priest could commit sins through ignorance.

Henry therefore condemns claims to act without error as a mark of Antichrist.

The animal was taken outside the camp and burned completely to ashes.

This pictured repentance: putting away sin as something hateful that our souls reject.

The offering was itself called **sin**.

What was done to the animal is what we must do to our sins.

The body of sin must be destroyed (Romans 6:6).

The apostle also applies the sacrifice's removal outside the camp to Christ (Hebrews 13:11–13).

## The whole congregation's sin offering (verses 13–21)

The people's leaders could make mistakes that led the whole community into wrongdoing.

In that case, an offering had to be brought, so that divine anger would not fall upon the congregation.

People on whose behalf a sacrifice was offered laid their hands upon the animal and confessed their sins.

When the sacrifice was for the whole community, the elders performed this action.

The guilt of the people was pictured as a burden placed upon an innocent animal.

Once the offering had been completed, the Lord declared that atonement had been made and the sin would be forgiven.

Whenever churches and nations are saved from destruction, they owe that deliverance to the satisfaction and mediation of Christ.

## The ruler's sin offering (verses 22–26)

People who possess authority to call others to account must remember that they themselves are accountable to the **Ruler of all rulers**.

Even a ruler can sin through ignorance.

That sin must be brought to the ruler's attention, either by conscience or by the correction of friends.

Even the best and greatest people should receive such correction thankfully rather than resentfully.

Each day we should pray, “Teach me what I do not see,” and “Show me where I have gone wrong.”

Then, if ignorance leads us into sin, ignorance will not keep us living in it.

## The ordinary person's sin offering (verses 27–35)

The law also provided an offering for anyone among the people.

A person accused of sin may say, “I acted without knowing,” or “Temptation took me by surprise.”

But that plea alone cannot secure acquittal.

The only sufficient plea is that **Christ has died**.

Even the ordinary person's sins of ignorance required sacrifice.

No one is too important to be under God's justice.

No one is so humble as to be overlooked by it.

Rich and poor meet here as equals.

Both are sinners, and both are welcome to come to Christ.

These laws teach us to hate sin, keep watch against it and greatly value Christ—the true and complete Sin Offering.

His blood cleanses from all sin.

The blood of bulls and goats could never actually take away sin.

When we have the Bible in our hands, our remaining mistakes and ignorance often result from pride, laziness and carelessness.

We therefore need to examine ourselves frequently.

We should study Scripture seriously and pray earnestly for the Holy Spirit's convicting work.

Then we may discover sins we did not recognize, repent of them and obtain forgiveness through Christ's blood.
