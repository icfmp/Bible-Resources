# Psalm 81 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God is praised for what he has done for his people. | 1–7 |
| Their obligations to him. | 8–16 |

## Praising the God who delivers (verses 1–7)

All the worship we can give God falls short of his excellence and of what we owe him—especially for **redeeming us from sin and judgment**.

Israel was commanded to remember God's mighty deeds through appointed public festivals.

To appreciate the greatness of a deliverance, we should remember how dreadful the danger or bondage was from which we were rescued.

We must not forget **the degrading and ruinous slavery** into which Satan, our oppressor, once brought us.

When distress of conscience drives us to cry to the Lord, he answers and sets us free.

Conviction of sin and the trials of affliction may become signs of his faithful concern for us.

If Israel remembered deliverance from Egypt on its feasts, how much more should Christians remember **the greater redemption accomplished through Jesus Christ** when gathering on the Christian Sabbath!

He has rescued us from a bondage far worse than Israel's slavery.

## God calls his people to listen and obey (verses 8–16)

We should expect **little from created things and much from the Creator**.

By faith and prayer, we may receive from God everything truly necessary.

The wickedness of the world arises from **human willfulness**.

People refuse true religion because they do not want to submit to it.

God is **not the author of their sin**.

He leaves stubborn sinners to the desires of their own hearts and to their own plans. If they fail to do good, they must bear responsibility.

The Lord does not desire that any should perish.

What terrible enemies sinners become to themselves!

Sin prolongs our troubles and delays our enjoyment of salvation.

The fertile hills and fields of Canaan pictured spiritual and eternal blessings held through **faith and obedience**.

Christ is **the Bread of Life**, the Rock of salvation, and his promises are as sweet as honey to a godly soul.

But those who reject him as **Lord and Master** cannot expect to enjoy him as **Savior and reward**.
