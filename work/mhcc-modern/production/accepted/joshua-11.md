# Joshua 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Divers kings overcome at the waters of Merom. | 1–9 |
| Hazor is taken and burned. | 10–14 |
| All that country subdued, The Anakims cut off. | 15–23 |

## The kings are defeated at the waters of Merom (verses 1–9)

God's miracles for Israel were intended to encourage the people to act energetically themselves.

In the same way, preaching the gospel against Satan's kingdom was initially accompanied and helped by miracles.

Now that the gospel has been clearly demonstrated to be from God, we depend on his grace in its ordinary operation and use the **sword of the Spirit**.

God encouraged Joshua.

Fresh dangers and difficulties call for fresh support from the word of God, which is always near us and available in every time of need.

God measures our trials according to our strength and supplies strength according to our trials.

Joshua obeyed God's command to destroy the enemy's horses and chariots. His obedience showed self-denial.

Possessing things on which the worldly heart is tempted to rely can harm faith and fellowship with God.

It is better to lose worldly advantages than endanger our souls through dependence on them.

## Hazor is captured and burned (verses 10–14)

The Canaanites had filled the measure of their sins.

As a judgment, God left them to their pride, stubbornness, hostility and Satan's power.

The restraints that might have checked them were removed.

God's providential dealings exposed them to despair.

Through their rebellion, they brought upon themselves the punishment they deserved.

The Israelites carried out the judgment God had commanded through Moses.

## The territory is subdued and the Anakim defeated (verses 15–23)

Let God's Israel never fear the descendants of Anak: their fall will come at the appointed time.

The land had **rest from war**.

That was not a peaceful alliance with the Canaanites, which God had forbidden.

It meant that the Canaanites were no longer at war with Israel.

A better rest remains for God's people. They will enter it after their spiritual warfare is complete.

What happened here fulfilled what God had said to Moses.

When God's word and his works are considered together, each helps explain the other.

If we obey our duty conscientiously, we may trust that God will fulfill his promise.

But believers must never lay aside their armor or expect permanent peace in this life.

As their strength and usefulness grow, they may face more difficult trials.

Yet God will not allow any enemy to attack them before he has prepared them for the struggle.

Jesus Christ lives forever to intercede for his people.

Their faith will not finally fail, however severely Satan may attack.

Even when spiritual conflict is long, sharp and difficult, we can endure patiently through joyful hope.

Before long, the believer will rest from both sin and sorrow in the heavenly Canaan.
