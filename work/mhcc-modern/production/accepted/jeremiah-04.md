# Jeremiah 4 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Return to God sincerely (verses 1–2)

The first two verses of this chapter should be read together with **the end of chapter 3**.

Sin must be removed **from the heart**.

Otherwise, it has not been removed from **God's sight**, because the entire heart is open before him.

## The hard heart must be broken up (verses 3–4)

A heart that has never been humbled resembles **ground left untilled**.

It is ground capable of improvement and entrusted to us for cultivation.

But it lies unused and is covered in **thorns and weeds**, the natural growth of a corrupt heart.

Let us beg God to **create a clean heart in us** and to renew a right spirit within us.

Unless a person is **born again**, that person cannot enter heaven's kingdom.

## Judah is warned of an approaching invader (verses 5–18)

A fierce conqueror who had already defeated the surrounding nations would bring desolation upon Judah.

Jeremiah was distressed that **false prophets** had soothed the people into a dangerous feeling of security.

He described the enemy's approach.

Some attention had been paid in Jerusalem to **outward reform**.

But this was not enough.

The people's **hearts needed washing**, through true repentance and faith, to remove the love and pollution of sin.

When lesser troubles do not awaken sinners or bring nations to reform, a more decisive judgment will be pronounced.

The Lord's voice announces that misery is approaching.

Henry especially warns those who **profess the gospel but live wickedly**.

When judgment falls, the bitterness of sin's fruit and the fatal nature of its end will become undeniable.

## Jeremiah grieves over Judah's coming ruin (verses 19–31)

The prophet took no pleasure in announcing messages of divine anger.

In a vision he saw the whole land thrown into confusion.

Compared with its former condition, everything had fallen into disorder.

Yet **Judah's ruin would not be final**.

The end of a particular earthly comfort is not necessarily the end of everything.

The Lord may correct his people **with great severity**, but he will not abandon them entirely.

Cosmetics, ornaments and flattering appearances will offer no protection from the coming disaster.

Neither outward privileges, religious profession nor human schemes can prevent judgment.

How miserable are people who remain **like foolish children in matters concerning their souls**!

Whatever else we fail to understand, may God give us wisdom in the ways of godliness.

Sin will ultimately find the sinner.

And sorrow, sooner or later, will find those who feel secure while rejecting God's warnings.
