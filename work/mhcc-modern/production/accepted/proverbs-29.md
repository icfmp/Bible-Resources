# Proverbs 29 — Modern English

## Verse 1 — Flee from the coming judgment

If God wounds, who can heal? His word warns everyone to **flee from the wrath to come** and take hold of the hope set before us in **Jesus Christ**.

## Verse 2 — Righteousness in government

People have reason to rejoice when their rulers are righteous and to mourn when those rulers are wicked.

## Verse 3 — Wisdom protects against lust

Divine wisdom is our strongest safeguard against the sinful desires that bring people to ruin.

## Verse 4 — Christ rules with justice

**The Lord Jesus** is the King who will administer true justice among his people.

## Verse 5 — Flattery disarms

Flatterers cause people to lower their guard, making it easier to draw them into foolish behavior.

## Verse 6 — Sin entangles; righteousness frees

Transgression ends in distress, while righteous people walk **in liberty and safety**.

## Verse 7 — Care for the poor

The righteous show compassion for people in poverty and distress. The wicked disregard their needs without feeling.

## Verse 8 — Mockery and public peace

Scornful people laugh at holy and serious matters. Those who advance **true religion and wisdom** help turn away God's wrath.

## Verse 9 — Argument with a conceited person

When a wise person disputes with a quarrelsome, conceited opponent, anger or ridicule often follows, and little good results.

## Verse 10 — Love for those who hate us

Christ warned his disciples that people would hate them. Yet the righteous, even when hated by bloodthirsty people, willingly do what they can **for those people's salvation**.

## Verse 11 — Keep counsel

A fool says everything that comes to mind or has been learned and cannot keep a confidence.

## Verse 12 — Bad leadership spreads lies

A ruler who welcomes flatterers and listens to slander makes liars and false accusers of those who serve him.

## Verse 13 — All live under God's gifts

Some people are poor, while others possess great wealth dishonestly acquired. They meet in daily business, and **God supplies life's comforts to both**. He also gives saving grace to some among both groups.

## Verse 14 — Defend those without power

Rich people usually look after their own interests. A ruler should **protect poor and needy people and plead their cause**.

## Verse 15 — Correct children wisely

Parents should consider both the good that comes from appropriate correction and the harm caused by excessive indulgence.

## Verse 16 — Do not be shaken by growing evil

Believers should not allow the multiplication of wickedness and wicked people to overthrow their faith or hope. **Wait patiently upon the Lord**.

## Verse 17 — Do not neglect necessary correction

When children act wrongly, parents must not simply leave them without correction.

## Verse 18 — The precious open vision of Christ

A place without **Bibles and faithful ministers** is spiritually exposed and vulnerable to the enemy of souls. The gospel is an **open vision** revealing Christ: it humbles sinners, exalts the Savior and promotes holiness in everyday conduct. These precious truths sustain spiritual life and preserve souls from destruction.

## Verse 19 — Serving only from fear

The servant described here is lazy, unprofitable and wicked. The service arises neither from love nor from conscience, but only from fear.

## Verse 20 — Rash self-conceit

A self-important person who speaks rashly and constantly quarrels can be less open to correction than even ignorant or openly immoral people.

## Verse 21 — Kindness is not indulgence

Good treatment of a servant does not mean indulgence that ultimately ruins the servant, just as it could ruin a child. **The body serves the soul**: pampering its every demand encourages it to forget that proper relationship.

## Verse 22 — The fruit of an angry spirit

A passionate, angry disposition provokes other people and also offends God.

## Verse 23 — Humility before honor

Only those who humble themselves will be exalted and firmly established.

## Verse 24 — Receiving stolen goods

Someone knowingly receiving stolen property shares in the thief's wrongdoing.

## Verse 25 — Do not be ashamed of Christ

Many people are ashamed to acknowledge Christ now. He will not acknowledge them at judgment. But **trust in the Lord** saves us from this snare.

## Verse 26 — Seek the supreme Ruler's favor

The wisest course is to seek God's favor, for he is **the Ruler of rulers**. Every creature becomes to us what God makes that creature to be.

## Verse 27 — Hate sin, not souls

The righteous hate the wicked person's sin and avoid corrupting company. Christ exposed human wickedness but **prayed for his persecutors while they crucified him**. A Christian must hate sin within oneself and others, while seeking sinners' salvation. Those who remain unholy, however, have a deep hostility to godliness.
