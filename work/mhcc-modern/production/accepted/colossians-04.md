# Colossians 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Masters to do their duty towards servants. | 1 |
| Persons of all ranks to persevere in prayer, and Christian prudence. | 2–6 |
| The apostle refers to others for an account of his affairs. | 7–9 |
| Sends greetings; and concludes with a blessing. | 10–18 |

## Masters must treat servants justly (verse 1)

Paul continued addressing the obligations of masters toward their servants.

They must provide not only **basic justice**, but **strict fairness and kindness**.

Masters should treat those under their authority in the same manner they hope **God will treat them**.

## Persevere in prayer and speak with gracious wisdom (verses 2–6)

No Christian duty can be performed rightly unless we continue in **earnest prayer** and remain alert **with thanksgiving**.

Believers should pray especially for **their ministers**.

They should also act rightly toward people who do not believe.

In every encounter with unbelievers, we ought to seek their good and commend the Christian faith **by every appropriate means**.

Use your time diligently.

Even when a Christian commits no obvious act of wrongdoing, simple carelessness may cause other people to develop **lasting prejudice against the gospel**.

Let all our conversation be **wise and suited to the occasion**.

The subject need not always be spiritual, but the conversation must always be conducted **with Christian grace**.

Even when discussing ordinary things, our manner should reflect the Lord we serve.

Grace is like **salt seasoning our speech**, keeping it from corruption.

It is not enough merely to answer another person's question.

We must answer **truthfully, wisely and in an appropriate way**.

## Fellow servants share Christ's privileges (verses 7–9)

Gospel ministers serve **Jesus Christ** and are also **fellow servants to one another**.

They share **one Lord**, though he assigns different responsibilities and abilities to each.

Knowing that other believers care about us brings tremendous comfort when we face life's troubles.

Earthly position or circumstances make no difference to the spiritual relationship between sincere Christians.

They share **the same gospel privileges** and should receive **the same respect and love**.

See the wonderful change divine grace produces!

**Previously unfaithful servants become faithful, beloved brothers**, and people who once acted wrongly become trustworthy partners in doing good.

## Paul commends faithful coworkers and ends with grace (verses 10–18)

Paul had once disagreed seriously with Barnabas over **Mark**.

But now he was not merely reconciled with Mark—he **recommended him to the churches**.

What an example of Christian forgiveness!

When someone has sincerely turned from a fault, we should not hold it against that person forever.

**We must forget as well as forgive**.

Paul drew strength and encouragement from **the fellowship of saints and gospel ministers**.

Some were fellow servants, others fellow prisoners, and all shared in working out their own salvation while seeking the salvation of other people.

**Earnest, effective prayer has great power**.

Yet some who are called to ministry fall short of their responsibilities.

Worldly praise or criticism, the influence of false teaching or selfish ambition can draw them into preaching and living in ways that fail to fulfill their calling.

But those who proclaim **the same gospel Paul preached** and follow his example may expect **God's favor and blessing**.
