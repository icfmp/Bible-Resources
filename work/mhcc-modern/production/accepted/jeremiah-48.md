# Jeremiah 48 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Prophecies against Moab for pride and security. | 1–13 |
| For carnal confidence and contempt of God. | 14–47 |

## Moab's proud security ends in ruin (verses 1–13)

The Chaldeans would destroy **Moab**.

We should be thankful that our calling is to seek **the salvation of people's lives and souls**, not to shed their blood.

But that privilege makes us all the more accountable if we perform such a good work **deceitfully or carelessly**.

Moab's cities would become ruins, and its countryside would be laid waste.

There would be great sorrow and confusion.

Even if sinners could be given wings, they still could not fly beyond the reach of God's righteous judgment.

Many people persist in wrongdoing without repenting, yet continue to enjoy outward prosperity for a long time.

The Moabites had remained corrupt and unchanged, secure and devoted to bodily pleasure.

They had not experienced disruptions to their prosperity, and therefore their hearts and lives remained unchanged (**Psalm 55:19**).

## Moab is warned, yet restoration is promised (verses 14–47)

The prophecy continues describing Moab's destruction.

These warnings were intended to awaken **national repentance and reform** that might prevent the disaster, or at least **personal repentance and reform** to prepare individuals for it.

As we read this long list of threats and the terrifying figures used to express them, it is more profitable to reflect on **the power of God's anger and the justice of his judgments** than to become preoccupied with explaining every image.

The destruction, however, would **not be permanent**.

The chapter ends by promising that Moab's captivity would be reversed **in the latter days**.

God would not contend even with the Moabites forever.

Henry reports that Jewish interpreters connected this promise with **the days of the Messiah**.

In that larger application, people from the nations who are held in bondage to **sin and Satan** are brought back by divine grace and become **truly free**.
