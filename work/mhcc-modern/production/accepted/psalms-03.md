# Psalm 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David complains to God of his enemies, and confides in God. | 1–3 |
| He triumphs over his fears, and gives God the glory, and takes to himself the comfort. | 4–8 |

## David trusts God while enemies surround him (verses 1–3)

The more an active believer is driven away from God by painful Providence or the accusations of enemies, the more firmly that person clings to him.

A child of God is alarmed even by the thought of **despairing of divine help**.

David knew that God provided his people with **three blessings**.

**First, safety**: the Lord was his shield, protecting him.

**Second, honor**: everyone whom God receives as his own possesses genuine dignity.

**Third, joy and deliverance**: even in the worst times, believers may lift their heads with gladness, knowing that everything will work for their good.

They recognize that God gives them both **reasons to rejoice and hearts capable of rejoicing**.

## Prayer, rest and victory through God (verses 4–8)

Distress and sorrow bring good when they lead us to pray earnestly.

David had repeatedly found God ready to answer.

Nothing can sever the connection between God's **favor toward us** and the faith he produces **within us**.

David had lived safely under God's protection.

Even the ordinary mercy of sleeping each night deserves our thanks every morning.

Many people cannot sleep because of pain, anxiety or frightening events.

But David's words especially describe his **inner calm amid danger**.

Through grace and the comforts of the Holy Spirit, the Lord gave him peace.

A mind resting firmly upon God is a great mercy in trouble.

Consider **the Son of David** lying down to rest on the cross, that bed of sorrows, and entrusting his Spirit to the Father's hands with confidence in a joyful resurrection.

Christian, let faith teach you **how to sleep and how to die**.

Sleep is a brief kind of death, and death is a longer sleep.

The same God watches over you on your bed and in your grave.

David's faith became triumphant.

He began by describing his enemies' strength and cruelty.

He finished by rejoicing in God's power and grace, recognizing that more were on his side than against him.

**Salvation belongs to the Lord**.

No danger exceeds his power to save.

Those who know the Lord as their God can be sure of salvation, for he is the God of salvation.
