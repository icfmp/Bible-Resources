# Psalm 20 — Modern English

## Original heading

**This psalm is a prayer for the kings of Israel, but with relation to Christ.**

## A prayer for the king in the light of Christ

Even the greatest human beings experience serious trouble.

Neither **the crown on a king's head** nor **grace within the heart** makes someone immune to suffering.

Even great people must therefore be people of prayer.

No one should expect to benefit from the prayers of the church or friends while neglecting personal prayer when capable of it.

The psalm asks God to **protect the king and preserve his life**.

It also asks that the king may continue working for the public good.

We may recognize that God accepts our spiritual sacrifices when his Spirit kindles **a holy fire of devotion and love** within our souls.

The psalm also asks the Lord to give success to the king's work.

In spiritual warfare, the first step toward victory is **trusting only God's mercy and grace**.

Those who depend upon themselves will fall.

Believers triumph in God and in the way he has revealed himself to them.

That distinguishes them from people who live as though God did not exist.

Those who praise God's name may also **trust God's name**.

This was shown when the power and pride of Jewish unbelief and pagan idolatry fell before the preaching and example of humble followers of Jesus.

It remains true in spiritual conflict today.

Believers engage their enemies **in Christ's name, Spirit and strength**.

It will also be seen at the last day, when the world and its evil prince are brought down.

Then believers will rise through the resurrection of the Lord, stand before him and sing his praise in heaven.

Let us rejoice in **the salvation Christ gives**.

Raise our banners in the name of the Lord our God.

Through the saving strength of his right hand, we may be confident of victory over every enemy.
