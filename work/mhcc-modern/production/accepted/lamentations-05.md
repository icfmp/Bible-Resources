# Lamentations 5 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The Jewish nation supplicating the Divine favour.**

## The people confess their sufferings and sins (verses 1–16)

Is anyone suffering?

**Let that person pray**, bringing the complaint directly to God.

That is what God's people do here.

They lament not merely dangers they imagine, but hardships they are already enduring.

When we experience suffering connected with the sins of our ancestors, we may hope in the Lord's returning mercy if we remain **repentant and patient**.

The people confess: **“Woe to us, for we have sinned!”**

Our sorrow is ultimately tied to our own sin and folly.

Although our sins and God's righteous displeasure cause suffering, we may hope for **his forgiving mercy, sanctifying grace and kind Providence**.

Yet the guilt accumulated through a lifetime of sin will finally be judged unless a person receives an interest in **the Savior who bore our sins in his own body on the tree**.

## The people pray for renewed favor (verses 17–22)

The devastation of **the temple** concerned God's people even more deeply than all their other troubles.

But despite every change on earth, **God remains the same**.

He is forever wise and holy, just and good.

With him there is **no variation or shadow of change**.

The people pray earnestly for mercy and grace:

**“Turn us to you, O Lord.”**

God never abandons people before they abandon him.

If his grace brings them back to himself in obedience, he will show them favor and mercy.

If he **renews our hearts**, he will also renew our days through his kindness.

Troubles may weaken our hearts and dim our eyes, but **the throne of mercy of our reconciled God remains open**.

In every trial, let us place our entire trust in his mercy, confess our sins and pour out our hearts before him.

Guard against resentment and despair.

All who trust, fear, love and serve the Lord will find that **all is well in the end**.

God's judgments still occur in the earth, as in Jeremiah's time.

Let us therefore remember Zion in our prayers and seek her welfare **above every earthly joy**.

**Spare your people, Lord**, and do not allow your heritage to become a reproach under the power of other nations.
