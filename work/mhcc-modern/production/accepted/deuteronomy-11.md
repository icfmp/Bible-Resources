# Deuteronomy 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| Careful study of God's word requisite. | 18–25 |

## Love for God expressed through obedience (verses 1–7)

Moses joined two duties that must always belong together: **love the Lord and keep his commands**.

Love leads to obedience.

Only obedience flowing from love is truly acceptable to God (1 John 5:3).

Moses recalled the great and fearful works of God that Israel had witnessed.

What we see with our own eyes, especially during our earliest years, should continue to influence us and improve our lives long afterward.

## Blessings and warnings for Israel (verses 8–17)

Moses placed life and death, blessings and curses, before the people.

Their future depended on how they responded to the Lord's commands.

Sin tends to shorten human lives and to shorten a nation's prosperity.

If the people loved and served God, the Lord promised them abundant good gifts.

Godliness includes promises for the present life.

But the favor of God brings more true joy to the heart than an increase in grain, wine or oil.

Turning from the Lord to idols would lead to Israel's ruin.

Take care not to let your heart deceive you.

Everyone who abandons God to set the heart on a created thing will eventually discover that it was a terrible mistake.

Such people have contributed to their own destruction.

Their guilt is greater because they neglected the warnings they had been given.

## Keeping God's word in heart, sight and speech (verses 18–25)

Henry identifies **three rules** for living under God's word.

**First, fill the heart with God's word.**

Good conduct cannot continue without good thoughts, desires and convictions within us.

**Second, keep the eyes fixed on God's word.**

Treat it as the guide for your path and the rule governing your work (Psalm 119:30).

**Third, employ the tongue in speaking about God's word.**

Thoughtful teaching and conversation help God's truth remain alive among the people.

Nothing contributes more to a nation's prosperity and the preservation of true faith than the faithful religious education of children.

## The blessing and curse announced (verses 26–32)

Moses summarized every reason to obey in two expressions: **the blessing** and **the curse**.

The people had to choose which they would receive.

He appointed a public and solemn proclamation of both, on Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal.

We have broken God's law and fallen under its curse.

We cannot remedy that condition through our own power.

But in mercy, the gospel places a blessing and a curse before us again.

There is blessing for those who answer its call to repent, believe in Christ and receive new hearts and transformed lives through him.

There is terrible judgment for those who neglect such great salvation.

Let us welcome this good news with thanksgiving and joy.

Do not harden your hearts.

Hear God's voice while it is still called **today**, while he invites us to approach his throne of mercy.

Be diligent to make your calling and election sure.
