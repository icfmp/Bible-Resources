# Acts 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The speech of Tertullus against Paul. | 1–9 |
| Paul's defence before Felix. | 10–21 |
| Felix trembles at the reasoning of Paul. | 22–27 |

## Tertullus accuses Paul (verses 1–9)

Here we see a danger faced by powerful people: **their accomplishments are praised beyond measure while their sins are never honestly exposed**.

Such flattery hardens people and encourages them to continue doing evil, as happened with Felix.

God's prophets were accused of disturbing the land, and the Lord Jesus himself was accused of misleading the nation.

The same accusations were now brought against Paul.

Selfish passions often drive people to persecute others.

Skills of speech and public persuasion can be used to mislead people and turn them against the truth.

How different Paul and Felix will appear on **the day of judgment** from the descriptions Tertullus offered!

Christians should neither seek the praise of irreligious people nor be troubled by their insults.

Worldly flatterers can make the most wicked people appear almost divine, while treating the best people on earth as dangerous rebels.

## Paul answers Felix (verses 10–21)

Paul gave a truthful explanation of his conduct.

It cleared him of criminal charges and exposed the real reason for the violence directed against him.

We must never abandon a good path simply because others give it **a bad name**.

When we worship God, it is a comfort to worship **the God of our fathers**, receiving no other rule for faith and conduct than the Scriptures.

Scripture also teaches that there will be a **resurrection followed by final judgment**.

The prophets and their teaching were to be tested by their fruit.

Paul labored to maintain **a conscience without offense toward both God and human beings**.

He diligently avoided sin and pursued godliness in every situation.

What if other people accuse us of loving God too much?

Do we shrink from that accusation?

Many people would rather be accused of almost any weakness—even serious wrongdoing—than of **earnest love for Jesus Christ** and devotion to his service.

Can such people really expect Christ to acknowledge them when he comes in glory before God's angels?

What would please the Savior more than seeing a faithful servant on earth gladly admit, if it is considered an offense, that he loves **the Lord who died for him** with all his heart, soul, mind and strength?

Such a believer will not silently watch God's word despised or hear his name profaned.

He will rather risk the world's ridicule and hatred than incur the disapproval of the gracious Lord whose love is better than life.

## Felix trembles but postpones repentance (verses 22–27)

Paul reasoned with Felix about **righteousness, self-control and the judgment to come**.

These were exactly the subjects the oppressive governor and the woman living with him needed to hear.

They needed repentance, forgiveness and the grace of the gospel.

**Righteousness** concerns our conduct in life, particularly toward other people.

**Self-control** concerns the ordering and government of our inner life before God.

Someone who neglects both has neither the true form nor the power of godliness and faces God's righteous anger at judgment.

The prospect of future judgment can make even the boldest person tremble.

**Felix trembled—but nothing more followed.**

Many hear God's word and become frightened without being changed.

They fear sin's consequences yet continue loving and practicing it.

**Delay is dangerous whenever eternal salvation is at stake**.

Felix deferred the conversation until a more convenient moment.

Scripture does not report that such a moment ever came.

**Now is the accepted time; today is the day of salvation**.

Felix eagerly ended the interview, but what business could be more urgent than reforming his life or saving his soul?

Many sinners start up like someone awakened by a sudden loud noise, then fall almost immediately back into sleep.

We must not be deceived by occasional outward displays of religious interest, whether in ourselves or others.

Above all, we must not treat God's word lightly.

Do we imagine that hearts naturally become softer with age, or that the world's influence inevitably weakens?

We are in danger now, and **tomorrow may be too late**.
