# Psalm 76 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist speaks of God's power. | 1–6 |
| All have to fear and to trust in him. | 7–12 |

## God is known among his people (verses 1–6)

Happy is a people whose land is filled with **the knowledge of God**.

Happy, too, are individuals whose hearts are filled with that knowledge.

It is the glory and happiness of a nation to have God present through **his appointed ordinances**.

The enemies of God's church may act with great pride. But the Lord will demonstrate that he is **greater than those opposing him**.

See how powerful even God's rebuke can be.

Christians may read these words joyfully in view of the benefits bestowed upon them through **the Redeemer**.

## Holy fear and confidence in God's justice (verses 7–12)

God's people are described as **the meek of the earth**—quiet and peaceable people who endure wrong but refuse to do wrong themselves.

The righteous Lord may seem silent for a long time. Eventually, however, he will make his judgment heard.

We live in an angry, provoking world.

Human rage often hurts us, and the anticipation of further harm may make us even more afraid.

But **God sets limits to human anger**.

Whatever will not ultimately serve his glory, he will prevent from coming to pass.

He restrains human wrath as he restrains the roaring sea: **it may come this far, but no farther**.

Everyone should therefore submit to him.

Our proper offerings are **prayers, praise and above all our hearts**.

God's name is glorious; he alone deserves our highest reverence.

The Lord can humble the proudest rulers with astonishing ease.

Henry compares the removal of a prince's courage to **picking a flower from its stem or a cluster of grapes from its vine**.

God can take away the boldness of the most defiant people.

Since no one can successfully contend against him, our wisdom and duty are to submit, seek **his favor as our portion**, and entrust all our concerns to his care.
