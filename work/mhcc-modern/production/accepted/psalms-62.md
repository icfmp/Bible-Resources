# Psalm 62 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David's confidence in God. | 1–7 |
| No trust to be put in worldly things. | 8–12 |

## David waits quietly on God (verses 1–7)

We find both duty and comfort when our souls wait upon the Lord: cheerfully placing ourselves and all our concerns under his will and wisdom, accepting the ways of his Providence and patiently trusting his goodness for the outcome.

David had good reasons for such confidence. God's **grace had sustained him**, and divine Providence had **delivered him**.

God alone could be his Rock and salvation. Created beings have no strength independently of him, so David looked beyond them to the Lord himself.

Trust in God establishes the heart. When God is for us, we need not fear what other human beings may do.

Remembering former occasions when waiting upon the Lord proved good, we should continually encourage our souls to depend upon him. This produces greater inner quietness.

If God saves my soul, I may safely leave everything else to his judgment, knowing that he will direct it toward my salvation.

As David's faith became steadier, his joy rose toward holy triumph. **Meditation and prayer** are blessed means of strengthening both faith and hope.

## Trust God, not people or wealth (verses 8–12)

Those who have discovered comfort in God's ways naturally invite others to join them. We lose **nothing** when others share these blessings.

Trust him at all times—not reserving for ourselves or any created thing the confidence that belongs to him alone.

Look to him for **guidance in doubt, protection in danger, provision in need and strength for every good word and work**.

Pouring out the heart means placing our needs and desires before God, then patiently submitting those desires to his will.

God is a refuge for all who come to take shelter in him.

Do not make people the foundation of your hope. An ordinary crowd is as changeable as the wind; great and wealthy people may promise much, but those depending upon their promises are often disappointed.

Weighed in Scripture's balance, all human ability to make us happy is **lighter than vanity**.

Even wealth gained honestly can become dangerous when our affections rest upon it. A pleasant and prosperous world can turn our hearts away from God.

A consistent believer receives possessions as a **trust from God**, managing them for his glory like a steward who will give an account.

God has declared that **power belongs to him**. He can judge and destroy. **Mercy also belongs to him**.

The fact that he rewards his people's imperfect service and forgives their transgressions **for the Redeemer's sake** displays his abundant mercy.

Let us therefore abound in his work and expect every blessing from his grace alone.
