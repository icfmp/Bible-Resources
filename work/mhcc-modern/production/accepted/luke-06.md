# Luke 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The disciples pluck corn on the sabbath. | 1–5 |
| Works of mercy suitable to the sabbath day. | 6–11 |
| The apostles chosen. | 12–19 |
| Blessings and woes declared. | 20–26 |
| Christ exhorts to mercy. | 27–36 |
| And to justice and sincerity. | 37–49 |

## The disciples gather grain on the Sabbath (verses 1–5)

Jesus defended his disciples because they had gathered ears of grain to satisfy **a genuine need for food** on the Sabbath.

Yet we must not turn that freedom for necessary work into an excuse for committing sin.

Christ calls us to remember that the day belongs to him and should be spent in his honor and service.

## Jesus heals on the Sabbath (verses 6–11)

Christ was neither ashamed nor afraid to display the purpose of his grace.

He healed the man even though he knew that his enemies would use the act as an accusation against him.

We should not allow opposition to draw us away from duty or discourage us from doing good.

The wickedness of the human heart can be astonishing.

## Jesus chooses the twelve apostles (verses 12–19)

We often think thirty minutes a long time to spend in meditation and private prayer.

Yet Jesus spent **whole nights** in prayer.

When serving God, our concern should be not to waste time, but to allow one good duty to lead into the next.

The twelve apostles are named here.

Few people have been granted greater privileges.

Yet **one of them was possessed by the devil and became a betrayer**.

People who live far from faithful gospel preaching should be willing to travel for it rather than go without.

It is worthwhile to travel a considerable distance to hear Christ's word and even to interrupt other business for that purpose.

The people also came to be healed, and Jesus healed them.

Christ has **more than enough grace and healing power for all, and enough for each individual**.

People usually regard physical disease as a worse evil than spiritual corruption.

Scripture teaches us to take a different view.

## Jesus announces blessings and woes (verses 20–26)

Much of this teaching also appears in **Matthew 5–7**.

Henry notes that some interpreters think Jesus preached these words at a different time and place.

Everyone who takes the commands of the gospel to heart and lives according to them may also take its **promises** as their own and live by them.

Jesus announced woes against prosperous sinners who are actually miserable, however much the world envies them.

Those whom Christ blesses are **truly blessed**.

Those against whom he pronounces judgment are in dreadful danger.

The difference between the eternal outcomes of believer and unbeliever is immeasurably greater than any difference between prosperity and suffering here on earth.

The righteous may suffer greatly now, while unrepentant sinners flourish.

Their final rewards will reveal an entirely different judgment.

## Jesus teaches mercy toward enemies (verses 27–36)

These commands are difficult for fallen human nature.

Yet when we are deeply rooted in **faith in Christ's love**, his commands become easier to accept.

Anyone who comes to Jesus to be washed in his blood and understands his great mercy may sincerely ask:

**Lord, what do you want me to do?**

Let us seek to be merciful as **our heavenly Father is merciful to us**.

## Jesus teaches justice and sincerity (verses 37–49)

Christ used these teachings often because they could readily be applied to daily life.

We should be very cautious when condemning other people, because we ourselves need mercy and understanding.

Those who are generous and forgiving also experience the benefit of that spirit.

The final and complete reward for our actions lies in the world to come.

Nevertheless, even in this life divine Providence gives encouragement to those who practice kindness.

People who follow crowds into wrongdoing take the broad road toward destruction.

**A tree is known by its fruit.**

May Christ's word be planted deeply in our hearts so that our words and actions bear good fruit.

Ordinary speech usually reveals what occupies the heart.

Only those who **think, speak and act according to Christ's words** are preparing wisely for the trials of life and eternity.

They build their hope on **Jesus Christ, the Rock of Ages**.

No other saving foundation can be laid.

At death and at judgment such people are secure, because through faith they are kept by **Christ's power for salvation** and will never perish.
