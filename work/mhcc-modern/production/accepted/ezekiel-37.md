# Ezekiel 37 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God restores dried bones to life. | 1–14 |
| The whole house of Israel is represented as enjoying the blessings of Christ's kingdom. | 15–28 |

## God raises dry bones to life (verses 1–14)

No created power can restore dead human bones to life. **God alone** can make them live.

In the vision, skin and flesh covered the bones. Then the wind was commanded to blow upon the bodies, and they came to life.

The wind pictured **God's Spirit and his power to give life**.

This vision encouraged the discouraged Jews. It foretold both their **restoration after the captivity** and their **recovery from the long dispersion that continued in Henry's day**.

It also clearly pointed toward **the resurrection of the dead**.

The same vision represents God's powerful grace in **converting even the most hopeless sinners**.

Let us turn to the Lord who will one day open our graves and bring us out to judgment. May he deliver us from sin now, place his Spirit within us and keep us through faith by his power until salvation.

## Judah and Israel united under Christ (verses 15–28)

The symbolic action showed that the Lord would **unite Judah and Israel**.

**Christ is the true David**, the King of Israel.

Those whom Christ makes willing through his power he also leads to obey his judgments and keep his commandments.

Henry says **future events will explain this prophecy further**.

Few things have hindered the spread of the gospel more than **divisions among believers**.

Let us therefore maintain **the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace**. Let us seek divine grace to avoid detestable things.

And let us pray that **all nations** become obedient and happy subjects of **the Son of David**, so that the Lord will be our God and we will be his people forever.
