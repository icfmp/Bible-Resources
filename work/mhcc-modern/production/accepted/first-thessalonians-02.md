# 1 Thessalonians 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle reminds the Thessalonians of his preaching and behaviour. | 1–12 |
| And of their receiving the gospel as the word of God. | 13–16 |
| His joy on their account. | 17–20 |

## Paul preached honestly, not for personal gain (verses 1–6)

Paul's ministry was not motivated by **worldly ambition or self-interest**.

Suffering for a good cause should strengthen our holy determination, not destroy it.

The gospel met powerful opposition from the beginning.

Its messengers struggled earnestly to proclaim it in the face of hostile resistance.

Paul's message was true and pure, and **his manner of speaking was without deceit**.

Christ's gospel aims to put sinful desires to death and bring people under **the living power of faith**.

Our strongest reason for sincere conduct is to remember that **God sees everything we do and knows even the most distant thoughts of our hearts**.

He searches our motives, and **from him alone** comes the reward we should seek.

Paul demonstrated sincerity by refusing **flattery**, **greed**, ambitious self-promotion and empty pursuit of praise.

## Gentle care and a worthy life (verses 7–12)

**Gentleness and tenderness** beautifully commend Christian faith.

They most closely resemble God's gracious way of dealing with sinners through the gospel.

They are also among the best means of winning people.

We should be faithful not only in our general Christian calling, but also in the **particular roles and relationships** God assigns to us.

Our great gospel privilege is that God has called us **to his kingdom and glory**.

Our corresponding duty is to **walk in a manner worthy of God**.

Our chief work in life should be to honor, serve and please him.

## The word of God bears fruit amid persecution (verses 13–16)

God's word should be received with affections suited to **its holiness, wisdom, truth and goodness**.

Human words are as fragile as the people who speak them.

They can be false, foolish and unreliable.

But **God's word is holy, wise, just and faithful**.

We should treat it accordingly.

That word worked powerfully within the Thessalonians, making them an example to others through **faith, good works and patient endurance of persecution**.

Murder and persecution are hateful to God.

**No kind of religious zeal can ever excuse them**.

Few actions more seriously fill up people's measure of sin than **opposing the gospel or blocking others from hearing the way of salvation**.

Many despise Christ's pure gospel, and its faithful proclamation has been obstructed in many ways.

But those who prevent spiritually dead sinners from hearing the message **cannot claim to please God**.

People who deny others access to **the Bible** have cruel hearts.

They oppose both **God's glory and the salvation of their neighbors**.

## Paul's longing for reunion and Christ's coming (verses 17–20)

Our time together in this world is **brief and uncertain**.

In heaven, holy souls will meet again and **never be separated**.

Paul wanted to return to his friends.

He could not come at that moment and feared he might never be able to see them again during earthly life.

Yet **Jesus Christ himself will return**, and nothing can prevent that coming.

May God provide faithful ministers to those who serve him in the gospel of his Son.

May he send such servants into places still darkened by unbelief.
