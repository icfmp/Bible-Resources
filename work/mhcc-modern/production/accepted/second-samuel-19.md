# 2 Samuel 19 — Modern English

## Joab challenges David's grief (verses 1–8)

It was very unwise and unworthy for David to continue mourning so intensely for such a wicked son as Absalom.

Joab criticized him.

But Joab did not speak to his king with the proper respect and deference.

A clear case can be presented fairly to those in authority.

They can even be corrected for their wrongdoing.

But correction must not be delivered with rudeness or insolence.

David accepted the reproof and the advice with prudence and gentleness.

Yielding promptly when we have taken a mistaken course usually prevents much of its harmful effect.

## David is invited back to Jerusalem (verses 9–15)

Through the appeals of the priests and the influence of **Amasa**, God brought the people to invite David back as king.

David did not act until that invitation came.

Likewise, the Lord Jesus rules in the hearts of those who invite him to take his throne there.

Before they invite him, he first moves their hearts and makes them willing on the day of his power.

Then he rules in the midst of his enemies (**Psalm 110:2–3**).

## David forgives Shimei (verses 16–23)

People who now insult and reject the **Son of David** may wish desperately to be reconciled to him when he comes in glory.

But then it will be too late.

Shimei did not delay in seeking David's pardon.

Because the insults had been directed against him personally, David found them easier to forgive.

This is the fitting response of godly people to personal wrongdoing.

## Mephibosheth is vindicated (verses 24–30)

David reversed the forfeiture of Mephibosheth's property.

Mephibosheth rejoiced at David's return.

A good person can endure personal losses contentedly while the people of Israel enjoy peace and **the Son of David is exalted**.

## David parts with Barzillai (verses 31–39)

Barzillai considered it an honor merely to have been able to help David.

In the same way, when Christ's people enter their inheritance, they will be amazed that their reward is so much greater than the little service they offered (**Matthew 25:37**).

A godly person does not wish to become a burden to other people.

If such a person must depend on someone, he would rather be a burden to his own household than to strangers.

Everyone should think seriously about dying.

That is especially appropriate for those who are growing old.

The grave is ready for me.

I should prepare to enter it.

## Israel and Judah quarrel (verses 40–43)

The people of Israel believed they had been slighted.

The people of Judah responded with increasingly angry words.

The quarrel led to very harmful consequences.

Much evil could be prevented if we watched against pride and remembered that **a gentle answer turns away anger**.

Even when we have justice and reason on our side, God is displeased if we defend our position fiercely and without kindness.
