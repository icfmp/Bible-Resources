# Luke 22 — Modern English

## Judas plans his betrayal (verses 1–6)

Jesus knew every person and had **wise and holy purposes** in choosing Judas as a disciple.

Luke tells us how a man who knew Christ so well could betray him: **Satan entered Judas**.

It is difficult to say whether God's kingdom suffers more harm through open enemies or through people who claim friendship while secretly betraying it.

Without the treachery of false friends, its public enemies often could not accomplish as much damage.

## Jesus observes the Passover (verses 7–18)

Jesus obeyed the ordinances of God's law, including Passover.

This teaches us to respect the ordinances he established for the gospel, especially **the Lord's Supper**.

Those who act on Christ's word need not fear that his instructions will fail them.

The disciples made the preparations exactly as he directed.

Jesus welcomed his final Passover.

He eagerly desired it, even knowing that suffering would soon follow, because it served **his Father's glory and humanity's redemption**.

With it he took leave of the old Passover ceremonies.

By fulfilling them, he brought the ceremonial law to its appointed conclusion.

The earlier Passover had pointed forward as a **type**.

Now the **substance** had come through Christ.

## Jesus institutes the Lord's Supper (verses 19 and 20)

The Lord's Supper is a **sign and memorial** of Christ, who has already come and rescued us through his death.

It sets his sacrifice before us.

**Breaking the bread** reminds us that Jesus' body was given for us.

Nothing is more nourishing or satisfying to the soul than knowing that **Christ has made atonement for sin** and that we may share its benefits.

We keep the Supper in **remembrance of what Jesus did for us** when he died, and as a testimony that we are joined to him through an **everlasting covenant**.

The **cup of wine** represents his blood, shed to make atonement.

## Jesus warns the disciples (verses 21–38)

How inappropriate that followers of Jesus should compete over worldly greatness, when their Master **became a servant** and humbled himself even to death on the cross!

On the way to eternal happiness, believers should expect Satan to sift and test them.

If he cannot destroy them, he will try to disgrace or distress them.

Few things warn more clearly of a coming fall than **self-confidence**, ignoring warnings and treating danger with contempt.

Unless we continually watch and pray, we may commit by evening the very sins we were determined to resist in the morning.

If left to ourselves, we would fall.

Believers remain secure through **God's power and Christ's intercession**.

Jesus also warned of a coming change in circumstances.

The disciples could no longer expect the same welcome or help from others.

They were to take provisions for their work.

Their enemies would become more hostile.

At first, the apostles understood his words about swords as instructions to bring **literal weapons**.

Henry says Jesus actually spoke of **the weapons of spiritual warfare**, especially **the sword of the Spirit**.

## Christ prays in agony (verses 39–46)

The Gospel accounts reveal the fearful nature of Jesus' inward struggle.

Our gentle, humble Lord **fully knew the suffering that lay ahead**.

Henry notes **three details** given in Luke's account.

**First**, an angel came from heaven to strengthen Jesus.

Being strengthened by a ministering spirit belonged to his willing humiliation.

**Second**, as his agony increased, **he prayed more earnestly**.

Prayer is appropriate at all times, but especially in terrible distress.

**Third**, his sweat became **like great drops of blood falling to the ground**.

This showed the weight of the suffering borne by his soul.

We should pray for strength to resist sin even to the shedding of our own blood, should faithfulness ever demand it.

Whenever we imagine the pleasures of a favorite sin, remember its terrible consequences as displayed in Gethsemane.

May the Savior's agony teach us to **hate and abandon sin**.

He prayed, struggled and shed his blood to ransom sinners from its power and judgment.

## Jesus is betrayed and seized (verses 47–53)

Nothing is more offensive or painful than betrayal by someone professing to follow and love Jesus.

People sometimes use the outward form of religion to resist its inward power.

Jesus demonstrated his own teaching by **doing good to an enemy**, as he would later pray for those who abused him.

Our fallen nature tends toward rash extremes.

In difficult situations, ask God for guidance before acting.

Christ accepted that his final victory would come only **after his suffering was finished**.

We must also be willing to wait.

The hour and power of darkness would be brief, like the temporary triumph of every wicked purpose.

## Peter denies Jesus (verses 54–62)

Peter denied knowing Christ or belonging to his disciples because he feared harm.

One lie creates a strong temptation to continue lying.

Once begun, it can spread like water breaking through a bank.

**The Lord turned and looked at Peter**.

Henry identifies **six meanings** in that look.

It was **convicting**, reminding Peter whom he had denied.

It was **rebuking**, confronting his wrongdoing.

It was **challenging**, recalling his earlier confident promise to remain loyal.

It was **compassionate**, recognizing how lost Peter would be without Christ's help.

It was **directing**, calling Peter to examine himself.

And it was **effective through grace**, bringing Christ's word back to Peter's heart and enabling him to repent.

God's grace works **through the word brought to remembrance**, pressing it upon the conscience.

Jesus looked at the religious leaders too, but they were not transformed as Peter was.

Peter's restoration did not come from a look alone, but from **divine grace accompanying it**.

## Jesus acknowledges that he is the Son of God (verses 63–71)

Those who condemned Jesus for blasphemy were themselves guilty of blaspheming God.

Jesus pointed toward his **future coming** as full proof of his identity, since they refused the evidence already available.

He acknowledged that he was **the Son of God**, even knowing that the confession would lead to further suffering.

They used his truthful words as the basis for his condemnation.

Their own spiritual blindness drove them forward.

Let us consider with wonder the One who endured such hostility from sinners.
