# Leviticus 16 — Modern English

## The yearly Day of Atonement (verses 1–14)

Without detailing every ceremony of the great Day of Atonement, we should remember that its observance was to remain binding while that former religious order continued.

As long as we continue sinning, we continue to need atonement.

The duty of humbling and grieving over our sins remains until we reach the place where every tear—even tears of repentance—will be wiped away.

The apostle notes that the sacrifices themselves could never take sin away or cleanse the conscience completely.

Their repetition every year brought the memory of sin back again (Hebrews 10:1, 3).

That repeated offering revealed the weakness of the old sacrifices to achieve final atonement.

Only the offering of Christ's body **once for all** could do that.

His sacrifice does not need to be repeated.

## The sacrifices and the scapegoat (verses 15–34)

The ceremonies pointed toward two great privileges of the gospel: **forgiveness of sin** and **access to God**.

Both come through our Lord Jesus Christ.

Look first at the removal of guilt.

Christ is both the **Priest who makes atonement** and the **Sacrifice by which atonement is made**.

He is everything necessary for our reconciliation to God.

The two goats pictured these aspects of his saving work.

The goat killed in sacrifice pointed to Christ's death for our sins.

The goat sent away pictured Christ rising again for our justification.

The priest symbolically placed Israel's sins on the head of the living goat and sent it away into an uninhabited wilderness.

Its departure represented the complete and free removal of the people's sins.

It bore away all their iniquities.

Likewise, Christ the Lamb of God takes away the world's sin by bearing it himself (John 1:29).

Look also at access to God.

The high priest's entry into the Most Holy Place pointed to Christ opening the way into heaven for us (Hebrews 9:7).

Israel's high priest came out again.

Our Lord Jesus lives forever, interceding continually and appearing in God's presence for us.

Finally, the ceremonies illustrated two great duties of the gospel: **faith** and **repentance**.

By faith, we place our hands upon the head of our offering, relying on Christ as the Lord our Righteousness and pleading his sacrifice as the only ground of pardon.

By repentance, we humble ourselves over sin.

That includes more than temporary fasting from bodily pleasures. It means sincere inward sorrow and a life of self-denial.

God is faithful and righteous to forgive people who confess their sins and to cleanse them from all unrighteousness.

Through Christ's atonement we receive rest for our souls and all the glorious freedom belonging to God's children.

If Christ's blood has not been truly applied to our souls, we cannot approach God with comfort or assurance of acceptance.

Receive that blood by faith and discover how completely it reconciles us to him.
