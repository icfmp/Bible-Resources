# Isaiah 49 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The unbelief and rejection of the Jews. | 1–6 |
| Gracious promise to the Gentiles. | 7–12 |
| God's love to the church. | 13–17 |
| Its increase. | 18–23 |
| And deliverance. | 24–26 |

## The Redeemer's mission reaches the nations (verses 1–6)

The great Author of redemption declares by what authority he undertakes his work.

The sword of **his word** destroys the sinful desires of his people and what is hostile to them.

His sharp arrows wound the sinner's **conscience**.

Yet the wounds will be healed when that sinner turns to him and asks for mercy.

Even the Redeemer, who spoke as no human being ever spoke, often seemed during his earthly ministry to **labor without success**.

But if Jacob will not return to God and Israel will not be gathered, **Christ will still be glorious**.

The promise finds part of its fulfillment in the calling of **the Gentiles**.

People perish in darkness, but Christ enlightens them.

Through that light he makes them **holy and happy**.

## A covenant Savior brings pardon and liberty (verses 7–12)

The Father is the Lord, Israel's Holy One and Redeemer, who sent the Son to accomplish redemption.

Human beings, whom Christ came to save, treated him with contempt.

He willingly accepted that contempt for their salvation.

Christ is the pledge of **all the blessings of the covenant**.

In him, God was reconciling the world to himself.

**Pardoning mercy** releases people from **the curse of the law**.

**Renewing grace** releases them from **the rule of sin**.

Both blessings come through Christ.

He calls those living in darkness to come out and show themselves.

His people should not only **see**, but also **be seen**, bringing glory to God and receiving the comfort that comes from acknowledging him.

The path to heaven contains many difficulties.

Yet God's grace will carry believers over them and make even the mountains into a road.

These words express the gospel's **free invitations, encouraging promises and outpouring of the Spirit**.

## God remembers his church with unfailing love (verses 13–17)

Let all creation rejoice: God will show mercy to the afflicted.

His **compassion** moves him toward suffering people, and his **covenant** secures mercy for those belonging to him.

We have no more reason to question his promises and grace than to doubt his Providence or justice.

God has a tender and lasting affection for his church.

He does not want his people to despair.

Even mothers sometimes neglect their children.

But God's compassion far exceeds even the tenderest human parent's love.

His description of his people as **engraved upon his hands** or sealed upon his arm means that he **never forgets them**.

As far as Scripture gives us evidence that we belong to his redeemed flock, we can be assured he will not forsake us.

Therefore let us diligently make our **calling and election sure**, and rejoice in the hope of God's glory.

## Zion is gathered and enlarged (verses 18–23)

Zion is pictured as a sorrowing widow who has lost her children.

But multitudes now gather to her.

God assures her that they come to strengthen and comfort her.

Sometimes the church is deserted and small in number.

But those desolate conditions will not last forever.

God can restore what has been ruined.

He can raise up friends for returning Israelites **among the Gentiles**, and those friends will bring their children to join God's people.

Let everyone care tenderly for **new converts and beginners in faith**.

Rulers will help protect the church.

It will become clear that God is sovereign over all.

Those who wait for the fulfillment of his promises **in faith, hope and patience** will never finally be put to shame.

## The captives are freed from a stronger enemy (verses 24–26)

Because of sin, we had become **lawful captives under God's justice**.

Yet we were delivered at **a price of incomparable value**.

God promises that even captives held by a terrible enemy will be rescued.

Henry applies this to **Satan losing his prisoners**, being bound and cast into the pit.

Every power seeking to enslave, persecute or corrupt the church will ultimately be destroyed.

Then the whole earth will know that **our Savior and Redeemer is Jehovah, the Mighty One of Jacob**.

Every effort we make to rescue fellow sinners from Satan's bondage contributes, in some measure, toward that great deliverance.
