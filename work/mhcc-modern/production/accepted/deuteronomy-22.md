## Neighborly kindness (verses 1–4)

If we consistently followed the rule of treating others as we would want them to treat us, many more detailed commandments would become unnecessary.

Finding an object that belongs to someone else never makes it our own property.

True religion teaches us to be good neighbors.

We should willingly do good and offer practical assistance to everyone.

We cannot know how soon we may need someone else's help.

## Instructions for everyday conduct (verses 5–12)

God's providence extends to even the smallest details of life, and his commands teach us to honor him in those details.

We live under his care and watchful eye.

Some instructions may seem minor.

But because they belong to God's law, they concern matters of real importance.

If we want to show that we are his people, we must value his will and glory above the meaningless fashions of the world.

Even when choosing clothing, just as when eating or drinking, we should take care to preserve the purity of our own hearts and actions and avoid harming others.

Our purpose should be clear, our hearts sincere, and our conduct consistent in every part of life.

## Laws concerning sexual purity (verses 13–30)

These and related regulations may have been necessary for Israel's historical circumstances.

But Henry says there is no need for Christians to investigate every difficult detail with undue curiosity.

Their continuing moral meaning is connected with the **seventh commandment**.

They restrain bodily lusts, which fight against the soul.
