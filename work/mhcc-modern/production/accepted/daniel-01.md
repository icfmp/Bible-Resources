# Daniel 1 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Daniel and his companions are taken to Babylon (verses 1–7)

In the first year of his reign, **Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon**, captured Jerusalem and carried away the people and possessions he chose.

Henry notes that most interpreters date **the seventy years of captivity** from this first deportation.

It is in a ruler's interest to employ **wise people**.

A wise ruler will identify and educate people with such abilities. Nebuchadnezzar therefore ordered the chosen young men to receive instruction.

The Hebrew names of these youths each contained a reference to **God**.

Their pagan captors gave them new names associated with **idolatry**, intending to make them forget **the God of their ancestors**, who had guided them since their youth.

It is painful to consider how frequently public education corrupts **people's principles and moral conduct**.

## The young men refuse the king's food (verses 8–16)

Whatever influence or favor we gain with others must be recognized as **God's gift**.

Daniel remained steadfast in his religion.

Whatever name others gave him, he retained **the spirit of an Israelite**.

The young men were concerned that eating the king's food might involve sin.

When God's people live in **Babylon**, they must take particular care not to share in its sins.

Young people deserve praise when they refuse to chase **sensual pleasures**.

Anyone hoping to grow in wisdom and godliness should learn early to practice **self-control**.

Daniel was determined not to defile himself by sin.

We should fear sin far more than **any outward suffering**.

It is easier to keep temptation **at a distance** than to resist it once it is near.

When other people favor us, we can make no better use of that favor than to ask their help in **keeping us from sin**.

People often fail to believe that avoiding excess and eating moderately can benefit bodily health until they try.

**Conscientious self-restraint** will bring more comfort even in the present life than sinful indulgence.

## The young men grow in wisdom (verses 17–21)

Daniel and his companions remained true to their religion.

God rewarded them with **outstanding knowledge and learning**.

Godly young people should try to become **more capable in useful work** than their peers.

Their aim should not be human applause but **the honor of the gospel** and preparation to serve others effectively.

A country benefits—and its ruler receives honor—when those in authority can identify **the people best suited for particular responsibilities** and promote them accordingly.

Young people should seriously consider this chapter.

Everyone should remember that **God honors those who honor him**, while people who despise him will be treated lightly.
