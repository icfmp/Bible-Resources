# Zechariah 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Fountain for the remission of sins, The conviction of the false prophets. | 1–6 |
| The death of Christ, and the saving of a remnant of the people. | 7–9 |

## The fountain opened for sin (verses 1–6)

A fountain would be opened to the rulers and people of the Jews for cleansing from sin.

Henry identifies it as **Christ's atoning blood united with his sanctifying grace**.

He understands the fountain as having been closed to unbelieving Israel but expects it to be opened to the people when **the Spirit of grace** humbles and softens their hearts.

**The opened fountain is the pierced side of Christ.**

We are all unclean because of sin.

Yet a fountain is open to wash us, and its streams of mercy continually flow.

Christ's blood, and the pardoning mercy of God made known through **the new covenant**, will never be exhausted.

This fountain is available to every believer.

Those who believe in Christ are his **spiritual offspring**, part of the family of David and members of the heavenly Jerusalem.

Through his grace, Christ removes **the dominion of sin**, even the sins to which the heart clings most strongly.

People cleansed through this fountain are **both justified and sanctified**.

They turn away from love of the world and from the desires of sinful flesh, the two great idols, to cling to God alone.

Henry sees a complete reformation following his anticipated **conversion of Israel to Christ**.

False prophets would recognize their guilt and folly and return to proper work.

When we realize we have strayed from duty, genuine repentance brings us back to that duty.

We should accept correction from friends who have helped expose our errors, even if that correction wounds our pride (**Proverbs 27:6**).

Above all, remember the wounds of **our Savior**.

He has often been wounded by professing friends and even genuine disciples when their conduct contradicts his word.

## The Shepherd is struck and a remnant is saved (verses 7–9)

Henry understands this passage as a prophecy of **the suffering and death of Christ**.

God the Father commanded the sword of his justice to rise against his Son when the Son willingly offered his soul for sin.

Because Christ is truly God, the Father calls him **“my Fellow.”**

**The Son and the Father are one.**

He is the Shepherd who lays down his life for the sheep.

As a sacrifice, he had to be slain; **without bloodshed there is no remission of sins**.

He had no sin of his own for which to answer.

The prophecy may encompass all his suffering, especially his agony **in the garden and upon the cross**, where he endured deep anguish until divine justice was satisfied.

When the Shepherd was struck, the sheep were scattered.

Jesus himself connected these words to the disciples' flight **on the night of his betrayal**.

Henry sees further application in the destruction of corrupt and hypocritical elements within the **professing church**.

He also identifies **Roman judgment upon many Jews** following the rejection and crucifixion of Jesus and opposition to the gospel.

That is **Henry's historical and theological interpretation**, not a newly established fact about Jewish people.

Yet **a remnant was to be saved**.

God would refine his people like gold.

He would be their God, and the ultimate result of their trials would be praise, honor and glory at the appearing of **Jesus Christ**.
