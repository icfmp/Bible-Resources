# Psalm 94 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The danger and folly of persecutors. | 1–11 |
| Comfort and peace to the persecuted. | 12–23 |

## Entrusting injustice to the righteous Judge (verses 1–11)

We may appeal to God with confidence because he is the almighty Judge before whom everyone must answer.

Those treated unjustly can be encouraged to endure the wrong without seeking private retaliation, **entrusting their cause to the One who judges righteously**.

Henry understands these prayers as **prophetic warnings** to violent people.

A day of reckoning will come for all the arrogant and cruel words ungodly people have spoken against God, his truth, his ways and his servants.

It is astonishing how many rational people live, think, speak, hear and act freely while behaving as though the Lord will never call them to account for **misusing his gifts**.

Every form of knowledge comes from him, so he certainly knows **every human thought**.

The inclinations of fallen hearts are continually evil.

Even thoughts that are good in themselves may be wandering and unsettled; this too can be called vanity.

We must therefore **watch our thoughts carefully**.

God notices them all.

**Our thoughts are words before him.**

## The comfort of divine correction and care (verses 12–23)

Blessed is the person whom God disciplines and teaches his truth through **Scripture and the Holy Spirit**.

Such a person can recognize mercy even within suffering.

**Rest remains for God's people** after their days of trouble, which will not continue forever.

The same Lord who sends affliction will also send relief.

When the psalmist's earthly friends failed him, he found help only in God.

We depend not only upon God's **power**, but also upon **his compassion**, to keep us spiritually secure.

If he preserves us from falling into sin or abandoning duty, all glory belongs to him.

We should share that encouragement with others.

The psalmist had many anxious thoughts about his situation, what he should do and how things might end.

Indulging such anxious speculation and fear adds to our cares and weakens trust, making circumstances appear darker and more confused.

Even faithful believers can be troubled by distressing thoughts about God.

They should return to **the great and precious promises of the gospel**.

Worldly comforts offer little relief to a soul overwhelmed by melancholy.

But God's consolation gives a peace the world cannot provide or remove.

**God is his people's refuge**, where they may safely shelter.

He will also judge wickedness.

No one can be made more miserable than their own wrongdoing will make them when the Lord finally calls it to account.
