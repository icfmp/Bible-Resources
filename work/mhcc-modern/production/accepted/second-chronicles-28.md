# 2 Chronicles 28 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The wicked reign of Ahaz in Judah.**

## Ahaz's wicked reign

Israel defeated Judah because the Lord was angry with Judah and used the northern kingdom as an instrument of his judgment.

Yet he also reminded Israel of its **own sins**.

Sinners have no right to act cruelly toward others.

How could they expect God's mercy if they refused mercy and justice to their brothers?

Remember that **every human being is our neighbor and fellow human being**, even when not a fellow Christian.

Henry makes an explicit statement here: anyone who knows God's word should be able to maintain confidently that **slavery is contrary to the law of love and the gospel of grace**.

How can someone hold a brother in bondage without breaking the rule to treat others as one would wish to be treated?

When sinners are abandoned to their own sinful desires, however, they grow more desperate in evil.

God commanded the Israelites to release the captives, and they obeyed.

The Lord brought Judah low.

People who refuse to humble themselves under God's word will rightly be humbled by his judgments.

Wicked people often have no real love for those who abandon true faith to join them, nor any desire to help them.

**Ahaz** became worse, not better, because of his suffering.

In distress he continued sinning more and set his heart even more firmly on wrongdoing.

When people misunderstand both the source of their troubles and the source of help, they turn their loyalties toward the wrong objects.

Wickedness and misery may grow rapidly together.

It is dreadful to consider a sinner dying and entering eternity while still hardened in sin.
