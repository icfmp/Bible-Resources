# Deuteronomy 8 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Remembering God's care in the wilderness (verses 1–9)

Obedience should have **three qualities**.

First, it must be **careful**: we should pay attention and actually carry out God's commands.

Second, it must be **complete**: we should obey all his commands, not merely the ones we find convenient.

Third, it must arise from a **right motive**: we should regard God as our Lord and worship him with holy reverence.

Moses asked Israel to look back over the journey.

We should also remember the ways God's providence and grace have led us through life's wilderness.

Such remembrance encourages us to serve him cheerfully and trust him.

Israel's hardships humbled its pride and exposed its stubbornness.

They showed the people—and others—what was truly in their hearts.

They also showed that God had not chosen them because they possessed any merit that earned his favor.

The Lord provided food and clothing miraculously.

His children therefore need not distrust their Father or resort to sin to obtain necessities.

Through honest diligence and obedience, they may rely on him to provide.

The lesson also has a spiritual meaning.

God's word nourishes the soul.

Jesus Christ is the Word of God, and we live through him.

Israel had been corrected severely, and not without cause.

The proper use of suffering is to turn us toward faithful duty.

Moses also directed the people to look forward to Canaan.

Looking **backward** at God's care and **forward** toward his promise both encourage obedience.

Canaan pointed to a better land.

The gospel church is the New Testament Canaan, watered by the Spirit's gifts and graces, filled with trees of righteousness and bearing the fruits of righteousness.

Heaven is the true good land, where nothing necessary is lacking and joy is complete.

## Warnings against pride when prosperous (verses 10–20)

Moses reminded Israel of its duty in prosperous times.

The people must remember their Benefactor and give thanks for everything they receive.

Prosperity brings strong temptations.

People who acquire wealth or engage in successful business may be tempted toward pride, forgetfulness of God and worldly-mindedness.

They may become anxious about many things.

In some ways, poor believers possess an advantage.

They often recognize God's provision more readily as an answer to trusting prayer.

Although they have less, they may find it easier to rely on him for each day's bread and may enjoy a particular sweetness in doing so.

They are also spared many temptations that come with wealth.

Remember God's former dealings with you.

His infinite wisdom and goodness lie behind the changes and trials he allows.

Israel's hard experiences were intended, in the end, **to do the people good**.

Human pride is deeply rooted.

Even people who had endured slavery in Egypt still needed the wilderness to humble them.

Hardly a week passes without our giving evidence of weakness, folly and sinful corruption.

Only a broken heart truly recognizes how precious the Savior is.

Even the most suitable outward trials cannot produce that response unless God's Spirit makes them effective within us.

God's gifts and our efforts are not competing explanations.

What we receive comes through his promises.

Those who follow other people into sin must expect to share their destruction.

If we behave as sinners do, we should not be surprised to suffer as sinners suffer.
