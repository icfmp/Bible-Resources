# 1 John 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Brotherly love is the effect of the new birth, which makes obedience to all God's commandments pleasant. | 1–5 |
| Reference to witnesses agreeing to prove that Jesus, the Son of God, is the true Messiah. | 6–8 |
| The satisfaction the believer has about Christ, and eternal life through him. | 9–12 |
| The assurance of God's hearing and answering prayer. | 13–17 |
| The happy condition of true believers, and a charge to renounce all idolatry. | 18–21 |

## New birth brings love and victory through faith (verses 1–5)

True love for God's people is different from ordinary kindness or loyalty to a group.

It is united with **love for God and obedience to his commands**.

The Holy Spirit who teaches love also teaches obedience. A person who deliberately continues in sin or neglects known duties cannot claim to love God's children genuinely.

God's commandments are **holy, just and good rules of liberty and happiness**.

Those born of God do not regard these duties as a burden. Instead, they grieve that they cannot obey him more fully.

Self-denial remains necessary, but believers possess a spiritual principle that helps them persevere through obstacles.

They may fall under trials, yet rise again to continue the struggle.

People without faith in Christ remain bound, in one way or another, to **the world's customs, opinions and interests**.

Faith is the means and spiritual armor of victory.

Through faith we cling to Christ and refuse the rule of worldly sinful desires.

Faith purifies the heart from the sensual appetites by which the world gains control.

Believers possess **the indwelling Spirit**, stronger than the power ruling this world.

Jesus' own life teaches them to renounce the world's sinful ways.

They cannot rest content with it, but continue striving toward heaven.

Either we overcome the world **through Christ's example and grace**, or it will finally overcome us.

## The Spirit, water and blood testify to Christ (verses 6–8)

Human beings are defiled **inwardly and outwardly**, especially through the power and pollution of sin in their nature.

Jesus Christ provides **regeneration and the renewing work of the Holy Spirit** to cleanse us.

Henry notes that **some interpreters** understand the two sacraments in the water and blood:

**Baptism** represents the Holy Spirit's washing and regeneration.

**The Lord's Supper** represents Christ's shed blood and receiving him by faith for forgiveness and justification.

These cleansing realities were also pictured in the old covenant's sacrifices and purification rites.

By **the water**, the soul is cleansed and made fit for heaven.

By **the blood**, the sinner receives justification, reconciliation and acceptance as righteous before God.

Christ's blood satisfies the law's curse, so that the Holy Spirit's cleansing work may be given.

Both **water and blood flowed from the crucified Redeemer's side**.

Jesus loved his church and gave himself to make her holy, cleansing her through the word and presenting her glorious and without stain (**Ephesians 5:25–27**).

The Holy Spirit confirms this saving work and cannot lie.

Henry also comments on **three heavenly witnesses** in the wording known from the received text tradition:

**The Father** testified repeatedly from heaven that Jesus is his beloved Son.

**The Word** declared unity with the Father and taught that seeing him reveals the Father.

**The Holy Spirit** descended upon Christ at baptism, spoke through the prophets and testified to his resurrection and mediatorial authority through gifts given to the apostles.

He expressly adds that **whether this particular passage is quoted or not, the doctrine of the Trinity in unity remains firmly established**.

He then discusses **three witnesses to Christ's person and salvation**:

**First, the Holy Spirit**, regenerating hearts that were naturally hostile to God.

**Second, the water**, displaying Christ's purity and cleansing power, visibly represented through the transformed lives of his people and the sign of baptism.

**Third, the blood**, his ransom for sinners and the fulfillment of the older sacrifices.

All three testify together to **the same Savior and the same salvation**.

Rejecting this testimony is not a minor disagreement: it amounts to dismissing the Spirit's witness concerning Christ.

## God's testimony gives assurance of eternal life (verses 9–12)

People readily act upon human testimony in everyday affairs.

It is unreasonable to dismiss **God's testimony concerning his Son** while treating ordinary human witnesses as credible.

True believers know their guilt and their need for such a Savior.

They discover that **Jesus Christ fits every spiritual need**.

His word has humbled, healed, awakened and comforted them.

Their attitudes, desires and enjoyments have changed.

They are not the people they formerly were, though they still struggle with remaining sin, worldly temptation and evil powers.

Through faith in Christ they receive strength to continue toward a better world.

A believer thus possesses **an inward witness**, even though seasons of darkness may trouble assurance.

Refusing God's record about the Son effectively calls God **a liar**.

Those who reject Jesus as **the divine Son**, refuse his teaching as **Prophet**, deny his atonement as **High Priest**, or resist his rule as **King**, remain spiritually dead and condemned.

Neither outward morality, scholarship, religious ceremonies nor doctrinal opinions can substitute for trusting him.

## Pray according to God's will and intercede for others (verses 13–17)

The testimony of Scripture calls us to believe in God's Son and receive the gospel promise of **eternal life**.

Believers should gratefully accept God's word and abound in his service, confident that faithful labor is not wasted.

Christ invites us to bring requests and anxieties to him, even while we struggle with sin.

But all prayer must be submitted **to God's will**.

Some prayers are answered quickly. Other answers come in a better way than the one we requested.

Pray for **other people as well as yourselves**.

We cannot rightly ask God to pardon people **while they deliberately remain impenitent and reject his salvation**, as though forgiveness could be granted while they remain determined to refuse it.

But we may pray **that they repent, receive faith in Christ and enjoy all the saving mercies that follow**.

Intercede for people who have stumbled, that they may be restored, and for those facing temptation or affliction.

We should be especially thankful that **no sin genuinely repented of is a sin unto death**.

## Remain in Christ and keep yourselves from idols (verses 18–21)

Humanity belongs to two opposing dominions: **God's kingdom and the power of the evil one**.

True believers belong to God, live through his grace and are directed toward his glory.

Others remain under the power of the wicked one, whatever religious label, social standing or outward profession they bear.

The Son brings believers to **the Father**. Through the indwelling and active work of the **Holy Spirit**, they share the love and favor of both Father and Son.

Blessed are those who know that God's Son has come and possess a heart willing to trust the One who is true.

May we know this privilege, turning from **all idols, false teachings and idolatrous love of earthly things**.

Through faith, God's power preserves his people for **eternal salvation**.

To the living and true God belong glory and dominion forever. Amen.
