# Daniel 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Daniel's enemies seek a charge against him (verses 1–5)

Daniel was now very old, yet still capable of important public work and **steadfast in his faith**.

This honored God.

Religious believers should conduct their responsibilities so faithfully that even their most watchful enemies find **no grounds for accusation**, except matters of worship where they follow their consciences.

## Daniel continues praying (verses 6–10)

To forbid prayer for **thirty days** was to deprive God, for that period, of the honor due him from human beings.

It also deprived people of the **comfort they receive from God**.

When we are in need or danger, are we not naturally moved to pray?

We cannot live a day without God's care.

How can we expect to live **thirty days without speaking to him**?

Yet many people who face no law forbidding prayer go more than thirty days without offering one serious, heartfelt request to God.

Their number may far exceed those who pray regularly with humility and thanksgiving.

Persecuting laws are usually justified by false claims.

Nevertheless, Christians should avoid **bitter complaints and abusive speech**.

It is good to have **regular hours for prayer**.

Daniel prayed openly and did not conceal his devotion.

Despite his heavy workload, he did not treat public responsibilities as an excuse to neglect **daily worship**.

How much less excuse do people with fewer responsibilities have for ignoring their souls!

During trials, we must take care that what we call **prudence** is not really **cowardice in God's cause**.

People who surrender the life of the soul—even to preserve physical life—act foolishly.

Daniel did not shorten his time of worship by praying without thanksgiving.

He fulfilled **the whole duty**, prayer **and** praise.

Prayer rests on the sufficiency of God as **almighty Creator and Redeemer**, and on our needs as sinful creatures.

We should look to **Christ**, turning toward him in prayer even while living in the foreign land of earthly exile.

## Daniel is thrown into the lions' den (verses 11–17)

It is common for acts performed faithfully from conscience toward God to be falsely portrayed as **stubborn contempt for civil government**.

Through thoughtlessness, we sometimes do things we later wish—like Darius—we had never done.

Daniel, a venerable and upright man, was treated like a criminal and thrown to the lions **solely because he worshiped his God**.

The stone placed over the den was, Henry says, surely arranged under **God's Providence** so that the miracle would be undeniable.

The king probably sealed it with his own signet **to prevent Daniel's enemies from killing him**, according to Henry's supposition.

Let us entrust **our lives and souls to God while doing what is right**.

We cannot entirely trust even people we serve faithfully.

But believers can always rely on God's **favor and consolation**.

## Daniel is preserved (verses 18–24)

The best way to sleep peacefully is to keep **a good conscience**.

We know what Darius did not know for certain: the living God's servants have a Master who is fully able to protect them.

Consider God's power over the fiercest animals.

He can also restrain the **roaring lion** that constantly seeks to devour.

Daniel was preserved completely because he **trusted in his God**.

Those who courageously trust God while fulfilling duty will find him present to help.

The righteous is delivered from trouble, while the wicked person falls into it.

The wicked person's brief triumph ends in ruin.

## Darius issues a decree (verses 25–28)

If we live in reverence for God and follow his commands, we may expect his peace.

The kingdom, power and glory belong **to the Lord forever**.

Yet some people proclaim God's wonderful works to others while remaining **strangers to his saving grace** themselves.

Let us therefore be **doers of the word as well as believers**, lest in the end we discover that we have deceived ourselves.
