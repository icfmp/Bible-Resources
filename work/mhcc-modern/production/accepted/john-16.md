# John 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Persecution foretold. | 1–6 |
| The promise of the Holy Spirit, and his office. | 7–15 |
| Christ's departure and return. | 16–22 |
| Encouragement to prayer. | 23–27 |
| Christ's discoveries of himself. | 28–33 |

## Jesus foretells persecution (verses 1–6)

Jesus warned his disciples about coming trouble so they would not be caught unprepared and overwhelmed by fear.

People genuinely opposed to God's service may pretend to be zealously serving him.

Religious language does not make wicked deeds righteous. Evil remains evil even when committed in God's name.

As Christ looked to the fulfillment of Scripture during his suffering, so should his followers during theirs.

Jesus had not previously needed to speak so fully about the Spirit's coming because he was still present to teach, guide and comfort the disciples.

When we suffer, asking **where the trouble came from** may leave us confused; asking **where God is leading it** can satisfy us, because his purpose is our good.

Sorrowful Christians often dwell on the cloud's dark side and refuse to hear the voice of joy.

The disciples' excessive attachment to this present life intensified their sadness.

Nothing so obstructs joy in God as love of the world and the worldly grief it produces.

## The Holy Spirit's coming and work (verses 7–15)

Christ's departure was necessary for the Comforter to come.

The Spirit's coming followed Christ's death, which his departure would accomplish.

Jesus' bodily presence was limited to one place at a time, but **his Spirit is present wherever his people gather in his name**, at every time and place.

The Spirit first **convicts**, and then **comforts**.

Conviction is his work, and only he can accomplish it effectively.

He convinces sinners of **sin** in its many aspects: its reality, guilt, folly, pollution, source in fallen human nature and final consequence in death.

He shows the whole world guilty before God.

The Spirit also convicts concerning **righteousness**.

Jesus of Nazareth is the righteous Christ. His righteousness is made available to believers for **justification and salvation**.

The Spirit shows sinners where righteousness may be found and how they can be accepted before God.

Christ's ascension demonstrates that the ransom has been accepted and the saving righteousness completed.

The Spirit convicts concerning **judgment**.

The ruler of this world has been judged. When Satan's power is broken, believers can be confident that no rival power can overcome Christ.

This also directs our thoughts toward the final day of judgment.

The Spirit's coming was therefore an immeasurable benefit to the disciples.

He is not merely a guide pointing to the way but **a companion helping them walk in it**.

Being led into truth means more than carrying correct ideas in the mind.

It means tasting the goodness and knowing the **power of truth in the heart**.

He will make every profitable truth known and reveal what is to come.

The Spirit's gifts and graces, the apostles' preaching and writing, their languages and miracles—all were intended to **glorify Christ**.

Each of us should ask whether the Spirit has begun a good work within us.

Unless we recognize our guilt and danger, we will not understand the greatness of Christ's salvation.

The more we pray for and depend on the Spirit, the more clearly we will see the Redeemer and the more deeply we will love him.

## Grief will become joy (verses 16–22)

We should remember how quickly our opportunities for grace can pass and use them faithfully.

The disciples' sorrow would soon give way to joy, like a mother's pain replaced by happiness at the sight of her newborn child.

The Holy Spirit would comfort them, and neither people nor demons, suffering nor death, could permanently steal their joy.

Believers' sorrow or gladness is closely connected with their sense of Christ's presence and favor.

The ungodly are approaching sorrow that nothing can relieve.

Believers, however, inherit **joy that no one can take away**.

Where is the happiness now of those who murdered the Lord, compared with the lasting joy of his friends?

## Jesus encourages prayer (verses 23–27)

Asking something of the Father acknowledges spiritual need, desires spiritual blessings and recognizes that **only God can supply them**.

Praying **in Christ's name** acknowledges our unworthiness and depends entirely on Christ as **the Lord our Righteousness**.

Until then Jesus had often taught through brief sayings or parables that his disciples did not fully understand.

After his resurrection he intended to speak more plainly about the Father and access to him through his intercession.

Jesus repeatedly commanded prayer in his name because his mediation teaches us our sinfulness and the value and power of **his atoning death**, by which we approach God.

We may address the Father in Christ's name or address the Son as God in human nature who reconciles sinners to himself.

These approaches belong together because **the Father and the Son are one**.

## Jesus' departure and the disciples' confidence (verses 28–33)

Jesus came from the Father as **God revealed in human nature**, and he would return to the Father and be received into glory.

The disciples understood more and their faith increased.

Yet they did not yet recognize their own weakness.

The divine nature never abandoned Christ's human nature during his sufferings.

It supported him, giving his suffering strength and worth.

When God is graciously present with us, we can be content even when everyone else deserts us.

True peace is found **in Christ alone**.

Through him we have peace **with God**, and therefore peace within our hearts.

Christ has already conquered the world.

Yet believers must remain watchful, for those who think they stand can still fall.

We do not know how we would behave under severe temptation.

**Watch and pray without ceasing**, so that God does not leave us to ourselves.
