# Ezekiel 26 — Modern English

## Original heading

**A prophecy against Tyre.**

## Tyre's selfish prosperity will be destroyed (verses 1–14)

It is an easily overlooked but serious sin to be secretly pleased when others decline or die because we expect to benefit, or when their downfall allows us to become more prosperous.

Such pleasure comes from **selfishness and greed** and from regarding the world as our source of happiness. Love for God expressly forbids that attitude.

God often brings to nothing the projects of those who hope to advance themselves through other people's ruin.

Many of the principles most commonly accepted in the world of trade directly oppose God's law.

The Lord will confront merchants whose **love of money and selfishness have hardened their hearts**, as they had hardened the hearts of the people of Tyre.

People have little reason to boast about goods that provoke others' envy and greed and constantly pass from one owner to another.

In acquiring, protecting and spending such wealth, people may offend God, whose anger can turn a joyful city into a heap of ruins.

## The fall of Tyre warns prosperous people (verses 15–21)

Consider how important and impressive Tyre once was—and how low it would be brought.

The downfall of others should **awaken us from careless confidence**.

Every clear fulfillment of a biblical prophecy is like a miracle that strengthens our faith.

Everything merely earthly is **vanity and frustration**.

Even those whose prosperity seems most secure will soon disappear from sight and be forgotten.
