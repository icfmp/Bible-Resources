# Ezekiel 36 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The land shall be delivered from heathen oppressors. | 1–15 |
| The people are reminded of former sins, and promised deliverance. | 16–24 |
| Also holiness, and gospel blessings. | 25–38 |

## The land will be delivered from its oppressors (verses 1–15)

Those who treat God's people with contempt and reproach will find that the same disgrace returns upon themselves.

God promises to show favor to his Israel. We need not complain if, as human beings grow more unkind, **God shows us still greater kindness**.

His people will return to their own borders.

This return was also **a picture of the heavenly Canaan**, which belongs to all God's children and into which they will be gathered together.

When God graciously returns to a people and they return to him in obedience, their grievances will be put right.

Henry expects **the complete fulfillment of this prophecy in a future event**.

## Former sins and promised deliverance (verses 16–24)

The restoration of Israel pictures **our redemption through Christ**. It shows that the ultimate purpose of salvation is **God's glory**.

A people's sin defiles their land. It makes the land offensive to God and unpleasant for those who live there.

God's holy name is his great name. **His holiness is his greatness**; nothing else makes a human being truly great.

## A clean heart and the blessings of the gospel (verses 25–38)

Water pictures the cleansing of souls polluted by sin. Yet water itself can remove only **outward bodily uncleanness**.

Henry understands water generally as **the sacramental sign of the Holy Spirit's sanctifying work**. That work is always connected with **the atoning blood of Christ**.

When Christ's blood is applied by faith to the conscience, cleansing it from evil works, the Holy Spirit also works in the powers of the soul to remove sin's pollution.

Everyone who belongs to the **new covenant** receives **a new heart and a new spirit** and is enabled to walk in newness of life.

God promises to give a **heart of flesh**: a gentle, tender heart responsive to his holy will.

Renewing grace changes a soul as profoundly as if a lifeless stone became living flesh.

God places **his Spirit within his people** as their Teacher, Guide and Sanctifier.

His promise to give the grace we need for obedience should encourage us to keep seeking and practicing our duty.

These promises may be pleaded in prayer by **all true believers in every age**, and God will fulfill them.
