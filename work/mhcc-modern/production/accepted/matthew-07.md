# Matthew 7 — Modern English

## Judging others without hypocrisy (verses 1–6)

We must examine ourselves and our own conduct. But we must not make our personal opinions a law for everyone else.

Do not judge rashly or condemn a brother or sister without grounds.

Do not insist on believing the worst about people.

Jesus corrected those who quarrel over the small faults of others while tolerating much greater sins in themselves.

Some faults are like **specks** and others like **beams**; some resemble a **gnat**, others a **camel**.

This does not mean any sin is harmless. Even a tiny splinter in an eye or a gnat in a throat causes pain and danger and must be removed.

Charity may teach us to describe our brother's fault as a small splinter; true repentance and godly grief should make us regard our **own** sin as a large beam.

It is astonishing that people can live in a sinful and miserable condition without noticing it, just as it would be astonishing to overlook a beam lodged in one's eye.

The god of this world blinds human minds.

The first rule for anyone seeking to correct another person is therefore **to reform oneself first**.

## Asking, seeking and knocking (verses 7–11)

Prayer is the appointed means of obtaining what we need.

Pray often, seriously and earnestly.

**Ask** like a beggar asking for help or a traveler asking for directions.

**Seek** like someone searching for a lost treasure or a merchant searching for precious pearls.

**Knock** like someone who longs to enter a house. Sin has shut and barred the door against us; through prayer we knock.

God grants what is requested according to his promises **when he knows it is fitting for us**. What more could we rightly desire?

The invitation extends to everyone who prays in faith: Jews and Gentiles, young and old, rich and poor, people of high or low standing, masters and servants, educated and uneducated.

All are equally welcome at the throne of grace.

Parents, though sometimes unwisely indulgent, generally wish to give good gifts to their children.

Our heavenly Father is perfectly wise. He knows what we need and what is truly good for us.

He would never command us to pray only to refuse to hear us—or to give us what would harm us.

## Treating others well and choosing the narrow way (verses 12–14)

Christ came to teach us not merely **what to know and believe**, but **what to do**.

His instruction governs relationships with God and with everyone else—not only people who share our convictions or belong to our own party.

We should put ourselves in our neighbor's position and treat that person as we recognize it would be reasonable and right for us to be treated.

Jesus then described **two ways**, the right and the wrong, leading to eternal life and destruction.

There is no third destination in the life to come and no middle path in the present one.

The gate of sin is wide and already open. People can enter it without denying their sinful appetites or passions.

The road beyond it is broad and contains many different kinds of wrongdoing; it is crowded.

Yet why should anyone willingly go to destruction **with a crowd** rather than accept eternal life without that crowd?

The way to life is narrow.

Passing through the narrow gate does not mean our journey is finished. Self must be denied, the body disciplined and corrupt desires put to death.

Daily temptations must be resisted and duties performed. We must remain watchful through many troubles.

Still, this way is inviting because it leads both to the present joy of God's favor and to eternal blessedness.

That hope makes the hardships bearable.

People in every age have tried to explain away Christ's clear teaching.

True disciples are often regarded as unusual or unfashionable, while those who follow the majority continue on the broad road.

Be firm in your faith.

When we hear of the narrow gate and the few who find it, should we not ask whether **we ourselves have entered** and how faithfully we are continuing?

## Recognizing false prophets (verses 15–20)

Few things prevent people from entering the narrow gate more effectively than **flattering, flesh-pleasing teaching** that contradicts the truth.

Such teachers are known by the **tendency and effects of their doctrine**.

Some aspect of their character and conduct will conflict with Christ's teaching.

A religious opinion that leads people into sin does **not** come from God.

## Hearing and obeying Christ's words (verses 21–29)

Calling Christ our Master with our lips is not enough.

We must **believe in him, repent of sin, live a holy life and love one another**.

Our sanctification is God's will.

Do not trust in outward religious privileges or activities as though they guarantee acceptance. Many deceive themselves in that way and perish.

Everyone who names Christ must turn away from sin.

Others hear the word but never obey it. Their minds accumulate religious ideas without producing faithful action.

Jesus compared these listeners to **two builders**.

His words may require difficult acts of obedience, but we must do them.

**Christ alone is the foundation. Everything else is sand.**

Some build their hope on wealth and prosperity, others on an outward profession of religion. Neither foundation can support our hopes for heaven.

A storm is coming that will test everyone's work.

At death, what hope can hypocrites retain?

The house built on sand falls at the very moment its owner most needs shelter—and when it is too late to build again.

May the Lord make us wise builders for eternity. Then nothing will separate us from the love of Jesus Christ.

The crowds were astonished at the wisdom and authority of his teaching.

His sermon remains fresh no matter how often it is read. Every word testifies to **its divine Author**.

Let us study the blessings and Christian graces he described with sustained attention—even spending weeks reflecting on particular ones—instead of settling for vague wishes to possess them all while actively cultivating none.
