## Offerings for the trumpet feast and the Day of Atonement (verses 1–11)

More sacred celebrations were held in Israel's seventh month than in any other month. The harvest was over, and the time to sow new seed had not yet come.

Whenever our urgent earthly duties become less demanding, we should devote more time to serving God directly.

The blowing of trumpets had been commanded previously. This chapter gives the sacrifices that were to accompany it.

Those who want to understand God's mind through Scripture should compare its different passages carefully.

Later revelations of divine truth explain matters that were obscure in earlier passages and supply what had not yet been disclosed.

In this way God's servants are equipped and brought toward maturity.

## Offerings during the Feast of Tabernacles (verses 12–40)

The Feast of Tabernacles came soon after the Day of Atonement.

On the Day of Atonement, the people were to humble themselves over sin; during the following festival, they were to rejoice before the Lord.

Their joyful days were also days of sacrifice and worship.

Cheerfulness is a blessing when it encourages rather than distracts us from serving God.

The people offered sacrifices throughout their stay in temporary shelters.

We, too, live for a time in the temporary shelter of earthly life. Maintaining communion with God continually is both our duty and our benefit.

Different sacrifices were appointed for each of the seven days.

But **every day included a sin offering**, as in the other feasts.

Our offerings of praise cannot be accepted unless we depend upon Christ's great sacrifice, in which he became the Sin Offering for us.

Nor should special seasons of worship replace ordinary, regular devotion.

All these ceremonies remind us how continually we need forgiveness.

Our earthly lives must be lived by faith in the Son of God until we go to be with him, see his glory and praise his mercy.

He loved us and washed us from our sins in his own blood.

To him be honor and glory forever. Amen.
