# 2 Samuel 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Joab procures Absalom's recall. | 1–20 |
| Absalom recalled. | 21–24 |
| His personal beauty. | 25–27 |
| He is admitted to his father's presence. | 28–33 |

## Joab arranges an appeal for Absalom (verses 1–20)

Notice how the woman presenting her case to David appeals to God's mercy and to his kindness toward guilty sinners.

The state of sinners is one of **banishment from God**.

Yet God never forgives in a way that dishonors his own law or justice.

Nor does he pardon people who remain unrepentant, encourage further wrongdoing or authorize harm to other people.

## David permits Absalom to return (verses 21–24)

David was inclined to show kindness to his son.

But to preserve the honor of his position as judge, he waited for another person to appeal on Absalom's behalf.

Henry sees in this a picture of God's gracious dealings.

God has compassion toward miserable sinners and does not delight in their destruction.

Yet sinners can be reconciled to him **only through a Mediator who pleads on their behalf**.

God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself.

Christ came into the land of our banishment in order to bring us back to God.

## Absalom's outward beauty (verses 25–27)

Nothing is said about Absalom's wisdom or devotion to God.

The passage praises only his physical appearance.

What poor praise for someone who possesses no greater virtue!

A morally polluted soul can live in a handsome body.

Absalom had an impressive head of hair.

It was so heavy as to become burdensome, yet he preferred to bear its weight rather than cut it.

People rarely complain about something that feeds their pride, even when it causes discomfort.

May the Lord give us **the beauty of holiness and the adornment of a gentle, quiet spirit**!

Only those who revere him are truly happy.

## Absalom gains admission to David's presence (verses 28–33)

Absalom behaved insolently toward **Joab** until Joab agreed to intercede for him.

His bold and insolent demand then secured what he wanted from the king.

When parents and rulers encourage such a person by yielding to his behavior, they may soon suffer serious consequences.

But if a human father's compassion could lead him to receive an **unrepentant** son, why should truly repentant sinners doubt the mercy of the **Father of mercies**?
