# Ruth 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The kinsman refuses to redeem Ruth's inheritance. | 1–8 |
| Boaz marries Ruth. | 9–12 |
| Birth of Obed. | 13–22 |

## The nearer relative declines redemption (verses 1–8)

The matter was governed by the laws Moses had given about family inheritances.

Henry assumes it was settled openly and according to the required legal procedures.

When the nearer relative heard all the conditions, he refused to redeem the property.

Many people respond similarly when presented with the great redemption offered by God.

They have heard good things about faith.

They have no particular criticism of it.

They may even speak respectfully of it.

But they remain unwilling to commit themselves, because they fear that doing so will reduce their worldly wealth or opportunities.

The nearer relative therefore surrendered his right to Boaz.

Everyone who desires to be a genuine Israelite, without deceit, must act with honesty and fairness in **contracts and trade**.

In the end, honest dealings prove the wisest policy.

## Boaz takes Ruth as his wife (verses 9–12)

People are quick to seize chances to enlarge their estates.

Yet few recognize how precious true devotion to God is.

The world calls some people wise whom the Lord calls foolish.

They neglect their souls and reject Christ's salvation because they fear losing earthly advantage.

God, however, honored Boaz by making him part of the **Messiah's family line**.

The other relative, who worried about reducing his property and position, is forgotten.

His name, family and inheritance have disappeared from the record.

## Ruth gives birth to Obed (verses 13–22)

Ruth bore a son, through whose descendants thousands upon thousands would belong to God.

As a direct ancestor of Jesus Christ, she played a part in bringing blessing to everyone who would be saved through him.

That included **Gentiles as well as Jews**.

Ruth's story was a witness to the Gentile world that God had not abandoned its peoples forever.

In due time they would become one with his chosen people and share in his salvation.

**Prayer** accompanied the marriage, and **praise** accompanied the birth of the child.

How sad that such expressions of faith are uncommon among Christians—or become empty formalities!

This passage also traces **David's descent from Ruth**.

The time eventually came when **Bethlehem in Judah** witnessed wonders far greater than those recorded in her story.

There, another child was born in humble circumstances.

His birth affected the decrees of the Roman ruler of the world.

It brought princes and wise men from the east to his feet, carrying gold, frankincense and myrrh.

His name will endure forever.

All nations will call him blessed.

In that promised Offspring, all the nations of the earth will receive blessing.
