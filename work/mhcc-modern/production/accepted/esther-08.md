# Esther 8 — Modern English

## Mordecai receives Haman's former position (verses 1 and 2)

Esther intended to use for good what Haman had meant to use for evil.

The responsibility and trust the king had given Haman now passed to Mordecai—a welcome change.

See the emptiness of accumulating treasure on earth.

The person who stores up wealth cannot know who will eventually enjoy it.

Had Haman known that his most hated enemy would take control of everything he worked to acquire, even looking at his possessions would have brought distress instead of pleasure.

We should therefore seek **riches that cannot be left behind**, but will accompany us into eternity.

## Esther pleads for her people (verses 3–14)

With the church of God in danger, earnest action was necessary.

Though Esther herself was safe, she fell before the king and begged him to rescue her people.

No tears are recorded when she sought protection for her own life.

But when her people's safety was at stake, **she wept**.

Such compassion and tender concern most closely resemble Christ.

Persian government treated royal decrees as unchangeable and impossible to recall.

Far from proving the wisdom of its rulers, this displayed their **pride and folly**.

It resembled the arrogant desire to **be like gods**.

Only God has the right to declare what cannot be changed or withdrawn.

Nevertheless, a solution was found: a second decree permitted the Jews to defend themselves.

The decree was published **in the languages of all the provinces**.

If earthly rulers make their laws available in languages their subjects understand, how could Christians justify keeping **God's words and commands in a language his servants cannot understand**?

## Mordecai is honored and the Jews rejoice (verses 15–17)

Mordecai now wore splendid royal clothing.

This mattered not for its own sake but as evidence of the king's approval and of God's favor toward the church.

A country is fortunate when positions of honor are filled by people of genuine devotion.

When God's church prospers, many will join it who would avoid identifying with it during persecution.

When believers experience peace and walk in **the fear of the Lord and the comfort of the Holy Spirit**, they grow in number.

Satan's attempts to destroy the church ultimately help bring more people into genuine Christian faith.
