# 2 Samuel 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The ark removed from Kirjath-jearim. | 1–5 |

| David brings the ark to Zion. | 12–19 |
| Michal's ill conduct. | 20–23 |

## The ark leaves Kiriath-jearim (verses 1–5)

God remains present with the souls of his people even when they lack outward signs of that presence.

But now that David was established as king, the ark began to receive proper honor again.

Let us learn to speak and think reverently about the Lord and about the means of worship he has appointed.

Those means are signs of his presence among us, just as the ark was for Israel (**Matthew 28:20**).

**Christ is our Ark.**

Through him God shows his favor and accepts our prayers and praise.

The ark especially pictured Christ's mediation, in which the Lord's name and glory are displayed.

The priests should have carried the ark **on their shoulders**.

The Philistines had moved it in a cart without being punished.

But when Israelites, who had received a specific instruction, used a cart, they did so at their peril.

God's appointed way of worship matters.

## Uzzah is judged; Obed-edom receives a blessing (verses 6–11)

Uzzah died after touching the ark.

God saw presumptuous irreverence in his heart.

Familiarity with holy things can lead people to treat them casually and with contempt.

How serious it is to claim the privileges of God's covenant without receiving them on the terms he has appointed!

**Obed-edom** welcomed the ark into his house without fear.

He knew that the ark brought judgment only upon those who treated it wrongly.

The same divine hand that judged Uzzah's arrogance rewarded Obed-edom's reverent confidence.

We should not despise the gospel because some people who reject it come under judgment.

Consider instead the blessings it brings to those who welcome it.

Heads of households should be encouraged to maintain genuine religion in their families.

A home that welcomes the ark is blessed, and everyone living there benefits.

## David brings the ark to Zion (verses 12–19)

People could see that those living near the ark were blessed.

Christ is a stumbling stone and a cause of offense to those who disobey.

But to believers he is a chosen, precious cornerstone (**1 Peter 2:6–8**).

Let us welcome the blessing of fellowship with him.

We do not need to deprive others of such blessing to receive it ourselves.

When David resumed the procession, he first offered sacrifices.

Our undertakings are more likely to go well when we begin with God and seek peace with him.

We are unworthy and our worship is imperfect.

Therefore even our greatest religious joy must remain joined to **repentance and faith in Christ's atoning blood**.

David celebrated with deep and wholehearted delight.

We should serve God with every power and ability of body and soul.

David put aside his royal clothing and wore **plain linen garments**.

He prayed with and for the people, and as a prophet he formally pronounced the Lord's blessing over them.

## Michal despises David's worship (verses 20–23)

After the celebration, David returned home to bless his family.

He intended to pray with them and for them and lead them in thanksgiving for this national mercy.

Even angels worship God.

Surely such service cannot degrade even the greatest person.

Yet the houses of kings are not free from family troubles.

Acts of sincere devotion often look foolish or undignified to people who have little religious life themselves.

If we can approve our conduct before God, acting sincerely in his presence, we need not fear their criticism.

Faithfulness to God deserves public acknowledgment.

We should not be careless in our service, or afraid or ashamed to profess it.

David was content to defend his conduct.

He did not continue rebuking Michal for her contempt.

But God judged her.

Those who honor the Lord will be honored, while those who despise him and his servants will be brought low.
