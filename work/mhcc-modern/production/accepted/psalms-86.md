# Psalm 86 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist pleads his earnestness, and the mercy of God, as reasons why his prayer should be heard. | 1–7 |
| He renews his requests for help and comfort. | 8–17 |

## Poverty, prayer, and divine mercy (verses 1–7)

When we recognize our poverty and misery, these needs become powerful reasons for approaching **the throne of grace**.

The safest way to preserve ourselves is to entrust ourselves to **God's keeping**.

The believer can appeal to the Lord as someone he has favored, set apart for himself, and made a participant in his **sanctifying grace**.

It is a great encouragement to prayer to know that God's converting grace has taught us to trust him and become his servants.

As we maintain fellowship with God, we may expect to receive comfort from him.

God's goodness is displayed in **both giving and forgiving**.

Whatever others may do, we should keep calling upon the Lord and commit our cause to him. We will not seek him in vain.

## Asking to know God's way and receive his help (verses 8–17)

Our God alone possesses **unlimited power and infinite love**.

**Christ is the Way and the Truth**.

The believing soul wants to be taught God's truth and his ways, in order to walk in them, **even more than it wants relief from earthly trouble**.

Those who refuse to set the Lord before them pursue the souls of believers.

Yet the Lord's compassion, mercy and truth provide refuge and consolation.

People whose parents served the Lord may also mention that heritage when asking him to hear and help them.

As we consider David's experience and our own, we must not forget **the One who was rich but became poor for our sake**, so that we might become rich through his poverty.
