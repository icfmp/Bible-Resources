# 1 Corinthians 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The necessity and advantage of the grace of love. | 1–3 |
| Its excellency represented by its properties and effects; | 4–7 |
| and by its abiding, and its superiority. | 8–13 |

## Without love, even great gifts accomplish nothing spiritually (verses 1–3)

The **more excellent way** mentioned at the end of the previous chapter is **love in its fullest meaning**, genuine love toward both God and other people.

It is not merely what people commonly call charity—**giving money or material goods**.

Without this love, even the most impressive spiritual gifts are worthless for the person possessing them and count for nothing in God's sight.

A clear mind and profound learning are of no value spiritually without **a loving and generous heart**.

Someone may give away money freely and even extravagantly while lacking genuine concern for others.

Doing good to another person brings us no spiritual benefit if we act without **love for God and goodwill toward people**.

Even giving up all our possessions brings no blessing if our hearts remain withheld from God.

Nor will the most painful suffering earn God's approval in that condition.

How deceived are people who expect acceptance and reward for works that are **limited, imperfect, and often corrupted by selfishness**!

## Christian love is recognizable in its character and actions (verses 4–7)

Paul described the effects of love so that we can examine whether **this grace is present in our hearts**.

If it is absent, we should not be satisfied until God grants it.

Christian love is **evidence of regeneration**, a test of our profession of faith in Jesus Christ.

The beautiful description also shows the Corinthian believers how far their actual behavior differed from it.

Love is the enemy of **selfishness**.

It does not put personal praise, reputation, profit or enjoyment above the good of others.

But this does **not** mean love destroys every proper concern for our own well-being.

A loving person need not ignore personal needs and legitimate interests.

Rather, love never pursues its own benefit **at someone else's expense** or by neglecting another person's welfare.

It willingly gives priority to others' needs when required.

How gentle and attractive Christian love is!

How beautiful Christianity would appear to the world if everyone professing it were governed by this **divine principle**, giving proper attention to the command upon which **Christ himself** placed such emphasis.

Let us ask ourselves: **Does this love live in my heart?**

Has it shaped my conduct toward everyone?

Am I willing to abandon selfish ambitions?

We need **watchfulness, diligence and prayer**.

## Love will remain when other gifts pass away (verses 8–13)

Love is more excellent than the impressive gifts the Corinthians admired because **it endures**.

Love lasts throughout eternity.

Our present knowledge resembles **childhood**, while the life to come resembles maturity.

How confused and limited a child's understanding appears compared with that of an adult!

When we reach heaven, we will look back upon our best earthly abilities and most valued spiritual gifts with similar recognition of their limitations.

Everything we understand now is dark and incomplete compared with future knowledge.

We see as though through a **mirror's reflection or a riddle**.

In heaven, however, our knowledge will be freed from confusion and error.

Only heaven's light will remove the clouds that now hide God's face from us.

Love surpasses not only extraordinary spiritual gifts, but even the other central graces—**faith and hope**.

**Faith** receives God's revelation and rests upon **the divine Redeemer**.

**Hope** looks toward future happiness and waits for it.

But in heaven **faith becomes sight** and **hope becomes enjoyment**.

We no longer need to believe that something unseen is true once we see it, or hope for what we already enjoy.

**Love, however, becomes perfect**.

In heaven we will love God perfectly and love one another perfectly.

What a blessed state, surpassing every earthly comfort!

**God is love (1 John 4:8, 16)**.

Where believers see him face to face as he is, love reaches its greatest height and its full perfection.
