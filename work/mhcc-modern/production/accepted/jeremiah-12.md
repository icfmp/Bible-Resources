# Jeremiah 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jeremiah complains of the prosperity of the wicked. | 1–6 |
| The heavy judgments to come upon the nation. | 7–13 |
| Divine mercy to them, and even to the nations around. | 14–17 |

## Why do the wicked prosper? (verses 1–6)

When God's ways are darkest to us, we must continue to think **rightly of his character**.

He has never acted unjustly toward any creature.

When we cannot understand a particular event affecting ourselves or others, we must return to the general truths we know.

**The Lord is righteous.**

God knows the condition of every heart.

He sees **the deceit of the hypocrite** and **the sincerity of the upright**.

Judgment will eventually pull wicked people out of their prosperous pasture like sheep led to slaughter.

The land became barren because of the sins of those living in it.

The Lord also corrected Jeremiah himself.

The opposition he had encountered at **Anathoth** was small compared with what awaited him from **Judah's rulers**.

Our grief over evil in the world is often mixed with irritability about the trouble **that evil causes us**.

We live in favored times and face comparatively small difficulties.

How would we behave if called to endure what **faithful believers in former generations** suffered?

## The Lord gives his beloved people over to judgment (verses 7–13)

God's people had been **beloved by him**, precious in his sight.

Yet their conduct led him to hand them over to their enemies.

Many professing churches become like **speckled birds**.

Their lives mix true religious profession with **the world's fashions, pursuits and corruptions**.

God's people may be a spectacle to others.

But in this case they had made themselves one through their own foolishness, leaving themselves vulnerable to the creatures called to prey upon them.

The whole land would become desolate.

Until judgment actually fell, almost no one would give God's warning serious attention.

When he raises his hand and people **refuse to see**, they may be made **to feel**.

Neither silver nor gold helps in the day of divine anger.

Attempts to escape suffering without **repentance and deeds appropriate to it** will end in confusion.

## Even neighboring nations may receive mercy (verses 14–17)

The Lord would defend the cause of his people against their wicked neighbors.

But afterward he would show mercy **even to those nations**, if they learned the true faith.

Henry sees this as possibly looking ahead to the day when **the fullness of the Gentiles comes in**.

Anyone who wants a share in God's people's inheritance and hopes to reach the same final happiness must **learn their ways and walk in them**.
