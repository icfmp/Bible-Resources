# Zechariah 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An exhortation to repentance. | 1–6 |
| A vision of the ministry of angels. | 7–17 |
| The security of the Jews and the destruction of their enemies. | 18–21 |

## Turn to the Lord before it is too late (verses 1–6)

God's almighty power and sovereign rule should move sinners to repent and return to him.

How desirable it is to have **the Lord of hosts as our Friend**, and how dreadful to have him as our enemy!

Consider what happened to earlier generations and the warnings God sent them through his prophets.

The only way to escape approaching ruin is to abandon our evil ways.

Where are our fathers, and where are the prophets who preached to them?

They once walked through the same streets, lived in the same houses, worked in the same shops and worshiped in the same places as we do.

Now they have all passed away.

But dying did not end their existence.

They have entered **the unchanging world of eternity**, toward which we are rapidly traveling.

Those who lived and died in sin are under judgment; those who lived and died in Christ are in heaven.

If we live and die as they did, we will soon join them in one condition or the other.

The disregard of earlier generations for their own souls gives us no reason to ruin ours.

Their prophets also died.

**Jesus Christ alone is the Prophet who lives forever**; the ministries of all other prophets have an appointed end.

What a serious thing it is that **dying ministers speak to dying hearers about souls that never die**!

Both stand on eternity's threshold.

The preachers died, and those who heard them died.

**But God's word did not die.**

Not one part of it fell to the ground, because God is righteous and faithful.

## The vision of the horsemen and the angels (verses 7–17)

Zechariah saw a dark grove of myrtle trees, sheltered by hills.

It pictured the low and distressed condition of the Jewish church.

Among those trees was a warrior on a red horse.

Henry understands this figure as **Christ present among his suffering people**, ready to come to their help.

Behind him stood angels, ready to carry out his purposes—some through judgment, others through mercy, and others through events containing both.

To understand the mysteries of God's kingdom, we should turn **not to angels but to Christ himself**.

Angels also have things to learn.

The Savior gladly teaches those who humbly wish to understand the things of God.

The nations around Judah were at peace, while the returned Jews still experienced insecurity.

This contrast prompted the intercession in the vision.

But **mercy must be sought through Christ**, whose prayer for his church prevails.

The Lord answered the **Angel of the Covenant** with good and comforting promises.

All the gospel's gracious promises come to us from Jesus Christ, who receives them from his Father in response to the intercession of **his blood**.

Ministers are to declare those promises throughout the world.

It is not unusual for God's enemies to enjoy worldly rest while his people undergo discipline, temptation, fear, oppression and persecution.

The prophecy offered hope for the revival of the Jews after their captivity.

Henry also regards those events as **shadows of the future restoration of the church after the oppression of “New Testament Babylon” has ended**.

That later application is **Henry's interpretive expectation**, not a newly established historical fulfillment.

## The four horns and the workmen (verses 18–21)

The church's enemies aimed to erase Israel's name.

The **four horns** symbolized power, strength and violent hostility.

At first the prophet saw them as so formidable that the safety of every faithful person and the success of every good work seemed doubtful.

Then the Lord showed him **four workmen** empowered to break the horns.

Our physical eyes see the dangers that surround God's church.

Whichever way we look, we see the power of those who oppose it.

But **only the eyes of faith see that the church is safe under God's protection**.

When the Lord has work to accomplish, he will raise up people to do it.

He will also provide others to defend the workers and protect the work.

How much reason we have to praise **the holy and eternal Spirit**, who cares for the present and eternal welfare of believers and, through God's word, teaches the church the wonders of salvation!
