# Luke 11 — Modern English

## Jesus teaches his disciples to pray (verses 1–4)

**“Lord, teach us to pray”** is a good and necessary request.

Only Jesus can truly teach us how to pray, through his **word and Spirit**.

Lord, teach me what prayer means, awaken in me the desire to pray and show me what I should ask and say.

Jesus gave the disciples a prayer very similar to the one he had taught in the Sermon on the Mount.

Matthew and Luke use slightly different wording, but Henry considers those differences unimportant to the prayer's meaning.

When praying for others and ourselves, we should approach **our heavenly Father**, trusting his goodness and power.

## Jesus encourages perseverance in prayer (verses 5–13)

Jesus encourages us to pray earnestly and continually.

We should ask for what we truly need, as someone asks a kind neighbor for bread.

If God does not immediately answer our requests, we should not give up. He will act in his appointed time.

Especially, ask for **the Holy Spirit**.

We need his help to pray well, but also because **all spiritual blessings are included in that one great gift**.

Through the Spirit we come to know God and ourselves, repent, believe in Christ and love him. We find comfort in this life and are prepared for the happiness of the next.

Our Father is more willing to give spiritual blessings to those who ask than a loving human parent is to give food to a hungry child.

Faithful prayer quiets and steadies the heart in God.

## Jesus defeats the devil's power and warns against empty reform (verses 14–26)

Casting out demons showed Jesus **destroying the power of evil spirits**.

Henry compares an unconverted heart to a palace where Satan exercises authority.

There can be a counterfeit peace in such a heart: a sinner feels perfectly safe, has no doubt about spiritual standing and has no fear of coming judgment.

True conversion changes everything.

It is **Christ's victory over the devil's rule in a soul**, restoring freedom and bringing the mind and body into service to Christ.

But a hypocrite may have a house that looks swept and decorated without being cleansed.

A sinner may confess unwillingly like **Pharaoh**, show false sorrow like **Ahab**, or carry out only a partial reform like **Herod**.

Such changes sweep up loose dirt while leaving a favorite sin untouched.

Outward virtues, abilities and religious appearances are decorations, not **true grace**.

A heart never given to Christ and never inhabited by the Holy Spirit remains unrenewed.

We should never be satisfied with an outward religion that someone may possess while still falling short of heaven.

Without Christ, the evil spirits return readily and resume their power.

May we seek real deliverance from this dreadful condition.

## Those who obey God's word are blessed (verses 27 and 28)

The scribes and Pharisees insulted Jesus' teaching, but a woman marveled at the wisdom and power of his words.

Jesus directed her attention to a greater blessing.

Hearing God's word is a privilege.

But **only those who hear, remember and follow it as their rule of life** are truly blessed by the Lord.

## Jesus warns those who demand signs (verses 29–36)

Jesus promised the **sign of Jonah**, which Matthew explains in relation to **Christ's resurrection**.

Even if Jesus himself preached constantly and worked miracles every day in a congregation, the hearers would not profit unless divine grace humbled their hearts.

Do not demand more signs or teaching than God has chosen to give.

Rather, pray for opened hearts and understanding so that the light already received will benefit us.

Beware especially that your supposed **light is not really darkness**.

Wrong governing principles will corrupt both judgment and conduct.

## Jesus rebukes the Pharisees (verses 37–54)

We must examine our hearts and seek to have them **cleansed and renewed**.

While fulfilling the great demands of the law and gospel, we must not overlook smaller duties God has commanded.

When others watch our words, seeking an excuse to trap us, Lord, give us **prudence and patience**.

Defeat their malicious purposes, and grant us meekness enough to bear insults for Christ with joy, resting under the grace of the Holy Spirit.
