# Acts 22 — Modern English

## Paul describes his conversion (verses 1–11)

Paul addressed the enraged crowd with the customary courtesy and goodwill.

He described his earlier life carefully and made clear that his conversion was **entirely the work of God**.

Condemned sinners are struck blind by **the power of darkness**, and that blindness continues, as it did among those who rejected the gospel.

But sinners brought under conviction are temporarily blinded **not by darkness but by light**, as Paul was.

For a time they are bewildered and uncertain so that afterward they may receive spiritual understanding.

A straightforward account of how the Lord brought us from **opposing** the gospel to professing and promoting it, told in the right spirit, can move hearers more deeply than elaborate speeches.

Such an account does not itself prove every Christian truth, but the transformation in Paul's life provided striking confirmation of God's power.

## Paul is sent to the Gentiles (verses 12–21)

Paul went on to describe how his change of life was confirmed.

When the Lord chooses a sinner to know his will, he humbles and enlightens that person and leads them to **Christ and his blessed gospel**.

Jesus is called **the Righteous One**, for he is Jesus Christ the righteous.

Everyone called to understand God's will must look to Christ, through whom God has revealed his goodwill toward us.

The great gospel privilege **signified and sealed by baptism is the forgiveness of sins**.

When Paul was commanded to be baptized and wash away his sins, he was called to receive the comfort of pardon **through Jesus Christ**, take hold of Christ's righteousness and seek power to put his sinful desires to death.

**Do not stop at the outward sign.** Make sure you possess the cleansing from sin that it signifies.

The gospel duty to which baptism commits us is to seek forgiveness **in Christ's name and in dependence upon him and his righteousness**.

God appoints his workers both **their time and their place**.

They ought to follow his direction even when it contradicts their preferences.

Providence plans more wisely for us than we plan for ourselves.

When Christ sends someone, his Spirit accompanies that servant and enables them to see fruit from their labors.

Yet **no one is reconciled to the gospel except through God's special grace**.

## Paul appeals to his Roman citizenship (verses 22–30)

The crowd listened to Paul's account of his conversion until he mentioned his mission to the **Gentiles**.

Their national prejudices were so strong that they refused to hear more.

Their violent behavior astonished the Roman commander, who assumed that Paul must have committed a terrible crime.

Paul invoked his rights as **a Roman citizen**, exempting him from coercive examinations and punishments intended to force a confession.

His manner showed remarkable peace and confidence.

The commander was surprised that a Jew living in modest circumstances possessed such a valuable privilege; the commander had paid a great deal for his own citizenship.

Paul replied that he had been **born** with it.

Let us place far greater value on the freedom into which **God's children are born**, a liberty no amount of money can purchase for someone who remains unregenerate.

Paul's appeal immediately stopped the unlawful treatment.

Many people avoid sinful actions because they fear **human authorities**, although they would not be restrained by reverence for God.

Paul simply asked, **“Is it lawful?”**

He knew God could sustain him through any suffering genuinely required for Christ's sake.

But if the threatened suffering was unlawful and could properly be avoided, his faith directed him to avoid it.

**He never turned away from the cross his divine Master placed along his path; neither did he leave that path in search of an unnecessary cross.**
