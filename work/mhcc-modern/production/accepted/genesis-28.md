## Isaac sends Jacob away with a blessing (verses 1–5)

Jacob had received promises of blessing for this life and for the world to come.

Yet he was now leaving home for a life of hard work and service.

That was part of the correction he received for deceiving his father.

He would indeed receive the promised blessing, but he would also suffer for the dishonest way he had tried to obtain it.

Isaac sent Jacob away with a solemn command: he must not marry one of the daughters of Canaan.

Those who profess true faith should not marry people who have no concern for religion.

Isaac also gave him a solemn blessing.

Earlier he had blessed Jacob without knowing whom he was blessing. Now he did it deliberately.

This blessing was fuller than the first.

It was a gospel blessing, reaching beyond the earth toward heaven itself.

Canaan was a picture of that better heavenly country for which Jacob and the other patriarchs were looking.

## Esau takes another wife (verses 6–9)

Good examples can make an impression even on people who are ungodly and filled with bitterness.

Yet Esau thought he could make up for his other wrongs by pleasing his parents in one matter.

People whose hearts are controlled by worldly desires are quick to imagine that they are good enough merely because they have improved in one small respect.

They overlook the ways in which they continue doing wrong.

## Jacob dreams of the ladder (verses 10–15)

Jacob's conduct up to this point had hardly shown simple fear of God and trust in him.

But now he was troubled and forced to flee.

He looked to God alone for safety.

He could lie down in the open air with a stone for his pillow and sleep in peace.

Every true believer would gladly accept Jacob's hard pillow if it came with Jacob's vision.

God often visits his people with comfort when every other comfort and every other comforter has been taken away.

Jacob saw a ladder reaching from earth to heaven.

Angels were ascending and descending, and God himself stood above it.

This vision represents two things.

**First, God's providence.**

Through providence, fellowship and communication continue between heaven and earth.

Jacob could therefore know that he had both a faithful Guide and a strong Protector.

**Second, Christ's mediation.**

Christ himself is the ladder.

Its foot rests on earth, representing his human nature, while its top reaches heaven, representing his divine nature.

Christ is the Way.

Every blessing God gives us comes through him, and every act of service we offer to God comes through him too (John 1:51).

Through Christ, sinners may approach God's throne of grace and be accepted.

Faith enables us to see this way. Prayer enables us to draw near along it.

And God answers our prayers by supplying all the blessings of providence and grace that we need.

There is no other way for us to reach heaven except through Christ.

When faith grasps this truth, every place can become pleasant and every future prospect full of joy.

God will never leave his people until he has fulfilled his final promise to bring them into everlasting happiness.

The Lord spoke comfortingly to Jacob from the top of the ladder.

All the good news we receive from heaven comes through Jesus Christ.

The Messiah would descend from Jacob.

Christ is the great blessing of the world.

Everyone who receives God's blessing receives it in Christ. No family is excluded from that blessing except through its own refusal.

Jacob feared danger from his brother Esau, but God promised to protect him.

He faced a long journey into an unfamiliar country, but God said, “I am with you,” and promised to bring him back.

He seemed to have lost all his friends, but God assured him, “I will not leave you.”

God never abandons those whom he loves.

## Jacob sets up the stone at Bethel (verses 16–19)

God made himself and his favor known to Jacob while Jacob was asleep.

The Spirit, like the wind, blows wherever he chooses.

God's grace, like dew, does not wait for human beings.

Jacob tried to make good use of the visit he had received.

Wherever we are—in a city or desert, at home or in a field, at work or in the street—we can maintain communion with heaven, unless we ourselves neglect it.

And the more we see of God, the more reason we discover for holy reverence and trembling before him.

## Jacob makes a vow (verses 20–22)

Jacob made a solemn vow.

Notice, first, **his faith**.

He trusted God to accompany and protect him. He depended on the Lord's promise.

Notice, second, **his modest desires**.

He did not ask for luxurious clothing or expensive food.

If God gives us much, we should be thankful and use it for his service.

If he gives us little, we should be content and gladly enjoy God amid our limited possessions.

Notice, third, **his devotion**.

Above all, he wanted God to remain with him and keep him safe.

We need nothing more than that to make us truly secure and happy.

Jacob resolved to remain faithful to the Lord as his covenant God.

When God gives us extraordinary mercies, our gratitude should grow accordingly.

Giving a tenth is a fitting way to devote our resources to God and his work.

Yet the amount may be more or less, according to how God has prospered us (1 Corinthians 16:2).

Let us remember our own Bethels—the times when we made solemn promises to the Lord.

We have pledged ourselves to take him as our God and devote everything we have and everything we are to his glory.
