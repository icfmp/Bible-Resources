# Micah 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The sins and desolations of Israel. | 1–5 |
| Their evil practices. | 6–11 |
| A promise of restoration. | 12–13 |

## The people plan evil and exploit their neighbors (verses 1–5)

Woe to people who plan evil during the night and rise early to carry it out!

It is bad to hurt others through a sudden thought.

It is worse to act with **deliberation and careful planning**.

How important it is to use hours of solitude wisely!

When **greed rules the heart, compassion disappears**.

Once the heart is captured in that way, the hands soon practice **violence and fraud**.

People who are arrogant and complacent in prosperity are often the first to despair when adversity arrives.

Woe to those from whom God turns away!

Some of the most severe judgments are those that remove us from **the fellowship of God's people** or deprive us of the benefits of worship.

## God's word rejected; the world cannot give rest (verses 6–11)

The people told God's prophets **not to preach**.

God would let their own wish become their punishment.

Why should a physician continue visiting a patient who **refuses to be healed**?

Those who silence faithful ministers and obstruct access to the means of grace are enemies **not only of God but also of their country**.

If people feel no reverence for God's word, what can restrain their conduct?

Sinners should not expect lasting peace in a land they have polluted with wickedness.

They would be forced to leave it, and it would become the instrument of their ruin.

This also teaches us about **our present world**.

Sin has corrupted it.

We should avoid sharing in its corruption.

This world was designed as **a place to pass through**, not our final possession.

It is **our inn, not our home**.

We have no lasting city here.

Let us rise and seek **the enduring city above**.

People determined to be deceived will find teachers willing to deceive them.

Ministers who promote **self-indulgence through their teaching and example** will appeal to such listeners.

## The Lord opens a way for his people (verses 12–13)

These verses may refer to **the captivity of Israel and Judah** and their eventual return.

Henry also sees **a prophecy of Jewish conversion to Christ**.

The Lord would bring his people out of captivity and multiply them.

Jesus Christ would open their way to God by **taking on human nature** and, through the Holy Spirit, **breaking Satan's chains within their hearts**.

He leads his people, and they follow.

In his strength they overcome the spiritual enemies that would block their way **to heaven**.
