# Psalm 61 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David seeks God upon former experience. | 1–4 |
| He vows to serve God. | 5–8 |

## Seeking shelter on the higher Rock (verses 1–4)

David began with tears and prayer, but ended with praise. A soul lifted toward God may recover peace and a sense of its true condition.

Wherever we find ourselves, there is a way open to the throne of grace. When earthly comforts are withdrawn, their absence should turn us toward **God, the fountain of every comfort**.

An overwhelmed heart may still be lifted up in prayer. Indeed, the act of crying to God can sustain and relieve it. **Weeping should make us more earnest in prayer, not prevent prayer**.

God's power and promises are a rock higher than we can reach by ourselves. **That Rock is Christ**.

David was like a shipwrecked sailor tossed by waves at the foot of a rock so high that he could not climb it without help. Though he wanted to rest upon divine mercy, he knew he could not place himself securely on the Rock of salvation.

We therefore need God to **lead us to Christ and establish us in him**. In him there is safety; in ourselves there is none.

David resolved to make service to God his constant occupation. Everyone who expects the Lord to be a shelter and strong tower must do the same. God's grace will then be a continual source of comfort.

## Service grounded in everlasting promises (verses 5–8)

God has a people who fear his name, and a particular inheritance belongs to them. Even now they experience comfort in the soul, an advance pledge of happiness to come.

Those who fear God have enough in him and should not complain. We need desire no better inheritance than theirs.

Those who live before God, serve him and walk in reverent fear live to a good purpose in this world; they will dwell before him forever.

Henry also understands these words to refer especially to **the Messiah**, of whom the angel said that he would receive David's throne and reign without end (**Luke 1:32**).

The promises of God do not make prayer unnecessary. They **encourage and strengthen prayer**.

No protection is better than God's mercy and truth. Through the grace and truth that came by **Jesus Christ**, believers have reason to praise him whatever their outward circumstances.

A renewed experience of God's mercy and truth toward his people in Christ is therefore the chief reason for our joy and thanksgiving.
