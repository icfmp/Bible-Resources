# Isaiah 54 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The increase of the church by the conversion of the Jews and Gentiles. | 1–5 |
| Its certain deliverance. | 6–10 |
| Its triumphant state is described. | 11–17 |

## The Lord enlarges his church (verses 1–5)

For a long time before Christ came, true religion appeared to have little influence across the world.

But through **the preaching of the gospel**, multitudes turned from idols to **the living God**.

This is cause for great rejoicing throughout the church.

Its boundaries are extended.

Its condition on earth remains humble and changeable, like **a tent or tabernacle**.

But a growing family needs a larger dwelling, and the church should expect its tent to be enlarged.

The more numerous the church becomes, however, the more carefully she must defend herself against **false teaching and corruption**.

**Your Maker is your Husband.**

Christ is **the Holy One of Israel** and the Mediator of the covenant established with God's people under the Old Testament.

For ages he had been called **the God of Israel**.

Now he will be known as **the God of the whole earth**.

He cleanses his people from sin and causes every true believer to rejoice in this sacred union.

We cannot admire this mercy enough or adequately value the privilege.

## Mercy greater than temporary judgment (verses 6–10)

God is **slow to anger and quick to show mercy**.

How sweet is the renewed comfort of his mercy when he returns to console his people!

He gathers them not because they deserve it but because **he is merciful**.

His compassion is great, and his kindness **lasts forever**.

The wrath is little when compared with the mercy.

His anger lasts **a moment**, while his loving-kindness is **everlasting**.

We should neither collapse in despair under present affliction nor give up hoping for relief.

Mountains have been shaken and moved from their places.

But **none of God's promises has ever been broken** by the events of history.

Mountains and hills may also represent powerful and influential human beings.

The people and created supports in which we place confidence will eventually fail.

But when friends abandon us, **God remains faithful**.

This promise applies both to **the whole church** and to **individual believers**.

God may correct his people for their sins, but he will not finally cast them off.

Let these assurances motivate us to **make our calling and election sure**.

## God's people are taught, protected and justified (verses 11–17)

When God's people are afflicted and storm-tossed, let them hear his words as words of **personal consolation**, showing that he sees their sorrows and fears.

The church becomes glorious through **the knowledge of God**.

No teacher can compare with him.

The passage promises the **teaching and gifts of the Holy Spirit**.

Everyone whom God truly teaches learns **to love other people**.

Henry sees a special connection with the glorious period that will follow the church's present troubles.

More than outward splendor, **holiness is the church's true beauty**.

God promises his protection.

There will be neither inward terror nor outward attack able to overturn his purpose.

Soldiers may honor themselves with splendid titles.

But God calls some of them **“destroyers made to destroy”**, because destruction is their work.

He created them and can direct them to fulfill his own purposes.

The day is coming when he will call wicked people to account for every cruel word (**Jude 15**).

**Security and final victory** belong to every faithful servant of the Lord.

Both **the righteousness by which believers are justified** and **the grace through which they are sanctified** are gifts from God, resulting from his particular love.

Let us ask him to sanctify our souls and employ us in his service.
