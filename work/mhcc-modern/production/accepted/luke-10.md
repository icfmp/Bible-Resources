# Luke 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Seventy disciples sent forth. | 1–16 |
| The blessedness of Christ's disciples. | 17–24 |
| The good Samaritan. | 25–37 |
| Jesus at the house of Martha and Mary. | 38–42 |

## Jesus sends out the seventy (verses 1–16)

Jesus sent **seventy disciples**, two by two.

This allowed them to **encourage and strengthen one another**.

The gospel ministry calls people to receive Christ as **both Prince and Savior**.

He will come with the power of his Spirit to every place where he sends his faithful messengers.

Those who receive God's grace in vain face a dreadful judgment.

To despise, belittle or mock Christ's faithful ministers is ultimately to **despise God and Christ**, whose message they carry.

## The joy of Jesus and his disciples (verses 17–24)

Every victory over Satan comes through **power received from Jesus**.

He must receive all the praise.

Beware of **spiritual pride**, which has destroyed many.

Jesus rejoiced in the prospect of many people being saved.

The Gospel particularly records this moment of joy.

There were relatively few such moments for him, because **he was a Man of Sorrows**.

Yet in the hour he learned of the disciples' success and saw Satan's power being defeated, he rejoiced.

God resists the proud and gives grace to the humble.

The more simply we depend upon the **Son of God** for teaching, help and blessing, the more we will know both Father and Son.

We will enjoy the sight of our Savior's glory and the hearing of his words and become more useful in advancing his cause.

## Jesus tells the parable of the good Samaritan (verses 25–37)

We must never speak carelessly about **eternal life and the way to receive it**.

Such careless speech dishonors God.

No one can love God and neighbor with pure spiritual love without **God's converting grace**.

The proud human heart resists this truth.

Jesus described a Jewish traveler robbed, wounded and left nearly dead.

He was neglected by people who should have been his friends.

He received help instead from **a Samaritan**, someone belonging to a nation Jews deeply despised.

How sorrowful to see selfishness in people of every social rank!

How many excuses do we make to avoid trouble or expense in helping someone else?

The genuine Christian has **the law of love written on the heart**.

The Spirit of Christ lives within, renewing Christ's image in the soul.

This parable explains **loving our neighbors as ourselves** without regard to nation, faction or other divisions.

It also illustrates **the kindness of God our Savior toward sinful and miserable people**.

We resemble the wounded traveler.

Satan, our enemy, has robbed and harmed us through sin.

The blessed Jesus looked on us with compassion.

He loved us and gave his life for us **while we were his enemies and rebels**.

Having shown us mercy, he commands us to **go and do likewise**.

Each of us, according to our opportunities and abilities, must help people who are in distress and need.

## Jesus visits Martha and Mary (verses 38–42)

A good sermon is not made worse simply because it is preached in a private home.

Visits among friends should be arranged so they contribute to the good of people's souls.

To sit at Jesus' feet means readiness to **receive his word and submit to its direction**.

Martha was preparing food and hospitality for Jesus and those with him.

Her care expressed genuine respect for her Lord and proper concern for her household.

Yet her conduct also deserved correction.

She wanted **much serving**: plenty of food, variety and careful attention to every detail.

Earthly responsibilities become a trap when they keep us from serving God or receiving spiritual good.

Even when hosting fellow believers, we can waste needless time and money on elaborate arrangements.

**Martha was wrong on this occasion, but she remained a genuine believer.**

Henry does not claim that her ordinary life neglected the one essential thing.

God's favor is necessary for happiness; Christ's salvation is necessary for safety.

When these are our first concerns, we can attend to other matters properly.

Mary chose the good portion by submitting herself to Christ's instruction.

The things of this world will eventually be taken from us, or we will be taken from them.

But **nothing can separate us from Christ's love** and the share we possess in it.

Neither human beings nor demons can take it away; God and Christ will not take it away.

Let us therefore give the one essential thing our deepest attention.
