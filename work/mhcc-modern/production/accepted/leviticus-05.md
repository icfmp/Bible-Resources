# Leviticus 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Different kinds of wrongdoing (verses 1–13)

Several offenses are addressed here.

First, a witness sins by concealing the truth when under oath to tell it fully. A person may be afraid of offending a friend or an enemy and therefore say nothing or reveal only part of the facts. But that person bears guilt. Unless something is done to remove it, guilt is a burden that leads to destruction.

Anyone summoned to give evidence should remember this law, speak openly and without deception, and treat an oath before the Lord as sacred.

Second, a person might touch something ceremonially unclean. The contact itself caused ceremonial defilement; but refusing to wash in accordance with God's law showed carelessness or contempt, and brought moral guilt.

When God's Spirit makes us aware of a sin or duty, we should respond promptly, without being ashamed to admit we were mistaken.

Third, a rash oath could bind a person to do something unlawful or impossible. Thoughtfulness and caution before speaking would prevent many such difficulties.

In these cases the sinner was required to confess the particular offense and bring the appointed sacrifice.

The sacrifice was not acceptable without confession and humble prayer for forgiveness.

It is not enough to admit vaguely that we are sinners. Everyone can say that, while remaining reluctant to confess any specific sin. Such vague language can conceal deceit.

Honest, detailed confession is the path toward assurance of pardon and greater watchfulness against future sins.

Even the very poor could bring flour for their sin offering. The cost was deliberately kept low so that poverty would never prevent anyone from seeking forgiveness.

When two birds were offered, one was a sin offering and the other a burnt offering. We must first be reconciled to God before expecting him to accept our services for his glory.

Unlike other grain offerings, the flour offered for sin received neither oil nor frankincense. Its lack of pleasant taste or fragrance was intended to show how hateful sin is.

These sacrifices assured repentant sinners that forgiveness was available, so they need not despair under guilt. They also warned against further sin: atonement was costly and required serious effort.

## Wrongdoing involving what belongs to the Lord (verses 14–19)

This section concerns trespasses connected with obligations toward God, including the unintentional misuse of something dedicated to him.

When we suspect that we have wronged God or someone else, we should be ready both to ask forgiveness and to make restitution, even before every detail of our fault is clear to us.

God's law extends very far. Opportunities to sin are numerous, and we are naturally inclined toward wrong. We should therefore stay watchful, consider our steps and pray continually to be kept from sin.

A true Christian acknowledges guilt before God each day and seeks pardon through the blood of Christ.

The gospel offers salvation freely enough for the poorest to receive it, and fully enough for even a heavily burdened conscience to find relief.

Yet the seriousness of sin is also exposed through that salvation. Everyone who has received pardon should learn to hate and fear sin.
