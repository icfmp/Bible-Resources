# Jeremiah 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A drought upon the land of Judah. | 1–7 |
| A confession of sin in the name of the people. | 8–9 |
| The Divine purpose to punish is declared. | 10–16 |
| The people supplicate. | 17–22 |

## Drought and prayerful confession (verses 1–9)

The people wept, but theirs was more **a cry over distress and sin** than the cry of genuine prayer.

Let us be thankful for water before its absence teaches us how precious it is.

Farmers depend completely on **God's Providence**: unless he waters their furrows, they cannot plow and sow with confidence.

Even wild animals suffered pitiably under the drought.

The people were slow to pray, but **Jeremiah prayed for them** and humbly confessed their sin.

Our sins both **accuse us and testify against us**.

The strongest arguments in our prayers are those drawn from **the glory of God's own name**.

We should dread **his departure** more than the loss of any created comfort.

God had given Israel **his word on which to hope**.

In prayer, his glory should concern us more than our immediate relief.

If we return to the Lord, he will save us **to the glory of his grace**.

## False prophets and their hearers face judgment (verses 10–16)

The Lord called the Jews **“this people”** rather than **“my people.”** They had abandoned his service; he would judge them according to their sins.

God forbade Jeremiah to intercede for them.

The false prophets were **most guilty**, and the Lord pronounced judgment upon them.

But the people themselves **wanted to hear their false assurances**, so they too would face judgment.

False teachers lead people to expect peace and salvation **without repentance, faith, conversion or holiness of life**.

Anyone who willingly believes a lie cannot offer deception as an excuse.

People who reject the warning will experience the very judgment they were told not to fear.

## The prophet pleads for the covenant and mercy (verses 17–22)

Jeremiah confessed **his own sins and the people's sins**, appealing to the Lord's covenant.

No Gentile idol could help them.

Even the heavens could not give rain **by themselves** without their Creator.

God will always preserve people who plead before **his mercy seat**.

He will heal **every genuinely repentant sinner**.

Even when God does not grant the national relief for which we pray, he will certainly bless with salvation everyone who **confesses sin and seeks his mercy**.

## Editorial review — Jeremiah 14

**Whole secondary original-English witness read:** [CCEL, Jeremiah 14](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xxv.xiv.html). The released `MHCC 0.1.1` remains authoritative for exact native-SWORD text, addresses and independent acceptance.

**Source structure anomaly:** the printed chapter outline has **four** groups (**1–7; 8–9; 10–16; 17–22**), but the actual source prose has **three** groups, the first labeled **Jeremiah 14:1–9**. This manuscript preserves both without inventing a fourth exposition unit.

- **1–9:** drought and **water/farming/wild beasts**, reluctant people and praying prophet, sin testifying against itself, **God's own glory above comfort**, repentance and grace — retained.
- **10–16:** **“this people” instead of “my people”**, no intercession, false prophets most guilty but willing hearers accountable, **false peace without faith/repentance/holiness** — retained.
- **17–22:** **personal and corporate confession**, covenant, idols and heavens powerless without God, all true penitents healed even if national relief is withheld — retained.
