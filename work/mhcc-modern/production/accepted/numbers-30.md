# Numbers 30 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Vows to be kept. | 1, 2 |
| The cases wherein vows might be released. | 3–16 |

## The obligation to fulfill a lawful vow (verses 1 and 2)

No human being can become obligated by a personal promise to do something that God's command already forbids.

A vow cannot make wrongdoing lawful.

In other matters, the rule is straightforward: we must not break our word.

We may change our minds later, but that does not release us from a valid promise.

## Cases in which a vow could be canceled (verses 3–16)

The law describes two circumstances in which another member of the household could confirm or cancel a vow.

The first concerns a daughter living in her father's home. When the father learns of her vow, he may allow it to stand or cancel it.

The second concerns a wife.

If the husband approves the vow, **even merely by remaining silent**, the vow stands.

If he objects, the marriage obligation takes precedence under the household order established by this law, and the vow is canceled.

Henry understands this provision in terms of the authority of husbands and fathers within the family. In his explanation, a wife is to be subject to her husband as to the Lord, and a man is to govern his household.

Rather than allowing a religious vow to break the family bonds and authority he believes God appointed, Henry argues that divine law releases the person from that vow.

He concludes that God's ordinances are intended to preserve the welfare of families and societies, so that the families of the earth may enjoy blessing.
