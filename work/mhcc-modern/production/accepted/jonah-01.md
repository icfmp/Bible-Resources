# Jonah 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jonah, sent to Nineveh, flees to Tarshish. | 1–3 |
| He is stayed by a tempest. | 4–7 |
| His discourse with the mariners. | 8–12 |
| He is cast into the sea, and miraculously preserved. | 13–17 |

## Jonah flees from his calling (verses 1–3)

How sad it is to think of the great amount of sin committed in the world's large cities!

The wickedness of **Nineveh** was an open and defiant insult to God.

Jonah was commanded to go there **immediately** and warn the people publicly.

But he refused.

Few of us can honestly claim we would have eagerly accepted such a dangerous mission without hesitation.

Jonah found what looked like **a providential opportunity to escape**.

But a person can be outside the path of duty and still receive **a favorable wind**.

**The easiest route is not always the right one.**

See how even the best human beings can act when God leaves them to themselves.

When we hear God's commands, we also need **the Holy Spirit to bring our thoughts into submission to his will**.

## The storm awakens the sailors (verses 4–7)

God sent **a terrible storm** in pursuit of Jonah.

Sin brings storms into **souls, households, churches and whole nations**.

It disturbs and destroys peace.

The sailors cried out to their gods and also did everything they could to save the ship.

If only people showed as much wisdom about their souls!

They would gladly part with the **wealth, pleasure and honor** they cannot keep without destroying faith, conscience and ultimately salvation.

Meanwhile, **Jonah slept deeply**.

Sin makes people numb and careless.

We must beware of hearts hardened by **its deceitfulness**.

What are sinners thinking when they sleep on despite the warnings of **Scripture and conscience**?

We should awaken one another and encourage everyone to **call on God for deliverance**.

The sailors concluded that the disaster had some moral cause.

Whatever affliction we experience, we should ask the Lord **what we need to learn from it**.

The lot fell upon Jonah.

God has many ways of uncovering sins and sinners that everyone imagined were safely hidden.

## Jonah confesses before the sailors (verses 8–12)

Jonah explained his faith.

Henry hopes he did so with **sorrow and shame**, confessing his guilt and honoring God's justice.

He taught the sailors about **Jehovah, the Maker of sea and land**.

They challenged him:

If he feared the Creator of land and sea, why had he imagined he could flee from God's presence?

When people who profess religion act wrongly, even those who do not profess it may justly rebuke them.

When sin has stirred a storm, we must address **the sin that caused it**.

Jonah spoke like a truly repentant person who does not want other people to suffer because of personal folly.

He accepted the sentence and acknowledged God's justice.

A guilty conscience will find no genuine peace until it **abandons the sin** that has troubled it.

Simply surrendering **money or possessions** cannot cleanse the conscience.

In the image of this account, **Jonah himself must be thrown overboard**.

## Jonah is thrown into the sea and preserved (verses 13–17)

The sailors tried rowing against **the wind of God's displeasure and the current of his purpose**.

But it was useless to seek safety without dealing with the cause of the judgment.

Even an ordinary human conscience naturally fears **becoming guilty of another person's death**.

When God leads us through his Providence, we ought to be satisfied with his wise purposes, even when they are difficult for us to accept.

The storm stopped when Jonah was cast into the sea.

God does not discipline forever.

His correction lasts until his purpose is fulfilled and his people submit to him.

The pagan sailors may rise in judgment against many people called Christians who offer **neither prayer in distress nor thanksgiving after deliverance**.

The Lord commands every creature.

He can make any of them an instrument of **mercy toward his people**.

How wonderful his power in preserving a drowning man, and how great his pity in preserving one who had fled from and offended him!

It was because of the Lord's mercy that Jonah was not destroyed.

He remained alive in the fish for **three days and three nights**.

This was impossible by ordinary natural means, but **everything is possible for the God who rules nature**.

Jonah's preservation made him **a type of Christ**, as Jesus expressly taught in **Matthew 12:40**.
