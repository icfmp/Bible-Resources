# Romans 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Believers must die to sin, and live to God. | 1, 2 |
| This is urged by their Christian baptism and union with Christ. | 3–10 |
| They are made alive to God. | 11–15 |
| And are freed from the dominion of sin. | 16–20 |
| The end of sin is death, and of holiness everlasting life. | 21–23 |

## Believers are dead to sin (verses 1 and 2)

Paul strongly insists upon **the necessity of holiness**.

He does not weaken or explain away the gospel's free grace.

Instead, he demonstrates that **justification and holiness are inseparably joined**.

We should reject with abhorrence any suggestion that believers ought to continue sinning so that grace may increase.

Genuine believers have died to sin; therefore they ought not continue living under its rule.

The same person cannot be dead to sin and at the same time alive to it as a chosen way of life.

Someone who wishes to be dead to sin but assumes that continual indulgence is permitted is deceiving themselves.

## Baptism signifies death and resurrection with Christ (verses 3–10)

Baptism teaches the need to **die to sin**, to be buried as it were to ungodly and unholy pursuits, and to rise to **new life with God**.

Unholy professing Christians may have received the outward sign of death to sin and a new birth into righteousness.

Yet they have never genuinely passed from **Satan's family to God's family**.

The fallen nature called **the old man**—inherited from humanity's first father Adam—is crucified with Christ in every true believer, through grace flowing from the cross.

That sinful nature is weakened and dying.

Nevertheless, it continues to struggle for life and even for victory.

But the whole **body of sin**, everything opposed to God's holy law, must finally be removed.

Believers are no longer to be enslaved by sin.

Instead, they live to God and find happiness in serving him.

## Alive to God, resist sin's remaining impulses (verses 11–15)

Paul provides some of the strongest reasons for avoiding sin and pursuing holiness.

Believers are freed from sin's reign, alive to God and expecting everlasting life.

It is therefore right for them to be deeply concerned about growing in holiness.

Sinful desires are **not entirely uprooted during this earthly life**.

Christians must resist their impulses, striving earnestly through divine grace so those desires do not gain victory while they live in mortal bodies.

Remember that this struggle will one day end.

That knowledge should encourage believers who are often deeply troubled by the impulses of sin.

Offer **every ability and every part of the body** to God as instruments or weapons ready for the work and warfare of righteousness.

God's **covenant of grace** provides strength.

**Sin shall not rule over you.**

God's promises are more powerful in putting sin to death than the promises we make to God.

Sin may struggle in genuine believers and cause them deep distress, but it will not reign over them.

It may trouble them without governing them.

Does such an encouraging promise permit someone to practice sin deliberately?

Certainly not.

Such an idea contradicts God's perfection and the very purpose of his gospel.

What stronger reason can we have to resist sin than **Christ's love**?

How could we sin against such goodness?

## Sin and obedience are two different kinds of service (verses 16–20)

Everyone serves the master whose commands they obey.

That master may be **the sinful desires of the heart**, which lead to death.

Or it may be **the new spiritual obedience planted by regeneration**, which leads to righteousness.

Paul rejoiced that the believers had obeyed the gospel **from their hearts**.

They had been placed into its teaching like metal into a mold.

Just as melted metal becomes a new vessel when cast into a different form, believers become **new creations**.

Service to Christ brings genuine freedom of mind and spirit, completely unlike slavery to sin.

Christians recognize their rightful Lord as **their Father** and themselves as **his adopted children and heirs**.

Sin's dominion consists in **willing slavery**.

It does not consist merely in being attacked by a hated power that the believer resists.

Those now serving God once served sin.

## Sin's wages and God's gift (verses 21–23)

The apparent pleasure and advantage of sin do not deserve the name **fruit**.

Sinners plow iniquity, sow emptiness and reap what they have planted.

Shame entered the world through sin, and shame continues to follow it.

**Sin ends in death**.

Its path may seem pleasant at first, but the end is bitter.

Believers are freed from that condemnation.

Where genuine and growing grace produces **fruit leading to holiness**, the outcome is **everlasting life**.

This is a blessed end.

The path may climb uphill through a narrow, thorny road, yet life awaits at its end.

**Eternal life is God's gift through Jesus Christ our Lord**.

Christ purchased it, prepared it, prepares us for it and preserves us until we receive it.

**He is everything in our salvation**.
