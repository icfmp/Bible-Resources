# Matthew 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Death of John the Baptist. | 1–12 |
| Five thousand people miraculously fed. | 13–21 |
| Jesus walks upon the sea. | 22–33 |
| Jesus healing the sick. | 34–36 |

## John the Baptist is killed (verses 1–12)

Herod could not silence the accusations of his conscience.

The fear and shame troubling him were warnings of **a future judgment**.

But **being frightened by conviction is not the same as being converted**.

People who profess to favor the gospel while continuing in wickedness must not be encouraged in their self-deception.

Faithful ministers should tell them the truth, as John did.

The world may condemn such rebuke as harshness, fanaticism or a lack of courtesy.

False professors of religion and timid believers may agree.

Yet the most powerful enemies can go no further than **the Lord permits**.

Herod feared that John's execution would trigger a popular rebellion. It did not.

He did not fear that it would stir up his own conscience. It did.

People sometimes fear **human punishment** for deeds they scarcely fear **divine condemnation** for committing.

Occasions of excessive amusement and pleasure often provide opportunities for wicked schemes against the godly.

Herod gave a lavish reward for an empty dance while giving imprisonment and death to the prophet who sought his soul's salvation.

Henry judges that beneath Herod's hesitation lay real malice.

Otherwise, he could have found a way to avoid fulfilling his rash and wicked promise.

When faithful **under-shepherds** are killed, the flock need not be lost as long as its people may go to **the Great Shepherd**.

It is better to be drawn to Christ through suffering and loss than never to come to him at all.

## Jesus feeds five thousand (verses 13–21)

When Christ and his word seem to withdraw, we should follow him, seeking spiritual instruction and God's grace before worldly advantage.

The presence of Christ and his gospel can make even a wilderness not merely bearable but desirable.

Jesus multiplied a small quantity of bread through **his creative power** until the great crowd was satisfied.

If we truly care about people's souls, we should have compassion on their bodies too.

We should seek God's blessing at meals.

We should avoid waste, since **frugality makes generosity possible**.

This miracle also represents Jesus, **the Bread of Life who came from heaven** to sustain perishing souls.

To worldly observers the provisions of the gospel can seem inadequate.

But everyone who receives Christ by faith with thanksgiving finds him fully sufficient.

## Jesus walks on water and rescues Peter (verses 22–33)

A person who cannot be alone with God and with personal thoughts is not living as a true follower of Christ should.

At special times, especially when prayer becomes a joy and our hearts are open before God, extended private prayer is valuable.

Christ's disciples may encounter storms while faithfully doing their duty.

Through these troubles Jesus displays his grace more fully for them.

He can deliver his people **by any means he chooses**.

Sometimes even the apparent approach of help frightens believers because they misunderstand Christ's actions.

Those who have Christ near them and know they belong to him need not fear even death itself.

Peter walked upon the waves not for entertainment or personal glory but **to reach Jesus**.

Christ sustained him in this pursuit.

Exceptional support may be expected in carrying out spiritual duties, not in performing needless demonstrations of courage.

We cannot approach Christ unless his power upholds us.

Jesus told Peter to come partly to show his Lord's power, partly to reveal Peter's own weakness.

God sometimes allows his servants to make choices that expose their need and make divine grace clearer.

When we turn our eyes away from Christ toward our difficulties, we begin to sink.

When we call to him, he reaches out to save us.

Those who would be saved must come to Christ and cry to him.

Often only the realization that we are sinking makes us understand our need.

Jesus rebuked Peter for doubting.

Greater faith would free us from many needless fears.

Weak faith and persistent doubt displease Christ, for he is present to help his disciples even in the fiercest storm.

Only **the world's Creator** could multiply the loaves.

Only **its Governor** could walk upon the sea.

The disciples recognized what the miracles revealed and worshiped him.

Whoever comes to God must believe; whoever truly believes comes to him (**Hebrews 11:6**).

## Jesus heals the sick at Gennesaret (verses 34–36)

Wherever Jesus went, he did good.

The people brought every kind of sick person to him and asked humbly for help.

The experiences of others may encourage and guide us in seeking Christ.

Everyone who touched him was fully healed.

**When Christ heals, the healing is complete.**

If more people understood both who he is and how sick their souls are, they would eagerly seek his healing power.

The cure was not produced by the fingers touching the garment.

It was associated with their **faith**—or, more precisely, it came from **Christ himself, whom faith reached for**.
