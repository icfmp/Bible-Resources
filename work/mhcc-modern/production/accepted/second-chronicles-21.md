# 2 Chronicles 21 — Modern English

## Jehoram's wicked reign (verses 1–11)

Jehoram hated his brothers and killed them for the same reason **Cain hated and killed Abel**: their godliness condemned his ungodliness.

In the mysterious workings of Providence, such wicked people sometimes prosper for a time.

Yet God has righteous reasons for permitting those events.

We can understand some of those purposes now; the rest will become clear in the future.

## Jehoram's miserable death (verses 12–20)

God sent Jehoram a warning.

The Spirit of prophecy may have directed **Elijah** to write it in advance, foreseeing Jehoram's crimes.

Jehoram was told plainly that his sins would bring ruin.

It is no surprise that sinners ignore warnings of punishment in the world to come, when even the certainty of suffering in this life—losing possessions or health—does not make them abandon wicked practices.

Jehoram lost every comfort.

God made clear that he was confronting both the king and his household.

Jehoram had killed his brothers to make himself secure.

Now all but one of his sons were killed.

Yet David's family would not be destroyed entirely as many northern royal families were, because **the promise of the Messiah** belonged to David's line.

Good people may also suffer disease, but for them illness is **fatherly correction**.

God's comfort can keep the soul at rest even while the body is in pain.

To be ill and poor or ill and lonely is distressing.

But to be ill **while living in sin, under God's judgment and without grace to endure it** is far more terrible.

Wickedness and irreverence make people contemptible even to those with little religion themselves.
