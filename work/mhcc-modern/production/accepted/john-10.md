## The sheep and their shepherd (verses 1–5)

Jesus used an illustration from the way shepherds cared for sheep in the East.

As creatures dependent on their Creator, people are the sheep of God's pasture.

The church is like a sheepfold in the world, exposed to false teachers and persecutors.

The great Shepherd knows everyone who belongs to him. He protects them through his providence, directs them through **his Spirit and word**, and leads them as shepherds who walk ahead of their flocks.

Ministers must care for the sheep in their spiritual needs. The Spirit of Christ will open the appropriate door before them.

Christ's sheep learn to recognize their Shepherd and are cautious toward strangers who would draw them away from faith in Jesus into **speculative notions about him**.

## Jesus, the door (verses 6–9)

Many people fail to understand Jesus' teaching because they are **unwilling** to understand it.

One part of Scripture helps explain another, and the Holy Spirit makes the Lord Jesus known.

Christ is the **Door**. What protection could be greater for God's people than having the Lord Jesus between the church and its enemies?

He opens the way of access and fellowship. His instructions for entering the fold are clear: we come **through faith in Jesus Christ as the one Mediator between God and humanity**.

Precious promises belong to those who enter that way.

As a good shepherd cares for a flock, Christ cares for his church and each believer. In return, he calls his people to depend on him and remain in his pasture.

## Jesus, the good Shepherd who lays down his life (verses 10–18)

Christ is a good Shepherd. Some other shepherds were not thieves, yet through negligence they caused considerable harm to their flocks.

Wrong principles often produce wrong conduct.

Jesus knows those he has chosen and is certain of them. His people know the One they have trusted and are confident in him.

Here is the grace of Christ: no one could demand his life from him. He **freely laid it down for our redemption**.

He willingly came to be our Savior. Because our need required a sacrifice, he offered himself.

He is **both the priest who offers and the offering itself**.

His voluntary death was a death **in the place of sinners**, securing freedom from sin's deserved punishment and obtaining forgiveness.

Jesus did not merely die to defend a doctrine. **He died for his sheep**.

## Different reactions to Jesus (verses 19–21)

Satan ruins many people by making them ashamed of God's word and appointed worship.

People would not usually let someone ridicule them into giving up the food needed to remain alive. Yet they allow ridicule to deprive them of spiritual nourishment, which is even more necessary.

If serving Christ earnestly, especially bringing his sheep into the fold, earns us insulting names, let us not be discouraged.

Our Master endured such insults before us.

## Jesus at the Feast of Dedication (verses 22–30)

Everyone who has a concern to bring to Christ may find him in the temple.

Jesus seeks to lead us to believe, but often **we create our own doubts**.

His opponents understood the claim he was making, though they could not turn his words into a charge they could sustain.

Christ described the character and blessed condition of his sheep.

They **hear and believe his word**, follow him as disciples and are promised that **none of them will perish**.

The Son and the Father are **one**.

Therefore Jesus can defend his people against every enemy, which shows he claimed **divine power and perfection equal to the Father's**.

## The attempt to stone Jesus (verses 31–38)

Christ's works of mercy and power proclaim that he is **God over all, blessed forever**.

They invite people to believe that **the Father is in the Son and the Son is in the Father**.

The One whom the Father sends, he sets apart as holy. The holy God will not choose people for his service without fitting them for it through holiness.

The Father's presence in the Son accounts for Jesus' miracles of divine power.

The Son's presence in the Father means Jesus knew the whole mind of the Father.

We cannot fully understand this mystery through searching, but we can **know and believe what Jesus has declared**.

## Jesus withdraws from Jerusalem (verses 39–42)

No weapon formed against Jesus can accomplish what God forbids.

He escaped not because he was afraid to suffer, but because **his appointed hour had not yet come**.

The One who could deliver himself can also deliver godly people out of temptation and provide a way of escape.

Persecutors may drive Christ and his gospel out of their own town or country. They cannot drive him or his gospel **out of the world**.

When we know Christ by faith, we discover that everything Scripture says about him is true.
