# Psalm 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Threatenings against the enemies of Christ's kingdom. | 1–6 |
| Promise to Christ as the Head of this kingdom. | 7–9 |
| Counsel to all, to espouse its interests. | 10–12 |

## The enemies of Christ's kingdom (verses 1–6)

The Psalm describes those who oppose Christ.

The present world is under Satan's rule, and people without saving grace, of every social position, party and character, can be stirred by him against God's cause.

Earthly rulers have often been particularly active in this opposition.

Christian truth and the commands of Christ expose the ambitions and selfish desires of worldly power.

His opponents want to cast off the **restraints of conscience** and the **commands of God**, treating them as chains to be broken.

Yet they can give no good reason for rejecting a government so righteous and holy that, if everyone received it, it would make earthly life resemble heaven.

Their resistance cannot succeed against so powerful a kingdom.

**Jesus Christ possesses all authority in heaven and on earth**.

He is Head over everything for the church, whatever plans his enemies make.

His throne is established within his church, **in the hearts of believers**.

## The Father's promise to the Son (verses 7–9)

The Messiah's kingdom rests upon the eternal decree of God the Father.

Jesus himself referred to that decree and conducted his mission accordingly.

God declared to him, **“You are my Son.”**

Each of us should answer, **“You are my Lord and Sovereign.”**

When the Son asks for the nations as his inheritance, he seeks **their happiness in him**.

He intercedes for them continually.

He lives forever to do this and can save completely everyone who comes to God through him.

Multitudes among the nations will become his willing and loyal subjects.

Christians belong to Jesus Christ.

They bring him honor and praise.

The Father gives them to the Son by working through the Spirit and grace so that they gladly submit to Jesus.

## The call to trust and serve Christ (verses 10–12)

Whatever we enjoy in this world, we should rejoice with reverence, because every earthly possession and circumstance is uncertain.

Welcoming Jesus Christ and submitting to him is both our duty and our greatest good.

Let him become precious to us.

Love him sincerely and deeply, as the woman who had been forgiven much showed her love by **kissing his feet** (**Luke 7:38**).

Greet the Son with a **kiss of loyalty**.

Accept his rule, submit to his laws, entrust your circumstances to his Providence and give yourselves entirely to his cause.

Unbelief rejects **the only remedy for sin**.

It will bring destruction to those who persist in it.

They will perish in their sinful path, in their empty hopes and in the loss of the true way to happiness.

**Christ is the way to God**.

People may suppose they are walking the right path, but if they reject Christ they will lose it.

Blessed are those who have made Christ their **Refuge** through faith.

They will be safe even on the day of divine wrath.
