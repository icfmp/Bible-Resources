# Joshua 18 — Modern English

## The tabernacle is established at Shiloh (verse 1)

Shiloh lay within the territory of Ephraim, Joshua's own tribe. It was fitting for the tabernacle to be near the chief governor's home.

The city's name was the same as the name used in Jacob's prophecy about the Messiah (Genesis 49:10).

Some have suggested that the city received this name when it was chosen as the resting place of the ark. The ark pictured our great Peacemaker and the way he opens for us to approach a God reconciled to us.

## The remaining land is surveyed (verses 2–10)

After a year or more, Joshua rebuked the people's delay and told them how to proceed.

Through his grace, God has given us a claim to a good land—the heavenly Canaan. Yet we are often slow to take possession of what he offers.

We fail to enter fully into the rest that we might already enjoy through **faith, hope and holy joy**.

How long will we continue hindering our own happiness and exchanging God's mercies for empty, deceitful things?

Joshua urged the Israelites to take possession of the lands assigned to them. He was prepared to do his part if they would do theirs.

## Benjamin's boundaries (verses 11–28)

Each tribe's portion had its boundaries drawn clearly and its inheritance settled.

God's wise appointment prevented arguments and selfish demands.

He distributed the hills and valleys, fields of grain and pasture, streams and rivers, towns and cities.

If a servant of Christ receives a portion of affliction and sorrow, it comes under the Lord's authority. Let the Lord do what seems good to him.

If we receive prosperity and peace, they also come from above.

We should be humbled when we compare our gifts with how little we deserve them.

Never forget the One who gives good things, and always be willing to surrender them when he commands.
