# Psalm 64 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Prayer for deliverance. | 1–6 |
| The destruction of the wicked, encouragement to the righteous. | 7–10 |

## Prayer against dangerous speech and plots (verses 1–6)

The psalmist earnestly asked God to preserve him from **paralyzing fear**.

The tongue is a small part of the body, but it boasts of great things and can do terrible harm.

Upright people become targets of the wicked, who refuse to speak peacefully either about them or to them.

No human precaution can fully guard against a false tongue.

Doing wrong is evil, but **encouraging oneself and others to do wrong is even worse**.

A heart firmly committed to wrongdoing shows how deeply it has hardened.

At the root of all wickedness lies a practical denial of **God's complete knowledge**.

The greatest comfort of **a good cause and a clear conscience** often appears when no person can rescue us from our enemies and **God alone** remains our help.

He is always present to save.

## The Lord judges evil and vindicates the innocent (verses 7–10)

When God turns upon wrongdoers the mischief they planned for others, their own schemes become a dreadful judgment against them.

Those who delight in cursing will find that curses return upon them.

Observers should recognize God's hand in these events; otherwise they fail to learn from the workings of Providence.

The righteous rejoice **in the Lord**, not because fellow human beings suffer or perish, but because **God is glorified, his word fulfilled and innocence effectively defended**.

They do not base their joy on people, their own cleverness, strength, riches, morality or any created pleasure.

They rejoice **in Christ**, in whom God's true people are justified and may glory, and in everything he has done and continues to be for them.
