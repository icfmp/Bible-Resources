# Psalm 109 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David complains of his enemies. | 1–5 |
| He prophesies their destruction. | 6–20 |
| Prayers and praises. | 21–31 |

## Turning to God when enemies accuse us (verses 1–5)

It is an inexpressible comfort to believers that **God is for them**, whatever enemies oppose them.

They may appeal to him with confidence because he willingly cares for their cause.

David's enemies ridiculed his devotion, but they could not make him abandon it.

## A prophetic warning of judgment (verses 6–20)

Henry understands these denunciations as words that **the Lord Jesus may speak in his role as Judge**, announcing the sentence awaiting certain enemies in order to warn others.

When people reject Christ's salvation, even their prayers may become sinful.

What leads some people to shameful deaths and causes others' families and property to collapse?

What brings disgrace, poverty, misery and ruin upon their descendants, making them and their families despised and hateful to others?

**Sin**—that destructive and poisonous evil—is the source of such devastation.

Consider the effect of the final sentence **“Go, you cursed”** upon the bodies and souls of the unrepentant.

What fear, pain, horror, anguish and despair it brings!

Sinners, take these warnings seriously: **tremble and repent**.

## Praying for mercy and trusting the risen Advocate (verses 21–31)

The psalmist appealed to God's comfort in great humility.

He was distressed in mind.

His body had grown thin and weak.

Yet it is better to have a weak body with **a spiritually healthy soul** than to feast the body while starving the soul.

He was mocked and insulted by his enemies.

But if God blesses us, we need not fear human curses.

People cannot place a curse upon those whom God has chosen to bless.

The psalmist pleaded for **God's glory and the honor of his name**.

Save me, Lord—not because I deserve it, for I claim no merit—but **because of your mercy**.

He ended with the joyful confidence of faith, convinced his present struggle would end in victory.

Everyone who suffers according to God's will should commit their souls to his care.

**Jesus**, unjustly put to death and now risen, is **the Advocate and Intercessor of his people**.

He is always ready to appear on their behalf against a corrupt world and the great accuser.
