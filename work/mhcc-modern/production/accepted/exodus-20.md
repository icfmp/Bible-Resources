## The preface to the Ten Commandments (verses 1–2)

God speaks to human beings in many ways: through conscience, through the events of his providence, and through his word.

We should listen carefully to them all.

But he never spoke in quite the same way as when he gave the Ten Commandments.

This law had already been given to humanity, written upon the human heart.

Sin had so thoroughly obscured it that God needed to renew people's knowledge of it.

The law is spiritual. It judges not only outward behavior but also the heart's hidden thoughts, desires and inclinations.

Its great demand is **love**. Without love, outward obedience is merely hypocrisy.

The law requires obedience that is perfect, unfailing and constant.

No legitimate law grants permission to disobey it.

Whoever keeps the whole law but breaks it at one point is guilty of all (James 2:10).

Whether in heart or action, in thought, word or deed, any departure from God's law is sin.

And the wages of sin is death.

## The first four commandments: our duty toward God (verses 3–11)

The **first table** of the law, consisting of its first four commandments, describes our duties toward God.

It is right that these come first.

Before a person had a neighbor to love, that person already had a Maker to love.

How can someone be faithful to a brother or sister while being unfaithful to God?

**The first commandment** concerns the One whom we worship: Jehovah, and Jehovah alone.

It forbids worshiping created things.

Any failure in perfect love, gratitude, reverence or worship breaks this command.

Whatever we do should be done for God's glory.

**The second commandment** concerns the way we worship the Lord.

It forbids making an image or picture of the Deity in any form or for any purpose, or worshiping any creature or representation.

But its spiritual meaning reaches further.

It forbids every kind of superstition and every attempt to replace God's appointed worship with inventions of our own.

**The third commandment** concerns the reverence and seriousness with which we worship.

It forbids false oaths, careless appeals to God's name, profane cursing, and all irreverent use of sacred things.

There is no worthwhile advantage, honor or pleasure in doing such things.

The Lord will not hold guiltless the one who misuses his name.

**The fourth commandment** begins with the word *Remember*.

Henry takes this to mean that the Sabbath was not instituted here for the first time: it was already known.

One day in seven must be kept holy.

Six days are given for ordinary work, though not in a way that excuses neglecting God or our souls.

We should finish our ordinary work during those days rather than leaving it for the Sabbath.

Christ allowed works of necessity, mercy and piety.

The Sabbath was made for human beings, not human beings for the Sabbath (Mark 2:27).

But works devoted to luxury, vanity or self-indulgence are forbidden.

Henry specifically includes trading, paying wages, settling accounts, writing business letters, secular study, idle social visits, unnecessary travel and frivolous conversation among activities that do not keep the day holy.

Laziness is a merely fleshly kind of rest, not holy rest.

The Lord's Sabbath should be rest from ordinary work **and** rest expressed through serving God.

Even its benefits for human health and happiness, alongside the opportunity it gives us to care for our souls, display the excellence of this command.

God has blessed the day and blesses people through it.

Henry notes that the command's blessing and call to holiness refer to the **Sabbath day**, rather than being restricted by their wording to the seventh day alone.

## The final six commandments: our duty toward one another (verses 12–17)

The **second table** of the law concerns the duties we owe ourselves and other people.

These six commandments explain the call to love our neighbor as ourselves (Luke 10:27).

Godliness and honesty belong together.

**The fifth commandment** concerns our duties toward family members, particularly our parents.

Honoring father and mother includes respecting them in our conduct and willingly obeying their lawful commands.

Children should come when called, go when sent, do what they are told, avoid what is forbidden, and do all this cheerfully out of love.

It includes receiving parental advice and correction.

Children should seek their parents' comfort, make their old age easier and support them if they need help.

Jesus expressly includes this duty of material support (Matthew 15:4–6).

Henry observes that obedient children have often enjoyed particular earthly blessings, while disobedience has brought the opposite.

**The sixth commandment** requires us to value the life and safety of other people as highly as our own.

Magistrates and their officers may enforce lawful judgments; truthful witnesses do not break this command.

Self-defense is lawful.

But much that human law does not classify as murder still counts as murder before God.

Violent anger and drunkenness do not excuse the taking of life.

Duels are especially guilty expressions of pride and revenge.

Henry also condemns fighting for pay, fame, anger or hatred.

Leading people into vices or crimes that shorten life may violate this command.

So may cruel behavior that breaks the hearts or shortens the lives of parents, spouses or other relatives.

It forbids envy, malice, hatred, anger, provoking words and insults.

It also forbids destroying one's own life.

Positively, it calls us to kindness, patience and forgiveness.

**The seventh commandment** concerns purity.

We should fear what defiles the body as seriously as what destroys it.

Whatever feeds impure imagination or stirs lust falls under this command—including indecent pictures, books and conversation.

**The eighth commandment** applies love to other people's possessions.

What we gain honestly is the portion of this world's goods God has allotted us.

We should receive it thankfully, be content with it and trust Providence for tomorrow while using lawful means of support.

Taking advantage of people's ignorance, willingness to trust or desperate need violates this law, even where society hardly condemns it.

Those who plunder whole nations are not exempt merely because they escape human courts.

Defrauding the public, taking on debts with no realistic prospect of repayment, evading legitimate debts, extravagance, living on charity without need and squeezing the poor through unjust wages are also violations.

The command calls for diligence, thrift and contentment.

In matters of property, we should treat others as we would want them to treat us.

**The ninth commandment** protects our own reputation and that of our neighbors.

It forbids lying, deliberate ambiguity, deceit and any scheme to mislead.

It forbids unjust attacks on another person's reputation.

This includes false testimony, slander, gossip, backbiting and making someone's wrongdoing appear worse than it was.

It also forbids trying to improve our own standing by damaging another person's good name.

How often people of every social rank break this command!

**The tenth commandment** reaches the root of sinful desire: *You shall not covet.*

Other commands forbid desires that would lead us to harm our neighbor.

This one also forbids wrong desires to possess something merely to gratify ourselves.

## Israel trembles before God's law (verses 18–21)

The law is so wide-reaching that we cannot measure all its demands, so spiritual that we cannot evade its judgment, and so reasonable that we cannot justly accuse it.

It governs our present conduct and will be the standard of God's future judgment.

Measured by it, we discover how thoroughly our lives have been marked by sin.

With such a holy law and such a solemn judgment before us, how could we despise Christ's gospel?

Knowing the law shows us our need to repent.

In every true believer, sin has been dethroned and crucified.

God writes his law on the heart and renews his own image there.

The Holy Spirit enables believers to hate sin and flee from it, and to love and obey God's law sincerely.

Nor do they ever cease repenting.

## The Lord gives further instructions about worship (verses 22–26)

Moses entered the thick darkness and heard God speak the commands that follow through the end of chapter 23.

Most of these instructions explain and apply the Ten Commandments.

The laws immediately given here concern God's worship.

God assured Israel that he would graciously receive its offerings.

Under the gospel, people are encouraged to pray everywhere.

Wherever God's people gather in his name to worship him, he is present among them.

He will come to them and bless them.
