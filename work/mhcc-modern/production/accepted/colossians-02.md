# Colossians 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle expresses his love to, and joy in believers. | 1–7 |
| He cautions against the errors of heathen philosophy; also against Jewish traditions, and rites which had been fulfilled in Christ. | 8–17 |
| Against worshipping angels; and against legal ordinances. | 18–23 |

## Christ contains the treasures of wisdom and knowledge (verses 1–7)

Our souls prosper when we acquire **clear knowledge of the truth as it is in Jesus**.

That truth should be believed in the heart and **confessed openly** when circumstances call for it.

Knowledge and genuine faith make a soul rich.

The stronger our faith and the warmer our love, the greater comfort we receive.

The treasures of wisdom are hidden in Christ **not to keep them from us, but to preserve them for us**.

They remain hidden from proud unbelievers, yet are displayed in **Christ's person and saving work**.

Beware of persuasive but misleading words.

Evil ideas and practices often appear attractive through clever disguises.

People who try to draw us into wickedness aim to **rob us of what is precious**.

Every professing Christian claims to have received **Jesus Christ as Lord**, agreeing to belong to him.

But we cannot grow in Christ until we are first **rooted in him and founded upon him**.

Having been established in faith, we should continue increasing in it and developing its fruits.

**Thanksgiving** must accompany this growth.

God justly withholds some blessings when people receive his gifts without grateful hearts.

## Complete in Christ, not in religious shadows (verses 8–17)

There is a form of philosophy that rightly exercises our minds.

By studying **God's works in creation**, it leads us toward knowledge of him and confirms faith.

But there is another kind of philosophy that is **empty and deceptive**, entertaining human imagination while undermining belief.

It involves speculative questions beyond our knowledge or of no real importance.

Following the world's patterns draws us away from Christ.

All the ceremonies of the old covenant were **shadows**.

Their true substance is found in **Jesus Christ**.

His complete sacrifice for sin and his revelation of God's will supply everything those outward observances could not provide.

To be **complete in Christ** means to possess everything necessary for salvation.

We are in him not merely when we look toward a distant Savior, but when the Holy Spirit creates **faith in our hearts** and joins us to **Christ our Head**.

Inward circumcision of the heart, putting sinful desires to death, dying and being buried **to sin and the world**, and rising to **newness of life** are realities portrayed in **baptism** and brought about through **Spirit-given faith**.

These show that God forgives sins and frees believers from the law's curse.

We were spiritually dead in sins, but **Christ makes us alive**.

His death is the death of our sins; his resurrection brings life to our souls.

Christ removed the ordinances that burdened Jewish believers and formed **a dividing wall between Jews and Gentiles**.

When the substance came, **the shadows disappeared**.

Every mortal human being is guilty under the written law's sentence of death.

How dreadful, then, the condition of those who scorn **the Son of God's blood**, through which alone that condemning record can be wiped away!

Do not be troubled by narrow, legalistic judgments concerning **food, drink and Jewish festivals**.

Setting aside time for **God's worship and service** is a moral obligation that does not change.

But the obligation is not inseparably tied to **the seventh day of the week**.

Christians keep **the first day, the Lord's Day**, in remembrance of his resurrection.

All Jewish ceremonies were shadows pointing toward the gospel's blessings.

## Christ alone is our Mediator and Head (verses 18–23)

Appealing to angels for help can appear humble, as though people admitted they were too unworthy to speak directly to God.

But this is **not permissible worship**.

It gives a created being the honor belonging **to Christ alone**.

Such apparent humility is actually pride.

Worshiping angels means failing to honor **Christ, the only Mediator between God and humanity**.

To seek other spiritual intercessors is to insult the Lord, **the Head of the church**.

Those who release their hold on Christ grasp at things that cannot help them.

Christ's body, the church, is a **growing body**.

Genuine believers cannot live according to the world's fashions.

Wisdom means remaining close to **the gospel's appointed means**, entirely submitted to Jesus Christ, **the church's only Head**.

Self-imposed hardship and fasting can look like exceptional spirituality or willingness to suffer.

But practices invented by human preference do not honor God merely because they are difficult.

They actually gratify **self-will, self-admiration, self-righteousness and contempt for others**.

Their appearance of wisdom is extremely shallow, and they do not provide true spiritual benefit or overcome sinful desires.

Let us regard as **morally indifferent** whatever the Lord has left to individual freedom.

Grant other believers the same liberty.

Remember how quickly earthly things pass away, and use them **to glorify God**.
