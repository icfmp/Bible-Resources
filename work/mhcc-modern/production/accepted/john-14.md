# John 14 — Modern English

## Jesus comforts the disciples (verses 1–11)

Jesus' command can be considered by stressing three words.

**Troubled:** Do not let fear and grief overwhelm you.

**Heart:** Keep your inward life steady through complete trust in God.

**Your:** Even if others are overcome by this world's troubles, **you**, his disciples, should not be.

More than anyone else, Christ's followers should remain quiet in spirit when everything around them is disturbed.

The remedy for a troubled heart is **faith**. Trusting Christ as Mediator between God and humanity brings comfort.

He describes heaven as **his Father's house**, with many enduring dwelling places for the many children he will bring to glory.

Jesus will finish what he began. If he has prepared a place for his people, he will also prepare **his people for that place**.

He is **the Way**, in his person as God made flesh, through his atoning sacrifice and through his advocacy before the Father.

He is **the Truth**, fulfilling the promises about the Savior and leading sinners to believe them.

He is **the Life**, giving spiritual life through his Spirit to those dead in sin.

No one comes to God as Father without being made alive by Christ the Life, instructed by Christ the Truth and brought by Christ the Way.

Through him our prayers reach the Father and God's blessings come to us.

He is the good old way leading to rest, and **the Resurrection and the Life**.

Everyone who sees Christ through faith sees the Father in him.

His teaching reveals God as the Father of lights; his miracles reveal God's power; his spotless life reveals divine holiness.

The Redeemer's works display both **his own glory and God's glory in him**.

## Jesus promises the Holy Spirit (verses 12–17)

Jesus answers petitions offered in his name that truly serve our good and are suited to our condition.

Praying **in Christ's name** means pleading his merit and intercession, not our own worthiness, and relying upon that plea.

The Holy Spirit is a gift secured by Christ's mediation, purchased through his merit and given through his intercession.

The word used of the Spirit describes **an advocate, counselor, guide and comforter**.

He will remain with Christ's disciples through the end of time, strengthening and comforting them through his gifts and graces.

Jesus' words describe **a Person**, whose work displays divine perfection, not an impersonal force.

The Spirit is given to Christ's disciples rather than to the unbelieving world.

This gift is a mark of God's special favor.

As the source of holiness and happiness, the Holy Spirit will **abide with every believer forever**.

## Christ's further promises of comfort (verses 18–31)

### Jesus will not leave his people as orphans (verses 18–24)

Christ promised to continue caring for his followers.

He would come to them at his resurrection, come **daily through his Spirit and grace**, and come certainly at the end of time.

Those who see him through faith will see him forever. The world will not see him again until his return, but his disciples can have fellowship with him even during his bodily absence.

These mysteries will be fully understood in heaven.

We must not merely store Jesus' commands in our minds. **We must keep them in our hearts and lives**.

The clearest evidence of genuine love for Christ is **obedience to his commands**.

Where this love is real, obedience follows from gratitude rather than mere compulsion.

God delights to show love to his people and to dwell with them as at home.

These privileges belong to those whose **faith works through love** and whose love leads to obedience, as recipients of the Spirit's renewing grace.

### The Spirit teaches and Christ gives peace (verses 25–27)

If we wish to understand Jesus' teaching for our good, we must pray for and depend upon the Holy Spirit.

He brings Christ's words to remembrance and makes difficulties clear that remain obscure to others.

The Spirit of grace serves as a **reminder and teacher** for believers. By prayer and faith we entrust to him the truths we have heard.

**Peace** here includes everything truly good that Christ has promised.

It includes peace of conscience arising from **justification before God**.

Jesus calls it **his own peace**, because he is our peace.

His peace differs from the false confidence of hypocrisy and empty religion: its effects are **humbling and holy**.

### Jesus prepares his disciples for departure (verses 28–31)

Christ invited the disciples to look beyond the earthly comfort they had considered most important.

His remaining time was short, so he gave them extensive counsel.

When we become ill or approach death, we may be unable to speak at length with those we love. Therefore **give necessary counsel while we are healthy**.

Jesus anticipated conflict not only with human opponents but with the powers of darkness.

Satan finds sinful weaknesses in us to exploit, because we have sinned. **In Jesus he found nothing sinful** through which to attack him.

The best proof of our love for the Father is **doing what he commands**.

Let us rejoice in Jesus' victory over Satan, the prince of this world, and imitate Christ's obedient love.
