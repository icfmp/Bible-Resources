# 2 John 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle salutes the elect lady and her children. | 1–3 |
| Express his joy in their faith and love. | 4–6 |
| Cautions them against deceivers. | 7–11 |
| And concludes. | 12, 13 |

## Grace, mercy and peace to the elect lady (verses 1–3)

Christianity turns courteous expressions into **genuine love and respect**.

An older disciple deserves honor, and an elderly apostle serving Christ deserves still more.

John wrote to a distinguished Christian woman and her children.

It is good when the gospel reaches people of high standing. But **the woman was loved for her holiness, not her rank**.

Families need encouragement and direction in their duties and affection at home.

Those who love truth and piety in themselves should also love them in others. Wherever genuine faith takes root, it remains.

John asks God to give **grace, mercy and peace**.

**Grace** is God's free favor and the source of every spiritual blessing.

**Mercy** is the freely given pardon Christians continue needing, even after receiving much grace.

**Peace** is a clear conscience and a tranquil heart grounded in reconciliation with God, along with whatever outward prosperity will truly serve their good.

All these blessings must be received **in truth and love**.

## Rejoice when children walk in truth and love (verses 4–6)

A godly education begun early in life is a wonderful blessing.

Children may be loved also for the faithful example of their parents.

It delighted John to find children following in their parents' faithful footsteps and likely to support the gospel in their own generation.

May God bless such households and raise up more to imitate them!

How sorrowful, by contrast, when parents teach their children unbelief, godlessness and moral corruption.

Walking rightly means living **according to God's word**.

The command of Christian love may be called new because **Christ announced and modeled it in a new way**, yet it has always been a divine command.

Because this love may weaken under temptation or apostasy, John returns to it repeatedly and earnestly.

## Resist false teachings about Christ (verses 7–11)

The deceiver brings error concerning **Jesus Christ's person or his saving office**.

Such teaching undermines the Lord's glory and kingdom.

Deceivers are no recent development; they were active even during the apostles' lives.

The more widespread they become, the more alert Christ's disciples must remain.

It is grievous for people to lose spiritual progress once made.

Persevering in Christ to the end is the way to receive **the full promised reward**.

Holding fast to the Son's truth unites believers to **the Father as well**, for Father and Son are one.

Reject both people who deny **Christ's true teaching** and conduct that contradicts **his commands**.

Anyone refusing to confess Christ as **the Son of God and the source of salvation from sin and guilt** should not be encouraged or given a platform to spread that error.

But John does not command rudeness toward every person who differs in secondary matters.

**Show genuine kindness and a good spirit toward Christians who disagree on lesser questions** while remaining firm in the essential doctrines of Jesus' person, atonement and holy salvation.

## Hope for the joy of meeting face to face (verses 12 and 13)

John reserves much for an in-person meeting.

Writing can strengthen and comfort people, but **seeing one another in person often does so still more**.

Maintain Christian fellowship by every suitable means, seeking mutual joy.

In fellowship with the saints we enjoy many present blessings and anticipate eternal happiness together.
