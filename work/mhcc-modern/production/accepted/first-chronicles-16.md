## The ark is established (verses 1–6)

God's word and appointed ordinances may seem clouded or eclipsed for a time, but they will shine again after that obscurity.

The ark was housed only in a tent, a humble dwelling. Yet David spoke of that tabernacle with deep affection throughout his psalms.

God had been gracious to David, and David now showed generosity toward his people.

Those whose hearts have expanded with holy joy should show that joy through openhanded generosity.

## David's psalm of praise (verses 7–36)

Let our praise bring glory to God, while also teaching and encouraging other people, so that even strangers to him may learn to worship him.

Let us rejoice in the Lord and trust him. People who give glory to God's name may rightly rejoice in that name.

God's everlasting covenant should be the great source of our joy. We should remember it with thanksgiving, as his people did in earlier times.

Day by day, announce his salvation—the salvation promised through Jesus Christ.

We have reason to celebrate it every day because we receive its benefits daily, and the subject can never be exhausted.

Even in the midst of praise, do not forget to pray for God's servants who are suffering.

## Worship is organized (verses 37–43)

Worshiping God should be part of **every day**. David established an orderly pattern.

At Jerusalem, where the ark stood, Asaph and his companions ministered continually with songs of praise.

No sacrifices or incense were offered there because the altars were elsewhere.

Yet David's prayers rose like incense, and his raised hands were like the evening sacrifice. Spiritual worship was already taking the place of ceremonial worship in this respect.

Still, **God had instituted the ceremonial ordinances, so they must not be neglected**.

At Gibeon, where the altars stood, the priests continued offering sacrifices and incense morning and evening, in accordance with Moses' law.

Those ceremonies pictured **the mediation of Christ**, making their appointed observance important.

The faithful presence of ministers whom God appoints is right in itself and encourages the people.
