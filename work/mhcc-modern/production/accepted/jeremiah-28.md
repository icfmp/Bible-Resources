# Jeremiah 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A false prophet opposes Jeremiah. | 1–9 |
| The false prophet warned of his approaching death. | 10–17 |

## Hananiah promises peace without repentance (verses 1–9)

**Hananiah** delivered a false prophecy.

His speech offered **not one word urging Judah to repent or return to God**.

He promised **earthly blessings in God's name**, but said nothing about the **spiritual blessings God had always connected with his promises**.

Jeremiah had often prayed for the nation, even while announcing judgment against it.

He appealed to **what would actually happen** as the test exposing Hananiah's falsehood.

A prophet who promises only peace and prosperity, without warning that **deliberate sin cuts people off from God's favor**, will prove to be a false prophet.

Anyone who refuses to proclaim **the alarming as well as the encouraging parts of God's word**—and fails to call people to **repentance, faith and holiness**—follows the pattern of the false prophets.

Christ's gospel encourages people to perform **deeds worthy of repentance**.

It never gives anyone permission to continue in sin.

## The false prophet receives a sentence of death (verses 10–17)

Hananiah was sentenced to die.

Jeremiah told him this boldly **only after receiving God's direction**, not before the Lord had entrusted him with the message.

People who assure sinners that they will enjoy peace while they harden themselves against God's word bear **terrible responsibility**.

The Lord's servant must be **gentle toward everyone**.

Such a servant should willingly surrender even legitimate personal rights and leave it to **God to defend the cause**.

Every effort by wicked people to overturn God's purposes will only **increase their own misery**.
