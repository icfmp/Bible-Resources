# Psalm 42 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The conflict in the soul of a believer.**

## Original source divisions

| Source commentary | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| First section | 1–5 |
| Second section | 6–11 |

## Longing for the living God (verses 1–5)

The psalmist regarded the Lord as his **highest good**, and directed his heart toward him.

By fixing his anchor there from the beginning, he was able to endure the storm.

A gracious soul cannot be content merely to attend worship if it does not encounter **God himself**.

Living souls cannot truly rest anywhere short of the **living God**.

The upright person longs to appear before him; the hypocrite dreads that meeting.

Few things trouble a believer more than attempts to shake confidence in the Lord.

David was not merely missing the pleasures of royal life. He grieved over the **free access to God's house** he formerly enjoyed and the happiness of worshiping there.

People who search their own hearts often discover thoughts that need correction.

Here is the remedy for sorrow: when the soul relies on itself, it sinks; when it holds fast to **God's power and promise**, it remains above the waves.

Present troubles are bearable when we know we will find consolation in God.

We have real reasons to mourn our sin, but despair and despondency often spring from unbelief and a rebellious will. We should therefore pray and struggle against them.

## Remembering God's mercies (verses 6–11)

The way to forget our misery is to **remember the God who has shown us mercy**.

David recognized that his suffering had been permitted by God, and this sometimes discouraged him.

Yet if troubles arrive one after another and seem to combine against us, remember that they remain subject to the Lord's appointment and government.

David regarded divine favor as the source of everything good he hoped to receive.

Let us hope and pray **in the Savior's name**.

One word from the Lord can calm a storm, turn midnight into midday and change bitter complaints into joyful thanksgiving.

Our confident expectation of mercy should make us more earnest in asking for it.

At last faith overcame David's fears. He determined to rely on the Lord and added the personal words, **“my God.”**

That thought carried him above sorrow.

Never imagine that the God who gives us life and is the Rock of our salvation has forgotten us, if we have taken refuge in his **mercy, truth and power**.

The psalmist struggled with discouragement until faith and hope prevailed.

Let us challenge unbelieving doubts, receive God's promises personally and then plead those promises in prayer.
