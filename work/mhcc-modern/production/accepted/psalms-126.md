# Psalm 126 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Those returned out of captivity are to be thankful. | 1–3 |
| Those yet in captivity are encouraged. | 4–6 |

## Thankfulness for deliverance (verses 1–3)

It is good to pay attention to **God's deliverances of his church** and to share in the joy those rescues bring.

How highly should we value the greater redemption that saves us from **the coming judgment, the power of sin and Satan**!

When a sinner becomes aware of guilt and danger, looks to **the crucified Savior**, receives peace of conscience and finds new strength to abandon sinful conduct, the change may be so wonderful that it hardly seems real.

## Praying and sowing through tears (verses 4–6)

The beginnings of God's mercy encourage us to pray that he will complete what he has started.

As long as we remain in this world, there will always be something to pray for, even when we have abundant reasons for praise.

Suffering believers often cry.

They share the hardships of human life and sometimes endure more than others.

Yet **they sow while they weep**.

They perform their duties even in a painful season.

**Tears must not keep us from sowing**.

We should seek spiritual benefit from times of suffering.

Those who sow to the Spirit, even amid tears of godly repentance, will from the Spirit reap **eternal life**.

What a joyful harvest that will be!

Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted forever.

When we grieve over sin or suffer for Christ, we are sowing in tears and will one day reap with joy.

Remember also that **God cannot be mocked**: we will reap what we sow (**Galatians 6:7–9**).

Disciple of Jesus, see here a picture of your present work and future reward.

The day is coming when your harvest will be abundant and **your joy in the Lord will be great**.
