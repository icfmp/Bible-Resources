# Ezekiel 20 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The elders of Israel are reminded of the idolatry in Egypt. | 1–9 |
| In the wilderness. | 10–26 |
| In Canaan. | 27–32 |
| God promises to pardon and restore them. | 33–44 |
| Prophecy against Jerusalem. | 45–49 |

## The elders are reminded of Israel's sins in Egypt (verses 1–9)

People have become terribly hardened when they ask God to let them **continue sinning**, even while already suffering for sin.

See **Ezekiel 20:32**.

God is rightly angry with people who remain determined to persist in their wrongdoing.

Ezekiel was to remind the nation of its ancestors' evil deeds so that they would recognize **the justice of God's earlier judgments**.

## The people rebelled in the wilderness (verses 10–26)

Israel's wilderness history is recalled in both **the Old Testament and the New Testament** as a warning.

God had done wonderful things for his people.

He gave them **his law** and renewed the ancient observance of **the Sabbath**.

Sabbaths are **privileges** and signs of belonging to him.

When we honor their purpose, we discover that God himself makes us holy and truly happy in this life, preparing us for the perfect holiness and happiness of heaven.

But the Israelites rebelled and were left to suffer the judgments their sins had brought.

Sometimes God permits **sin to become its own punishment**.

Yet **God is never the author of sin**.

People may be made thoroughly miserable simply by being handed over to **their own evil desires and passions**.

## Israel's rebellion continued in Canaan (verses 27–32)

After the people settled in Canaan, they continued rebelling.

The elders listening to Ezekiel seemed to be considering **merging with the surrounding nations and their worship**.

An outward profession of faith accomplishes nothing if it remains **only a profession**.

Sinful compromise brings no real advantage.

The worldly schemes of hypocrites will ultimately be useless to them.

## God promises to restore and renew a remnant (verses 33–44)

Wicked Israelites who followed other nations in sin would not share those nations' prosperity.

Instead, God would separate them for judgment.

No one can escape the Lord's rule.

Those who refuse to submit to **the power of his grace** will face **the power of his wrath**.

But God will not lose **a single one of his jewels** amid the confusion of the world.

He will bring the Jews back to **Israel's land** and give them **genuine repentance**.

Their hearts will be overcome by **his kindness**.

The more we know of God's holiness, the more clearly we recognize how hateful sin is.

People who remain unmoved despite God's gracious means, and who insist on living without Christ like the surrounding world, are moving toward destruction.

## Judgment is announced against fruitless Jerusalem (verses 45–49)

Judah and Jerusalem had been full of people, like **a forest filled with trees**.

Yet they produced **no good fruit**.

God's word speaks against those who refuse to bear **the fruits of righteousness**.

When he determines to bring a nation low, who can rescue it?

Even the clearest truths sounded like **parables** to the people.

Those who refuse to respond to God's word commonly **blame the message** rather than admit their unwillingness to obey.
