# Psalm 40 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Confidence for deliverance. | 1–5 |

| Prayer for mercy and grace. | 11–17 |

## Deliverance after patient waiting (verses 1–5)

Fears and doubts about our eternal state can feel like **a dreadful pit full of sticky mud**, even to a dearly loved child of God.

Yet the Lord has enough power to help the weakest and enough grace to help the most unworthy person who trusts him.

The psalmist waited patiently, continuing to **believe, hope and pray**.

Henry sees these words as applying especially to **Christ**, whose agony in Gethsemane and on the cross resembled that dreadful pit.

Those who wait for God will never wait in vain.

Believers who have suffered spiritual despair and then found relief by God's grace recognize themselves in **verse 2**.

**Christ is the Rock** on which a struggling soul can stand securely.

Where God grants firm hope, he also expects a steady and orderly life.

David received not only peace through faith but also joy.

Multitudes have looked in faith to Christ's suffering and glory and learned both to fear God's justice and to trust his mercy.

God loads us daily with benefits through **Providence and grace**.

## The work of redemption (verses 6–10)

Here David foretold the wonder of **redemption through Jesus Christ**.

The true substance had to appear. Animal sacrifices could never give God the glory or human beings the saving grace accomplished by Christ.

The psalm speaks of Jesus being set apart to serve as **Mediator**.

The scroll of the book spoke of him.

Henry also sees the covenant of redemption as recorded in God's eternal counsel, and points to the Old Testament's testimony about Christ (**John 19:28**).

Now that the price of salvation has been paid, its proclamation goes out, inviting people freely and openly to receive it.

Preaching Christ is not easy. People commissioned to proclaim the gospel may face pressure to hide its truth, but Christ sustains those he calls.

May we believe his testimony, trust his promises and submit to his authority.

## Seeking mercy and grace (verses 11–17)

Even the best saints know they would be undone without **God's continuing protection and grace**.

See how seriously David regarded sin. This makes the discovery of a Redeemer especially welcome.

When he examined each part of his life, he found something wrong.

A clear view of our sins without an equally clear view of the Savior would overwhelm us.

Yet Christ has triumphed over our spiritual enemies, and through him **we are more than conquerors**.

Let everyone who seeks God and loves his salvation rejoice and praise him.

No poverty or sorrow can make a person who fears the Lord truly miserable. God himself, together with all he has done and promised, is the source of that person's joy.

The prayer of faith draws upon his inexhaustible fullness, sufficient for every need.

His promises are certain and their fulfillment approaches.

**The One who first came in humility will return in glorious majesty**.
