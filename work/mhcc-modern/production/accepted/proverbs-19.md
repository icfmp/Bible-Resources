# Proverbs 19 — Modern English

## Verse 1 — The honor of poor believers

A poor person who fears God is **more honorable and happier** than someone lacking wisdom and grace, however rich or powerful.

## Verse 2 — Think before moving

A soul without knowledge cannot live wisely. Rushing ahead without considering where our feet are going is sinful.

## Verse 3 — Folly followed by resentment

People get themselves into trouble through foolishness, then become angry about **God's providential arrangements**.

## Verse 4 — The power money holds

This verse exposes how strongly money can attract and govern human affection.

## Verse 5 — Small lies grow

People who habitually lie in ordinary conversation are moving toward the still graver sin of **false testimony**.

## Verse 6 — All gifts come from God

We have no excuse for withholding wholehearted love from God. His gifts are innumerable, and even other people's kindnesses are fruits of **his generosity**.

## Verse 7 — Christ understands abandonment

Even Christ was deserted by his disciples, but **the Father remained with him**. The Savior's experience of poverty and rejection encourages our faith.

## Verse 8 — Love your soul rightly

Only those who acquire **true wisdom** love their own souls as they ought.

## Verse 9 — The danger of lying

Lying is a destructive sin that brings condemnation.

## Verse 10 — True enjoyment requires grace

Without wisdom and grace, a person has no rightful claim to lasting joy. Those enslaved to sin should not oppress people whom **God has made free**.

## Verse 11 — Overcome evil with good

The greatest honor belongs to those who most consistently seek to **overcome evil through good**.

## Verse 12 — Christ the King

Christ's wrath against his enemies is terrifying like **a roaring lion**, while his favor toward his people is refreshing **like dew**.

## Verse 13 — The uncertainties of comfort

The world's vanity appears when our greatest griefs arise from relationships or possessions in which we expected the greatest comfort.

## Verse 14 — A virtuous spouse is precious

A prudent and godly wife is worth more than houses and wealth.

## Verse 15 — Sloth brings want

A lazy disposition produces poverty and need. The warning applies to **spiritual life as well as earthly affairs**.

## Verse 16 — Grace does not cancel obedience

Keeping God's word protects us from what is truly harmful. We abuse the doctrine of **free grace** if we claim it makes obedience unnecessary. Those who live carelessly will die; the warning should awaken even hardened sinners.

## Verse 17 — God values care for the poor

God has chosen people who are poor in this world to be **rich in faith and heirs of his kingdom**.

## Verse 18 — Correct children with wise affection

Parents must overcome misguided softness and correct their children; this is one way to help children grow into people who bring comfort and are happy themselves.

## Verse 19 — Indulgence feeds violent anger

A child allowed every desire without correction is likely to become an adult with an uncontrolled temper.

## Verse 20 — Learn while young

Those hoping to be wise at life's end must receive teaching and proper guidance while they are young.

## Verse 21 — Let our plans agree with God's will

What better thing can we desire than that all our plans should agree with **God's holy purposes**?

## Verse 22 — Good intentions without ability

It is better to have the **heart to do good but lack the means**, than to have ample means but no desire to help.

## Verse 23 — Reverence brings true satisfaction

Those who live in the fear of God gain lasting security, contentment and happiness.

## Verse 24 — Laziness can become helplessness

Habitual idleness grows until a person no longer has energy even for necessary self-care.

## Verse 25 — Gentle correction is effective

A mild rebuke often does more to reach someone with understanding than harsh punishment.

## Verse 26 — Honor parents and protect them

A young person who wastes a father's possessions or leaves an aged mother destitute behaves shamefully and will be disgraced.

## Verse 27 — Avoid corrupting speech

Young people wisely refuse to listen to conversation that would implant **sinful principles** in the mind.

## Verse 28 — Delighting in sin

The worst sinners actively welcome opportunities to do wrong.

## Verse 29 — Christ's suffering guarantees judgment's reality

Human unbelief cannot cancel God's warnings. When **Christ bore sins that were not his own, he was not spared**: divine justice fell upon our blessed Surety. How could stubborn sinners expect impunity?
