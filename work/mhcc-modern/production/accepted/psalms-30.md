# Psalm 30 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Praise to God for deliverance. | 1–5 |
| Others encouraged by his example. | 6–12 |

## Praise after deliverance (verses 1–5)

The great things the Lord has done for us through **Providence and grace** call us to grateful service.

We should do all we can to advance his kingdom, even though our greatest efforts are small.

The saints in heaven sing God's praises; why should not believers on earth sing to him too?

Of all God's attributes, none inspires more fear in the wicked or more comfort in godly people than **his holiness**.

It is a good sign that we share something of his holiness when we sincerely rejoice to remember it.

Our happiness rests upon God's favor.

If we possess that favor, we have enough, whatever else may be missing.

Yet while a believer feels God's displeasure, sorrow continues.

## Learning from prosperity and trouble (verses 6–12)

When life goes well, we easily begin to imagine that it will always be so.

Recognizing our mistake should make us ashamed of **carnal self-confidence**.

If God hides his face, a faithful person may be distressed even without any other suffering.

But if God turns away for wise and just reasons, it would be foolish for us to turn away from **him**.

Instead, **learn to pray in the dark**.

A sanctified soul that returns to God will praise him forever.

The body that has become dust cannot perform the earthly service of God's house: the grave is a place of silence and no further earthly labor.

We rightly ask to remain alive when we want our lives to be devoted to **God's praise**.

In due time the Lord delivered David.

The tongue is our glory, and it is never better used than when blessing him.

David intended to continue praising God until he entered the place where praise would become his everlasting work.

Yet everyone must beware **complacency during prosperity**.

Neither external success nor inward comfort is permanently secure in this life.

God's favor grounds a believer's safety as firmly as a deep-rooted mountain, but temptations and trials still come.

If we become careless and sin, the Lord may hide his face; comfort fades and troubles return.

Let us therefore remain watchful, dependent upon his grace and constant in praise.
