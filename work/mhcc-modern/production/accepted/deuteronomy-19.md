# Deuteronomy 19 — Modern English

## Cities of refuge and the distinction between murder and accident (verses 1–13)

The law distinguished between the blood of an innocent person who had been murdered and the guilt of the murderer.

It also provided cities of refuge so that a person would not die as a criminal for causing a death that was **not intentional**.

Jesus Christ, the Lord our Righteousness, is a refuge for sinners who flee to him through faith.

But there is no shelter in Christ for people who presume upon mercy while deliberately continuing in sin.

Those who flee **from their sins to Christ** are safe with him.

Those who expect him to protect them **while they remain in their sins** are deceiving themselves.

## Boundary markers must not be moved (verse 14)

The Lord commanded Israel to preserve boundary markers in Canaan.

He wants everyone to know what belongs to them, and safeguards to be maintained so that no one wrongs another person or is wronged.

This is a moral command that continues to apply.

Each person should be content with their own portion and act justly toward neighbors in every matter.

## False witnesses and public justice (verses 15–21)

A person must not be condemned on the word of only one witness.

Someone who deliberately gives false testimony should be subject to the penalty that the witness had attempted to inflict upon the accused.

Henry regards this as a just provision.

Christians should therefore be careful not only when giving evidence before a court but also in private conversation.

They must not join in slander.

And anyone whose conscience accuses them of sin should flee immediately to the refuge offered in Jesus Christ.
