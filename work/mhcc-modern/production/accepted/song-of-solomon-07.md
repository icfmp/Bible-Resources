# Song of Solomon 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The graces of the church. | 1–9 |
| The delight of the church in Christ. | 10–13 |

## The beauty of holiness in the church (verses 1–9)

The images in this chapter differ from those used earlier. Henry explains that their original language evokes **splendid and beautiful clothing**.

The saints have such honor through being **clothed with Christ**. Their character should make the teaching of God their Savior attractive in every respect.

Believers who act consistently with their confession honor Christ, commend the gospel and may awaken sinners to its truth.

The church is compared to a tall, spreading palm tree. Her love for Christ and the obedience arising from that love bear precious fruit from **the true Vine**.

The King is pictured among the galleries. Christ delights in the assemblies and appointed worship of his people. He takes pleasure in **the fruit of his own grace** within them.

These images describe the beauty of holiness in the church and in each believer, who will ultimately be presented to the heavenly Bridegroom.

## The church desires deeper fellowship with Christ (verses 10–13)

The church rejoices that she belongs to Christ and that Christ belongs to her.

She humbly asks him to walk with her, guiding, teaching and comforting her. She wants to speak freely with him about every need and grief, without interruption.

All who have been made holy sincerely desire this fellowship. Those who would walk with Christ must turn away from the world's sinful attractions.

We can enjoy communion with God wherever he places us. **We should never go willingly somewhere we cannot ask him in faith to accompany us.**

Begin seeking him early: early in life and early each day. Even a poor dwelling becomes pleasant through God's presence, while the loveliest countryside cannot satisfy a soul that lacks the Beloved.

Our souls are **vineyards**. They should produce useful fruit, and we should frequently ask whether our lives are bearing righteousness.

Christ's presence makes the vines flourish, as sunlight renews a garden. If we can sincerely say to him, **“You know all things; you know that I love you,”** and if his Spirit assures us that our souls are growing, we have reason for confidence.

Yet we must also ask him to search our hearts and show us our true condition.

Every gracious act and fruit of holiness pleases Jesus. Keep such fruits ready for him, so that by bearing much fruit we glorify him.

**All grace comes from Christ; therefore all its fruit should be offered to Christ.**
