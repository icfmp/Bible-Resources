# 1 Samuel 6 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## The Philistines prepare to return the ark (verses 1–9)

The Philistines endured punishment for **seven months** while the ark remained among them.

They prolonged their own misery because they refused to send it away sooner.

Sinners often do the same when they will not part with their sins.

The Israelites themselves made no effort to recover the ark.

How rarely concern for true religion outweighs other concerns!

In a time of public disaster, people fear for themselves, their families and their nation.

But who is concerned for God's ark?

We have been granted the gospel, yet it is often neglected or despised.

We should not be surprised if that privilege is removed.

For many people, even the loss of the gospel—one of the gravest possible judgments—would bring no sadness.

They would be as satisfied with almost any religious profession as with Christianity.

But other people prize God's house, word and ministry more highly than their greatest possessions.

They dread losing these blessings even more than death.

Ungodly people are eager to avoid the conviction of their own consciences.

When trouble comes, they insist it is merely **chance** rather than acknowledging a warning from God.

## The ark comes to Beth-shemesh (verses 10–18)

The two cows recognized their great Owner—the Lord—whom **Hophni and Phinehas** had failed to recognize rightly.

God's providence attends even to animals and makes use of them for his purposes.

When the harvesters saw the ark return, they rejoiced.

Their joy over the ark was greater than their joy over the harvest.

When public worship and God's appointed ordinances return after persecution or trouble, there is good reason for great rejoicing.

## Judgment follows irreverent curiosity (verses 19–21)

People seriously offend God when they pry into secret matters that do not belong to them (Deuteronomy 29:29; Colossians 2:18).

Humanity's ruin began with the desire for forbidden knowledge.

The Lord will not allow his ark to be treated irreverently.

God cannot be mocked.

Those who refuse to revere his goodness or receive his gracious gifts with respect may be made to experience his justice.

Henry notes that **the number of people killed is expressed unusually in the original text**, and he considers **1,170** a probable interpretation.

This is his textual estimate—not a change to the biblical text or a claim about the exact numbering of any modern Bible edition.

The people now wanted the ark removed.

What foolishness, to run from one extreme to another!

Instead of demanding that the ark leave, they should have asked, **“How may we be reconciled to God and recover his favor?”** (Micah 6:6–7).

Similarly, when God's word awakens terror in a sinner's conscience, some refuse to accept their own guilt.

They quarrel with Scripture and push it away.

Many suppress their convictions and thereby reject the salvation offered to them.
