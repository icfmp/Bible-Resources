# Isaiah 57 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The blessed death of the righteous. | 1, 2 |
| The abominable idolatries of the Jewish nation. | 3–12 |
| Promises to the humble and contrite. | 13–21 |

## The righteous enter peace at death (verses 1 and 2)

The righteous are delivered from **death's sting**, though not from the experience of dying itself.

The careless world fails to recognize this.

Few people mourn the loss of a righteous person as **a loss to the whole community**.

Even fewer regard the death of such a person as **a public warning**.

Yet God may remove the righteous in compassion, to spare them from seeing, sharing in or being tempted by the evils approaching.

When a righteous person dies, that person enters **peace and rest**.

## The destructive slavery of idolatry (verses 3–12)

God summons **apostates and hypocrites** to stand before him.

When warned about their sins and the judgments approaching, they mocked his word.

The Jews committed idolatry **before the Babylonian exile**, though Henry says they did not return to the same practices afterward.

Their eagerness in serving false gods may shame **our indifference in the worship of the true God**.

Serving sin is degrading slavery.

People who willingly lower themselves toward hell should not be surprised at where that road leads.

Human beings are drawn toward religious practices that inflame their **unholy desires**.

They may consent to terrible acts if they imagine those acts will atone for previous crimes or purchase permission to indulge **a favorite sin**.

Henry applies this observation to pagan, Jewish and what he calls **antichristian idolatry**.

Anyone who replaces God with something else as the basis of hope and confidence is moving toward ruin.

Leaving the one right path leads into countless wrong paths.

The pleasures of sin quickly become tiring but **never satisfy**.

People who disregard God's word and Providence reveal that they have no reverent fear of him.

Sin brings **no true profit**.

It destroys and ruins.

## Mercy for the humbled, but no peace for the wicked (verses 13–21)

Idols and their worshipers will finally come to nothing.

But those who place their confidence in **God's grace** will enter heaven's joy.

The Lord has **no beginning, no end and no change**.

His name is holy, and everyone must acknowledge him as the holy God.

He cares tenderly for people who see their condition honestly, fear his judgment and are humbled before him.

He dwells with them to **revive and comfort their hearts**.

Even good people may begin to think harshly of God when their suffering continues for a long time.

Therefore God will not contend forever with them.

He will not abandon the work of his own hands or allow **the purchase of his Son's blood** to fail.

**Covetousness** is a sin that particularly exposes people to divine displeasure.

See how sinful sin is!

And notice that hardship cannot reform people unless **God's grace works within them**.

The Lord announces **perfect peace**.

It is proclaimed by **preaching lips** and sought by **praying lips**.

Christ came preaching peace to **Gentiles and Jews** alike.

The promise also reaches later generations, who were distant in time, as well as people distant in place.

But the wicked refuse healing through God's grace and therefore cannot receive the comfort that grace brings.

Uncontrolled desires and passions make them like **a troubled sea**.

Fear and guilt also disturb the pleasures they seek.

God has declared—and no one can undo his word—that **there is no peace for people who knowingly persist in sin**.

If we have been rescued from this condition, it is **only by God's grace**.

The Holy Spirit gives the new heart from which grateful praise, the **fruit of our lips**, rises.

Salvation, with all its fruits, hopes and comforts, is God's work.

To him belongs every part of the glory.

There is no peace for the wicked.

But let the wicked abandon their ways and their unrighteous thoughts.

Let them return to the Lord, who will show mercy and **abundantly pardon**.
