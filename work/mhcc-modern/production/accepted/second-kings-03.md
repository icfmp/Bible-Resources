# 2 Kings 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jehoram, king of Israel. | 1–5 |
| War with Moab, The intercession of Elisha. | 6–19 |
| Water supplied, Moab overcome. | 20–27 |

## Jehoram rules Israel (verses 1–5)

Jehoram learned something from God's judgments and removed the image of Baal.

Yet he continued to support worship of the golden calves.

Those who abandon only the sins that have brought them obvious loss, while clinging to sins they expect to profit from, have not truly repented or reformed.

## War with Moab; Elisha's intercession (verses 6–19)

Israel's king lamented the army's desperate lack of water and the danger it faced.

Yet he had summoned the other kings into the war.

Now he blamed Providence for the trouble.

Human foolishness corrupts our path, and then the heart becomes angry with the Lord (**Proverbs 19:3**).

Jehoshaphat did well to ask for the Lord's guidance, but he should have done it **before entering the war**.

Even good people sometimes neglect their duties until hardship drives them to perform them.

Wicked people often benefit from companionship with faithful believers.

Elisha told the kings to dig the valley full of trenches to receive water.

This tested their faith and obedience.

People expecting God's blessings should prepare to receive them—digging pools for promised rain, as in **the Valley of Baca**, making it a place of springs (**Psalm 84:6**).

There is no need to speculate where the water came from.

God is not restricted to ordinary natural causes.

People who sincerely seek the dew of his grace will receive it and become more than conquerors through it.

## Water comes; Moab is defeated (verses 20–27)

It is a great blessing to live among people who are able to approach God through effective prayer.

A kingdom may survive and prosper because of the earnest prayers of those who are precious in God's sight.

We should esteem most highly the people whom he values most.

When sinners assure themselves that all is peaceful and safe, destruction may suddenly overtake them.

Despair then follows their reckless confidence.

Under Satan's influence, human beings have committed horrible acts that naturally make the heart recoil.

The **king of Moab sacrificed his son** in such a desperate and dreadful act.

It is wise not to drive even very wicked people to desperation when that can be avoided.

Rather, we should leave them to God's judgment.
