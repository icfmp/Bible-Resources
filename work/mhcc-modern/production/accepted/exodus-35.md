## The Sabbath is to be kept (verses 1–3)

Christ's yoke is gentle and easy.

Because of him, our Sabbath duties are more joyful and its restrictions less burdensome than they were for the Jews under the old covenant.

But that only makes us more guilty if we neglect them.

God wisely gave us a Sabbath for merciful purposes.

It is sinful to treat this gift with contempt.

How can we despise a blessed day that the generous Lord has given us to grow in grace with the church on earth and prepare for happiness with the church in heaven?

## Freely given materials and skilled work (verses 4–19)

The tabernacle would be dedicated to God's honor and used in his service.

Therefore, every contribution toward it was an offering to the Lord.

The rule was simple: whoever had a willing heart should bring a gift.

Everyone with the necessary skill was to do the work.

God distributes different gifts among his people.

Each person should use the gift received in service to others (1 Peter 4:10).

Those with wealth were to provide materials.

Those with skill were to use their abilities for the sanctuary.

The two groups needed each other, and the tabernacle needed them both (1 Corinthians 12:7–21).

## The people give willingly (verses 20–29)

Without willing hearts, even very expensive offerings would be hateful to God.

But with genuine willingness, even the smallest gift is accepted.

We show that our hearts are willing when we gladly help the work of God advance.

People who work diligently and contentedly in humble occupations are just as acceptable to God as those who perform impressive public service.

The women who spun goats' hair were called **wise-hearted**, because they did the work wholeheartedly for the Lord.

Likewise, a laborer, craftsperson or servant who carries out ordinary work in faith and reverence for God may show as much wisdom in that calling as a highly useful minister.

Both are accepted by the same Lord.

Our wisdom and duty lie in giving God the glory for our abilities and using them for his purposes, whether we have many talents or few.

## Bezalel and Oholiab lead and teach the work (verses 30–35)

God himself appointed the master craftsmen.

This prevented disputes over who should lead the work.

It also ensured that everyone who took part knew whose instructions to follow and to whom they were accountable.

God filled those whom he called by name with his Spirit.

Skill—even skill used in ordinary trades—is God's gift from above.

Yet some people are very good at assigning tasks to others.

They can explain exactly what everyone else should do, but refuse to lift a finger themselves.

Such people behave like lazy servants.

Bezalel and Oholiab were to do more than invent plans.

They had to carry out the work themselves and teach others to do it.

Those who lead should teach.

And anyone to whom God has given knowledge should gladly share it for the benefit of other people.
