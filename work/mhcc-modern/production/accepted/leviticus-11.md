# Leviticus 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter heading

**What animals were clean and unclean.**

## Clean and unclean animals

Henry sees several purposes behind the laws distinguishing clean and unclean animals.

**First**, they tested Israel's obedience, just as Adam's refusal to eat from the tree of knowledge was a test.

They also taught self-denial and control over bodily appetite.

**Second**, they kept Israel distinct from other nations.

Many of the forbidden animals were objects of pagan superstition or idolatry.

**Third**, the laws taught the people to distinguish between the holy and the unholy when choosing companions and close relationships.

**Fourth**, the law prohibited not only eating unclean animals but also touching them.

Those who wish to avoid sin must also be careful to avoid its temptations and circumstances that draw them close to it.

The regulations contained very detailed exceptions.

Those details required continuous attentiveness and precise obedience, training the people to obey God's commands.

Christians are now free from these burdensome ceremonial rules.

Yet we must not misuse our freedom.

The Lord has redeemed and called his people to be holy as he is holy.

We must turn away from the sinful patterns of the world and avoid unnecessary close ties with those who remain spiritually dead.

We should be eager to do good, devoted to God and glad to keep company with his people.
