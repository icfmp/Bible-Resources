# Hosea 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Under a figure, is represented the shameful idolatry of the ten tribes. | 1–7 |
| The calling of the Gentiles, and the uniting Israel and Judah under the Messiah. | 8–11 |

## The unfaithfulness of Israel represented (verses 1–7)

Israel was **prosperous** when Hosea boldly exposed its sins and predicted its destruction.

We should not flatter sinners merely because they enjoy worldly success.

That success will not continue if they persist in their wrongdoing.

The prophet had to show Israel **how hateful its idolatry was**.

Giving a created thing the glory belonging only to God is an offense against him.

It is like a wife giving herself to a stranger instead of remaining faithful to her husband.

The Lord surely had wise reasons for the command he gave Hosea.

The prophet's experience would provide a moving image of **God's undeserved goodness and tireless patience**, compared with **Israel's rebellion and ingratitude**.

We would become tired and bitter after enduring far less unfaithfulness from other people than the unfaithfulness with which we grieve **God's Spirit**.

We should be ready to bear whatever cross the Lord assigns us.

The names of Hosea's children announced the people's impending ruin.

The name of the first child, **Jezreel**, meaning **“dispersion”** according to Henry, foretold **the fall of the royal family**.

The second, **Lo-ruhamah**, meant **“not loved”** or **“not shown mercy”**, announcing that the nation would be abandoned.

God had shown Israel great mercy, but the people had abused his gifts.

Sin turns away God's mercy, even from people who profess to belong to him.

If **pardoning mercy** is withdrawn, no other mercy can be relied upon.

Nevertheless, although some are broken off through unbelief, **God will preserve a church in the world until the end**.

Our salvation comes through **his mercy**, not through any merit of ours.

When he undertakes to save, no one can prevent him.

## God's people will be gathered (verses 8–11)

A third child was called **Lo-ammi**, meaning **“not my people”**.

The name announced Israel's temporary rejection.

God disowned the relationship the nation had claimed.

We love God because **he loved us first**.

But when we are cast out of covenant fellowship, **our own folly** is to blame.

Even in wrath, God remembers mercy.

The rejection would be **neither total nor final**.

The same hand that wounded the people would reach out to heal them.

Precious promises are made here concerning **the Israel of God**.

Henry notes that some interpreters expect their fullest realization only through **a future general conversion of the Jewish people**.

The apostles **Paul (Romans 9:25–26)** and **Peter (1 Peter 2:10)** also apply this promise to the gospel and to **Jews and Gentiles being brought together**.

To believe in Christ is to receive him **as our Head**, willingly submitting to **his guidance and rule**.

Let us pray for the glorious day when **one Lord will reign over all the earth**.
