## Child sacrifice and dishonoring parents (verses 1–9)

The cruelty of ancient idol worshipers who sacrificed their own children rightly shocks us.

But Henry warns that parents can also bring spiritual ruin on their children by teaching them evil, setting a wicked example and showing them habits of sin.

He regards this as an especially grievous wrong because it helps lead children toward eternal destruction.

What account will such parents give before God, and what will they say when they face their children at judgment?

Children, meanwhile, should remember the severe judgment pronounced on anyone who cursed a father or mother.

Christ confirmed the continuing moral force of this command.

These regulations repeat laws already given, this time with explicit penalties.

People ought to avoid evil because God's law declares it sinful.

But if they disregard that, they should at least fear the severe penalties for their own preservation.

Amid these solemn laws stands the command to consecrate ourselves and be holy.

The Lord himself makes his people holy and will accomplish his work, even when it is difficult.

His grace does not make our own effort unnecessary.

Rather, it powerfully encourages us to work out our salvation, because God is at work within us.

## Repeated commands and the call to holiness (verses 10–27)

The laws in this passage repeat earlier instructions.

Such repetition was necessary: people need truth explained again and again.

How greatly we should praise God for showing us the evil of sin and the sure way to be delivered from it.

May his grace enable us to honor the teaching of God our Savior through everything we do.

May we refuse to participate in fruitless works of darkness and instead expose them.
