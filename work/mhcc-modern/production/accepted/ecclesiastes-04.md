## The misery caused by oppression (verses 1–3)

Solomon grieved when he saw **power overcoming justice**.

Wherever we look, we see sorrowful evidence of human wickedness and misery.

People create suffering both for themselves and for one another.

Those treated cruelly may become so miserable that they wish they had never been born.

But a godly person, though suffering in this life, has no true reason to make that wish.

Such a person may be **glorifying the Lord even in the fire** and will ultimately enjoy everlasting happiness.

The ungodly have still greater reason to desire that their present lives continue, despite their troubles, because if they die unrepentant their future condition will be far worse.

If earthly and human things were the highest possible good, then given all the world's oppression, **not existing would be preferable**.

But they are not the highest good.

## Envy and indolence both cause trouble (verses 4–6)

Those who work diligently and succeed often attract envy and opposition.

Other people, seeing the difficulties and hostility faced by hardworking neighbors, imagine they would find greater happiness in doing nothing.

But idleness is a sin that brings **its own punishment**.

Let us earn enough through honest work to obtain our necessities—the metaphorical **one handful**—rather than grasping for **two overflowing handfuls** and filling our lives with anxiety.

Moderate effort and moderate gain are best.

## Selfish greed cannot be satisfied (verses 7 and 8)

Often the more people possess, the more they desire.

They become so absorbed in accumulating wealth that they cannot enjoy what they already own.

**Selfishness lies at the heart of this folly**.

Such people care for almost no one—not even enough for themselves to allow sufficient rest for themselves or their workers.

They may have everything necessary for their employment and family but still feel that **their eyes have not seen enough**.

Worldliness can rob people not only of God's favor and eternal life but also of this life's ordinary pleasures.

Relatives or strangers may inherit the fortune they accumulated, yet will feel no gratitude for the sacrifice.

Greed grows with practice and advancing age, sometimes becoming strongest when the grave is near.

How astonishing that people claiming to follow **Christ, who became poor for our sake though he was rich**, can still obsessively hoard money while excusing themselves through talk of prudent provision and the dangers of extravagance!

## Mutual assistance and fellowship (verses 9–12)

Someone working hard to support loved ones gains more satisfaction than a miser laboring only to accumulate money.

Cooperation makes many things safer and more effective.

This is especially true of **fellowship between Christians**.

Believers strengthen one another through encouragement and friendly correction.

They warm one another's hearts by speaking of **Christ's love** and singing his praise together.

Let us use our opportunities for Christian friendship well.

Not everything in such fellowship is vanity, although its joys remain imperfect in this life.

When two people unite in holy love and fellowship, **Christ by his Spirit joins them**, forming a **threefold cord**.

## Even royal favor changes (verses 13–16)

People are rarely satisfied for long.

They become eager for change and easily grow tired of their rulers.

That is nothing new.

Kings can find themselves rejected by people they made great efforts to help.

Such is the emptiness and frustration of worldly honor.

But the willing servants of **our King, the Lord Jesus**, rejoice in him alone, and their love for him will grow forever.
