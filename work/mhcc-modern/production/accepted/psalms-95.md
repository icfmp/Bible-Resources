# Psalm 95 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| part. An exhortation to praise God. | 1–7 |
| A warning not to tempt Him. | 7–11 |

## Worshiping the Lord and our Shepherd (verses 1–7)

Whenever we come into God's presence, we should come with **thanksgiving**.

There is no shortage of reasons to praise him; the problem is often that our hearts are not ready to do so.

How great is the God to whom the whole earth and everything in it belong!

He directs and disposes of all creation.

**The Lord Jesus is the great God whom we are called to praise**.

Among his titles are **the mighty God** and **God over all, blessed forever**.

He has received authority in heaven and on earth.

He is our God, and we should praise him.

He is our Savior and **the source of our true happiness**.

The gospel church is his flock.

Christ is the **great and good Shepherd** who searched for his people when they were lost and brought them safely into his fold.

## Hearing Christ's voice today (verses 7–11)

Christ calls his people to listen to his voice.

If we call him Master and Lord, we ought to become **willing and obedient servants**.

Listen to his teaching, his commandments and the voice of his Spirit speaking through them.

Do not merely hear; **pay attention and yield**.

We must hear Christ's voice **today**.

Today's opportunity will not last forever.

We should use it while it remains.

To hear Christ truly is to believe in him.

**Hardness of heart lies beneath all distrust of God**.

Other people's sins should warn us not to repeat them.

Israel's murmuring in the wilderness was recorded for our instruction.

God is not ruled by passions as we are, yet **he is truly angry at sin and at sinners**, as Henry explicitly says.

His threats are as certain as his promises.

We must watch the evil tendencies of our hearts, which would draw us away from him.

There is **a rest appointed for believers**, an eternal refreshment that begins in this life and reaches its fullness in the life to come.

This is the rest God calls **his own rest**.
