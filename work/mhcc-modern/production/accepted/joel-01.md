# Joel 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A plague of locusts. | 1–7 |
| All sorts of people are called to lament it. | 8–13 |
| They are to look to God. | 14–20 |

## The devastating plague of locusts (verses 1–7)

Even the oldest people could not remember a disaster like the one about to strike Judah.

Swarms of insects would consume **the crops and fruit of the land**.

Henry says the language can also describe destruction by **a foreign invading army**.

He sees a possible reference to **the devastation caused by the Chaldeans**.

God is **the Lord of hosts**.

Every created thing obeys his command.

When he chooses, he can humble a proud and rebellious people through **the smallest and most despised creatures**.

God is just to remove comforts that people misuse through **luxury and excess**.

The more we place our happiness in bodily pleasure, the more painful it is when outward blessings are taken away.

Every extra earthly comfort we treat as **necessary for happiness** increases our vulnerability to loss.

## Everyone is called to mourn (verses 8–13)

Those who labor only for **food that perishes** will eventually find that their efforts bring disappointment.

People whose joy depends upon bodily pleasures lose that joy as soon as the pleasures are interrupted or removed.

**Spiritual joy**, however, can flourish precisely when outward comforts fail.

Notice how uncertain and temporary created comforts are.

We need to live in constant dependence upon **God and his providential care**.

Notice too the destruction produced by sin.

When poverty causes **true worship and godliness** to weaken and deprives people of spiritual instruction, the judgment is especially severe.

Yet God's judgments can also be **merciful awakenings**, stirring his people from sleep and drawing their hearts back to **Christ and his salvation**.

## The people must turn to God (verses 14–20)

The sorrow of the people was to become **repentance and humility before God**.

Alongside signs of grief and shame, they were to **confess and mourn their sins**.

A day was to be set apart for this purpose.

People would leave ordinary work to give more attention to God's worship and abstain from **food and drink** as they fasted.

Everyone had contributed to the nation's guilt, and everyone shared in its calamity.

Therefore **everyone was called to join in repentance**.

When joy disappears from God's house, sincere religion declines and love grows cold, **it is time to cry out to the Lord**.

The prophet described the severity of the disaster.

Even animals suffered because of human wrongdoing.

Those who cry to God only because they want food and drink, without concern for their sin, behave little better than animals.

Yet even an animal's cries for food shame the hardness of people who **never cry to God for anything**.

Whatever becomes of nations or churches that persist in wickedness, believers will still find **comfort in being accepted by God**, when the unrepentant face his judgment.
