# 2 Corinthians 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle threatens obstinate offenders. | 1–6 |
| He prays for their reformation. | 7–10 |
| And ends the epistle with a salutation and blessing. | 11–14 |

## A warning and an examination of genuine faith (verses 1–6)

The Lord patiently bears with sinners because he is gracious.

But **his patience does not mean that he will never judge**.

Those who remain obstinate and refuse to repent will eventually meet the judgment they have resisted.

At the crucifixion, Jesus appeared weak and helpless to the world.

But **his resurrection and living power** revealed that he was truly divine.

Similarly, the apostles often appeared insignificant and contemptible in human eyes.

Yet God used them as instruments through which **his power** became evident.

Paul called the Corinthians to examine themselves.

They should test their **attitudes, conduct and spiritual experience** as gold is tested with a touchstone.

If they could see genuine evidence that **Christ had accepted them**, they ought also to recognize the authenticity of Paul's apostolic service.

We should examine whether **Jesus Christ lives in us**.

His presence is known through the Holy Spirit's work, the graces he produces and the establishing of Christ's kingdom in our hearts.

Let us question our own souls sincerely.

We are either **genuine Christians** or deceiving ourselves.

Unless Christ dwells within us through his Spirit and the power of his love, our claimed faith is dead and we remain unapproved by the One who will judge us.

## Pray for holiness rather than merely escape from suffering (verses 7–10)

The greatest good we can pray for, both for ourselves and for those we love, is that **we should not sin**.

We have far more reason to pray to be preserved from **doing evil** than merely to be spared from **suffering evil**.

Paul wanted the Corinthians not only to turn away from wrongdoing, but also to **grow in grace and holiness**.

When we warn other people, we should also pray earnestly that they may stop doing wrong and learn to do what is good.

We should gladly see others become strong in Christ's grace, even if their growth reveals our own weakness.

And we should ask God to enable us to use every ability and opportunity he has entrusted to us.

## Final encouragement and blessing (verses 11–14)

Paul ended his letter with several valuable exhortations.

God is **the Author of peace and Lover of harmony**.

He loves his people and is willing to live at peace with them.

We should therefore seek to live in such a way that separation from believing friends is only temporary.

We long to meet again in the world where **parting will be unknown**.

Paul's blessing asks that believers receive every benefit made available through **the free grace and favor of Jesus Christ**, purposed by **the Father's free love**, and **applied and given by the Holy Spirit**.

What a fitting conclusion to a letter containing both stern warnings and tender pastoral care!
