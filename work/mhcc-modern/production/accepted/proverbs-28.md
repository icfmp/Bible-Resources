# Proverbs 28 — Modern English

## Verse 1 — Sin makes cowards

Sinful people are often fearful. Those walking in their duty before God can remain courageous even amid real difficulties.

## Verse 2 — Public wrongdoing disturbs a nation

A nation's sins undermine its public peace and stability.

## Verse 3 — Oppression by the needy

When poor or needy people obtain power to exploit others, they may oppress more severely than the already wealthy.

## Verse 4 — Sin encourages sin

Wicked people support and strengthen one another in their wrongdoing.

## Verse 5 — Seek God to understand rightly

Seeking the Lord is evidence of some true understanding and one of the best means of gaining more.

## Verse 6 — Godly poverty is better

An honest and reverent poor person is better than a wealthy but ungodly one, having greater inward comfort and bringing more good to the world.

## Verse 7 — Bad company shames a family

Those who join the company of reckless people both grieve their parents and bring shame on them.

## Verse 8 — Dishonest gain cannot endure

Wealth acquired through evil practices will not last, however large it grows. God may use its loss to help the poor and honor his justice.

## Verse 9 — Do not reject God's commands while praying

The prayer that displeases God comes from someone who **stubbornly refuses obedience** to his known word.

## Verse 10 — The misery of wicked success

The apparent success of ungodly people ultimately contributes to their own suffering.

## Verse 11 — Wealth can blind judgment

Rich people are often flattered until they imagine themselves superior to everyone else.

## Verse 12 — Freedom for the righteous benefits the land

A nation enjoys honor and well-being when faithful people have liberty.

## Verse 13 — Confess sin to receive mercy

Excusing and concealing sin cannot bring real peace. Whoever **confesses sin humbly with genuine repentance and faith** finds mercy. **The Son of God is our great atoning sacrifice**; in our guilt and danger we may seek the mercy that reigns through righteousness to eternal life in Jesus Christ.

## Verse 14 — Reverent fear without terror

There is a holy fear that brings happiness. Faith and love deliver believers from the fear of eternal condemnation, but we should always **fear offending God and falling into sin**.

## Verse 15 — A wicked ruler is a predator

Whatever title society gives a cruel ruler, Scripture compares that ruler to **a roaring lion and a roaming bear**.

## Verse 16 — Oppression is foolish

Oppressors lack understanding and damage even their own honor, ease and security.

## Verse 17 — The burden of murder

People guilty of murder may be pursued by overwhelming terror. Henry speaks of their deserved punishment and the reluctance of others to protect them from it.

## Verse 18 — Uprightness brings security

Integrity gives people holy confidence even in dangerous times, while dishonest and deceitful people can never feel truly secure.

## Verse 19 — Honest labor provides

Diligent people choose the path toward a stable and comfortable livelihood.

## Verse 20 — Holiness and honesty before quick wealth

The best route to happiness is a holy, honest life, not gaining a fortune rapidly without regard for right or wrong.

## Verse 21 — Judgment must be impartial

Justice is corrupted whenever any consideration other than what is truly right governs a decision.

## Verse 22 — Riches can vanish

People who rush to become wealthy seldom reflect on how quickly God may strip them of riches and leave them poor.

## Verse 23 — Faithful rebuke is better than flattery

After reflection, most people value someone who corrected them honestly more than someone who merely soothed them with praise.

## Verse 24 — Do not exploit parents

It is wicked to treat robbing one's parents as no sin, whether through manipulation, threats, squandering their goods or running up debts against them.

## Verse 25 — Trusting grace brings peace

Those who continually depend upon God and his grace, living by faith, find inward rest.

## Verse 26 — Do not trust the deceitful heart

A fool places confidence in personal strength, merit and righteousness, and in a heart that is deceitful and has already misled that person many times.

## Verse 27 — Look for people in need

A selfish person not only fails to seek opportunities for compassion, but deliberately looks away when the need becomes visible.

## Verse 28 — Wicked authority and Christ in Proverbs

When power belongs to the wicked, wise people may withdraw from public office. Henry concludes that even where Proverbs seem to speak least directly of Christ, careful reading will uncover truths **that lead to him**.
