# 1 Samuel 30 — Modern English

## Ziklag is plundered (verses 1–6)

When we travel in the path of duty, we may confidently hope God will care for our families during our absence.

But we cannot claim that assurance when we deliberately abandon duty.

If we return from a journey and find our home peaceful rather than devastated, as David found his, we should thank the Lord.

David's men complained bitterly about him.

People who possess great faith must expect severe trials.

But notice that David was brought to this very low position immediately before being raised to the throne.

When circumstances appear worst for God's church and people, improvement may already be near.

**David strengthened himself in the Lord his God.**

His men's sorrow turned into anger and impatience, adding to their suffering.

David had more cause than any of them to grieve, yet he handled the calamity better.

They gave their passions free rein.

He **put his spiritual graces to work**.

While they discouraged one another, he kept his spirit calm by seeking encouragement in God.

Those who have chosen the Lord as their God can find strength in him even in the darkest circumstances.

## David pursues the Amalekites (verses 7–15)

We should acknowledge God in all our ways, even when—as here—the justice of what we are doing appears beyond doubt.

Then we may trust him to direct our steps, as David did.

David showed tenderness toward his exhausted men.

He refused to make them continue beyond their strength.

The Son of David likewise considers the condition of his followers.

They do not all possess equal energy or strength in spiritual effort and conflict.

Where we are weak, Christ is kind.

More than that, **his strength is made perfect in weakness** (**2 Corinthians 12:9–10**).

A young Egyptian servant, abandoned and almost dead, became the means by which David found the raiders.

God used this cruelly treated servant in the defeat of the Amalekites.

He hears the cry of the oppressed.

Those who withhold compassion from people in distress do not deserve the name of true Israelites.

We should neither harm nor refuse kindness to anyone.

One day, even the most insignificant person may have the power to repay a kindness—or an injury.

## The stolen goods are recovered (verses 16–20)

Sinners are often closest to ruin when they feel most secure and proclaim peace and safety.

They put the thought of the coming day of judgment far from themselves.

Indulgence in bodily pleasures gives our spiritual enemies a special advantage.

Eating, drinking and dancing have provided a pleasant-looking path by which many have gone down to death.

David recovered everything that had been stolen.

Nothing remained lost, and much more was gained.

## David shares the spoils fairly (verses 21–31)

Whatever God gives us should be used to do good.

David distributed the recovered goods with both justice and generosity.

People who take pleasure in imposing hardships on their brothers and sisters, caring nothing if others go hungry as long as they themselves have plenty, are truly wicked.

David treated his friends generously and kindly.

Everyone who recognizes the Lord as the Giver of abundance should distribute what has been received with **fairness and liberality**.
