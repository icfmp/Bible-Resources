# Amos 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The near approach of the ruin of Israel. | 1–3 |
| Oppression reproved. | 4–10 |
| A famine of the word of God. | 11–14 |

## Israel is ripe for judgment (verses 1–3)

Amos saw a **basket of summer fruit**, picked and ready to eat.

This showed that Israel was **ripe for destruction**, because the period of God's patience was nearing its end.

Summer fruit cannot be kept fresh through winter.

It must be used quickly.

Yet the people would not acknowledge either **God's righteousness** or **their own wrongdoing** when the announced judgments came.

Sinners keep postponing repentance because they mistake God's delay for **a promise that he will never judge them**.

## Oppressors despise worship and exploit the poor (verses 4–10)

The wealthy and powerful were especially guilty of oppression and idolatry.

They resented **Sabbaths and new-moon festivals**, when ordinary business stopped.

Many people calling themselves Christians behave similarly.

For people whose hearts are wholly worldly, a Sabbath becomes either **a day to profane** or **a tiresome obligation**.

Yet how could we use the day better than in **fellowship with God**?

Even while attending religious duties, these people were mentally busy with **trade and markets**.

They thought worship wasted valuable business time.

People who would rather sell grain than worship God show **little regard for him**.

And those who lose reverence for God often soon lose **ordinary honesty toward other people**.

They cheated their customers.

They exploited **neighbors' ignorance and desperate need**, especially in trading the food needed by **working poor people**.

How many forms of greed and fraud make commerce hateful to the Lord!

People whose wealth comes from the misery of the poor will eventually be ruined by their own riches.

To despise the poor is to **dishonor their Maker**, who created rich and poor alike.

God remembers such injustice.

He will call those who commit it to account.

Desolation will arrive **when they least expect it**.

All earthly pleasures, possessions and even life itself are uncertain.

**In the midst of life, we are near death**.

How bitter will be the grief following sinful and sensual pleasures!

## A famine of God's word (verses 11–14)

The worst sign of divine displeasure is **a famine of hearing the word of God**.

Such a famine is especially terrible during distress, when people most need God's word.

Some will think little of its absence.

Others will feel the loss deeply and travel great distances simply to **hear a faithful sermon**.

They will suffer the loss of mercies other people carelessly threw away.

When God judges a church that has turned from him, its members' plans to invent a path of salvation **will accomplish nothing**.

Even the most attractive and energetic young people will perish spiritually **without the water of life that only Jesus Christ gives**.

Let us value the privileges we have, seek to benefit from them and fear losing them through sin.
