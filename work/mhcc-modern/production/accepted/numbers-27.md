## The daughters of Zelophehad seek an inheritance (verses 1–11)

Zelophehad's five daughters were concerned about their inheritance.

Their father had died, and they had no brothers to inherit land on behalf of the family.

They trusted that God would keep his promise to give Canaan to Israel.

They wanted their own family to share in that promised inheritance.

They made their request modestly and honestly, without hidden resentment or complaining.

Henry sees three praiseworthy qualities in their conduct.

**First, faith:** they firmly believed God's power and promise concerning Canaan.

**Second, earnest desire:** they wanted both a place and a family name in the promised country, which pictured heaven.

**Third, respect for their father:** they treasured his memory after his death.

Their father had not been guilty of the particular rebellion that would exclude his children from a claim.

It comforts dying parents when, although they may have suffered because of their own sins, they do not know of any wrongdoing that will bring judgment upon their children.

God himself decided the matter.

His care extends beyond nations to individual households.

He governs their circumstances according to his wisdom.

The daughters' request was granted.

Those who seek an inheritance in the land of promise will obtain what they seek, with other blessings added.

## Moses is warned that he will die (verses 12–14)

Moses had to die.

But God granted him the comfort of seeing the land he had promised Israel.

That sight of Canaan pointed toward Moses' believing expectation of a better, heavenly country.

Death would not finally cut him off.

It would bring him into the rest already enjoyed by the faithful patriarchs.

If we have lived as they lived and hope to reach the same peaceful end, we need not fear the passage through the valley of death.

## Joshua is appointed Moses' successor (verses 15–23)

Envious people dislike those who succeed them.

Moses was not like that.

We should pray and work for the next generation, so that faith may continue and flourish even after we are buried.

God appointed Joshua to succeed Moses.

Joshua had shown courage in the battle against Amalek, humility while assisting Moses, and faith and honesty when he opposed the unbelieving report of the spies.

God chose a man in whom his Spirit dwelt.

Joshua had the grace of the Spirit.

He feared God, hated greed and acted from principle.

He possessed the judgment and courage needed to lead.

He also had the Spirit's prophetic gift.

No one is fully qualified for service in Christ's church by human abilities alone.

The grace and gifts of the Holy Spirit are indispensable.

Joshua's succession also reminds us that the law was given through Moses.

Because of sin, the law cannot bring us into the heavenly inheritance.

Grace and truth come through Jesus Christ, who brings salvation to everyone who believes.
