# Psalm 35 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David prays for safety. | 1–10 |
| He complains of his enemies. | 11–16 |
| And calls upon God to support him. | 17–28 |

## David prays for protection (verses 1–10)

It is no surprise for righteous people or righteous causes to have enemies. The hostility of **the serpent's offspring against the woman's Seed** has been present since the beginning.

David in affliction, Christ in suffering, the persecuted church and the tempted Christian all look to Almighty God to defend their cause.

We often justify anxiety and anger over unjust treatment by saying we gave others no reason to mistreat us.

Yet our innocence should actually bring comfort: we may more confidently entrust the case to the Lord.

David asked God to make his presence known in the trial and to grant **inward consolation amid outward distress**.

If God's Spirit assures us inwardly that he is our salvation, we need nothing else to make us truly happy.

**If God is our Friend, it does not matter who opposes us**.

Henry understands David's words against his enemies as **prophetic announcements of just divine judgment**, anticipating the fate of Christ's enemies.

He adds an essential distinction: Christians must not desire or pray for the destruction of human opponents. The enemies whose ruin we should seek are **our sinful desires and the evil spirits that seek our destruction**.

A traveler caught at night on a slippery, dangerous road pictures the sinner moving through temptation.

Having entrusted his cause to the Lord, David expected deliverance and pledged to praise God **with all his strength**.

Such language is even more fitting when applied to heavenly rescue in Christ.

## David describes the ingratitude of enemies (verses 11–16)

Few accusations are worse than that someone has repaid kindness with cruelty.

That was the character of David's enemies. In this experience he pictured Christ.

David had shown genuine tenderness while his enemies suffered.

We should grieve over the sins of people who do not grieve for themselves.

Doing good to the ungrateful is never ultimately wasted.

Let us learn patience and gentleness from David—and still more from **Christ's example**.

## David asks God to rescue his soul (verses 17–28)

God's people seek peaceful lives, yet their enemies often devise false accusations and plots against them.

David prayed: **My soul is in danger. Lord, rescue it. It belongs to you, the Father of spirits; claim and save what is yours. Do not stand far off as though I were a stranger.**

The One who exalted the once-suffering Redeemer will also defend his people.

The roaring lion will not destroy their souls, any more than he could destroy **Christ, their Surety**.

Believers entrust their souls to God's hands. Through faith they are united with Christ, precious in God's sight and assured of deliverance from ultimate destruction.

Their rescue will lead them to **give thanks in heaven**.
