# Jeremiah 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The remains of the dead exposed. | 1–3 |
| The stupidity of the people, compared with the instinct of the brute creation. | 4–13 |
| The alarm of the invasion, and lamentation. | 14–22 |

## Even the dead are dishonored (verses 1–3)

No real injury can be inflicted upon a body after death.

Nevertheless, dishonoring the remains of wicked people may **alarm those still alive**.

It reminds us that God's justice and punishment extend **beyond the grave**.

Whatever happens to us in this life, let us humble ourselves before the Lord and ask him for mercy.

## Judah ignores reason, conscience, Providence and Scripture (verses 4–13)

What brought this disaster upon the people?

Henry identifies **four causes**.

**First, they refused to listen to reason.** They would not exercise ordinary prudence concerning the eternal welfare of their own souls. Sin is a falling back—from the road to life toward the road to destruction.

**Second, they refused to listen to conscience.** They would not take the first step of genuine repentance: **asking seriously what wrong they had done**, convinced that they had acted wrongly.

**Third, they failed to consider God's Providence and recognize his voice within it** (**Jeremiah 8:7**). They did not use the seasons of grace he gave them. Many boast of religious knowledge, yet without **the teaching of God's Spirit**, even the natural instinct of birds and other creatures guides them more reliably than their supposed wisdom.

**Fourth, they refused to attend to the written word of God.** Many people possess ample opportunities to learn: **Bibles, ministers and other means of grace**. Yet they have all these resources without truly benefiting from them.

They would soon be ashamed of their own schemes.

The people posing as especially wise were **the priests and false prophets**.

Those leaders flattered others in their sins until the flattery led toward destruction.

They quieted fear and silenced complaints with the assurance, **“Everything is well.”**

Self-interested teachers promise **peace when there is no peace**.

Thus sinners encourage one another to continue in wrongdoing.

But when the day of judgment comes, they will find nowhere to hide.

## There is healing in Christ, but the people refuse it (verses 14–22)

At last the people began to recognize **God's hand raised against them**.

When God himself confronts us, everything opposing us becomes frightening.

Because **salvation is found only in the Lord**, we should make use of **the present opportunity**.

Is there no medicine to heal this sick and dying nation?

Is there no skilled and faithful physician able to apply it?

**Yes. God can help and heal.**

If sinners die from their wounds, they bear responsibility for rejecting the remedy.

**The blood of Jesus Christ is the balm of Gilead.**

**His Spirit is the Physician there**, completely sufficient to heal.

The people **could be healed, but would not receive the cure**.

So sinners die **unforgiven and unchanged**, because they refuse to come to Christ for salvation.
