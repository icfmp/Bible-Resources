# Joshua 12 — Modern English

## The two kings conquered under Moses (verses 1–6)

New blessings should not make us forget the mercies God gave us earlier.

Nor should the new work he gives us cause us to forget the good work others accomplished before us.

Moses gave part of the land promised to Israel to the two and a half tribes on the east side of the Jordan.

It was rich, fertile land.

But Joshua was to give the rest to Israel's tribes.

In this Henry sees a picture of Christ, the true Joshua, giving the inheritance of heavenly Canaan to all God's spiritual Israel.

## The kings conquered under Joshua (verses 7–24)

The record names **thirty-one kings** defeated by Joshua.

Henry describes Canaan as exceptionally fruitful and pleasant at the time Israel entered it, so that many petty rulers could live there in relative prosperity.

He contrasts that description with the land's condition in his own day.

He interprets its loss of fruitfulness as connected to the Jewish rejection of Christ and appeals to **Deuteronomy 29:23**.

This is Henry's historical and theological assessment, not an independently verified modern claim about the land or its people.

God's judgments are righteous.

We should also remember that he fulfills his promises.

Let the record of former deliverances teach us gratitude for our own mercies.

Let it also lead us to trust God for the blessings still promised to his church.
