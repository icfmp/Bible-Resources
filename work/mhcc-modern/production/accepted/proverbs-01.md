# Proverbs 1 — Modern English

## Why these proverbs teach us (verses 1–6)

The lessons in Proverbs are straightforward and will greatly benefit people who **recognize how little they know and how much they need to be taught**.

Young people who govern their lives by these instructions will grow in both understanding and discretion.

Solomon teaches the most important truths. But **one greater than Solomon** speaks here.

**Christ speaks through his word and Spirit**.

He is the Word and **the Wisdom of God**, and he himself has become wisdom for us.

## Reverence for God and instruction from parents (verses 7–9)

Fools are people who possess no real wisdom and follow whatever schemes they invent, disregarding both reason and reverence for God.

Children are rational creatures.

When we explain what they should do, **we ought also to explain why**.

Yet children are fallen and often stubborn, so instruction must be accompanied by **rules and authority**.

Let us consider God's truths and commandments worthy of the highest honor.

If we truly treasure them, their excellence will be evident in our lives.

## Refusing to follow sinners (verses 10–19)

Wicked people eagerly invite others onto paths leading to destruction.

Sinners like company in their sins, and for that reason **their responsibility becomes even greater**.

Young people should be exceedingly careful.

**Do not consent.**

Do not speak as they speak, act as they act or do what they want you to do.

Do not share in their wrongdoing.

How terrible that someone could enjoy another person's destruction!

They prize worldly wealth as though it were something truly substantial or precious.

But many thousands have ruined themselves by **valuing the world's riches too highly**.

Sinners imagine that wrongdoing will benefit them.

The path of sin, however, runs **downhill** and soon escapes their control.

Those who want to avoid destruction in this life and the next must refuse even **the first step** onto that path.

Greed drives people into practices that shorten their lives and harm others.

What would a person gain by acquiring the entire world while losing life—**and how much worse to lose the soul**?

## Wisdom calls sinners to repentance (verses 20–33)

Solomon has shown the danger of listening to Satan's temptations.

He now shows how dangerous it is **to refuse God's invitation**.

**Christ himself is Wisdom**.

He addresses three groups.

**First, the simple:** people content with shallow ideas of good and evil, prejudiced against the Lord's ways, who flatter themselves about their sins.

**Second, mockers:** proud people who laugh at everything holy and turn sincere religion into a joke.

**Third, fools:** those who positively hate being instructed and have become deeply hostile to godliness.

The command is plain: **Turn when Wisdom rebukes you**.

We have not responded rightly to rebuke unless we turn **from evil to good**.

God gives extraordinary encouragement.

No one can accomplish sincere conversion through personal strength, but he promises: **I will pour out my Spirit to you**.

Conversion requires **special grace**, and that grace will not be refused to those who seek it.

Christ's love and promises should make us listen carefully even when he rebukes us.

How long do sinners expect to remain safe on such a dangerous path, knowing how uncertain life is and what it means to die without Christ?

They may now feel secure and dismiss every warning.

But calamity will come.

Now God offers to hear their prayers; those who persist in rejecting him may later cry out **too late**.

Are we still despising Wisdom?

Let us listen diligently and obey **the Lord Jesus**, finding peace of conscience, confidence in God and deliverance from evil **in life, death and eternity**.
