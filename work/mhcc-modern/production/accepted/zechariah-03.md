# Zechariah 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The restoration of the church. | 1–5 |
| A promise concerning the Messiah. | 6–10 |

## Joshua is cleansed and restored (verses 1–5)

Zechariah saw **Joshua the high priest** standing before the Lord, accused by Satan.

Both **guilt** and **corruption** make it difficult for sinners to approach a holy God.

Because of the sins we have committed, we are liable to his justice.

Because sin still lives and works within us, we are offensive to his holiness.

Even God's people face these dangers.

Their remedy is **Jesus Christ**, whom God has made both **righteousness and sanctification** for them.

Joshua stood accused but was justified.

When we seek to worship God or serve him publicly, we should expect Satan's malice and cleverness to oppose us.

But Satan is rebuked by **the One who has conquered him**.

Those belonging to Christ will find him ready to defend them when the accusation is fiercest.

A converted soul is **a burning brand pulled from the fire through a miracle of free grace**.

Christ will not leave such a person to Satan.

Joshua appeared polluted, but he was cleansed.

He represented God's people, who remain unclean until they are washed and sanctified in **the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of God**.

Israel had now turned away from idolatry, yet much was still wrong.

Enemies within their spiritual lives were even more dangerous than hostile neighboring nations.

**Christ detested the filth on Joshua's garments but did not reject Joshua.**

He acts this way toward people chosen to serve him as priests.

**Pardoning mercy removes the guilt of sin. Renewing grace breaks sin's power.**

Christ washes sinners in his own blood and makes them kings and priests to God.

Those he appoints as spiritual priests stand before God clothed in **the spotless robe of Christ's righteousness** and adorned with **the graces of his Spirit**.

The righteousness of the saints is both **imputed and implanted**.

It is pictured by the clean white linen worn by the Lamb's bride (**Revelation 19:8**).

Joshua was restored to his former honor and responsibilities.

The crown of the priesthood was placed on him again.

When God intends to revive true religion, he moves prophets and people to **pray for that renewal**.

## The Branch of righteousness and forgiveness in one day (verses 6–10)

God equips those he calls to his service, finding them fit or making them so.

He removes believers' sins through sanctifying grace and enables them to walk in newness of life.

Just as promises to David often become promises about the Messiah, promises to Joshua point to **Christ**, whose priesthood Joshua's foreshadowed.

Whatever trials or duties we face, our dependence must rest upon **Christ, the Branch of righteousness**.

Christ is God's Servant, carrying out his Father's work, obeying his will and living for his glory.

He is the Branch from whom all our spiritual fruit comes.

His Father's eye rested upon him, especially in his suffering.

Henry observes that Christ was hidden in the grave like a foundation stone buried from sight, but adds an important qualification: **the prophecy more directly describes the attention given to this precious Cornerstone**.

Believers before the Savior's coming looked toward him through types and promises.

Believers after his coming look to him with faith, hope and love.

Christ appears for his chosen people like the high priest carrying Israel's names on the stones of his breastplate.

God has given the redeemed to Christ to bring them by grace to glory.

Through Christ **both sin's guilt and its dominion are removed**.

The decisive work was accomplished **in one day—the day he suffered and died**.

Once sin is taken away, nothing can ultimately destroy those sheltered by the Savior.

They can rest joyfully under his protection.

And when gospel grace works powerfully in hearts, it makes people eager to invite others to share that blessing.
