# Psalm 74 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The desolations of the sanctuary. | 1–11 |
| Pleas for encouraging faith. | 12–17 |
| Petitions for deliverances. | 18–23 |

## Lament over a devastated sanctuary (verses 1–11)

Henry understands this psalm as **appearing to describe** the destruction of Jerusalem and its temple by the **Chaldeans**.

The people of God laid their terrible situation before the Lord and left it in his hands.

They recalled what he had previously done for them.

If the deliverance from Egypt encouraged Israel to trust that God would not abandon his people, Christians have even stronger reason to believe he will not reject those **redeemed through Christ's own blood**.

Unbelievers and persecutors may silence faithful ministers, close places of worship and boast that they will destroy both God's people and their religion.

For a long time such schemes may appear successful.

The oppressed may see no possibility of escape.

Yet God preserves a **remnant of believers**, the seed of a future harvest.

The church that appears despised today has outlived many enemies who once celebrated victory over her.

When hostile power is most terrifying, take refuge in **God's power through earnest prayer**.

## Remember the God of creation and covenant (verses 12–17)

The church turned from complaint to the memory of God's ancient works as her King.

What he had done before encouraged his people to trust him again.

These deliverances were **his work**, which no one else could have accomplished.

Remembering Providence strengthens faith and hope under present difficulties.

The God of Israel is also **the God of nature**.

He faithfully maintains the order of day and night.

He will likewise remain faithful to those he has chosen.

We have as much reason to expect affliction in this world as to expect **night and winter**.

But we have no more reason to despair of comfort's return than to despair that **day and summer** will return.

In the world to come these changes will finally disappear.

## Prayer for the poor and oppressed (verses 18–23)

The psalmist asked God to defend his church against enemies.

The folly of those who despise his gospel and servants will become evident.

We should pray that he will **enlighten nations living in darkness** and rescue his people.

Then the poor and needy will have reason to praise his name.

Blessed Savior, you are **the same yesterday, today and forever**.

Make your people more than conquerors.

Be everything to them in every place and every circumstance, so that even your poor and afflicted servants will praise your name.
