## The three annual feasts (verses 1–17)

Moses repeated the instructions for the three yearly festivals: **Passover, Pentecost and Tabernacles**.

He also repeated the general requirement that Israel's people gather before God.

Believers must never forget the depth of guilt and suffering from which they have been rescued, or the great price their Redeemer paid.

Remembering that price allows **gratitude and joy** to live alongside **sorrow for sin and patience** through the hardships on the road to heaven.

The Israelites were to rejoice in what they received from God and in the worship and offerings they returned to him.

Our duty should be our delight.

Those who lived under the law were commanded to rejoice before God.

How much more should those living under the grace of the gospel rejoice!

Our duty is to rejoice continually and to rejoice in the Lord always.

When we rejoice in him, we should help others rejoice as well, comforting people who grieve and providing for those in need.

Everyone whose joy rests in God can rejoice in hope, because the One who made the promises is faithful.

## Just judges and the rejection of idols (verses 18–22)

Moses gave instructions for the proper administration of justice.

Personal favoritism must be set aside.

Everyone should receive what is right, and no one should be wronged.

The people were also warned not to adopt the idolatrous practices of the surrounding nations.

Nothing misrepresents God more seriously—or damages human minds more deeply—than trying to portray the almighty and eternal Spirit, present everywhere, through a physical image and then worshiping that image.

Sadly, even under the gospel, with its better promises, human hearts still tend to create idols in one form or another.
