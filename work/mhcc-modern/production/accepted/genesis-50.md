## Joseph mourns his father (verses 1–6)

When godly relatives or friends die, we may grieve our loss even if they lived to a good old age and we are confident they have gone to glory.

It is right to honor their memory by mourning them.

Grace does not destroy natural affection.

Instead, it purifies, moderates, and directs it.

We can no longer show tokens of affection to the departed soul.

Yet it is fitting to honor the body, from which we hope for a glorious and joyful resurrection, whatever may happen to its remains in this world.

Joseph showed both faith in God and love for his father.

He ordered Jacob's body to be embalmed, or preserved by wrapping it with spices.

Notice how frail and lowly our bodies become once the soul has departed.

Without preservation, they quickly decay and become offensive.

## Jacob is buried (verses 7–14)

Jacob's body was accompanied not only by his family, but also by the leading people of Egypt.

As the Egyptians came to know the Hebrews better, they began to respect them.

Those who profess faith should seek, through wisdom and love, to overcome other people's prejudices against them.

Onlookers recognized how deeply everyone mourned.

The death of godly people is a loss to any place, and it deserves sincere lament.

## Joseph forgives and reassures his brothers (verses 15–21)

Many reasons may have led Jacob's sons to remain in Egypt, despite Abraham's prophetic vision that his descendants would one day suffer bondage there.

Judging Joseph by the usual tendencies of human nature, they feared that he would now avenge himself on those who had hated and injured him without reason.

They could neither resist him nor run away.

So they sought to soften his heart by humbling themselves.

They pleaded as servants of the God whom Jacob had worshiped.

Joseph was deeply moved to see his dreams so completely fulfilled.

He told them not to fear him, but to fear God—to humble themselves before the Lord and seek his forgiveness.

He assured them that he himself would continue to treat them kindly.

What an excellent spirit Joseph showed!

Let us learn from him to repay evil with good.

He comforted his brothers and spoke tenderly to them, removing their fears.

People whose spirits have been broken need to be supported and encouraged.

If we love and forgive people, we must not only do good to them. We should speak kindly to them as well.

## Joseph gives instructions about his bones and dies (verses 22–26)

Joseph had honored his father, and his days were long in the land God had given him for the time being.

When he knew his own death was approaching, he comforted his brothers with God's promise that their people would return to Canaan in due time.

We should comfort others with the comfort God has given us and encourage them to depend on the promises that sustain us.

To declare his own faith and strengthen theirs, Joseph instructed them not to bury his remains permanently until the great day when his people would settle in the promised land.

By faith in the resurrection and in God's promise of Canaan, Joseph gave instructions concerning his bones.

Those instructions would help keep alive the expectation of their departure from Egypt.

They would constantly turn the people's thoughts toward Canaan and keep Joseph's descendants closely bound to their brothers.

Joseph's death, like his life, showed the excellence of his faith.

Both encourage us to persevere in serving God.

How blessed it is to begin the race toward heaven early, continue steadfastly, and finish the course with joy.

Joseph did so, and by God's grace we may do the same.

Even when death's pains come, if we have trusted the One in whom the patriarchs, prophets, and apostles trusted, we need not fear to say:

“My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.”
