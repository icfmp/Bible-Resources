# Psalm 58 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Wicked judges described and reproved. | 1–5 |
| A prayer that they may be disabled, and their ruin predicted. | 6–11 |

## Corrupt judgment and sinful human nature (verses 1–5)

When injustice is committed **under the appearance of law**, it is especially evil.

It is particularly grievous when people who claim to belong to God unite to attack his people.

We should thank the Lord for restraints that prevent the full expression of human wickedness.

We should earnestly seek **renewing grace**, watch our own conduct and show patience with the effects of fallen nature in others.

Corruption of human nature is the root of bitterness from which wrongdoing grows.

Even in childhood we can see the beginning of the world's wicked tendencies.

Children turn away from God and duty as soon as sinful desires begin to express themselves.

How early they learn to lie!

Parents therefore have a duty to teach children carefully and, above all, to pray that **God's converting grace will make them new creatures**.

Although the poison of sin is already present, much of its harmful influence can be restrained.

When the Savior's word is received, even a person once like a dangerous serpent can become harmless.

But those who finally refuse heavenly wisdom face dreadful destruction.

## A prayer against the power of evil (verses 6–11)

David asked God to prevent enemies of his church and people from continuing to cause harm.

We may pray with faith **against the harmful plans of those opposing God's people**.

The psalm also foretells their ruin.

Who can measure God's righteous anger?

The victories of **the Just One, Jesus Christ**, both personally and through his servants, produce joy that springs **not from a desire for revenge** but from recognizing God's mercy, truth and justice.

Through Christ's victory we see the redemption of his chosen people, the punishment of persistent wickedness and the fulfillment of divine promises.

Anyone who thinks seriously about these things should seek the reward of righteousness and worship the Providence that orders everything rightly in heaven and on earth.
