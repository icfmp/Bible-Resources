# Titus 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Obedience to magistrates, and becoming behaviour towards all, are enforced from what believers were before conversion, and what they are made, through Christ. | 1–7 |
| Good works to be done, and useless disputes avoided. | 8–11 |
| Directions and exhortations. | 12–15 |

## A gracious salvation produces civil duty and kindness (verses 1–7)

Spiritual privileges do not weaken civil obligations; they **confirm** them.

Good intentions and pleasing words are not enough unless accompanied by **good deeds**.

Christians must not be quarrelsome but should show gentleness toward everyone, not only their friends, exercising wisdom (**James 3:13**).

Christ's followers should not act harshly even toward the weakest, lowest or most objectionable person.

People enslaved to sin have many competing masters: pride pulls them one way, greed another. They become hateful toward one another.

It is the misery of sinners to hate; it is the **duty and happiness of believers to love**.

We are delivered from this condition entirely by **God's mercy and freely given grace**, Christ's merits and sufferings and the Holy Spirit's work.

God the Father is **God our Savior**, the source of the Spirit's teaching, regeneration and saving work, all given through Christ.

The beginning is God's kindness and love for humanity. His gracious love changes the heart through the Spirit.

**Good works must be found in saved people, but they do not cause salvation.**

God creates in believers a new principle of grace and holiness, making them new people.

Many want heaven eventually without wanting holiness now. They want the end without the necessary beginning.

**Baptism** is the outward sign and seal of this inward new birth—the **washing of regeneration**.

We must neither disregard baptism nor place confidence **merely in the outward washing**.

Without the response of a good conscience, the outward act brings no saving benefit.

It is the Holy Spirit who inwardly renews us, enables us to kill sin, perform duty, walk in God's ways and produce the fruits of righteousness.

The Spirit and his gifts come through Jesus Christ our Savior, whose work brings us from grace to eternal glory.

**Justification** means a sinner is freely forgiven and accepted as righteous through **Christ's righteousness received by faith**.

God remains righteous and upholds his own law because Christ has satisfied divine justice. The sinner cannot earn such acceptance.

Eternal life is promised; the Spirit creates **faith and hope** that bring that promised future close to our hearts and fill us with joy.

## Insist on good works and refuse empty disputes (verses 8–11)

After declaring God's saving grace, Paul presses the need for good works.

Those who trust God must seek **opportunities to do good**, moved by love and gratitude.

Avoid foolish controversies, clever but useless distinctions, vain speculation and the constant pursuit of novelty.

Love instead the sound doctrine that most effectively builds up the church.

Sins we now dismiss as small may feel unbearably heavy when God awakens our consciences.

## Live fruitfully and ask for grace (verses 12–15)

Christianity is not an empty profession.

Believers must produce **fruits of righteousness through Jesus Christ**, to God's glory and praise.

It is not enough to refrain from wrongdoing. We must actively do good.

Let the members of our households engage in **honest work** and support themselves and their families. Every Christian should seek legitimate work and fulfill that calling in God's presence.

Paul closes with friendship and sincere prayer.

**“Grace be with you all.”** He desires God's favor, its fruits and its growing presence in their souls.

Grace is the greatest gift we can ask for ourselves or others. In it is contained **every true good**.
