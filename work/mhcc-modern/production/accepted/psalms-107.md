# Psalm 107 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's providential care of the children of men in distresses, in banishment, and dispersion. | 1–9 |
| In captivity. | 10–16 |
| In sickness. | 17–22 |
| Danger at sea. | 23–32 |
| God's hand is to be seen by his own people. | 33–43 |

## God's help for travelers and exiles (verses 1–9)

These verses refer to Israel's deliverance from Egypt and perhaps also **the return from Babylon**, though the latter connection is uncertain.

They also describe the dangers experienced by travelers crossing the deserts of those lands.

It is difficult to imagine their suffering while wandering across featureless sand under **the burning sun**.

These travelers picture people whom the Lord has redeemed from **Satan's bondage**.

Believers pass through a world that can resemble a dangerous, desolate wilderness, sometimes fainting under trials, fears and temptations.

Those who hunger and thirst for **righteousness**, for God himself and for fellowship with him will be satisfied by the goodness of his house, **now in grace and ultimately in glory**.

## God's mercy to prisoners (verses 10–16)

The prisoners described here are isolated and sorrowful.

Henry notes the severe treatment inflicted in eastern prisons.

Suffering should lead us toward **humility**.

If our hearts remain proud and unbroken through hardship, we lose an important benefit of the trial.

Earthly captivity pictures a sinner's rescue from a far worse imprisonment.

Once awakened to sin, a person recognizes guilt and misery.

After attempting in vain to secure freedom through personal effort, the sinner discovers that help can come only through **God's mercy and grace**.

The Lord forgives and breaks **the power of sin and Satan**, while the Holy Spirit brings sanctification and comfort.

## Healing bodily and spiritual illness (verses 17–22)

Had humanity never sinned, **sickness would not have entered the world**.

Sinners behave foolishly when they damage their bodies through excess and endanger their lives by indulging uncontrolled appetites.

Such conduct is its own folly.

Illness weakens the body, but recovery comes through **God's power and mercy**.

We should thank him for every restoration of health.

Christ's miraculous healings also pictured his power to heal **the diseases of the soul**.

The Spirit of grace accomplishes such spiritual healing.

God sends **his word** to convict, convert and make people holy.

Even in ordinary bodily recovery, Providence operates at God's command.

Likewise, his word and Spirit restore the soul to **spiritual health and holiness**.

## Deliverance from danger at sea (verses 23–32)

People who travel and work on the sea have special reason to reflect upon God's greatness.

Sailors witness terrible storms and extraordinary rescues that others can barely imagine.

How appropriate it is to **pray during such danger**!

Storms at sea also picture the terrors of a troubled conscience and the painful experiences some Christians endure.

When they cry to the Lord, he can **turn their storm into calm** and bring their trials to an end with joy.

## Recognizing God's providence in changing fortunes (verses 33–43)

Human circumstances change in surprising ways.

The desolate condition of Judea and other lands offered illustrations to Henry's readers.

People may grow from humble beginnings to prosperity, yet fall from that success into nothing just as quickly.

**Worldly riches are uncertain**.

Those who possess them may suddenly lose them.

God has many ways of making people poor.

The righteous have reason to rejoice.

Such reversals will eventually silence those who deny divine Providence.

Sinners who see God justly taking away blessings they abused will have no valid defense.

We greatly benefit from recognizing God's goodness and allowing it to affect our hearts.

Wisdom calls us to perform **our duty** and leave **our comfort** to him.

A genuinely wise person will treasure this psalm and learn from it both **human weakness and misery** and **God's power and lovingkindness**—shown not because we deserve them but **because he is merciful**.
