# Psalm 101 — Modern English

## Original heading

**David's vow and profession of godliness.**

## A household and a kingdom governed under God

David declared how he intended to manage **his household** and **his kingdom**, resisting wrongdoing and encouraging godliness.

The psalm therefore applies to private families as well as rulers; it can be called **the householder's psalm**.

It teaches everyone who has authority, whether great or small, to use it so that evildoers are restrained and those who do right are encouraged.

David chose to sing of **God's mercy and judgment**.

The Lord's dealings with his people commonly include both.

He sets them beside each other, like **rain and sunshine**, for their good.

We should thank God appropriately for both his mercies and his judgments.

Family blessings and family afflictions alike should move us to maintain **faithful worship at home**.

Public responsibility never excuses neglect of one's household.

Indeed, those who hold public office have an even stronger duty to set a good example by governing their families well.

When someone has a home, that person should seek to have **God dwelling there**.

Those who walk faithfully, sincerely and uprightly may expect the Lord's presence.

David resolved first to refuse evil **in his own conduct**.

He also resolved not to surround himself with wicked servants or place ungodly people in positions of trust.

He did not want his household corrupted by their example.

A perverse and quarrelsome heart is destructive to fellowship, which is bound together by **Christian love**.

David would neither tolerate slanderers who delight in damaging their neighbors' reputations nor favor proud and deceitful people who lie or cheat without remorse.

Everyone should work diligently to reform **their own heart and conduct**, starting without delay.

Remember the coming day when **the righteous King will remove evildoers from the heavenly Jerusalem**.
