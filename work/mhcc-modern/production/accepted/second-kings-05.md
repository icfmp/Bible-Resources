# 2 Kings 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Naaman seeks healing (verses 1–8)

Although the Syrians were idol worshipers who oppressed God's people, Scripture attributes the deliverance Naaman had achieved to **the Lord**.

This is the right way to speak about history.

People who write about ordinary affairs often seem not to think of God at all.

No amount of human greatness or honor can keep severe suffering away.

Beneath fine clothing, many important people suffer in sick and broken bodies.

Every person has some **“but”**—a weakness that qualifies outward success, diminishes glory or interrupts pleasure.

Naaman's servant, a young Israelite girl, knew about the famous prophet among her people.

Children should be told about God's wonderful works while they are young, so they can speak of him wherever life takes them.

Though she was a captive and served against her will, she desired her master's recovery.

How much more should people serving others by choice seek their employers' welfare!

Those working in households can become a great blessing by speaking faithfully of God's glory and his servants.

Naaman did not reject the girl's counsel because of her low social position.

How good it would be if people felt the weight of **sin** as keenly as they feel bodily illness!

When we seek the blessings God grants in answer to prayer, we must come as beggars receiving a **free gift**, not as lords demanding or purchasing one.

## Naaman is cleansed (verses 9–14)

Elisha knew Naaman was proud.

He wanted him to learn that all people stand on the same level before the great God.

Every command of God tests the human heart, especially those telling sinners how to seek salvation.

Naaman's reaction showed pride's foolishness.

He was unwilling to accept a cure unless it came with the ceremony and spectacle he expected.

He wanted to be healed **on his own terms**.

Likewise, the way sinners receive forgiveness and holiness—through **Christ's blood and Spirit, by faith in his name alone**—does not flatter human pride or provide a role for self-salvation.

Human wisdom prefers to invent supposedly better methods.

People in authority should listen to reason.

We should refuse evil advice even from prominent and respected people.

But we should be ready to receive good counsel even from those under our authority.

Naaman's attendants asked whether he would not do something difficult to be healed.

When spiritually sick people are willing to do anything, submit to anything and give up anything to receive healing, they have begun to hope rightly.

The remedy for sin is plain.

No one may claim it was hidden or needlessly complicated.

**Believe and be saved. Repent and be pardoned. Wash and be clean.**

Believers follow the Savior's instructions without neglecting them, changing them or adding to them.

Thus they are cleansed of guilt.

Those who reject his way remain in the leprosy of sin.

## Elisha refuses Naaman's gifts (verses 15–19)

Naaman was more deeply affected by the **mercy** of his cure than by the mere miracle.

People who experience divine grace themselves are best equipped to speak about its power.

Naaman expressed gratitude to Elisha.

The prophet refused payment.

He did not do so because receiving gifts was always unlawful; he had accepted them on other occasions.

Instead, he wanted this new convert to understand that the servants of Israel's God regarded worldly wealth as unimportant.

The entire work came from God.

The prophet would not give directions beyond what the Lord had authorized him to say.

We should not always oppose every smaller mistake immediately when someone first comes under conviction of divine truth.

People cannot be moved forward faster than the Lord prepares them to receive instruction.

Nevertheless, anyone entering a covenant relationship with God while knowingly **reserving a favorite sin** breaks that covenant.

People who truly hate evil will make an effort to avoid even its appearance.

## Gehazi's greed and lies (verses 20–27)

Naaman was a Syrian court official and soldier, with many servants.

Those servants acted wisely and kindly.

Elisha, a holy prophet, had only one servant, and he turned out to be a dishonest liar.

**Love of money**, the root of every kind of evil, lay beneath Gehazi's wrongdoing.

He imagined he could deceive Elisha.

But he discovered that the Spirit of prophecy could not be deceived.

Lying in this situation was an attempt to lie to the **Holy Spirit**.

It is foolish to commit a sin with the hope that nobody will discover it.

When we stray into a hidden path, our conscience still accompanies us.

God's eye follows us too.

Those who cover wrongdoing will not prosper.

A lie lasts only briefly.

All the schemes of people devoted to the world lie open before God.

There are times when seeking more wealth is altogether wrong—particularly when it would dishonor God or religion or injure other people.

Gehazi was punished.

If he insisted on receiving Naaman's money, he would receive **Naaman's disease** along with it.

What had Gehazi gained by receiving **two talents**, if he lost his health, reputation, peace and service—and, unless he repented, his soul forever?

Let us beware of hypocrisy and greed and fear the lasting spiritual leprosy of sin.

## Editorial review — 2 Kings 5

**Complete secondary original-English witness directly read:** [CCEL, 2 Kings 5](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xiii.iv.html). Exact released **MHCC 0.1.1** remains the primary original for native SWORD mapping, independent readback, annotations and editorial acceptance.

- **1–8:** Syrian victory still **attributed to God**, illness beneath rank, **every person has some “but”**, captive young servant blessing master, good counsel from a low position, salvation as **free gift not purchased** — retained.
- **9–14:** Naaman's pride and demand for ceremony, **Christ's blood/Spirit through faith** rather than self-salvation, accepting good advice from servants, **believe/repent/wash** commands exactly distinct — retained.
- **15–19:** cure's **mercy more important than miracle**, refusal of gifts **not a general ban**, wealth relativized, restraint in correcting smaller errors of new converts, **no knowingly reserved sin** — retained.
- **20–27:** Naaman's wise servants contrasted with **Gehazi's lies**, love of money and **Holy Spirit not deceived**, conscience and God witnessing secret acts, **two talents and loss of health/honor/peace/service/soul unless repentance** — retained.

**Secondary witness error:** CCEL's 15–19 prose names **“Elijah”** as refusing Naaman's gifts, while the chapter explicitly concerns **Elisha**. The modern manuscript uses Elisha but flags this for exact-native **MHCC 0.1.1** and independent print collation. No silent correction of the original is proposed.
