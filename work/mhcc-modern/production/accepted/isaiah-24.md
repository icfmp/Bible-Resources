# Isaiah 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The desolation of the land. | 1–12 |
| A few shall be preserved. | 13–15 |
| God's kingdom advanced by his judgments. | 16–23 |

## Sin brings desolation (verses 1–12)

People who place all their treasure and happiness in this world will eventually discover their poverty and misery.

We should apply Scripture's teaching about the **vanity and frustration of everything under the sun** to our own lives.

Sin has turned the earth upside down. The world is now very different for humanity from the dwelling God originally prepared.

Even at its finest, earthly beauty resembles a **flower that fades while we are still holding it**.

This world brings disappointment and tears. Our lives are short and full of trouble.

The curse of sin reaches every social rank and situation. God can empty and lay waste whatever people possess.

Human sin has polluted the earth, and the Lord's righteous judgment lays it desolate.

The pleasures of sinful indulgence will end in heaviness.

God has many ways to make wine and strong drink bitter to the people who depend upon them. **Bodily illness, mental anguish and the destruction of a person's estate** can transform pleasure into suffering.

Let us mourn for our sin and rejoice in God. **No person or event can take away joy that is rooted in him.**

## A small faithful remnant remains (verses 13–15)

Even after the general destruction, **a few faithful people will survive**.

They will be scattered like the olives left after harvest, **hidden among the leaves**.

The Lord knows those who belong to him, although the world does not recognize them.

When worldly people's laughter stops, the saints can still rejoice, because **the covenant of grace never fails**. It is both the fountain of their comfort and the foundation of their hope.

Faith enables them to rejoice even under affliction and to encourage other sufferers to do the same.

Some may glorify the Lord **in the fires** of severe suffering; others may do so in low, dark and muddy valleys.

In every place, however remote, and amid every trial, however hot, let us continue thinking rightly of God's goodness.

If those trials cannot turn us from trusting him, we glorify the Lord in the fires.

## God's final rule over judgment (verses 16–23)

Believers may be driven to the farthest regions of the earth and still be **singing rather than sighing**.

Yet Isaiah mourned the miseries he foresaw pouring across the earth like a torrent, and the small number of faithful people among its inhabitants.

Sin would abound, and evil would pursue those who practiced it.

Earthly security is deeply uncertain.

Worldly people imagine they occupy the earth as though it were **a palace or impregnable castle**. But their habitation is more like **a temporary hut set up for one night**.

The creation bears a heavy burden under sin and groans beneath it.

Ultimately the present order will fall, and **new heavens and a new earth will appear, in which only righteousness dwells**.

People who imagine their wealth or public office makes them unreachable will discover God's judgment against pride and cruelty.

We should not presume to announce the full meaning of God's dealings **before the appointed time**.

No one should feel secure merely because life is prosperous. Yet no sufferer need despair merely because circumstances are miserable.

God will receive glory through every judgment.

**The mystery of his Providence has not yet been fully revealed**.

The defeat of the Redeemer's enemies must make way for Christ's kingdom. Then **the Sun of Righteousness will shine in full glory**.

Let every unrepentant sinner consider the final outcome. The ungodly will sink beneath their transgressions and not rise again, while believers enjoy everlasting happiness.
