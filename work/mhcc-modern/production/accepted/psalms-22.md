# Psalm 22 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Complaints of discouragement. | 1–10 |
| With prayer for deliverance. | 11–21 |
| Praises for mercies and redemption. | 22–31 |

## The suffering of Christ and the complaint of abandonment (verses 1–10)

The **Spirit of Christ**, who spoke through the prophets, testified in this Psalm about **Christ's sufferings and the glory that would follow**.

The Psalm begins with a sorrowful complaint about God's apparent withdrawal.

It may also give language to believers crushed by grief and fear.

For a child of God, **the felt loss of God's presence** is more painful than any other suffering.

Even the anguish of that loss can show that spiritual life remains active, because the soul still longs for God.

Complaining, *Why am I sick? Why am I poor?*, can reveal dissatisfaction with the world and its comforts.

But crying, **“Why have you forsaken me?”**, reveals a heart whose happiness depends upon **God's favor**.

These words belong especially to Christ.

He spoke them upon the cross as he poured out his soul before God (**Matthew 27:46**).

Being truly human, Jesus naturally recoiled from such dreadful sorrows.

Yet his zeal and love prevailed.

In his deepest suffering Christ still declared **the holiness of his heavenly Father**.

Indeed, his suffering itself demonstrated that holiness and became reason for his people to praise God more than for all their other deliverances.

No one who has truly hoped in God has ultimately been ashamed of that hope.

No one who sincerely sought him has done so in vain.

The Psalm also describes the contempt and scorn of human beings.

The Savior expressed the humble and despised condition into which he had been brought.

The history of **Christ's birth and suffering** helps explain the prophecy.

## Christ suffers and prays for deliverance (verses 11–21)

Here we see **Christ suffering** and **Christ praying**.

His example prepares us for suffering and teaches us to seek God within it.

Henry sees even the manner of Christ's death foretold, though crucifixion was not practiced by the Jews in the psalmist's time.

His hands and feet were pierced and nailed to the cursed tree.

His body was suspended in extreme pain and torment.

His natural strength gave way under the suffering and the divine wrath he bore.

Who can stand before God's anger?

The life of sinners had been forfeited.

The life of the **Sacrifice** therefore had to become their **ransom**.

Jesus was stripped of his clothes upon the cross so that he might **clothe us in his righteousness**.

These things happened as Scripture foretold, confirming that Christ was the true Messiah.

They should also deepen our love for him as the best of friends, who endured all this for us.

During his agony Jesus prayed earnestly.

He asked that the cup of suffering might pass from him.

When we cannot rejoice in God as the object of our song, let us still **rest upon him as our strength**.

When the feeling of spiritual delight is absent, we can still receive the support of his grace.

Christ prayed to be delivered from the wrath of God.

The Lord has delivered, continues delivering and will deliver.

We should meditate upon **Christ's suffering and resurrection** until we experience in our souls the power of his resurrection and fellowship in his sufferings.

## Praise for redemption and the risen Savior (verses 22–31)

Now the Savior speaks **as risen from the dead**.

Christ himself used the opening words of the Psalm's lament upon the cross.

The opening words of its triumphant praise are expressly applied to him in **Hebrews 2:12**.

All true praise must ultimately return to **the work of redemption**.

The suffering of our Redeemer was accepted by the Father as a **full satisfaction for sin**.

Although he offered himself on behalf of sinful people, the Father did not reject that offering.

This deserves our continual thanksgiving.

Humble hearts renewed by grace can find complete satisfaction and happiness in Christ.

Those hungering and thirsting for his righteousness will not waste their labor on what cannot satisfy.

People who pray much will also give thanks much.

Those who turn to God will devote themselves carefully to worshiping him.

Every tongue should confess **Jesus Christ as Lord**.

People of every social condition—high and low, rich and poor, enslaved and free—meet in him.

Since we cannot preserve our own lives, it is wise to entrust our souls through obedient faith to **Christ, who can save and keep them alive forever**.

A generation will continue serving the Lord.

God will maintain **a church in the world until the end of time**.

He will regard those future believers as his own, just as he cared for previous generations.

They will proclaim **his righteousness, not their own**, as the foundation of their hope and source of their joy.

Redemption by Jesus Christ is **God's own work**.

Here we see the free love and compassion of the Father and the Lord Jesus toward sinful people—the source of all grace and consolation, the pattern for Christian conduct and a guide through the treatment believers can expect in this world.

Every humbling and saving lesson can be found here.

Let those who attempt to **establish their own righteousness** ask why God's beloved Son would have needed to suffer so terribly if human deeds could atone for sin.

Let those who claim Christ's name while despising God's law ask whether Jesus suffered in order to give them permission to disobey it.

Let careless sinners flee the coming wrath.

Let trembling souls entrust themselves to this merciful Redeemer.

And let distressed and tempted believers confidently expect that the Lord will bring **a happy end to every trial**.
