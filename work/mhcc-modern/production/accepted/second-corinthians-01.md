# 2 Corinthians 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle blesses God for comfort in, and deliverance out of troubles. | 1–11 |
| He professes his own and his fellow-labourers' integrity. | 12–14 |
| Gives reasons for his not coming to them. | 15–24 |

## God comforts us and enables us to comfort others (verses 1–11)

We are encouraged to come confidently to the **throne of grace** to receive mercy and find the help we need.

The Lord can give peace to a troubled conscience and calm the most violent passions of the soul. He gives these blessings as the **Father of his redeemed family**.

Our Savior himself tells us not to let our hearts be troubled.

Every true comfort comes from God, and our sweetest comforts are found in him.

He speaks peace to the soul through the **free forgiveness of sins**. He also comforts us through the life-giving work of the **Holy Spirit** and the abundant mercy of his grace.

He heals broken hearts, binds up painful wounds and gives **hope and joy even amid the heaviest sorrows**.

God does not comfort us merely to make us happier. He also intends his gifts to **make us useful to other people**. He provides enough comfort to sustain those who sincerely trust and serve him.

Even when suffering brings us to the point of despairing of life itself, we may trust the God who is able to **bring people back from death**.

That trust does not disappoint.

Remembering deliverance in the past strengthens our faith and hope for the future. Earlier mercies place us under an obligation to trust the Lord again.

We also owe one another more than prayers for relief. We should join in **praise and thanksgiving** when prayers are answered, giving thanks to God for benefits received.

In this way both our trials and the mercies that follow them may become blessings **to ourselves and to others**.

## Paul appeals to the integrity of his conduct (verses 12–14)

Paul knew that, considered as a sinner, he had no ground for boasting except **Jesus Christ**.

But as a believer, he could rightly rejoice that his conduct agreed with the faith he professed.

Conscience bears witness not merely to an isolated action but to the **settled direction and pattern of a person's life**.

We should therefore examine our conduct as a whole, rather than judging ourselves by one good or bad act taken alone.

A life is properly ordered when it proceeds from **a principle of grace within the heart**.

With such a conscience, we can entrust our reputation to the Lord.

Nevertheless, there are times when we should take suitable steps to clear our name, especially when the **honor of the gospel** or our ability to serve others depends upon it.

## Why Paul changed his travel plans (verses 15–24)

Paul answered the accusation that his failure to visit Corinth showed him to be fickle or unreliable.

Faithful Christians should guard their reputation for **sincerity and steadiness**.

They should make decisions carefully and not change them without substantial reasons.

God's own promises never become less certain because human plans change.

They are given through **Jesus Christ** and are confirmed by the extraordinary works God accomplished in his Son's life, resurrection and ascension.

The **Holy Spirit** establishes believers firmly in gospel faith.

His life-giving work is a **pledge of everlasting life**, while his consolations anticipate **everlasting joy**.

Paul delayed his visit because he hoped to spare the Corinthians the painful rebuke he might otherwise have needed to administer before seeing the effect of his first letter.

Our spiritual strength comes through faith, and so must our joy and comfort.

Faith is accompanied by **holy dispositions and the fruits of grace**.

These fruits help protect us against self-deception in a matter so important.
