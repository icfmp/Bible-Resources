# Ezekiel 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God has no respect of persons. | 1–20 |
| The Divine providence is vindicated. | 21–29 |
| A gracious invitation to repentance. | 30–32 |

## Everyone is responsible before God (verses 1–20)

**“The soul that sins shall die.”**

With respect to eternal life, people are treated according to their true standing: either under **the old covenant of works** or under **the new covenant of grace**, as their conduct shows.

People may suffer outwardly because of sins committed by others.

But we also have sins of our own, and God governs every circumstance for **the eternal good of believers**.

Every soul belongs to **the great Creator**.

He deals with all in justice or mercy.

No one is condemned eternally merely for another person's sin while remaining personally innocent of guilt.

All of us have sinned.

If God judged us only by his holy law, we would be lost.

Yet he invites us to come to **Jesus Christ**.

Consider the children of different parents.

A person whose faith is demonstrated by good works may have a wicked child whose character is entirely unlike that parent's.

The child cannot expect to escape judgment through **the parent's godliness**.

Likewise, a wicked person's child who walks righteously before God will not be destroyed for the sins of the parent.

That righteous child may still suffer in this life, but will share in salvation.

**This chapter is not explaining how anyone earns justification.**

It describes the Lord's righteous dealings with **the wicked and the righteous**.

## God receives the person who turns from sin (verses 21–29)

A wicked person who turns from evil ways may be saved.

**The person who truly repents truly believes**.

Former sins are no longer held against that person.

The righteousness shown afterward is **the fruit of faith and the result of conversion**, not the purchase price of salvation.

The passage does not settle the separate question of whether **a genuinely righteous believer can finally apostatize**.

Many people who were **thought righteous for a time** later turn away from God.

But **verses 26–27** display the fullness of his forgiving mercy.

When sin is forgiven, it is blotted out and **remembered no more**.

The believer lives **in righteousness**, not **because that righteousness pays for former sins**.

Holy living itself is a blessing purchased through **the Mediator**.

What encouragement this offers sinners returning to God!

Verse 28 describes both **the beginning of repentance** and how it continues.

Genuine believers remain watchful and prayerful and continue to the end, and thus are saved.

Whenever we dispute with God, **he is right and we are wrong**.

## The Lord calls his people to repentance (verses 30–32)

The Lord will judge each Israelite according to that person's ways.

On this basis he calls them to **repent and receive a new heart and new spirit**.

God commands his people to take seriously the responsibilities within their reach and to **pray for the inward change that only he can give**.

He has appointed means of grace and ordinances and given both directions and promises.

Those who desire a new heart should therefore **seek it from him**.
