# Philippians 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle cautions the Philippians against judaizing false teachers, and renounces his own former privileges. | 1–11 |
| Expresses earnest desire to be found in Christ; also his pressing on toward perfection; and recommends his own example to other believers. | 12–21 |

## Christ's righteousness matters more than every human privilege (verses 1–11)

Sincere Christians **rejoice in Jesus Christ**.

The prophet referred to false prophets as **silent dogs** (Isaiah 56:10). Paul seems to have that language in mind when he warned against dangerous teachers. Like fierce dogs, they barked at and attacked those who faithfully professed Christ's gospel.

They insisted upon human works in opposition to faith in Jesus, but Paul called them **evil workers**. They were the **concision**, cutting apart and dividing Christ's church.

Every religious exercise is empty unless **the heart is truly engaged**. We worship God through the Holy Spirit's strength and grace, taking our joy in Christ rather than in merely outward practices or experiences.

We cannot guard too seriously against doctrines that oppose or misuse the gospel of **free salvation**.

If Paul had wanted to boast about human advantages or rely upon his own religious record, he had more reason than almost anyone to do so.

But everything he had counted as **gain during his life as a Pharisee** he now counted as **loss because of Christ**.

Paul was asking the Philippians to do nothing he had not already done, and to trust their souls to no foundation other than the One on whom he had staked **his own eternal hope**.

He regarded worldly possessions, religious distinctions and claims of personal merit as worthless compared with **knowing Christ personally through faith in his salvation**.

This was not merely an easy claim made while he still enjoyed life's comforts. Paul had actually **lost everything for the privilege of belonging to Christ**.

He went further. Whatever tried to rival Christ in his heart, or pretended to provide merit before God, he counted as **the foulest refuse**, something thrown away.

True knowledge of Christ changes a person's **judgments, desires, conduct and character** so deeply that the person seems to have been made anew.

It is far better to lack worldly wealth than to be without **Christ and his word**.

Paul therefore resolved to hold fast to Christ and heaven.

Guilty sinners need **a righteousness in which they can appear before God**. Christ provides that righteousness, and it is **complete and perfect**.

People who trust themselves cannot receive its benefit. **Faith in Christ's blood** is the appointed means by which we receive this saving gift.

We become conformed to his death as we **die to sin**, just as Christ died **for sin**. Through his cross, the world becomes crucified to us and we to it.

Paul was willing to act or suffer in whatever way was necessary on his path toward **the glorious resurrection of believers**.

This hope sustained him through all his labor and trouble.

He did not expect to attain that resurrection through **his own righteousness or merit**, but through **the merits and righteousness of Jesus Christ**.

## Keep pressing forward toward Christ and heaven (verses 12–21)

Paul's sincere dependence upon Christ did not mean he had already obtained the prize or had already become perfectly like the Savior.

He refused to be satisfied with **past service** or **his present measure of grace**.

He reached forward eagerly to become more like Jesus.

A runner must not stop before finishing the race. Likewise, Christians who hope for heaven must continue pressing toward it through **holy desires, confident hope and persevering effort**.

Eternal life is **God's gift in Jesus Christ**. We cannot enter heaven as our home except **through Christ, who is the Way**.

Believers seek to become increasingly like him, including in suffering and death: they **die to sin and crucify sinful desires**.

Genuine Christians differ greatly in the progress they have made, but all experience something of this change. Christ becomes everything to them, and their hearts turn toward the world to come.

They may also differ in judgment about lesser questions.

Those differences should not cause believers to **condemn one another**.

They are united in Christ and expect soon to meet in heaven. Let them therefore **stand together on the great truths they share** and patiently await further light on less important matters.

By contrast, the enemies of Christ's cross set their minds upon **sinful bodily appetites**.

Sin is shameful, and is even more disgraceful when a sinner takes pride in it.

The life of those absorbed in worldly desires may seem pleasant for a time, but it ends in **death and judgment**. People who choose that path will share that end.

A Christian's life belongs in heaven, where **Christ the Head** is and where the believer's lasting home awaits.

Our citizenship and deepest affections should therefore be directed there.

Christ has also reserved **glory for the bodies of his people**.

At the resurrection, their bodies will not simply live again. They will be **raised into a glorious condition**, transformed by **Christ's almighty power**.

Let us be prepared for the return of our Judge.

Ask him daily to **renew our souls in holiness**, rescue us from spiritual enemies and make both our bodies and souls instruments of righteousness in his service.
