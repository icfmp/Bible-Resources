# John 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The miracle at Cana. | 1–11 |
| Christ casts the buyers and sellers out of the temple. | 12–22 |
| Many believe in Christ. | 23–25 |

## Jesus changes water into wine at Cana (verses 1–11)

It is a great blessing when Christ is present at a wedding.

Anyone desiring his presence and blessing upon marriage should **invite him through prayer**.

He will hear.

In this life we can find ourselves lacking necessities even when we expected abundance.

At this wedding, the wine ran out.

Anyone entering ordinary worldly responsibilities should be prepared for trouble and disappointment.

When we approach Christ with a need, we should humbly explain our situation and **leave him free to do what he considers best**.

His reply to his mother was **not disrespectful**.

He addressed her with the same word in a loving way from the cross.

Nevertheless, his response stands as a warning against the **misplaced worship of Mary** that Henry sees in later centuries.

Our prayers may be answered when we have no idea how to proceed.

**A delay in mercy is not a refusal of prayer**.

Those who expect Jesus to bless them should be ready to obey his instructions.

The path of duty is the path of mercy.

We should not object to the methods he chooses.

**Moses' first miracle turned water into blood** (**Exodus 7:20**).

**Christ's first miracle turned water into wine**.

Henry sees here a picture of the difference between the Mosaic law and Christ's gospel.

Jesus makes the ordinary comforts of creation truly beneficial to believers.

Every work of Christ is intended for practical use.

Has he changed your water into wine by granting you **knowledge and grace**?

Use those blessings.

The wine was excellent.

Christ's work is good even when people fail to recognize its Author.

Henry notes that miracles produced what was **best of its kind**.

Jesus' action permits the proper use of wine, but it does not cancel his warning against **excessive eating and drunkenness** (**Luke 21:34**).

We may enjoy appropriate celebrations with friends, but should conduct them as though we could welcome the Redeemer to join us.

Frivolity, luxury and excess dishonor him.

## Jesus cleanses the temple (verses 12–22)

The first public action John records Jesus performing after Cana was driving traders out of the temple.

The priests and leaders had allowed people motivated by greed to turn its courts into a marketplace.

People still make God's house a place of commerce when their minds are occupied with worldly business during worship or when they perform religious duties **for financial profit**.

After cleansing the temple, Jesus gave a sign of his authority.

He foretold that his enemies would put him to death: **“Destroy this temple.”**

He would allow them to destroy his body.

He also foretold that **he himself would raise it again in three days**.

Jesus took his life up again by his own power.

His hearers misunderstood figurative language as though it were merely literal.

The disciples understood his words after the resurrection.

Observing how Scripture's promises and prophecies are fulfilled helps us understand the divine word.

## Jesus knows what is in every person (verses 23–25)

Jesus knows every human being's nature, desires, character, motives and plans.

He knows us far more thoroughly than we know one another—or even know ourselves.

He understands the hidden schemes of his enemies and the real character of those who profess friendship with him falsely.

He also knows who truly belongs to him, recognizing both **the uprightness and the weaknesses** of sincere believers.

We can observe what someone does.

**Christ sees what is in that person's heart**.

Beware of an empty profession of Christianity or a faith that is merely outward and dead.

People may deceive others and even themselves, but they cannot deceive **the God who searches hearts**.
