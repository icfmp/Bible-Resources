# Psalm 140 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David encourages himself in God. | 1–7 |
| He prays for, and prophesies the destruction of, his persecutors. | 8–13 |

## Praying for protection from open and hidden danger (verses 1–7)

The greater the danger, the more seriously we should turn to God in prayer.

Everyone sheltered by the Lord is safe.

**If God is for us, who can be against us?**

We should especially watch and pray that the Lord will establish our steps in his ways and keep us from stumbling.

God is just as able to preserve his servants from **hidden deceit** as from **open violence**.

Our experience of his care in one danger should strengthen our confidence that he will help in other dangers too.

## The Lord's judgment and the righteousness of Christ (verses 8–13)

Believers may rightly pray that God will not grant the desires of wicked people or help their evil schemes succeed.

Those who make false accusations bring their own trouble upon themselves, even the fiery coals of divine judgment.

The righteous will dwell before God and thank him forever.

Real thanksgiving must become **thanks-living**.

Every deliverance should move us to serve the Lord more faithfully and cheerfully.

Those who are slandered or mistreated by others can nevertheless be accepted as righteous before God.

That is possible only through **the righteousness of Jesus Christ, imputed to them and received through faith**.

Its effect in their lives is that they begin to live **self-controlled, upright lives**.

They give thanks to God for the righteousness by which they are counted righteous and for every gift of grace and mercy they receive.
