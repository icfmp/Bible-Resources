# Genesis 20 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Abraham stays at Gerar; Abimelech takes Sarah. | 1–8 |
| Abimelech rebukes Abraham. | 9–13 |
| Abimelech returns Sarah. | 14–18 |

## Abimelech is warned by God (verses 1–8)

Dishonest schemes do not prosper. They place both ourselves and others in danger.

God warned Abimelech that he was in danger of committing sin and of dying for it.

Every person who deliberately lives in sin is under a sentence of death.

But Abimelech pleaded that he had acted in ignorance.

When our conscience assures us that, although we may have been tricked into a dangerous situation, we have not deliberately sinned against God, that assurance brings comfort in a time of trouble.

Honest people can take comfort in knowing that God sees their honesty and will acknowledge it.

It is a great mercy to be prevented from committing sin, and God deserves all the glory for that protection.

Yet if we have done wrong without knowing it, our ignorance will not excuse us if we continue to do wrong after we learn the truth.

Everyone who does wrong—ruler or ordinary citizen—will certainly answer for that wrong unless they repent and, wherever possible, make restitution.

## Abimelech rebukes Abraham (verses 9–13)

We find much to blame even in Abraham, the father of the faithful.

He failed to trust God.

He worried too much about his own life, intended to deceive others, and placed temptation in their path.

His actions brought suffering upon other people and exposed both himself and Sarah to justified rebuke.

Yet he still tried to excuse himself.

Scripture records these events to warn us, not to give us examples to imitate.

Even Abraham had no reason to boast before God.

He could never be justified by his works.

His justification depended entirely on the righteousness God gives to everyone who believes.

Yet we must not immediately condemn as hypocrites all who fall into sin, provided they do not continue in it.

Those who remain proud and refuse to repent must never use God's grace as an excuse for continuing to sin.

Abimelech received God's warning and took it seriously.

He truly feared both sin and its consequences.

So he rose early to carry out the instructions God had given him.

## Sarah is restored and God brings deliverance (verses 14–18)

We often trouble ourselves because of suspicions that have no foundation.

Those fears can even draw us into temptation and sin.

Sometimes we discover reverence for God in the very people among whom we least expected to find it.

Agreements to deceive others usually end in shame and grief.

We should also thank God when he restrains us from sin, even if he does so through painful circumstances.

Though the Lord rebukes his people, he also forgives and delivers them.

He can give them favor in the eyes of those among whom they live.

When his people humble themselves over their failings, God can overrule those very weaknesses so that they become useful both to themselves and to others.
