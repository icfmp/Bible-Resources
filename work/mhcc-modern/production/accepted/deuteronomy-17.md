# Deuteronomy 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| All sacrifices to be perfect, Idolaters must be slain. | 1–7 |
| Difficult controversies. | 8–13 |
| The choice of a king, His duties. | 14–20 |

## Proper offerings and judgment on idolatry (verses 1–7)

No animal with a defect was to be brought to God as a sacrifice.

This reminds us of Jesus Christ, whose sacrifice was perfect, pure and without blemish.

It also reminds us to give God the best of our abilities, time and possessions.

If we deliberately withhold our best, our claimed obedience becomes hateful to him.

The Mosaic law ordered an Israelite idol worshiper to be put to death by stoning.

Henry treats this ancient, severe judicial sentence as a warning about how seriously God judges idolatry.

He urges people in his own day who have set up idols in their **hearts** to consider the judgment ancient Israel faced.

This is a description of Israel's historical law and Henry's spiritual application, not an instruction for private punishment today.

## Difficult cases and the courts (verses 8–13)

Courts of judgment were to be established in Israel's towns.

The decisions of those courts did not have the direct authority of a divine oracle.

Nevertheless, they were to be made by wise, prudent and experienced judges.

They also benefited from God's promise concerning the administration of justice.

## Choosing and instructing a king (verses 14–20)

The Lord himself was Israel's King in a special way.

If the people placed a human king over themselves, that person had to be the one God chose.

Later, when Israel asked for a king, the people approached Samuel, a prophet of the Lord.

Whenever God's choice can be known, it should guide and overrule our own preference.

Moses gave instructions for Israel's future king.

He must avoid everything that would turn him away from God and faithful worship.

Three major obstacles to devotion are **wealth, honor and pleasure**.

These correspond to the desires of the flesh, the desires of the eyes and the pride of life.

They are especially dangerous to people who hold positions of authority.

The king was therefore required to study God's law and make it the standard for his rule.

He was to write his own copy of Scripture and read it every day throughout his life.

Merely owning a Bible is not enough.

We must use it, and use it daily for as long as we live.

Christ's disciples never outgrow Scripture.

They will continue needing it until they reach the place where love and knowledge are made perfect.

And the king's copying and reading were worthless if he did not **practice** what he wrote and read.

Those who fear God and keep his commandments will benefit even in this present life.
