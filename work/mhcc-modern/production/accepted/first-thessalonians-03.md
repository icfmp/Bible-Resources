# 1 Thessalonians 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle sent Timothy to establish and comfort the Thessalonians. | 1–5 |
| He rejoiced at the good tidings of their faith and love. | 6–10 |
| And for their increase in grace. | 11–13 |

## Timothy strengthens believers facing persecution (verses 1–5)

The more we enjoy walking in God's ways, the more strongly we should desire **to persevere in them**.

Paul sent Timothy to **strengthen and comfort** the Thessalonian believers.

He wanted to confirm their faith in its object—**Jesus Christ, the Savior of the world**—and in its promised reward, which was more than sufficient to compensate for all their losses and repay their labor.

But Paul was concerned that the work he and his companions had accomplished might prove **fruitless**.

If Satan cannot stop ministers from teaching God's word, he will try to prevent that teaching from **bearing fruit**.

No faithful servant wants to work in vain.

It is God's purpose that his people should enter his kingdom **through many afflictions**.

The apostles did **not** flatter converts with promises of worldly prosperity.

They plainly taught them to expect **trouble and suffering**, following the example of **Jesus, the Author of our faith**.

Because Christians would face danger, it was important that they be warned in advance.

Knowledge of the trial to come would help protect them from **the tempter's schemes**.

## Good news of faith and love encourages Paul (verses 6–10)

Our thanksgiving is imperfect in this world.

But one great purpose of preaching is **to strengthen and advance believers' faith**.

The means God uses to bring faith into existence are also the means he uses **to increase and confirm it**.

His appointed ordinances and especially **the hearing of his word** serve both purposes.

**Faith comes through hearing, and it is strengthened through hearing**.

## Prayer for growing love and holiness (verses 11–13)

Prayer is an act of **religious worship**, and worship belongs **to God alone**.

We approach God in prayer as **our Father**.

Prayer is not only offered **in the name of Jesus Christ**.

Because Jesus is our Lord and Savior, **prayer can be addressed directly to him**.

Acknowledge God in all your ways, and he will guide your paths.

**Mutual love** is required of every Christian.

Love comes from God and fulfills both **the gospel's command** and **the moral law**.

We need the Holy Spirit's work for continued growth in grace, and **prayer is the appointed means of seeking that help**.

Everyone who hopes to enter heaven must pursue **holiness**.

Our behavior must not contradict our profession of holiness.

The Lord Jesus will certainly return **in glory**, bringing his holy people with him.

Then we will see both **the beauty and the necessity of holiness**.

No heart will remain firmly established at that final day without it.

And nobody who lacks it will avoid condemnation.
