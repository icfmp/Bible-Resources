# Jeremiah 30 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Troubles which shall be before the restoration of Israel. | 1–11 |
| Encouragement to trust Divine promises. | 12–17 |
| The blessings under Christ, and the wrath on the wicked. | 18–24 |

## God commands Jeremiah to record promises of restoration (verses 1–11)

Jeremiah was to write the very words God spoke to him.

They were **words taught by the Holy Spirit**.

Promises committed to writing **at God's command** are truly his word.

He had to record the suffering the people were experiencing and the further trouble still ahead.

Yet their calamities would ultimately have **a happy end**.

The church may suffer for a long time, but **its afflictions will not last forever**.

The Jews would be restored.

Henry sees in this prophecy not only deliverance from Babylon, but also **Israel and Judah's future happy condition when converted to Christ, the Son of David and their King**.

He also understands it as referring to hardships among the nations before Christ's coming.

All people must **honor the Son as they honor the Father** and approach the Father's worship and service **through him**.

Our gracious Savior **pardons the sins of believers** and breaks the yoke of **sin and Satan**.

Thus they can serve God without fear, in **righteousness and true holiness** throughout the remainder of life, as redeemed subjects of **Christ the King**.

## The Lord heals what sinners cannot heal (verses 12–17)

When God is against a people, who can genuinely help them?

No one can give them the rescue they need.

**Incurable suffering grows out of stubborn, incurable sinful desires**.

The exiles deserved correction and lacked the power to deliver themselves.

Yet God intended to appear on their behalf and to punish their oppressors.

He would continue to do so.

Every effort to heal ourselves spiritually remains fruitless if we neglect **our heavenly Advocate, Jesus Christ, and the sanctifying Holy Spirit**.

The way God graciously treats **each genuine convert and every returning backslider** is, in spiritual effect, like the way he restores his people.

## God's renewed covenant and the Messiah's mediation (verses 18–24)

The prophecy continues announcing God's favor after the people's troubles have ended.

The proper work of **Christ as Mediator** is to draw near to God **on our behalf**.

He acts as **the High Priest whom we profess**.

His own undertaking, in obedience to his Father's will and compassion for fallen humanity, engaged him in this work.

In doing all this, Jesus Christ is truly wonderful.

God would renew his covenant relationship with the people in keeping with his covenant with their ancestors.

**“I will be your God”** summarizes his gracious goodwill toward his people.

But his wrath against hardened wickedness is terrifying, like **a whirlwind**.

His purposes of judgment and his purposes of love **will both be fulfilled**.

The Lord comforts everyone who returns to him.

But whoever approaches him must do so with **reverence, devotion and faith**, engaging the heart sincerely.

**How will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation?**
