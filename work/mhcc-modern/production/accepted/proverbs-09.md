# Proverbs 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The invitations of Wisdom. | 1–12 |
| The invitations of folly. | 13–18 |

## Wisdom invites people to Christ (verses 1–12)

Christ has prepared **ordinances** through which those who believe receive spiritual nourishment in this life, and **dwelling places in heaven** where they will be received afterward.

Ministers of the gospel go out to invite guests to this feast.

The invitation is open to everyone; it excludes no one **except those who refuse to come**.

Our Savior came not to call people who consider themselves righteous, but sinners.

He did not come to call those who are **wise in their own eyes** and think they already see clearly.

We must leave the companionship and foolish pleasures of ungodly people if we are to enjoy the pleasures of a holy life.

Seeking out the company of wicked people in the confident expectation of doing them good is dangerous: we are often more likely **to be corrupted by them**.

It is not enough to abandon the foolish; we must join **those who walk in wisdom**.

True wisdom is found in the way of godliness, and true life lies at the end of that way.

Those who embrace it are blessed.

We cannot add anything to God's own happiness or profit him through our obedience. The benefit is **ours**, not his.

But consider the disgrace and destruction awaiting those who disregard Wisdom's invitation.

**God is not the author of sin**.

Satan can tempt, but **he cannot force anyone to sin**.

Those who scorn this offer must bear the loss of what they have rejected. Their contempt makes their condemnation greater.

## Folly offers a deceptive invitation (verses 13–18)

See how diligently the tempter works to draw unwary people into sin.

Worldly and sensual pleasures **dull the conscience** and extinguish the first sparks of conviction.

Folly cannot offer sound reasons for following her.

When she rules a person's soul, knowledge of holy things is forgotten and lost.

Her invitations are persistent and demanding.

We need to seek true wisdom and pray for it, because Satan has many ways of drawing our souls away from Christ.

Not only bodily lusts and openly wicked seducers endanger people.

**False teachers** also lead thousands toward destruction with doctrines that flatter pride and grant permission to sinful desire.

They are especially dangerous to people whose first serious concern for their souls has not yet taken deep root.

Satan's depths are **the depths of hell**.

Unrepented sin ends in destruction without remedy.

Solomon shows us **the hook hidden beneath the bait**. Those who believe his warning will not reach for it.

Notice how miserable, empty, unsatisfying and deceptive are the pleasures sin offers, even when they seem to have been stolen for our enjoyment.

May our desire for the eternal enjoyment of **Jesus Christ** become so strong that we live by faith for him each day now and soon live with him in glory.
