# Judges 15 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Samson is denied his wife, He smites the Philistines. | 1–8 |
| Samson kills a thousand of the Philistines with a jaw-bone. | 9–17 |
| His distress from thirst. | 18–20 |

## Samson is denied his wife and fights the Philistines (verses 1–8)

When relatives disagree, the wisest and best are those most willing to forgive, forget offenses, and yield for the sake of peace.

In considering Samson's unusual methods, we should recognize God's power in making them successful.

Through them God humbled the Philistines' pride and punished their wickedness.

The Philistines had threatened to burn Samson's wife and her father's household.

Trying to save herself and please her countrymen, she betrayed her husband.

But the very disaster she feared and tried to avoid through wrongdoing came upon her.

Her own countrymen burned her and her father's household.

Often, by using unlawful means to escape trouble, we bring that very trouble upon ourselves.

## Samson kills a thousand Philistines with a jawbone (verses 9–17)

Sin destroys courage and blinds people to what would bring them peace.

The Israelites blamed Samson for acting against their enemies, as though his efforts to defend them were a terrible offense.

In a similar way, the Lord Jesus performed many good works, yet people were ready to stone him for them.

When the Spirit of the Lord came upon Samson, the cords binding him came loose.

Where the Lord's Spirit is, there is freedom.

People whom the Spirit frees are truly free.

Christ likewise triumphed over the powers of darkness when they believed they had him helplessly in their control.

Samson defeated many Philistines using nothing more impressive than a **donkey's jawbone**.

God chose a foolish-looking instrument to show that the excellent power was his, not human.

The victory came neither from the weapon nor from Samson's arm, but from **the Spirit of God moving the weapon through his arm**.

We can do everything through the One who strengthens us.

When a poor Christian resists temptation with the help of advice that seems weak or unimpressive, we see another Philistine overcome by an unlikely jawbone.

## Samson prays for water (verses 18–20)

The people of Judah showed so little concern for their deliverer that he was nearly left to die for lack of drinking water.

Those who perform the greatest service may receive the greatest ingratitude.

Samson prayed to God in his distress.

People who forget to praise the Lord may find themselves compelled to pray when trouble comes.

Remembering God's power and kindness in the past gives us good reasons to ask him for fresh mercy.

Samson pleaded that his suffering would expose him to God's enemies.

Our strongest grounds for prayer are those concerned with **God's glory**.

The Lord provided relief at just the right time.

The place was called **Lehi**, after the jawbone.

There God caused a spring of water to appear near Samson.

We would be more grateful for ordinary water if we considered how quickly life would fail without it.

The people of Israel now submitted to the man they had earlier betrayed.

God was with him, and he began to lead them as their judge.
