# 2 Kings 9 — Modern English

## Elisha sends a messenger to anoint Jehu (verses 1–10)

In this and similar events, we should acknowledge the hidden work of God, who moves people to accomplish his purposes concerning them.

God specially chose Jehu to be Israel's king.

Even then the Lord had a remnant of faithful people, and he intended to preserve his worship among them.

Jehu was reminded of this when he received his commission.

He was commanded to destroy Ahab's house.

To the extent that he acted **in obedience to God and from right motives**, he need not be discouraged by criticism or opposition.

The killing of God's prophets received special attention in the judgment announced.

Jezebel persisted in idolatry and hatred toward Jehovah and his servants until her wrongdoing reached its full measure.

## Jehu and the officers (verses 11–15)

People who faithfully bring God's message to sinners have often been dismissed as mad.

Their judgment, language and conduct differ from those of other people.

They are willing to suffer for aims and purposes that worldly people cannot understand.

The accusation is especially often made by the ungodly, who are themselves truly irrational.

By contrast, faithful servants of God demonstrate that their beliefs and actions are wise and reasonable.

Some confidence in God's word seems to have strengthened Jehu for the undertaking before him.

## Jehu kills Joram and Ahaziah (verses 16–29)

Jehu possessed an intensely energetic temperament.

God's wisdom is seen in the different characters of the people he employs.

Yet being known for **fury** is no honor to anyone.

A person who rules his own spirit is greater than a mighty warrior.

Joram met Jehu on the land belonging to **Naboth**.

God sometimes arranges circumstances so that a punishment corresponds strikingly to the crime, as one face reflects another in a mirror.

The path of sin can never become a path of peace (**Isaiah 57:21**).

What real peace can sinners have with God while they persist in sin?

But when sins are repented of and abandoned, peace is possible.

Joram died under the sentence of divine law.

Ahaziah had bound himself to Ahab's family and become one of them through wrongdoing.

Association with evildoers is dangerous because it entangles us in their guilt and suffering.

## Jezebel dies (verses 30–37)

Jezebel did not hide or show fear of God's judgment.

Instead, she continued defiantly to mock the very danger facing her.

A heart hardened against God may resist him boldly to the very end.

Nothing more surely predicts destruction than a heart still proud and unrepentant under circumstances meant to humble it.

Anyone who tempts other people into wickedness or leads them away from truth and righteousness should consider Jezebel's behavior and death.

Jehu called for assistance, and her attendants handed her over.

Thus her life ended.

The destruction of pride and cruelty calls us to acknowledge the Lord's righteousness.

When we indulge bodily desires, remember how perishable the body is.

Soon it will decay, whether beneath the ground or exposed to animals above it.

May we all flee from the divine wrath revealed against human ungodliness and unrighteousness.
