# Ecclesiastes 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The vanity of riches. Also of long life and flourishing families. | 1–6 |
| The little advantage any one has in outward things. | 7–12 |

## Riches, children and long life cannot guarantee happiness (verses 1–6)

A person may possess everything needed for earthly comfort and yet remain so enslaved to **greed or other sinful dispositions** that it becomes impossible to use those gifts wisely or enjoy them peacefully.

Eventually those possessions pass into the hands of strangers.

Such a life is vanity and a grievous affliction.

Among the Hebrews, a large family was highly desired and honored.

Nearly everyone also wants a long life.

Yet even when a person enjoys both advantages, that person may still gain no happiness from wealth, family or length of days.

Someone in this condition seems to have passed through the world **without accomplishing anything useful or good**.

Solomon even says that a child who lives for only a moment and then dies has a better portion than someone who lives a very long life in nothing but continual suffering.

## Outward advantages cannot satisfy the soul (verses 7–12)

Only a little is needed to support life in reasonable comfort.

Great abundance can do no more than provide for those basic needs.

Worldly wealth has nothing that can satisfy the soul's deepest desires.

The poor can enjoy comfort just as the wealthy do, and poverty does not necessarily put them at a true disadvantage.

We should never say that **what the eyes can see** is better than the soul's rest in God.

It is better to live by **faith in future blessings** than to be governed only by immediate sights and pleasures.

God has appointed our circumstances.

What he chooses to give should satisfy us.

Even the greatest possessions and honors cannot lift us above the ordinary changes and hardships of human life.

What then do we finally gain through all our worldly plans and pursuits, when those pursuits only multiply vanity?

Life upon earth is measured in **days**.

It is short and uncertain, and it contains little that can sustain our hopes permanently.

Let us return to God, trust **his mercy through Jesus Christ** and submit to his will.

Soon we will pass through this troubled world and enter the place where joy is full and pleasures last forever.
