# Isaiah 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The peaceful character of Christ's kingdom and subjects. | 1–9 |
| The conversion of the Gentiles and Jews. | 10–16 |

## The Messiah's righteous and peaceful kingdom (verses 1–9)

The Messiah is called **a Rod and a Branch**, terms suggesting a small, delicate shoot that might easily be broken.

He grows from **Jesse's stem**, after David's royal family has been cut down and brought almost to the ground.

By the time Jesus was born, that house had sunk very low.

His humble beginning showed that **his kingdom was not of this world**.

The Holy Spirit rests upon him, with every gift and grace.

The fullness of deity dwells in him (**Colossians 1:19; 2:9**).

Henry notes that many readers recognize **seven gifts of the Spirit** in this description, and sees the passage as clearly teaching the Spirit's work.

The Messiah governs with perfect justice and righteousness.

He accomplishes his judgments by **his word and Spirit**.

Great peace belongs to his kingdom.

The gospel transforms human nature. Those who once oppressed gentle people become gentle and kind themselves.

This transformation has appeared throughout Christian history, although it awaits a **far fuller manifestation in the latter days**.

As **the great Shepherd**, Christ cares for his flock and changes the significance of suffering and death so thoroughly that these things cannot do his people ultimate harm.

God's people are freed not only from evil itself but also from the **fear** of it.

Who can separate them from Christ's love?

As we grow in the knowledge of the God of love, we become more like him and more loving toward those who bear his likeness.

Knowledge of the Lord will spread as widely as the sea extends.

Christians in every age have witnessed something of this blessed power, though **the most glorious fulfillment remains ahead**.

Meanwhile, let us live and work in ways that honor **Christ and his kingdom of peace**.

## The Gentiles and Israel gathered to Christ (verses 10–16)

When the gospel is openly preached, Gentiles will seek **Jesus Christ as Lord and Savior** and find rest for their souls.

When the Lord's time arrives to deliver his people, even mountains of opposition become level ground before him.

He can quickly change dark days into glorious ones.

Henry looks forward to the Lord gathering **his ancient people into the church**, along with the **fullness of the Gentiles**, so that all may be united in holy love.

Until that time, let us follow **the highway of holiness** prepared for the redeemed.

Wait for the mercy of Jesus Christ unto eternal life.

Trust him to prepare the way through **death, the river separating this world from eternity**.
