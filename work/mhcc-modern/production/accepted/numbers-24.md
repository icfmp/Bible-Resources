# Numbers 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Balaam, leaving divinations, prophesies the happiness of Israel. | 1–9 |
| Balak dismisses Balaam in anger. | 10–14 |
| Balaam's prophecies. | 15–25 |

## Balaam foretells Israel's happiness (verses 1–9)

Balaam was no longer expressing his own wishes.

He spoke words given by the Spirit who came upon him.

It is possible for someone's eyes to be opened while the heart remains closed.

People may receive spiritual knowledge without becoming holy.

Knowledge that only increases pride can serve to illuminate a person's road toward destruction.

The blessing resembled the earlier ones.

Balaam admired the beauty of Israel, whose righteous people were more excellent than their neighbors.

He spoke of Israel's fruitfulness and growth, dignity and advancement, power and victories.

He recalled the mighty things God had already done for the people.

He spoke of their courage and security.

The righteous can be as bold as lions without being aggressive toward others, because they rest safely in God's protection.

He also declared that the way other people treated Israel would matter to God himself.

The Lord regarded good or evil done to his people as good or evil done to him.

## Balak sends Balaam away in anger (verses 10–14)

Balak's plan to bring a curse upon Israel had failed.

He became enraged at Balaam and expressed his frustration openly.

Balaam could answer that God had prevented him from speaking the curses he wanted to speak, and had compelled him to give blessings he did not want to give.

## Balaam's final prophecies (verses 15–25)

Under the Spirit's prophetic power, Balaam announced Israel's future prosperity and far-reaching rule.

He boasted of having open eyes.

The prophets of earlier times were sometimes called **seers**.

Balaam had heard God's words.

But a person can hear the words of God without truly listening to God.

He claimed knowledge of the Most High.

Yet someone may possess extensive theological knowledge while remaining entirely without divine grace.

Balaam called God the Most High and the Almighty.

He seemed outwardly reverent, but lacked true love, fear and faith.

A person can advance very far in religious profession while never entering salvation.

Balaam's vision pointed to the great King who would be Israel's glory.

David partly fulfilled the pattern.

But the prophecy chiefly pointed to Jesus Christ, the promised Messiah.

Balaam would see Christ, but not in the way Job had seen his own Redeemer by faith.

He would see him **from far away**, without receiving him as his own.

When Christ returns in the clouds, everyone will see him.

Yet many will see him from a distance, as the rich man in torment saw Abraham.

The promised ruler would arise from Jacob and Israel as a **Star** and a **Scepter**.

The star spoke of his splendor and glory.

The scepter spoke of his power and royal authority.

Christ would reign not only over Israel but over the whole world.

Everyone will either willingly submit to his gracious royal rule or fall under his judgment.

Balaam also foretold what would happen to the Amalekites and Kenites, whose lands were nearby.

A nest high among the rocks cannot give permanent protection against God's purposes.

Henry understands the later prophecies to reach as far as the Greeks and Romans, showing that the rise and fall of kingdoms belongs to the Lord.

These upheavals would bring widespread destruction, leaving some survivors only as people snatched from a fire.

May God prepare us for the worst times!

Henry goes further in his confessional interpretation.

He understands Balaam as cursing **Amalek as an early enemy of the church and Rome as its last enemy**, and applies the latter not only to pagan Rome but also to **papal Rome**, which he associates with Antichrist and antichristian powers.

This is Henry's historical Protestant polemic and is preserved as his interpretation, not adopted as an independent modern editorial judgment.

What do we have that Balaam lacked?

Skill in public speaking, eloquent preaching or prayer, religious knowledge and even remarkable gifts cannot save anyone by themselves.

Balaam possessed such gifts, yet loved the rewards of wrongdoing and died an enemy of God.

Simple trust in the Redeemer's atoning blood and sanctifying grace, willing submission to God's will, and steady efforts to honor God and help his people may appear less impressive.

But those qualities truly accompany salvation.

No proud hypocrite possesses them, while even the weakest believer has something of them and prays daily for more.

## Editorial review — Numbers 24

- **15–25:** **prophetic knowledge without grace**, David as type and Christ chiefly fulfilled, Job's personal Redeemer versus Balaam's distant sight, **Star/glory and Scepter/authority**, worldwide judgment, Amalekites/Kenites/Greek/Roman horizons, Henry's **explicit anti-papal historical interpretation attributed to him without alteration**, spiritual gifts versus saving faith — retained.
