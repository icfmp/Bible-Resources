## The second temple will have greater glory (verses 1–9)

Those who serve the Lord sincerely will find encouragement to continue.

The builders could not construct a temple as splendid as Solomon's.

God is pleased when we do our best in his service.

But our proud hearts are often dissatisfied unless we can do as much as others whose abilities and opportunities are far greater than ours.

The Lord nevertheless encouraged the people to continue.

**God was with them**, and his Spirit and special presence remained among them.

Though he corrects his people for their wrongdoing, his faithfulness does not fail.

The Messiah, **the One who was to come**, would eventually be among them.

Great changes would take place among the Jewish people, but first there would be upheaval among the nations.

Henry understands the promised **“Desire of all nations”** as **the Messiah**.

He is desirable to every nation because in him all the peoples of the earth may receive the greatest blessings.

Believers had long expected and desired him.

The temple under construction would be filled with a glory surpassing the first temple's magnificence.

It would be glory **of an entirely different nature**.

If we possess silver and gold, we should use them to serve and honor God, because everything belongs to him.

If we possess neither, we should honor him with what we do have, knowing he will receive our service.

Henry sees the greater glory of the second temple in the **personal presence of Jesus Christ, the Son of God, in human nature**.

No merely material splendor could equal that.

For Henry, Jesus is the promised Christ; believers should look for no other.

He also understands the prophecy as a challenge to those Jews who rejected him, and anticipates that they will one day recognize his worth.

The later temple still witnessed much suffering.

But the promised peace finds its fulfillment in **the spiritual peace Christ purchased through his blood** for everyone who believes.

The changing kingdoms of the world will prepare the way for people of all nations to recognize the value of Christ.

## The people's uncleanness obstructs their worship (verses 10–19)

Some people harmed this good work by attempting it with unholy hearts and hands.

They could not expect to benefit from what they were doing while holding fast to sin.

The two examples taken from the law make an important distinction.

**Holiness is not easily transferred to someone else, but moral uncleanness readily spreads.**

Their sinful hearts and conduct contaminated their work and offerings in God's sight.

The same warning applies to us.

Even when performing a good duty, we should watch against our own corruption, lest it defile our service.

Once we begin to take God's commands seriously and obey them conscientiously, we may expect his gracious care.

Those who are wise will learn to recognize the Lord's steadfast kindness.

He can turn what the wicked call blessings into curses and make the prosperity of careless people bitter.

But he can also **sweeten the cup of suffering** for those who serve him diligently.

## Zerubbabel and Christ's kingdom (verses 20–23)

The Lord promised to preserve **Zerubbabel and the people of Judah** in the midst of their enemies.

Henry also understands the passage as announcing the establishment and continuation of **Christ's kingdom**.

Those united to Christ are marked by the Holy Spirit and bear his image, distinguishing them from others.

Henry sees the prophecy reaching forward to the time when the kingdom of Christ would overthrow and take the place of the empires opposed to his cause.

The promise has special reference to Christ because **Jesus descended from Zerubbabel**.

He alone builds the spiritual temple of the gospel.

Jesus is **the signet on God's right hand**, the One to whom all authority has been given and from whom rightful authority is derived.

In him, all God's promises are **Yes and Amen**.

Whatever changes occur in the world, the Lord can turn them toward the eventual comfort, honor and happiness of his servants.
