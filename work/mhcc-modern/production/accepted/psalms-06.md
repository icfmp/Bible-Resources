# Psalm 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist deprecates God's wrath, and begs for the return of his favour. | 1–7 |
| He assures himself of an answer of peace. | 8–10 |

## A humbled heart seeks divine favor (verses 1–7)

These words express a truly humbled heart, a broken and contrite spirit enduring great suffering.

The affliction was allowed to awaken the psalmist's conscience and help put sinful desires to death.

Illness reminded him of his sins, and he feared that it showed God's displeasure.

**Bodily suffering can be endured when the soul enjoys God's consolation**.

In his own suffering, **Christ's deepest complaint** concerned the anguish of his soul and the apparent withdrawal of his Father's favor.

Every part of Scripture declares that **salvation belongs to the Lord alone**.

Human beings are sinners, whose condition can be remedied only by mercy.

That mercy shines especially brightly when God restores people who have wandered from him.

We may rightly pray that, if it is God's will and he has further work for us or our loved ones to do on earth, he will preserve our lives for continued service.

For the saints, **departing to be with Christ** is the greater happiness.

Yet remaining alive may be more useful for the church.

## Confidence that God will answer (verses 8–10)

What a remarkable change in the psalmist's words!

After laying his request before God, he became confident that sorrow would turn into joy.

Through grace working within his heart, he knew his prayer had been accepted and trusted that an answer would come in due time.

Our prayers are acceptable because they rise to God **through the hands of Christ our Mediator**.

The words describe prayer to God as **the righteous Judge and the God of the believer's righteousness**, who will defend the right and put wrongs right.

Through **Christ's blood and righteousness**, a believer can approach a righteous God to ask for forgiveness and cleansing.

God is both just and faithful to grant these blessings.

The psalmist's words regarding his enemies may be either **a prayer for their conversion** or **a prediction of their downfall**.
