# 1 Corinthians 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The true character of gospel ministers. | 1–6 |
| Cautions against despising the apostle. | 7–13 |
| He claims their regard as their spiritual father in Christ, and shows his concern for them. | 14–21 |

## Ministers are servants entrusted with Christ's work (verses 1–6)

The apostles were **servants of Christ**, and should never be treated as if they were their Master. Yet they should not be undervalued, because the Lord had entrusted them with something of extraordinary importance. Their calling was honorable precisely because of this responsibility.

Paul rightly cared about his reputation. But someone whose chief ambition is **to please other people** cannot remain a faithful servant of Christ.

It is a comfort that human beings will **not be our final judges**.

Nor can we make ourselves safe simply by giving ourselves a favorable verdict. **Our judgment of our faithfulness is no more dependable than our own works are sufficient to justify us before God.**

A day is approaching when everything now concealed will be brought into the light. People's hidden sins and the secrets of their hearts will be disclosed.

Then every believer wrongly slandered will be vindicated, and every faithful servant will be approved and rewarded.

For now, **God's word is our best standard for judging people and their conduct**.

Pride frequently lies beneath quarrels. The same self-importance that leads us to admire ourselves too highly can also make us give **excessive honor to the teachers we prefer**.

We will not become inflated in favor of one leader against another if we remember that all ministers are instruments **appointed by God and equipped with gifts from him**.

## The apostles' sufferings expose worldly boasting (verses 7–13)

We have no grounds for pride.

Everything genuinely good in who we are, what we possess and what we do comes from **God's rich, freely given grace**.

It would be absurd for a sinner rescued from destruction by sovereign mercy to boast about gifts received from that mercy.

Paul described the apostles' circumstances by an image drawn from **the cruel spectacles of the Roman games** (verse 9).

Men were compelled to fight one another for the crowd's entertainment. Even a victor who survived one fight was kept for another, until eventually he too would be killed.

Believers should remember that many eyes are upon them when they struggle with trouble or temptation. Such knowledge should encourage **faithfulness and patience**.

Paul wrote, **“We are weak, but you are strong.”**

Christians do not all experience the same trials. Some endure much greater hardships than others.

The apostles suffered in body and reputation. They were treated like the most contemptible people on earth, **the dirt swept away and the refuse discarded**.

Yet love for Christ and devotion to his work carried them through their suffering.

Everyone determined to be faithful to Jesus must be prepared for **poverty and contempt**, if these come.

Christ's disciples are called to follow his example and obey his commands even when the world despises or mistreats them.

It is far better to be rejected as Paul was than to possess the world's approval while abandoning Christ.

Those regarded by the world as worthless may be **precious to God**. He will receive them and grant them a place with him.

## Correction should be loving and spiritually effective (verses 14–21)

When correcting wrongdoing, distinguish between **the person and the sin**.

A loving and affectionate warning is more likely to lead to genuine reform than a harsh rebuke.

Although Paul could speak with a father's authority, **he preferred to appeal to the Corinthians in love**.

Ministers should provide an example, and believers should imitate them **only insofar as they imitate Christ in faith and conduct**.

Christians may misunderstand matters or differ in their opinions. Yet **Christ and the truth he has revealed remain the same yesterday, today and forever**.

Where the gospel is truly effective, it comes **not only in words but also in the power of the Holy Spirit**.

The Spirit gives life to sinners who were spiritually dead.

He releases people from slavery to sin and Satan, renews them **inwardly and outwardly**, and comforts, strengthens and establishes the saints.

Persuasive human speech cannot accomplish these things. **They require God's power**.

It is a blessed disposition when **love and gentleness govern our conduct** while we still exercise the rightful authority entrusted to us.
