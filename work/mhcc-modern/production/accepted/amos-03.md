# Amos 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Judgments against Israel. | 1–8 |
| The like to other nations. | 9–15 |

## Israel's privileges do not exempt it from judgment (verses 1–8)

The special blessings God has given us **cannot protect us from judgment** if we use them as permission to sin.

People cannot expect fellowship with God without first seeking **peace with him**.

Where there is no friendship, there can be no fellowship.

God and humanity cannot walk together unless they are agreed.

We cannot walk with him unless we **seek his glory**.

External privileges provide no guarantee without **particular, sanctifying grace**.

The warnings contained in God's word and Providence are certain.

When he sends affliction, he will not remove it until it has fulfilled its purpose.

**The evil of sin comes from ourselves**; we are responsible for committing it.

But **the evil of trouble** is under God's government, whatever human or natural agents he uses.

Henry is explicit that here **“evil” means calamity, not moral evil or sin**.

That should lead us to bear shared suffering patiently and ask what God intends to teach through it.

A warning given to an indifferent world increases its responsibility before the coming judgment.

How astonishing that unbelieving people can disregard both **God's terrors and his mercies**!

## The ruin of unjust power (verses 9–15)

Power used as an instrument of injustice will rightly be brought low.

Property and wealth **obtained and kept dishonestly** will not remain secure for long.

Some enjoy comfortable lives, but a day of reckoning will come.

Everything in which they took pride or placed their confidence will fail them.

God will examine the wrongdoing done **within their own homes**: the robbery they stored up and the luxury they enjoyed.

Splendid and pleasant houses provide no defense against divine judgment.

Instead, their loss may make suffering feel even more severe.

Yet God will preserve **a remnant chosen by grace**, as sheep rescued from the jaws of destruction by **the great and good Shepherd**.
