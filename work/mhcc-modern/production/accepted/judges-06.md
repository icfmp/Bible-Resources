# Judges 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Israel oppressed by Midianites. | 1–6 |
| Israel rebuked by a prophet. | 7–10 |
| Gideon set to deliver Israel. | 11–24 |
| Gideon destroys Baal's altar. | 25–32 |
| Signs given him. | 33–40 |

## Israel suffers under Midian (verses 1–6)

Israel renewed its sins, and the same pattern of suffering returned.

Everyone who persists in sin should expect painful consequences.

The Israelites hid in caves and rocky shelters.

Their guilty consciences had made them fearful.

Sin robs people of courage.

The invaders left them no food except what they managed to hide away in the caves.

The people had devoted to Baal what ought to have been used to serve God.

The Lord therefore justly allowed an enemy to take their produce away at harvest time.

## A prophet calls Israel to repentance (verses 7–10)

The Israelites cried out to God for a deliverer.

First he sent a prophet to teach them.

When God provides a country with faithful ministers, it is a sign that he has mercy in store for that country.

The prophet accused Israel of rebellion.

God intended to bring the people to repentance.

Repentance is genuine when we grieve chiefly because our sin was **disobedience against God**, not merely because of the trouble that followed it.

## Gideon is called to deliver Israel (verses 11–24)

Gideon had courage and an active character, but these gifts remained obscure because of Israel's distress.

God now stirred him to a great task.

The Lord was truly with Gideon because the Angel of the Lord was with him.

Yet Gideon's faith was weak.

He struggled to reconcile God's promised presence with the suffering Israel had endured.

The Angel answered his objections and told him to act as Israel's deliverer.

Gideon needed no further qualification than the divine call and assurance.

Bishop Hall observed that **when God called Gideon valiant, he also made him valiant**.

The Lord delights in raising up humble people.

Gideon asked for his faith to be confirmed.

But under the Spirit's ministry today, we should not expect the same visible signs Gideon received.

Instead, we should pray for God to show us the inward evidence of his grace through the powerful work of the Holy Spirit in our hearts.

The Angel turned Gideon's food into an offering consumed by fire.

This showed that he was not an ordinary human being who needed food, but **the Son of God**, worthy of sacrificial worship and destined to offer himself as the sacrifice in the fullness of time.

The sign assured Gideon that God favored him.

Since human sin brought guilt and the divine curse, a heavenly message often terrifies sinful people.

The invisible spiritual world is unfamiliar and awe-inspiring to us.

Gideon grew afraid, but God spoke peace to him.

## Gideon destroys the altar of Baal (verses 25–32)

God's grace showed its power by raising up a reformer, and its kindness by choosing a deliverer from the household of a leader in idolatry.

Gideon could not be content merely to avoid worshiping at Baal's altar.

He had to remove that altar and offer sacrifice in another place.

He needed to make peace with God before making war on Midian.

Until sin has been forgiven through the great Sacrifice, we can expect no real good.

The Lord governs every heart.

He moved **Joash**, Gideon's father, to defend him from the supporters of Baal—even though Joash had formerly worshiped Baal himself.

Let us do our duty and entrust our safety to God.

Joash challenged Baal to show whether he could do good or harm.

The challenge exposed how foolish it was to pray for help to a god who could not even defend his own altar.

## Gideon receives the signs of the fleece (verses 33–40)

The signs God gave were genuinely miraculous and full of meaning.

Gideon was preparing to lead a small Israelite force against the larger Midianite army.

Could God distinguish between **Israel's small fleece** and **Midian's broad threshing floor**?

The miracle showed that he could.

Gideon wanted the dew of God's grace to fall on him personally.

The fleece became wet with dew, reassuring him that God could do so.

He also wanted God to be like refreshing dew to all Israel.

Then the entire ground became wet.

We Gentile believers have every reason to bless the Lord.

The heavenly gifts that once seemed restricted to Israel have now been spread among the peoples of the earth.

Nevertheless, the means of grace are still received in different measures according to God's purposes.

Even within one congregation, one person's soul may resemble the dew-covered fleece while another remains like dry ground.
