# Deuteronomy 27 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The law to be written on stones in the promised land. | 1–10 |
| The curses to be pronounced on mount Ebal. | 11–26 |

## God's law and the altar of uncut stone (verses 1–10)

As soon as Israel entered Canaan, the people were to erect a monument on which God's law would be written.

They were also to build an altar.

**God's word and prayer belong together.**

Israelites were not free to set up other altars wherever they pleased.

But God could command one for a particular occasion.

The altar was to be built from rough, uncut stones found in the field.

Christ, our Altar, is the Stone cut from the mountain without human hands.

He was rejected by the builders as lacking outward beauty, yet God the Father accepted him and made him the chief cornerstone.

The Old Testament's written law carries a curse against transgression.

That curse would terrify us if the New Testament did not also reveal an altar—Christ—to console and save us.

Printed copies of Scripture now spare us from the need to write its words publicly in the particular manner Israel was commanded.

The purpose of gospel ministry should be to make God's word **as clear as possible**.

Yet even the clearest preaching cannot make us wise for salvation unless the Holy Spirit gives it saving power.

We should therefore pray continually and earnestly for the Spirit's work.

## The curses announced on Mount Ebal (verses 11–26)

The six tribes assigned to pronounce blessings descended from the free women.

Henry relates this to the promise belonging to the free woman in Galatians 4:31.

Levi was included among the tribes.

Ministers should apply God's warnings and promises first to their **own consciences**.

They should agree with faith-filled **Amen** to what they announce to others.

They must encourage people through promised blessings and also warn them through the threatened curse.

Every curse was met with an Amen from the people.

That meant they accepted that God truly judges the ungodliness and unrighteousness of human beings.

They also acknowledged that those who commit such wrongs deserve his judgment.

The final curse reached beyond the particular offenses named.

It included **everyone who fails to carry out the whole law**.

Not only committing the evil the law forbids but also neglecting the good the law commands brings guilt.

Without Christ's atoning blood, sinners cannot enjoy fellowship with a holy God or offer him anything truly acceptable.

God's righteous law condemns whoever breaks it, however briefly or in whatever way.

We remain under its curse until Christ's redemption is applied to our hearts.

Saving grace also teaches believers to reject ungodliness and worldly desires and to live with self-control, righteousness and reverence for God in the present world.

Such people assent to God's law and delight in it inwardly.

The truly holy life is the place where solid joy and genuine peace are found.
