# Colossians 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle Paul salutes the Colossians, and blesses God for their faith, love, and hope. | 1–8 |
| Prays for their fruitfulness in spiritual knowledge. | 9–14 |
| Gives a glorious view of Christ. | 15–23 |
| And sets out his own character, as the apostle of the Gentiles. | 24–29 |

## Faith, hope and love grow from the gospel (verses 1–8)

All genuine Christians are **brothers and sisters in Christ**.

Faithfulness should mark every Christian role and relationship.

**Faith, hope and love** are the three great graces of the Christian life, and we have reason to bring each of them before God in prayer and thanksgiving.

When we place our hope firmly in **the reward God has prepared in the life to come**, we become more willing to use earthly resources for the good of others.

The heavenly inheritance is safely stored for believers; no enemy can take it away.

The gospel is **God's true word**, and we can safely entrust our souls to its promises.

Those who hear the gospel should also **bear its fruit**.

They ought to obey it, allowing it to shape **their convictions, principles and conduct**.

Worldly affection often arises from advantage or similarity in habits. Carnal affection seeks pleasure.

Both forms of affection are easily mixed with selfishness and other corrupt motives.

But Christian love is **born of the Holy Spirit** and is marked by holiness.

## Paul's prayer for knowledge, strength and endurance (verses 9–14)

Paul prayed continually that the believers would be filled with **knowledge of God's will and spiritual wisdom**.

Good words are not enough without **good works**.

The God who gives his people strength possesses **glorious power**, and the blessed Holy Spirit is the source of this work within them.

God's promises are abundant. We need not restrict our prayers or hopes for spiritual strength as though he lacked resources to provide it.

God's grace in the believer's heart is **his power**, a power full of glory.

A special purpose of this strength is to sustain us **during suffering**.

There remains work to be done even when we are afflicted.

Amid trials, believers can give thanks to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, whose special grace prepares them to share in **the inheritance of the saints**.

Those once enslaved by Satan become **willing subjects of Jesus Christ**.

Everyone destined for heaven must be **prepared for heaven while still here**.

Those who receive the inheritance of God's children must also receive **the training and character of his children**.

Through faith in Christ, they enjoy redemption, purchased through **his atoning blood**, and thus receive **forgiveness of sins** and every spiritual blessing.

What mercy to be delivered from Satan's kingdom and brought into the kingdom of Christ!

All present suffering will eventually end, and every believer will be among those who come through **great tribulation** into glory.

## Christ creates, sustains and reconciles all things (verses 15–23)

In his **human nature**, Christ makes **the invisible God visible**.

Whoever has seen Christ has seen the Father.

We should contemplate these mysteries with humble faith, recognizing God's glory in Jesus Christ.

Scripture describes the Son as born or begotten **before any creature existed**, representing his **eternity** in language we can understand.

**All things were created through him and for him**.

His power brought them into existence.

They exist according to his will and for his praise and glory.

He also **sustains everything by his powerful word**.

As Mediator, Christ is **the Head of the church**, which is his body.

All its grace, strength and life come from him.

**All fullness dwells in Christ**: fullness of merit and righteousness, strength and grace sufficient for all who believe.

God displayed his justice by requiring **complete satisfaction for sin**.

Christ's death was the fitting means through which sinners could be redeemed and reconciled to their Maker.

Though God hates sin, he was pleased to reconcile fallen human beings to himself through Jesus Christ.

We were formerly **enemies in our minds**, displaying hostility through wicked actions.

Yet through Christ's sacrifice and death **in our human nature**, we can be reconciled.

We should neither explain away these mysteries nor imagine that our limited minds can comprehend them completely.

Rather, we should behold the beauty of this plan of salvation and rejoice in **the hope it gives**.

If God has shown such extraordinary love toward us, how should we respond?

Be **constant in prayer** and diligent in holy service.

Do not continue living for yourself.

**Christ died for us** not so that we would persist in sin, but so that we would **die to sin and live for him**.

## Paul serves Christ's church through suffering (verses 24–29)

The sufferings of **Christ the Head** and of **his church as the members of his body** are spoken of together.

Yet their purposes are not the same.

**Christ suffered to redeem his church. His people suffer for other reasons**.

The afflictions we endure are a small share compared with the bitter cup he drank for us.

A Christian can be said to complete what remains of Christ's sufferings when that person **takes up the cross**, follows his example and patiently bears the trials God assigns.

Let us thank God for revealing mysteries hidden through many generations and for making known the riches of his glory among us.

Since Christ is preached among us, we should ask seriously: **Does he truly live and reign within us?**

Only Christ's indwelling provides a firm ground for **the hope of his glory**.

We must remain faithful **even to death**, enduring every trial, so that we may receive the crown of life and reach the final purpose of our faith—the **salvation of our souls**.
