# Ezra 9 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Ezra grieves over Israel's wrongdoing (verses 1–4)

Even the most careful leaders may fail to see some of the corruption hidden among the people.

Some Israelites had disobeyed God's explicit command concerning marriage with the nations (**Deuteronomy 7**).

Their unwillingness to trust God's complete sufficiency lay behind the compromises they made to help themselves.

Through those relationships they exposed themselves and their children to the idolatry that had already ruined their church and nation.

People who merely profess religion may dismiss such concerns or explain away God's commands about separation.

Those who know Scripture well recognize their seriousness.

It should astonish and grieve faithful believers when people who profess Christ defend serious wrongdoing.

Everyone claiming to be God's people should support those who stand and act against sin and irreverence.

## Ezra confesses the people's sins (verses 5–15)

The sacrifice, especially the **evening sacrifice**, pointed toward **the Lamb of God**, who in the latter time would take away sin through his own offering.

Ezra confessed his people's sins in a spirit of repentance.

There is comfort for true penitents: although sins reach to heaven, **God's mercy also reaches to heaven**.

Ezra spoke with **shame**.

Genuine repentance needs holy shame as well as sorrow.

He spoke with **amazement**.

The more we consider guilt, the worse sin appears.

Let us cry, **“God, have mercy on me, a sinner.”**

Ezra also spoke with **fear**.

There is hardly a clearer sign of approaching ruin than returning to wrongdoing after severe judgments and great deliverances.

Everyone in God's church should be astonished that they have not exhausted the Lord's patience and brought destruction upon themselves.

What, then, of the openly ungodly?

The true penitent has nothing to plead in self-defense.

But **our heavenly Advocate pleads powerfully on our behalf**.
