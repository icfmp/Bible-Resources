# Psalm 119 — Modern English

## Original authorial introduction

The chief purpose of this psalm is to honor **God's law** and show its greatness.

Henry identifies **ten terms** used here for divine revelation, each teaching us something about what the Lord expects of us and what we may expect from him.

1. **His law** is enacted by God, our Sovereign.
2. **His way** describes the rule of his Providence.
3. **His testimonies** are solemnly declared to the world.
4. **His commandments** are given with divine authority.
5. **His precepts** are binding rather than optional.
6. **His word or saying** declares God's mind.
7. **His judgments** proceed from infinite wisdom.
8. **His righteousness** is the standard of what is right.
9. **His statutes** remain continually binding.
10. **His truth or faithfulness** endures forever.

The original secondary witness presents this introduction before the first exposition, without assigning it to an invented verse section.

## Wholehearted obedience through grace (verses 1–8)

This psalm can be read as **the spiritual experience of a believer**.

Our attitudes, desires and affections come from the Holy Spirit only to the extent that they agree with what the psalm expresses.

**God's forgiving mercy in Christ** is the only source of lasting happiness for a sinner.

The happiest people are those preserved most carefully from sin's pollution, who believe the Lord's testimony and rely on his promises.

A heart divided between God and the world is in a dangerous condition.

The saints do not claim to have no remaining sin. They know there are many things within them that hinder obedience.

Yet they do not willingly embrace the wickedness that would draw them entirely away from the Lord's paths.

The tempter would persuade people that obeying God's word is merely a personal choice.

But a godly person's desires and prayers agree with **God's will and commands**.

We cannot expect obedience in one duty to purchase permission for disobedience in others.

Such hypocrisy will eventually be exposed and bring shame.

The psalmist wanted to learn God's law and give him glory.

Believers know that if God abandons them to themselves, **the tempter will be too strong**.

## God's word treasured in the heart (verses 9–16)

To the corruption inherited from Adam, all people have added their own actual sins.

Young people may ruin themselves by living without any standard or by choosing a false one.

Let them use **Scripture as their rule of life**.

Distrusting our own wisdom and strength and depending upon God show a sincere desire for holiness.

God's word is a treasure worth storing.

Its safest resting place is **within the heart**.

We then answer sin's demands with God's precepts, its attractions with his promises, and its threats with his warnings.

Ask the Lord to teach his statutes, so that sharing in **his holiness** we may also share in his blessedness.

Those nourished by **the bread of life** ought to feed others through their words.

God's commands lead us toward **the unsearchable riches of Christ**.

But meditation is not truly useful unless good thoughts produce **good actions**.

We should not merely consider God's statutes, but practice them gladly.

Test the sincerity of obedience by examining **what motivates it**, and test love for God by whether his appointed duties are performed cheerfully.

## Strangers on earth, sustained by God's counsel (verses 17–24)

If God judged us with complete strictness, every one of us would perish.

Our lives should therefore be spent in his service.

We find true life through keeping his word.

To see the wonders of God's law and gospel, we must ask for understanding **through his Spirit's light**.

Believers know they are strangers on earth.

They fear losing their way or forfeiting comfort through disobedience.

A sanctified soul hungers for God's word as for food without which life cannot continue.

Every deliberate sin contains an element of pride.

God can silence liars.

Reproach and contempt may humble us and do us good before they are removed.

If the cross seems too heavy for us, **the One who carried it for us** can give us strength to carry it.

He will not let those upheld by him finally sink.

It is especially painful when people who should protect the innocent betray them.

The psalmist nevertheless continued in duty and found comfort in Scripture.

When other consolations become bitter, the Lord's word may become even sweeter to the believer.

To delight in God's testimonies, we must **accept their guidance**.

May he direct our repentance and our faith in Christ.

## Repentance and an enlarged heart (verses 25–32)

Worldly-minded people cling to the earth as their ultimate possession.

Believers, by contrast, grieve over the remaining earthly desires within their own hearts.

It comforts them deeply to know how tenderly God receives their complaints.

We can speak of the wonders of redeeming love when we understand God's way and walk in it.

Repentance melts the heart with sorrow for sin.

Even a patient believer may be overcome by affliction and should then pour out the soul before God.

The **way of falsehood** includes every deceptive path—whether we deceive others, ourselves, or are misled by Satan and his agents.

Those who know and love God's law want to know it better and love it more.

The way of sincere godliness is **the way of truth** and the only true road to happiness.

We must take it seriously at every step.

Those who hold to God's word may pray in faith for acceptance.

Lord, do not let me act in a way that brings shame upon me, and do not reject my service.

Those journeying toward heaven must continue pressing forward.

God's Spirit **enlarges the heart** as he gives wisdom and freedom from sin.

## Understanding God's law through his Spirit (verses 33–40)

Teach me your statutes, Lord—not merely their words, but how they apply to my life.

The Holy Spirit gives right understanding.

Having revelation written in Scripture is not enough unless **the Spirit gives wisdom in the heart**.

God puts his Spirit within believers and moves them to obey his statutes.

The psalmist prays particularly against **covetousness**.

To have the love of God rooted in our hearts, we must uproot the love of the world.

Friendship with the world is hostility toward God.

Give me new life in your way, so that I redeem my time and perform my duties eagerly.

Staring at empty attractions slows us down.

A traveler cannot reach the destination while stopping to inspect every passing sight.

God's promises assure believers of his preserving care.

When Satan persuades one of God's children to compromise with the world, he later reproaches that believer for the very fall he caused.

**Victory comes through Christ's cross**.

The sweetness of God's commands makes us want to know them more.

Where the Lord has given the will to obey, he will also provide the strength to act.

## Freedom through obedience (verses 41–48)

Lord, faith has shown me your mercies.

Let prayer bring me to enjoy what you promised.

When the salvation of the saints is complete, everyone will see that trust in God's word was never wasted.

Pray that we will never be ashamed or afraid to acknowledge God's truth before other people.

The psalmist resolved to continue obeying, without turning back.

**Serving sin is slavery. Serving God is freedom**.

Complete happiness and genuine liberty cannot be separated from following the Lord's law.

We must openly confess our faith without fear or shame.

The more we delight in God's service, the more closely we approach the holiness for which we were created.

We should do more than approve the law as good.

We should enjoy it **as good for us** and exert our strength to follow it.

Something of this attitude of **Christ** belongs to every true disciple.

## Remembering God's promises in adversity (verses 49–56)

Those who have made God's promises their portion may humbly yet boldly appeal to them.

The Spirit who creates faith within us will also act for us.

God's word gives comfort during suffering.

If it makes us holy through grace, it can sustain our peace in any condition.

Be sure that what you believe is genuinely taught by **divine Scripture**.

Then do not allow mockers to frighten you away from it.

God's earlier judgments comfort us because he remains the same.

Sin becomes hateful to those whom the Lord sanctifies.

Soon believers will be absent from the body and present with him.

Meanwhile his statutes give us constant reasons for praise.

During affliction and in the quiet hours of night, the psalmist remembered the Lord's name and returned to his commandments.

Everyone who has made true religion a priority will recognize how richly it has benefited them.

## The Lord is my portion (verses 57–64)

Faithful believers choose the Lord as their inheritance.

Nothing less can satisfy them.

The psalmist prayed with his whole heart because he valued the blessing he requested.

He desired **the promised mercy** and depended upon God's promise to receive it.

He abandoned wrong paths and returned to the Lord's testimonies **without delay**.

Sinners should hurry to escape destruction, and believers should be equally eager to glorify God.

Neither anxiety nor sorrow should erase Scripture from our minds or rob us of its comfort.

There is no earthly situation in which a believer lacks reasons for thanksgiving.

We should be ashamed if others are more willing to lose sleep for sinful pleasures than we are to rise and praise the Lord.

Let us pray earnestly for hearts filled with **mercy, grace and peace**.

## Affliction that teaches God's statutes (verses 65–72)

However God has treated us, **he has treated us better than we deserve**, acting in love for our ultimate good.

Many people have knowledge but little judgment.

Those with both are better prepared against Satan's traps and for God's service.

We are often most inclined to wander from the Lord when life is comfortable.

Because we do not know everything that is good for us, we should entrust our concerns to him.

Lord, you are a generous Benefactor.

Direct our hearts toward faith and obedience.

The psalmist resolved to continue faithfully and steadfastly.

Proud people become so filled with worldly riches and pleasures that they grow spiritually dull and careless.

God sometimes afflicts his people **so they will learn his statutes**.

Not only his promises but also his commands, however difficult they seem to unbelievers, are desirable and beneficial.

They guide us toward **eternal life** with safety and joy.

## The compassion that sustains the upright (verses 73–80)

God created us to serve and enjoy him.

But our own sin has made us unfit to do either.

We must continually ask him, through **the Holy Spirit**, to give us understanding.

When other believers experience comfort from God, it should make us rejoice with them.

It is easy to say God's judgments are right until those judgments affect us personally.

Every support we receive during suffering comes from his mercy and compassion.

His mercies are tender.

They resemble **a father's care** and **a mother's compassion for her child**.

They come toward us even when we are unable to go toward them.

Groundless reproach does not truly harm us and should not divert us from duty.

The psalmist found comfort in continuing faithfully.

He prized the goodwill of fellow believers and desired fellowship with them.

**An upright heart** means sincere dependence upon God and wholehearted devotion to him.

## Hope that continues when strength fails (verses 81–88)

The psalmist asked to be delivered from **sin, enemies and fear**.

Waiting for salvation made him weary; his eyes had grown tired looking for relief.

Yet **when our eyes fail, faith must not fail**.

His suffering was severe.

He compared himself to a **leather bottle hung in smoke**, dried out and shriveled.

Even then, we should remember God's statutes.

The days of believers' mourning will end; beside eternity, those days are brief.

Their enemies use cunning as well as strength, despising God's law.

But his commands remain reliable guides toward peace and safety.

We can especially hope for God's help when, like our Master, **we do good and suffer because of it**.

Wicked people may nearly destroy the believer on earth, but the believer would rather abandon everything else than abandon the Lord's word.

For every good work we depend upon divine grace.

**The surest sign that God favors us is his good work within us**.

## God's word stands forever (verses 89–96)

The permanence of God's word in heaven stands against the changes and revolutions of the earth.

His covenant is established **more firmly than the earth itself**.

All created things fulfill the purposes for which they were made.

Should humanity, uniquely endowed with reason, become the one unprofitable burden upon creation?

Scripture can be our pleasant companion at any hour.

Yet the written word will not give spiritual life **without God's grace**.

The best aid for imperfect memory is **strong affection**.

Even if we forget the exact words, remembering their true meaning is valuable.

**I belong to you**, Lord—not to myself or the world.

Save me from sin and ruin.

God gives peace to the person whose mind remains fixed on him.

The perfections of this world all have limits and eventually fade like grass.

But God's word is **complete and sufficient for every age and every situation**.

It leads us away from confidence in human wisdom, strength and righteousness, directing us to seek happiness and comfort **in Christ alone**.

## Loving and meditating on Scripture (verses 97–104)

We naturally think often about what we love.

All true wisdom comes from God.

A faithful believer carries the Bible everywhere—not necessarily in the hand, but **in mind and heart**.

Meditating on Scripture can give us deeper understanding than respected teachers possess, particularly when we learn to understand our own hearts.

**The written word is a more reliable guide to heaven** than all the church's older teachers and traditions.

We cannot confidently approach God in worship while knowingly clinging to guilt or walking along sinful paths.

Divine grace enabled the psalmist to receive the Lord's instruction.

The soul has tastes and appetites, just as the body does.

Our delight in God's word grows as our appetite for worldly and fleshly pleasures diminishes.

Every path of sin is wrong.

The better we understand God's commands, the more deeply we hate sin.

And the better we know Scripture, the better equipped we become to **answer temptation**.

## A lamp for every step (verses 105–112)

God's word directs our work and our path.

Without it, this world would be terribly dark.

His commandments are like a lamp, kept burning by **the oil of the Spirit**.

They guide both our choice of direction and the steps we take along it.

This obedience belongs to a sinner living under **mercy** and a believer who shares in **the covenant of grace**.

Though frequently afflicted, the psalmist continued longing for holiness and praying daily for life-giving grace.

Nothing we offer to God is acceptable unless he first teaches us how to offer it.

Holding our life continually in our hands means living under constant danger.

Yet the psalmist did not forget God's promises or commandments.

Wicked people set countless traps.

Blessed is the servant whom those traps do not turn away from the Master's way.

Heavenly treasures are **an everlasting inheritance**.

Those who value them can be content with little wealth on earth.

Comfort is found in the way of **duty faithfully performed**.

Through grace, a godly person brings the heart fully into the work and performs it well.

## Watching against the first movements of sin (verses 113–120)

The psalmist feared even the first beginnings of sinful desire.

The more we love God's law, the more carefully we guard against empty thoughts that would draw us away.

To progress in obedience, we must separate ourselves from the influence of evildoers.

Believers cannot live spiritually without grace.

But when God's hand supports them, **their spiritual life is preserved**.

Their security rests upon divine help.

Departing from the Lord's statutes is error that leads toward destruction.

Wicked people's clever schemes are ultimately deceitful.

A day is coming when they will face divine judgment.

They will be removed like **dross cast into a fire**.

Considering sin's consequences, we should fear coming short of the promised heavenly rest (**Hebrews 4:1**).

## Justice, grace and Christ our Surety (verses 121–128)

Happy are those who practice justice toward others **from gospel principles**.

**Christ our Surety** has paid the debt and ransom of his people and secured all the blessings of salvation for genuine believers.

The psalmist expected the fulfillment of God's righteous word.

He looked for no salvation beyond what God had promised.

That word cannot fail.

We deserve no favor from God.

Our hearts find most rest when we entrust ourselves entirely to **his mercy**.

Those resolved to obey as God's servants will be taught his testimonies.

We should do everything within our power to support true religion, then ask God **to take the work into his own hands**.

Claiming to love his commandments more than our earthly interests is hypocrisy if our conduct proves otherwise.

The path of sin is a false path because it contradicts God's righteous commands.

Those who truly value his law **hate sin and refuse to make peace with it**.

## Tears over disobedience (verses 129–136)

The wonders of redeeming love should fill our hearts with reverent admiration.

Scripture shows us **what we were, what we are, and what we shall become**.

It reveals God's mercy and justice, heaven's joy and hell's suffering.

In a short time, even people without advanced education can learn from it truths that philosophers searched for over many centuries.

Believers tired by the cares of life and the struggle against sin long for the comfort God's word supplies.

Everyone may pray: **Look upon me and show me mercy, as you do to those who love your name**.

Ask the Holy Spirit to direct every step.

We should fear sin's domination and earnestly pray against it.

Human oppression may become more than we can endure in our own strength.

The God who knows our frame can remove that pressure in answer to prayer.

Henry argues that the ancient Israelites' confidence in approaching God's throne indicates they understood more of **gospel blessings through sacrifices and worship** than people often suppose.

We should approach the same gracious throne through **the name and merit of Jesus**.

Those whose hearts have been transformed by grace often express that grace in tears.

Lord, receive the tears our blessed Redeemer shed for us during his earthly life, and teach us also to grieve over our own sins and the suffering of others.

## God's righteousness and Christ's obedience (verses 137–144)

God has never done and never can do **anything unjust**.

He fulfills every promise faithfully.

Zeal against sin should make us act against it, beginning with greater faithfulness in our own lives.

Love for Scripture demonstrates love for God because it aims to make us participants in **his holiness**.

Genuine excellence makes people humble in their own eyes.

When we are overlooked or despised, we especially need to remember God's commands for encouragement.

His law is **truth, the standard of holiness and the rule of happiness**.

Yet **only Christ's obedience justifies the believer**.

Even saints experience sorrow and many trials.

Sometimes they taste the sweetness of God's word most deeply when suffering and distress surround them.

**Eternal life is to know the only true God and Jesus Christ whom he has sent** (**John 17:3**).

May we live now by faith and grace, then enter the life of glory.

## Seeking the Lord morning and night (verses 145–152)

Wholehearted prayer belongs to people who truly desire God's salvation and love his commandments.

Where should a child go but to the Father?

Lord, save me from my sins, corruptions, temptations and everything that hinders obedience.

Christians who have good health should not waste the early hours of morning.

Hope in God's promises encourages us to keep praying.

**It is better to give up some sleep than to find no time for prayer**.

We may approach God at every hour.

When our first thoughts in the morning turn to him, we are better prepared to live in his fear throughout the day.

Ask him to give life, joy and cheerfulness.

He knows our needs and what is good for us.

When we are engaged in his service, we need not fear those who place themselves as far as possible from the commands of his word.

**When trouble comes near, God is also near**.

All his commandments are true, and all his promises will be fulfilled.

Everyone who has trusted him has found him faithful.

## The living word and the inexhaustible mercy of God (verses 153–160)

The more closely we hold God's word as both **our rule and our support**, the stronger our confidence in deliverance.

**Christ is the Redeemer and Advocate of his people**.

Those whom the Spirit once made alive when they were dead in sins still need fresh grace to revive them according to God's promises.

The wicked do not obey God's statutes; **they do not even seek to know them**.

They flatter themselves that they are traveling toward heaven.

But as long as they continue in sin, they move further from it.

God's tender mercies are **a fountain that never runs dry**.

The psalmist begged for renewed life-giving grace.

Someone steadily performing duty need not fear numerous enemies.

Those who truly hate sin hate it **because it violates God's law** and breaks his word, not merely because it is inconvenient.

Obedience is pleasing to God and joyful to us only when it springs from **love**.

In every age, everyone who receives Scripture with faith and love discovers that every word of it is faithful.

## Great peace through love for God's law (verses 161–168)

Those who stand in awe of God's word will endure human anger rather than break his commandments.

Through Scripture we receive immeasurable benefit.

Everyone dislikes being lied to.

But we should hate **telling lies** even more, because that insults God directly.

The more we love truth's beauty, the more we recognize the ugliness of falsehood.

We should praise God even for trials, because his grace can use them for our good.

People who love this world experience great unrest because it disappoints them.

Those who love God's word find **great peace**, because it exceeds their expectations.

Genuine love for the Lord keeps us from unnecessary doubts and from constantly taking offense at other believers.

A sound hope of salvation motivates us to obey.

Our love for Scripture must also weaken sinful desires and uproot fleshly attachments.

**It must engage the heart, or it accomplishes nothing**.

We keep God's commands through **obedience**, and keep his promises through **reliance upon them**.

God sees us continually.

That should make us careful in all our conduct.

## Seek me and give me life (verses 169–176)

The psalmist asked for the grace and strength needed to pray, and for God to receive his petitions.

He wanted to know **more of God in Christ**, to understand Scripture's teachings and carry out his duties.

He knew his unworthiness and approached with reverent concern.

He appealed to what God had promised, not to his own merit.

We have not truly learned from God unless we have learned **to praise him**.

His word must govern our speech.

We must avoid both **sinful speaking** and **sinful silence**.

Our hands are insufficient, and no creature can provide all the help we need.

We look to the One who made us for support.

The psalmist had deliberately chosen to follow God.

Every saint longs for **eternal salvation** and therefore prays for help along the way.

May all God's ordinances and providential dealings advance his glory in our lives.

The believer looks back with both shame and thankfulness on being lost.

Still needing tender care, we pray to **the Shepherd who purchased his flock with his own blood** and grants eternal life.

**Seek me** means **find me**.

God never seeks in vain.

Turn me, Lord, and I shall be turned.

Let this psalm examine our hearts and lives.

Do our hearts, washed in **Christ's blood**, truly make its prayers, promises and confessions our own?

Is the Lord's word **the standard of what we believe and how we live**?

Do we plead its promises before Christ for what we need?

Happy are those who find delight in living this way.
