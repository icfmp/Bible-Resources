# Revelation 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Epistles to the churches in Asia, with warnings and encouragements, To the church at Ephesus; | 1–7 |
| at Smyrna; | 8–11 |
| at Pergamos; | 12–17 |
| and at Thyatira. | 18–29 |

## Ephesus: return to your first love (verses 1–7)

The churches differed greatly in purity of teaching and genuine godliness.

Christ's messages to them therefore continue speaking to churches and believers in every age.

Although Jesus reigns in heaven, he walks **among the churches on earth**, observing their strengths, weaknesses and needs.

He commended Ephesus for diligent service.

**Christ remembers every hour of work his servants perform**; no faithful labor is wasted.

But diligence alone is not sufficient. We also need **patience in suffering and patience in waiting**.

Although Christians owe gentleness to people, they must not be indifferent to sin.

The Ephesian church had not ceased loving Christ altogether. It had **lost the intensity of its first love**.

Christ is displeased when believers become cold and careless toward him.

Calling this decline harmless or normal excuses a sin Christ requires us to repent of.

The church should grieve over this spiritual cooling, confess it humbly and recover its former zeal, prayerfulness and watchfulness.

Indifference toward truth and error, good and evil, may be mislabeled charity, but it is not Christian charity.

The Christian life remains a struggle against **sin, Satan, the world and the flesh**.

Persevering believers receive the promised **Tree of Life**, not merely a return to Eden but perfect, everlasting joy in heaven.

During earthly life, anticipate that joy through faith, fellowship with Christ and the Spirit's comforts.

Do not expect uninterrupted peace or freedom from spiritual conflict **before heaven**.

## Smyrna: remain faithful through suffering (verses 8–11)

Jesus is **the First**, because he created everything and existed before all things, with God and himself God.

He is **the Last**, because he will judge everyone.

The One who died and lives again is the suffering believer's **Brother and Friend**.

Consequently even people in severe poverty may be spiritually rich, honored amid humiliation and happy under trials.

Some wealthy people are spiritually destitute. Some outwardly poor Christians are rich in **faith, good works, privileges, gifts and hope**.

When people lose earthly possessions for Christ and a clear conscience, he makes up for the loss through spiritual wealth.

Jesus arms his people against approaching trials: **Do not fear**.

He not only commands courage but supplies strength to practice it.

The trials were permitted to **test, not destroy**, his faithful people.

His promised **crown of life** is certain and fitting: a life exhausted or surrendered for Christ is rewarded with a far greater, eternal life.

The **second death** is immeasurably more terrible than bodily death because it is everlasting. Henry describes its terror as **dying and always dying**.

Those spared that judgment through Christ can patiently bear any suffering encountered in this world.

## Pergamum: reject false liberty and hold fast (verses 12–17)

God's word is a **two-edged sword**, able to strike at both sin and sinners.

Believers need not fear the sword of Christ's judgment if they remain obedient.

Jesus knows both **the opportunities and the temptations** arising from the places where his people live.

Pergamum remained faithful through difficult trials, refusing both open apostasy and accommodation merely to avoid suffering.

Christ commended its faithfulness but rebuked its failure to confront false teaching.

A distorted understanding of Christian liberty can become **a bitter root from which serious sin grows**.

Repentance is required not only of individuals but also of **congregations and communities that sin together**.

The conqueror receives Christ's hidden spiritual nourishment and consolations, unknown to the world.

The **new name** signifies adoption into God's family.

The Holy Spirit makes believers understand the reality of this new identity through the work he has performed in their hearts.

## Thyatira: love must not tolerate corrupt teaching (verses 18–29)

Jesus notices good works born of **love, faith, zeal and patience**.

Yet his flaming eyes also see any wrongdoing the church permits.

He rebukes, disciplines or judges such evil.

He praised Thyatira's ministers and members for growth in goodness and wisdom.

All Christians should desire **their latest works to be their best works**.

But the church tolerated people who seduced others into wickedness.

By judging deceivers, Christ shows that he knows **hearts, motives, plans and the hidden spirit** of every person.

Those remaining undefiled receive encouragement.

It is dangerous to disregard God's mysteries; it is also dangerous to become fascinated by **Satan's supposed secret depths**.

The less experience believers have of his deceits, the better.

Christ cares tenderly for his faithful servants and imposes no burden that is not for their good.

He promises rich reward to those who persevere, including fitting wisdom and authority.

Christ brings the dawn of **grace and glory** into the soul and gives himself for its enjoyment.

After every spiritual victory, continue resisting the enemy until the end.
