# Genesis 18 — Modern English

## Abraham welcomes the visitors (verses 1–8)

Abraham was ready to welcome any tired traveler who came his way.

There were no inns like those we have today.

As Abraham sat there, he saw three men approaching.

They were three heavenly beings appearing in human bodies.

Some believe all three were created angels. Others believe one of them was the Son of God, the Angel of the covenant.

Washing guests' feet was a common act of hospitality in hot countries where people wore sandals.

We should not neglect to welcome strangers. In doing so, some have welcomed angels without knowing it (Hebrews 13:2).

More than that, we welcome the Lord of angels himself whenever we show kindness for his sake to the least of his brothers and sisters.

Cheerful and thoughtful kindness is one of the finest ornaments of a godly life.

Our gracious and humble Lord no longer visits us personally in the way he visited Abraham.

Yet through his Spirit he still stands at the door and knocks.

When we are willing to open, he graciously enters and provides a rich feast of comfort, which we share with him (Revelation 3:20).

## Sarah's unbelief is corrected (verses 9–15)

The visitors asked where Sarah was.

Abraham replied, “In the tent.”

She was close by, in the right place, attending to her household duties.

There is nothing to gain from wandering around aimlessly.

Those who are faithfully carrying out their duties are most likely to receive comfort from God and his promises (Luke 2:8).

We are often slow to believe, and we need to hear the same truth repeatedly.

Other people may receive blessings through God's ordinary providence.

Believers receive those same blessings through his promises, which make them sweeter and more certain.

Abraham's spiritual descendants owe their life, joy, hope, and everything else to God's promise.

Sarah thought the news of a son too wonderful to be true.

She laughed because she could not yet bring herself to believe it.

At first we might not see any difference between Sarah's laughter and Abraham's laughter in Genesis 17:17.

But God, who searches the heart, saw that her laughter arose from unbelief, while his arose from faith.

Sarah then denied that she had laughed.

One sin often leads to another. When we question God's truth, we are less likely to speak the truth ourselves.

Yet the Lord rebukes, convicts, silences, and brings to repentance those whom he loves when they sin before him.

## God tells Abraham about Sodom (verses 16–22)

Two of the visitors, understood to be created angels, went toward Sodom.

The third, called *Jehovah* throughout this account, remained with Abraham.

He chose not to hide from Abraham what he intended to do.

God sometimes shows great patience toward sinners.

They may wrongly conclude that he neither notices nor cares about what they do.

But when the day of his judgment comes, he will look toward them.

God told Abraham what he intended to do, giving him an opportunity to intercede and showing him the justice of his actions.

Abraham's character shines especially in the care he took over his family.

He not only prayed with them but taught and governed them conscientiously.

Those who expect the blessings of a godly family must also fulfill the duties of a godly family.

Abraham did not fill his household's minds with doubtful controversies.

He taught them to worship God sincerely and seriously, and to deal honestly with everyone.

How few households could receive such praise today!

How little care is often taken to teach those under our responsibility the foundations of true religion.

Do we watch, week after week, to see whether they are making progress or falling behind?

## Abraham intercedes for Sodom (verses 23–33)

Here we find the first solemn prayer recorded in Scripture.

Abraham prayed that Sodom might be spared if only a few righteous people could be found there.

We should learn from him how deeply to care for sinners and how earnestly to pray for them.

The fervent prayer of a righteous person can accomplish much.

Abraham did not obtain what he requested for the whole city.

Yet Lot was delivered in a miraculous way.

Let this encourage us to pray earnestly for God's blessing upon our families, friends, and neighborhoods.

But we must do more than pray. We must also live as Abraham did.

Abraham knew that the Judge of all the earth would do what was right.

He did not argue that the wicked deserved to be spared on their own account.

Nor did he suggest that destroying them would be unjust or excessively severe.

He pleaded for the sake of the righteous people who might still be among them.

Only righteousness can serve as a plea before God.

How, then, did Christ intercede for transgressors?

Not by criticizing God's law or offering excuses that minimize human guilt.

He pleaded **his own obedience, even to death**.
