# Psalm 39 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David meditates on man's frailty. | 1–6 |
| He applies for pardon and deliverance. | 7–13 |

## Guarding speech and remembering mortality (verses 1–6)

When an evil thought arises, we should suppress it.

**Habitual watchfulness is the bridle on the horse's head; watchfulness in particular moments is the hand holding the bridle**.

When we cannot avoid the company of wicked people, we should remember that they watch our words and may twist them to injure us.

Sometimes silence is necessary, even when what we could say is good. But generally we do wrong if we continually avoid opportunities for speech that would help others.

Impatience begins within us, often through brooding thoughts. Its outward consequence is a burning agitation of the soul.

Even in excellent health and prosperity, every person is fragile and passing away. No one can live long by earthly standards, and death may arrive very soon.

This is certain, yet we are reluctant to accept it.

Let us ask God to enlighten our minds by his Holy Spirit and fill our hearts with grace, so that we are prepared to die **at any day or hour**.

## Pardon, deliverance and hope (verses 7–13)

Created things cannot provide lasting satisfaction.

We find it only in the Lord and fellowship with him; disappointments should bring us back to that truth.

If the world is empty and passing, may God prevent us from seeking our ultimate portion in it.

When confidence in created things fails, we still have **God to whom we can turn and in whom we can trust**.

Recognizing that a good God orders our affairs helps us accept his discipline without accusing him.

David sought forgiveness and asked to be protected from shame.

We must **watch and pray** against sin. When God corrects us, we should seek relief from him, not put our confidence elsewhere.

Our own conduct often brings trouble, as though we are struck by a rod **we made ourselves**.

Human beauty is a poor foundation for pride. It will certainly fade, perhaps very quickly.

The body is like a garment worn by the soul. Sin has introduced a **moth** into that garment, wearing away its beauty, then its strength, and finally its very substance.

Watching a slow disease or the progress of age makes this image painfully clear.

Suffering can stir us to prayer. If it does, we have reason to hope God will hear us.

Believers may expect weariness and poor treatment while traveling toward heaven, but their journey here is short.

Walking by faith, they can continue without abandoning their course or collapsing under hardships.

How blessed it is to hold earthly things loosely as we go home to our Father's house, using the world without misusing it.

Let us look toward **the city whose Builder and Maker is God**.
