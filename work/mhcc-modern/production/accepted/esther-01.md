# Esther 1 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

| Vashti's refusal to appear, The king's decree. | 10–22 |

## Ahasuerus holds a royal feast (verses 1–9)

Pride rose in Ahasuerus's heart as his kingdom became magnificent, and he gave an extravagant feast.

This was an exhibition of **empty self-glory**.

A simple meal of vegetables enjoyed in peace is better than an enormous banquet of wine accompanied by noise and disorder.

Unless grace rules a person's heart, **self-exaltation and self-indulgence** will govern life in one form or another.

Yet the king did not require anyone to drink. Those who became drunk had only themselves to blame.

Even this precaution by a pagan ruler should shame many who call themselves Christians.

Some pressure their companions into drinking to excess under the pretense of celebrating one another's health.

In doing so they encourage sin and bring spiritual death alongside it.

God's warning against such people is serious (**Habakkuk 2:15–16**).

Let them read it and tremble.

## Vashti refuses; the king makes a decree (verses 10–22)

Through his own folly Ahasuerus turned his festival into distress.

Special celebrations often end with trouble and annoyance.

People in authority should be careful not to give commands that others may reasonably refuse to obey.

When wine governs a person, sound judgment is lost.

Ahasuerus ruled **127 provinces** but could not govern **his own temper**.

Whether the decree served his anger or his political interests, divine Providence used it to prepare the way for Esther to become queen.

God was already defeating Haman's wicked plot **before Haman even imagined it or rose to power**.

We should rejoice that the Lord reigns.

He can overrule human frenzy and foolishness to accomplish his own glory and secure his people's welfare and happiness.
