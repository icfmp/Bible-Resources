# Isaiah 31 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The sin and folly of seeking help from Egypt. | 1–5 |
| God's care for Jerusalem. | 6–9 |

## The folly of trusting Egypt (verses 1–5)

God opposes the help that his people seek **from those who practice wickedness**.

Sinners can sometimes recognize how foolish their actions are when the truth is stated plainly. Yet they may still refuse to live as though they believed it.

No one can escape God's judgments; evil follows the unrepentant.

The Lord of hosts will come to defend **Mount Zion**.

**The Lion of the tribe of Judah** will fight for his church.

He will also protect Jerusalem with the tenderness of **birds hovering over their young**, sheltering them from harm.

God's protection joins **great strength with profound compassion**.

His people may trust him to preserve them.

## Backsliders called to return (verses 6–9)

Israel had wandered far from God.

Yet they were still described as **his children**.

Let them turn back, and the Lord will heal their backsliding, however deeply sin has brought them into misery.

People often make **idols of silver and gold**.

Love of possessions draws hearts away from God.

Those who truly repent become ready to surrender whatever they have treated as a rival to him.

When the people abandoned their idols, **the Assyrian would fall through an angel's sword**.

That messenger strikes with more power than a mighty warrior, though more quietly and unexpectedly than an ordinary person.

God can make even the bravest human heart tremble.

Meanwhile, if his people keep the fire of **holy love and devotion** burning in their hearts and homes, they may depend upon his protection.
