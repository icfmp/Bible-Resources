# Acts 23 — Modern English

## Paul defends himself before the council (verses 1–5)

Here we see what an honest person is like.

Such a person sets God continually before them and lives as in his sight.

Their conscience governs their words and deeds. According to the best of their knowledge, they avoid evil and cling to what is good.

They act conscientiously in every part of life.

People who live this way before God can, like Paul, have confidence both toward God and toward others.

Paul's answer to the high priest included a deserved rebuke and a warning of judgment.

Nevertheless, Henry judges that **Paul seems to have spoken with too much anger** after being struck.

Even people occupying high office may be told about their failures and may rightly face public complaint.

But these objections must be expressed appropriately, because God's law still requires **respect for those in authority**.

## The council divides; Christ reassures Paul (verses 6–11)

The Pharisees held the traditional Jewish belief in resurrection.

The Sadducees rejected Scripture and divine revelation in this matter. They denied a future state, and therefore had neither hope of everlasting joy nor fear of everlasting suffering.

Paul could truthfully say that he was being tried because of **the hope of the resurrection of the dead**.

It was legitimate for him to state his position on this disputed question and thereby move Pharisees to protect him from unlawful violence.

How easily God can defend his cause!

The Jewish leaders appeared united in opposition to Christianity, but their motives differed.

**The wicked have no secure friendship with one another**.

God can turn their apparent agreement into open disagreement in an instant.

The Roman commander rescued Paul from violent people, but could not tell him what the future held.

God's **own consolation** was much more valuable.

Whoever opposes us, we need not fear if the Lord stands beside us.

Christ desires his faithful servants to remain joyful and courageous.

Paul may have thought that he would never reach Rome.

But God assured him that he would testify there too, because Paul's desire to go to Rome arose from a wish to honor Christ and do good.

## A conspiracy is exposed; Paul is sent to Caesarea (verses 12–24)

False religious beliefs, when adopted by people governed by sinful passions, can provoke astonishing cruelty.

Yet God easily defeats the most carefully designed schemes of wickedness.

Paul understood that **divine Providence ordinarily works through reasonable and prudent means**.

Had he neglected the means available to protect himself, he could not have rightly expected God to preserve him by extraordinary intervention.

A person unwilling to use available abilities and opportunities has **neither reason nor revelation** guaranteeing God's assistance.

By trusting the Lord, we may expect him to preserve us from every evil work and bring us safely into his heavenly kingdom.

Heavenly Father, through your Holy Spirit and for Christ's sake, grant us this precious faith.

## Lysias writes to Felix (verses 25–35)

God has instruments for every task he intends to accomplish.

The natural abilities and moral virtues of **unbelieving people** have often been used to protect his persecuted servants.

Even people who care little about Christian doctrine may recognize the difference between the honest conduct of upright believers and the zeal of false religious professors.

All human hearts are in God's hand.

Blessed are those who trust him and commit their way to him.
