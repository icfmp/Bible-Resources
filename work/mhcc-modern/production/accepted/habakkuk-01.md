# Habakkuk 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

## Widespread wrongdoing and the coming Chaldeans (verses 1–11)

The Lord's servants are deeply distressed when they see irreverence and violence prevail, especially among people who profess to know the truth.

People had become willing to injure their neighbors without hesitation.

We should long for the world in which holiness and love reign forever and violence will never again be seen.

God has wise reasons both for showing patience toward wicked people and for allowing his faithful servants to express their concern.

The day will come when the cry raised by wrongdoing will testify against those who committed it, while the prayers of people who suffered wrong will be heard on their behalf.

God's people were to watch what was happening among the surrounding nations, especially the rise of **the Chaldeans**.

They were to understand that their own land would be punished through that nation.

Yet many people assume their prosperity must continue indefinitely.

Others expect disaster to happen only after their own lifetime.

The Chaldeans were a bitter, swift, violent and merciless people.

Their forces would overwhelm those who opposed them.

But they would also commit a serious sin common among the proud: **claiming all the glory of success for themselves**.

The conclusion of the prophecy nevertheless contained a glimpse of comfort for God's people.

## God's holiness even when a worse nation prospers (verses 12–17)

Whatever happens around us, the Lord remains **our God and our Holy One**.

We have offended him, but we must not therefore entertain unjust or harsh thoughts about him or his service.

It is deeply comforting that human beings may intend evil while **the Lord intends good**.

His counsel will stand.

Even if wicked people prosper for a time, God remains perfectly holy and does not approve of their evil.

He cannot commit injustice, nor can he look on wickedness with approval.

We must hold to this principle even when temporary events seem difficult to reconcile with it.

Habakkuk complained that people were abusing God's patience.

Because punishment did not follow their crimes immediately, their hearts grew bolder in doing wrong.

The conquerors caught some victims **one by one, like fish taken on a hook**; they gathered others **in nets and drag-nets**, capturing whole groups together.

They admired their skill and strategy.

We are all inclined to take credit for our success and good fortune.

When people worship the clever methods that made them successful, they are **sacrificing to their own nets**—making idols of themselves.

God will bring their impressive robberies to an end.

Death and judgment will stop those who prey upon others; in turn, they themselves will face judgment.

Whatever advantages we possess, let us give **all glory to God**.
