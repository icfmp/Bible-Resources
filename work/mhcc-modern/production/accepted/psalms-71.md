# Psalm 71 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| Prayers that God would deliver and save. | 1–13 |
| Believing praises. | 14–24 |

## A prayer for protection throughout life (verses 1–13)

David asked God never to let him be ashamed for trusting him.

Every true believer can come boldly to **the throne of grace** with the same plea.

Remember the gracious care of divine Providence from our birth and throughout infancy.

The One who has been our **Help from birth** should also be **our Hope from youth**.

We should not expect lasting ease or comfort from the world.

Those who love the Lord are often hated, persecuted or regarded as strange because of their convictions and conduct.

Yet God has always been their strong refuge.

His faithful servants may trust that he will **not reject them in old age or forsake them when their strength declines**.

## Praising Christ's righteousness through old age (verses 14–24)

David resolved to speak about **Christ's righteousness** and the great salvation obtained through it.

This would occupy his speech not only on Sabbaths but on every day of the week, every year and throughout his life.

He would proclaim it not merely during appointed seasons of worship but **on every occasion, all day long**.

Why return so often to this subject? Because **its riches cannot be counted**.

Christ's righteousness is beyond expression, and the salvation it provides is everlasting.

God does not abandon his grey-haired servants when they can no longer work as they once did.

Indeed, he often strengthens their inner lives while their bodily strength declines.

Older disciples of Christ owe a testimony to those who come after them.

They should speak earnestly about the benefits of true religion, the certainty of God's promises and, especially, **the Redeemer's everlasting righteousness**.

With confidence in God's final deliverance, let us spend whatever days remain praising the Holy One of Israel with all our abilities.

While we tell of his righteousness and sing his praise, faith can lift us above fear and frailty and grant us **foretastes of heaven's joy**.

Above all the Lord's works, **redemption** deserves our praise.

The Lamb who was slain and redeemed us to God is worthy of every blessing and all thanksgiving.
