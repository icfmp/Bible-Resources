# Psalm 149 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Joy to all the people of God. | 1–5 |
| Terror to their enemies. | 6–9 |

## A new song for the new creation (verses 1–5)

New mercies call for **new songs of praise**, on earth and in heaven.

The children of Zion should thank God for creating them, but also for **making them new creations in Christ Jesus for good works**. They were not merely made human; he formed them as saints.

The Lord takes pleasure in his people; they should delight in him.

When sinners feel their need and unworthiness, God clothes them with **the graces of his Spirit** and restores his image within them. Their joy will continue forever.

Let believers sing even during wakeful nights. On **their deathbeds**, they can rejoice in the hope of eternal rest and glory.

## Christ's victory is not spread by human violence (verses 6–9)

Certain servants of God in ancient times were **specifically appointed to carry out his judgments**, not from private revenge or political ambition, but in obedience to a particular command.

But the victory promised to all God's saints concerns the **enemies of their salvation**.

**Christ did not intend his gospel to be spread by fire and sword, nor his righteousness by human wrath.**

Instead, let his praises be in our mouths and **the sword of the word of God** in our hands, with **the shield of faith** as we struggle against **the world, the flesh and the devil**.

Through **the blood of the Lamb and his testimony**, believers will become more than conquerors. Final judgment will be carried out **on the great day**.

Henry also asks us to picture Christ and his gospel church, especially in what he calls **her millennial condition**.

Christ and his people rejoice in one another. Through their prayers and efforts, they work with him as he advances in the **chariots of salvation**, conquering sinners by grace, or in **chariots of vengeance**, bringing destruction upon his enemies.

## Editorial review — Psalm 149

**2026-11-18 targeted CCEL re-collation (secondary witness only):** Restored Henry's contrast between *chariots of salvation* and *chariots of vengeance* and his explicit contrast between conquering sinners by grace and destroying enemies in judgment. The earlier draft softened this into an undifferentiated reference to judgment. These are **Henry's eschatological images**, not any mandate for believers to spread the gospel through coercion; the same authorial section expressly rejects fire and sword as gospel means.
