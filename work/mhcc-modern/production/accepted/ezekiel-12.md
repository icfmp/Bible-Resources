# Ezekiel 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The approaching captivity. | 1–16 |
| An emblem of the consternation of the Jews. | 17–20 |
| Answers to the objections of scoffers. | 21–28 |

## Ezekiel pictures the coming exile (verses 1–16)

Ezekiel prepared belongings for departure and broke through the wall of his house at evening, as someone trying to escape an enemy might do.

These actions pictured **Zedekiah's behavior and fate**.

When God delivers us from trouble, we must **give him glory** and help others learn from what happened, including by **acknowledging our sins**.

People brought through suffering to recognize the Lord may themselves become a means through which **others come to know him**.

## The prophet eats and drinks in fear (verses 17–20)

Ezekiel was to eat and drink **with fear, anxiety and trembling**.

This pictured what the inhabitants of Jerusalem would experience during the siege.

Ministers announcing judgment upon sinners should speak as people who **know the terror of the Lord**.

Although afflictions may be deeply painful to our bodies and emotions, they can become blessed if they **increase our knowledge of God**.

## Scoffers cannot postpone God's word (verses 21–28)

The Jews took God's patience—which should have led them to repent—as a reason to become **more stubborn in sin**.

Calling something **a common saying** does not excuse using it to speak wrongly.

Only a short step separates us from **eternity and judgment**.

We should therefore prepare for the life to come.

No one can avert the day of judgment by dismissing its warnings or imagining it is far off.

The only safe response is to **seek peace with the Lord**.
