# Job 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job speaks of man's life. | 1–6 |
| Of man's death. | 7–15 |
| By sin man is subject to corruption. | 16–22 |

## Job reflects on human life (verses 1–6)

Job continued reflecting on the human condition while also addressing God.

Every descendant of fallen Adam has a short life.

Human beauty, happiness and splendor vanish before disease or death, like a flower cut down or a shadow fading away.

How can a person's conduct be entirely free from sin when **the heart is unclean by nature**?

Henry regards this as clear evidence that Job understood **original sin**.

Job seems to have raised this truth to ask God to treat him **not according to his own works, but according to mercy and grace**.

God has determined how long each of us will live.

Our times are in his hands, and natural processes operate under his rule.

In him we live and move.

Remembering how brief and uncertain life is, and how quickly earthly pleasures fade, can help us greatly.

But even more important is to understand the **cause and the remedy** of our condition.

Until the Holy Spirit gives us new birth, nothing spiritually good dwells in us or comes from us.

Even the little good performed by regenerated people remains mixed with sin.

We should therefore humble ourselves and depend entirely upon God's mercy through **our divine Surety, Christ**.

Seek the Holy Spirit's daily renewal and look toward heaven as the only place of perfect holiness and happiness.

## Job considers death and resurrection (verses 7–15)

A tree that has been cut down may sprout again if water is available.

A human being who dies, however, is permanently removed from the former place in **this world**.

Human life resembles floodwaters that spread widely and then quickly dry up.

Henry understands Job's words as expressing belief in **the resurrection**.

Job's friends had proved such miserable comforters that he turned toward the hope of a future change.

If our sins have been forgiven and our hearts made holy, heaven will be our souls' rest.

Meanwhile our bodies will lie hidden in the grave, no longer harmed by enemies or troubled by the corruption and discipline of earthly life.

## Job struggles with sin, suffering and mortality (verses 16–22)

At moments Job's faith and hope became stronger and the work of grace appeared renewed.

But soon his remaining sinful nature prevailed again.

He spoke as though God had pushed his judgment to the furthest extreme against him.

The Lord will prevail against everyone who tries to contend with him.

God may permit illness and pain, remove the companionship of those we love and take away every earthly hope.

Yet he will receive the believer into eternal happiness.

What a different end awaits the prosperous unbeliever when summoned before God's judgment seat!

The Lord still sits upon a **throne of mercy**, ready to show grace.

May sinners consider their final end while there is still time.

The body that human beings hesitate to lay aside may suffer intensely near death.

The soul they hesitate to surrender may experience sorrow.

**Dying can be difficult work and death's pain severe**.

It is foolish to postpone repentance until a deathbed, when weakness may make the one most necessary task difficult to perform.
