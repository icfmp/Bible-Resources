# Deuteronomy 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The conquest of Og king of Bashan. | 1–11 |
| The land of Gilead and Bashan. | 12–20 |
| Moses encourages Joshua. | 21–29 |

## Og, king of Bashan, is defeated (verses 1–11)

Og was extremely powerful.

Yet he learned nothing from the destruction of Sihon.

He could have sought peace, but he continued trusting in his own strength.

His stubbornness led toward his destruction.

Those whom God's judgments against other sinners fail to awaken become more prepared to receive the same judgments themselves.

## The tribes settle east of the Jordan (verses 12–20)

The lands of Gilead and Bashan were assigned to Reuben, Gad and half the tribe of Manasseh (Numbers 32).

Moses repeated the condition these tribes had accepted: they must help their brothers complete the conquest before settling down themselves.

When we have received peace, we should desire peace for others too.

We ought to do what we can to help other people obtain it.

We were not born only for ourselves.

We are members of one another.

## Moses encourages Joshua and accepts God's answer (verses 21–29)

Moses strengthened Joshua, who would succeed him.

Older and more experienced believers should do everything they can to encourage younger people beginning to serve God.

Remember what God has done and what he has promised.

If God is for us, who can overcome us?

We dishonor our Leader when we follow him trembling with fear.

Moses prayed that, if it were the Lord's will, he might cross the Jordan and enter Canaan with Israel.

We should not cherish any desire that we cannot honestly present to God in prayer.

The answer Moses received combined mercy and judgment.

God sometimes chooses to deny us what we want.

A prayer can be accepted even though the exact request is not granted.

If God's providence does not give us our desire, but his grace makes us content without it, we have still received a great blessing.

Let it be enough to have God as our Father and heaven as our inheritance, even if we cannot possess everything we would like in this world.

God promised Moses a view of Canaan from the summit of Pisgah.

He would not possess it, but he would see it.

Even great believers now see heaven from a distance.

God also provided a successor for Moses.

Friends of Christ's church can be encouraged that the Lord's work will continue through others after they themselves have died.

If we have the pledge of heaven and can already see its prospect, let us be content with that.

We should submit to the Lord's will and stop insisting on what he has wisely refused.
