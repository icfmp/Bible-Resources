# Job 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job reproves his friends. | 1–12 |
| He professes his confidence in God. | 13–22 |
| Job entreats to know his sins. | 23–28 |

## Job challenges his friends' claims (verses 1–12)

Job spoke too highly of himself when he insisted that his friends had nothing to teach him.

Those who argue are tempted to magnify themselves and belittle others.

Whenever fear of judgment, spiritual temptation or suffering overwhelms us, we should turn to **the Physician of our souls**.

He rejects no one, never prescribes the wrong remedy and never leaves a case without a cure.

We may speak to him whenever we need.

For broken hearts and wounded consciences, every created comfort without Christ is an ineffective physician.

Job spoke to his friends in unmistakable anger.

His friends had expressed some truths that were relevant to Job, but an unhumble heart does not receive human correction gently.

## Job expresses confidence in God (verses 13–22)

Job resolved to stand by his conscience's witness to his integrity.

He relied on God for **justification and salvation**, the two great blessings we hope for through Christ.

Job expected little in the way of earthly deliverance.

But he remained confident concerning his eternal salvation.

He believed God would not only save him and make him happy but also be **his salvation**, the One whose presence and enjoyment would be his happiness.

He knew he was not a hypocrite and concluded that God would not finally reject him.

We should be satisfied to have God as our Friend, even at times when his dealings seem those of an enemy.

We must trust that all things will work for our good, even when everything appears to be against us.

Cling to God even when he gives no immediate sense of comfort.

In the hour of death, seek living comfort from him.

This is what it means to **trust him though he slays us**.

## Job asks God to reveal his sins (verses 23–28)

Job wanted his sins to be made known to him.

A genuinely repentant person is willing to know the worst about personal wrongdoing.

We should all desire to see our transgressions clearly so that we can confess them and avoid them in the future.

Job lamented how severely God seemed to deal with him.

Time does not erase the guilt of sin.

When God brings bitter things to our remembrance, he may intend us to recall neglected sins and turn from them.

Young people should beware of indulging sin.

Even in this life, **moments of sinful pleasure** can produce **months of sorrow**.

It is far wiser to remember the Creator in youth and gain an assured hope and a peaceful conscience that will comfort us in later years.

Job also complained that his present missteps seemed to be examined with excessive strictness.

But God does **not** treat us according to what our sins deserve.

Job's complaint arose from a gloomy view of his circumstances.

If God examined every part of our lives strictly for judgment, both body and soul would face his righteous sentence.

That will be the dreadful condition of unbelievers.

But **salvation has been planned, provided and revealed in Christ**.
