# Job 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job complains of his hardships. | 1–7 |
| He pleads with God as his Maker. | 8–13 |
| He complains of God's severity. | 14–22 |

## Job complains of suffering (verses 1–7)

Job had grown weary of life and resolved to express his pain.

Yet he did not intend to accuse God of injustice.

He asked to be delivered from **the sting of his afflictions—sin**.

When God afflicts us, he may be contending with us.

There is a reason for his dealings, and it is useful to learn that reason so we can repent and leave behind whatever wrong he confronts.

But when we speak from bitterness, as Job did, we can add guilt and further distress to our situation.

We should not cherish harsh thoughts of God.

Later we will understand that they had no foundation.

Job knew that God does not investigate or judge things as humans do.

He therefore struggled to understand why God continued afflicting him, as though time were needed to discover his sin.

## Job appeals to the God who made him (verses 8–13)

Job spoke as though God had formed and preserved him only to make him suffer.

God made us; we did not make ourselves.

How sad it is when bodies capable of becoming **temples of the Holy Spirit** are instead used as instruments of unrighteousness!

The soul, however, is the center of life and personhood, and it too is God's gift.

Remembering our Creator should move us to duty: **God made me and keeps me alive**.

The same fact gives us grounds to ask for mercy:

**You have made me. Remake me. I am yours; save me.**

## Job complains of God's severity (verses 14–22)

Job did not deny that, as a sinner, he deserved suffering.

But he felt that justice was being applied to him with exceptional harshness.

His gloom, unbelief and bitter thoughts about God came not only from outward trials and the sin still remaining within him.

They also arose from **Satan's inward temptations and his anguish over what he felt as divine displeasure**.

Our Creator has also become **our Redeemer in Christ**.

He will not destroy his own work in a humble believer.

He will renew that person in holiness and grant eternal life.

If earthly suffering can make the grave seem attractive, how dreadful is the state of those condemned to everlasting darkness!

May every sinner seek rescue from such a fate.

May every believer thank Jesus, who delivers us from the wrath to come.
