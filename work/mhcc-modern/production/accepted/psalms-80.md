# Psalm 80 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist complains of the miseries of the church. | 1–7 |
| Its former prosperity and present desolation. | 8–16 |
| A prayer for mercy. | 17–19 |

## A cry to the Shepherd of Israel (verses 1–7)

The One who dwells upon the mercy seat is **the good Shepherd of his people**.

Yet we cannot enjoy the consolation of his love or expect the protection of his mighty arm unless we receive **his converting grace**.

When the Lord seems displeased even with his people's prayers, several explanations may apply.

Their aims in praying may be wrong.

They may be secretly indulging a cherished sin.

Or he may be testing their **patience and perseverance in prayer**.

When God is displeased, his people may weep while their enemies rejoice.

Every salvation comes from **his favor**; no true conversion takes place apart from **his grace**.

## The vine planted and cared for by God (verses 8–16)

The church is pictured as **a vine and a vineyard**.

**Christ is the root; believers are the branches**.

A vine is weak and needs support, yet it can spread widely and produce abundant fruit.

But if it produces no fruit, hardly any tree is so useless.

Have we not been planted in a carefully tended garden with every opportunity to bear **the fruits of righteousness**?

Yet empty leaves of religious profession and barren branches of doctrinal talk and ceremonial form often far outnumber **genuine godliness**.

The vineyard had been ruined.

There was a righteous reason for the change in God's dealings with his people.

Our condition is good or bad according to whether his favor rests upon us.

When we observe even the most faithful part of the visible church, we should not be surprised if God sends severe correction.

The psalm calls upon God to protect and restore the vine.

**Lord, you formed this vineyard for yourself**, so we may humbly commit its care to you.

## The Man at God's right hand (verses 17–19)

**The Messiah, Protector and Savior of the church**, is the Man at God's right hand, the Arm of the Lord, to whom all authority has been given.

Through him we receive the strength to **persevere to the end**.

The vine cannot finally be destroyed, and no genuinely fruitful branch will perish.

But barren branches will be cut off and thrown into the fire.

We were redeemed so that we might **serve the One who redeemed us**, not return to the sins from which he rescued us.
