# Proverbs 13 — Modern English

## Verse 1 — Respect faithful correction

Children who honor their parents give reason for hope. Little hope remains for those who refuse to listen to people who faithfully correct them.

## Verse 2 — Accountability for speech

By our words we will be **justified or condemned** (**Matthew 12:37**).

## Verse 3 — Guard the tongue

Thinking before we speak, and refusing to utter evil thoughts, protects the soul from much guilt and grief. Many have ruined themselves through an uncontrolled tongue.

## Verse 4 — Desire without diligence

Lazy people desire what diligent people gain but refuse the effort needed to obtain it. Their desires remain unsatisfied. This is especially true in **spiritual life**.

## Verse 5 — Sin makes us repulsive

Where sin rules, a person becomes detestable. A conscience awakened to sin would lead to self-abhorrence and repentance **in dust and ashes**.

## Verse 6 — A sincere desire to do right

The honest determination to do what is right guards a person from disastrous mistakes more effectively than **a thousand elaborate distinctions**.

## Verse 7 — Poverty and wealth can be appearances

Some people are poor but spend and trade as though rich; this is sinful and leads toward shame. Others are wealthy but pretend to be poor, showing ingratitude to God and injustice and lack of charity toward others. Many hypocrites lack grace but refuse to see their poverty. Many timid Christians possess great spiritual riches yet believe themselves poor; their doubt, complaint and sorrow make them unnecessarily miserable.

## Verse 8 — Riches invite dangers

Great wealth can expose its owner to threats and violence from which the poor are often free.

## Verse 9 — Light that endures

A righteous person's light resembles the **sun**: clouds or an eclipse may obscure it, but it continues to shine. **The Spirit is their light and gives full joy**. The wicked person's light is like a lamp lit by human hands, easily extinguished.

## Verse 10 — Pride feeds quarrels

Disputes between people, families, churches and nations begin and continue through pride. Without pride, disagreements would often be prevented or quickly settled.

## Verse 11 — Dishonest wealth wastes away

Wealth obtained through fraud or sin carries a hidden curse and soon disappears.

## Verse 12 — Hope delayed and fulfilled

Long delays in receiving something eagerly expected cause pain; receiving it brings delight. Henry says **spiritual blessings** are chiefly in view here.

## Verse 13 — Reverence for God's word

Those who fear God and honor his word escape destruction and receive the reward of reverent obedience.

## Verse 14 — Wisdom is a fountain

The principles that guide a wise person's life become a fountain of **life and happiness**.

## Verse 15 — Sin is harsh slavery

The sinner's path harms other people and the sinner too. **Serving sin is slavery**, and the road toward hell is strewn with the thorns and thistles of the curse.

## Verse 16 — Do not speak beyond knowledge

It is foolish to speak confidently on subjects we know nothing about or to undertake work we are wholly unqualified to perform.

## Verse 17 — Faithful messengers bring healing

Those who are unfaithful to **Christ and other people's souls** bring and suffer harm. Faithful messengers find that sound words heal both themselves and those who hear them.

## Verse 18 — Despising instruction leads down

Those who refuse to be taught will eventually be humbled.

## Verse 19 — The false pleasure of sin

Human beings long intensely for happiness. But people unwilling to abandon their sins should not expect any sweetness that can truly satisfy their souls.

## Verse 20 — Choose companions wisely

Bad company brings multitudes to ruin. Those who make themselves wicked will be destroyed.

## Verse 21 — God's justice overtakes sinners

When God pursues sinners in judgment, he will overtake them. He will also reward the righteous.

## Verse 22 — The best provision for children

A servant of God who does not live in anxiety over becoming wealthy is actually following the best way to provide for children.

## Verse 23 — Industry and discernment

Poor people who work hard may prosper modestly, while those with great resources often become poor through foolish management.

## Verse 24 — Loving discipline

Parents who indulgently allow sinful habits to grow strong act as though they hated their children. Such habits bring sorrow now and may bring misery hereafter.

## Verse 25 — What truly satisfies

Wicked people find that even their bodily appetites continually demand more. The righteous feed their souls on **God's word and appointed ordinances**, finding satisfaction in gospel promises and **Jesus Christ, the Bread of Life**.
