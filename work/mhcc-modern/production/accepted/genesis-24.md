# Genesis 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Abraham takes care over Isaac's marriage. | 1–9 |
| Abraham's servant journeys to Mesopotamia and meets Rebekah. | 10–28 |
| Rebekah and her relatives agree to the marriage. | 29–53 |
| Isaac and Rebekah meet and marry. | 54–67 |

## Abraham's concern for Isaac's marriage (verses 1–9)

The effects of godly example, careful teaching, and worship in a household will usually be seen in the devotion, faithfulness, wisdom, and affection of its servants.

It is a blessing from God either to live in such a household or to have servants of that character. We should value these blessings and receive them with gratitude.

Yet few decisions in life matter more to ourselves, to other people, or to God's church than marriage.

Marriage should therefore be approached with great care and wisdom. Above all, we should seek God's will and pray for his guidance and blessing.

When good parents are neither consulted nor respected, we cannot reasonably expect God's blessing.

Parents, in helping to arrange their children's marriages, should consider carefully what will benefit their souls and help them toward heaven.

Notice Abraham's instructions to his servant. He entrusted the task to someone whose conduct, loyalty, and affection for the household had been proved over many years.

Abraham remembered how wonderfully God's grace had called him away from his homeland.

He therefore trusted God to prosper his efforts without taking his son back to the country God had called him to leave.

What we undertake sincerely for God's glory will eventually bring us comfort.

## The servant meets Rebekah (verses 10–28)

Abraham's servant openly acknowledged God.

We are free to bring our particular concerns before divine providence and pray about their details.

The servant asked for a sign.

He did not mean that he would abandon his task unless God gave him exactly the sign he requested. Rather, he was asking God to provide a good wife for his young master.

That was a good prayer.

He wanted a woman who was modest, hardworking, humble, cheerful, helpful, and hospitable.

Whatever fashions may change, both sound judgment and godliness show the value of those qualities in a wife and mother—a companion for her husband, a manager of the household, and someone entrusted with forming the minds of children.

When the servant went to look for a wife for Isaac, he did not go to a place of sinful pleasure or empty amusement and pray to find someone there.

He went to a well, expecting to meet someone properly engaged in ordinary duties.

He asked God to make the right path plain.

The timing of events, no less than the events themselves, is in God's hands.

But we should not tell God too boldly what he must do. If events do not turn out as we demanded, our faith might be weakened rather than strengthened.

God answered his servant and made the way clear.

Rebekah possessed all the qualities the servant was seeking.

She came to the well, filled her jar, and began to return home.

She did not stand staring at the stranger and his camels. She attended to her work and let nothing interrupt it except an opportunity to do good.

She did not begin an immodest or overly familiar conversation. She answered the stranger respectfully.

When the servant realized God had answered his prayer, he gave Rebekah ornaments customary in eastern countries and asked about her family.

Once he discovered that she belonged to Abraham's relatives, he bowed in worship and blessed God.

His words were addressed to the Lord, but Rebekah could hear them and understand something about who he was and where he had come from.

## Rebekah's family agrees to the marriage (verses 29–53)

Scripture tells us in unusual detail how Isaac's marriage to Rebekah was arranged.

We should recognize God's providence in the ordinary events of life and practice wisdom and other Christian virtues in the midst of them.

Laban went to invite Abraham's servant into the house—but only after seeing the jewelry on his sister's hands.

We learn about Laban's character from his later actions. It is reasonable to think he might not have been so welcoming if he had not hoped for a generous reward.

The servant remained focused on his task.

Though he had finished a long journey and arrived at a comfortable home, he would not eat until he had explained why he had come.

Doing the work entrusted to us, whether by God or by others, should matter more than our food.

Our Savior said that doing his Father's will was his food and drink (John 4:34).

The servant told them what Abraham had instructed him to do and why.

He described what had happened at the well to support his request, clearly showing how God had guided the matter.

Events that appear to result from our choices, our plans, or chance are appointed by God.

That does not discourage us from using appropriate means. Rather, it encourages their wise and faithful use.

Rebekah's family willingly and gladly accepted the proposal.

A matter that comes from the Lord is likely to bring comfort.

The servant thanked God for the success of his mission.

He was humble, and humble people are not ashamed to acknowledge their position in life, whatever it may be.

Even our ordinary earthly concerns become sweeter when godliness is woven into them.

## Isaac and Rebekah meet and marry (verses 54–67)

Abraham's servant preferred his work to his own pleasure and was eager to return home.

A wise and godly person who takes duty seriously has no place for needless delay and idleness.

Children should not marry without their parents' consent.

Likewise, parents should not marry off their children without their own consent.

Rebekah agreed to go and was willing to leave immediately.

Her good character shows that her response was not wrong, even though it did not follow the marriage customs common in Henry's own society.

We may hope she understood something of the faith and godliness of the household she was about to join.

Perhaps that helped make her willing to leave her own people and her father's home.

Her relatives sent her with suitable companions and sincere good wishes.

They blessed Rebekah.

When people we love enter a new stage of life, we should commend them to God's grace and blessing through prayer.

Isaac was engaged in good work when he met Rebekah.

He had gone out in the quiet of the evening, to a solitary place, for meditation and prayer.

Through these holy practices we commune with God and examine our own hearts.

Godly people love times of quiet. It benefits us to be alone often, provided we use the time well.

Indeed, we are never less alone than when we are alone with God.

Notice what a loving son Isaac was.

About three years had passed since his mother died, and only now did he find comfort.

Notice also what a loving husband he became.

Those who are dutiful sons are likely to become loving husbands. People who faithfully honor their first calling in life are likely to do the same in the responsibilities that follow.
