# 2 Samuel 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## David desires to build a house for God (verses 1–3)

Now that David enjoyed peace in his palace, he began considering how best to use his free time and prosperity in God's service.

He planned to build a temple for the ark.

Nathan encouraged him.

In this conversation, however, Nathan was not speaking as a prophet with a divine message.

He was expressing his **private judgment as a godly man**.

We should make every effort to encourage other people's good purposes and help them undertake worthwhile work whenever we have an opportunity.

## The Lord makes a covenant promise to David (verses 4–17)

God promised blessings to David's family and descendants.

These promises concerned Solomon, David's immediate successor, and the continuing royal line of Judah.

But they also reached beyond those kings to **Jesus Christ**, who is often called David or the Son of David.

God gave Christ all authority in heaven and on earth, including power to judge.

Christ would build the gospel temple, a house honoring God's name: **the spiritual temple of true believers**, where God dwells through the Holy Spirit.

The promise that David's house, throne and kingdom would be established forever finds its complete fulfillment **only in Christ and his kingdom**.

David's earthly royal dynasty eventually came to an end.

The statement about a son committing wrongdoing, however, cannot apply to the sinless Messiah himself.

It applies to **his spiritual descendants**, true believers who still possess weaknesses and faults.

They may expect divine correction, but not final rejection.

## David prays and gives thanks (verses 18–29)

David's prayer breathes deep love and devotion toward God.

He thought humbly of his own merits.

Everything we possess must be regarded as a divine gift.

David spoke with great reverence and gratitude about the Lord's favor.

Considering what human beings are, and their fallen condition, it is astonishing that God should deal so graciously with them.

**The promise of Christ contains every blessing.**

If the Lord God belongs to us, what more could we ask or imagine (**Ephesians 3:20**)?

God knows us better than we know ourselves.

Let us therefore be satisfied with his dealings.

What could we add to our prayers that he has not already anticipated through his promises?

David ascribed everything to **God's free grace**—both what the Lord had done for him and what he had made known to him.

All was for his word's sake, which Henry understands as **for Christ's sake, the eternal Word**.

Many people begin praying while their hearts are wandering.

David had **found his heart**: gathered it away from distraction, fixed it upon God and devoted it to prayer.

Words from the tongue alone do not please God.

Prayer must rise from the heart, which is lifted up and poured out before him.

David's confidence rested upon the certainty of divine promise.

He asked God to fulfill what he had said.

Unlike human beings, **God's speaking and God's doing cannot be separated**.

He will act as he promised.

His promises are not addressed to each of us by name as they were to David.

Yet they belong to everyone who believes in Jesus Christ and appeals to them in his name.
