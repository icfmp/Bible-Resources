# Song of Solomon 4 — Modern English

## Christ praises the graces of his church (verses 1–7)

Henry cautions readers against giving a confident spiritual meaning to **every detail of every comparison** in this poem. Many such explanations are uncertain, and fanciful guesses can lead to serious mistakes.

The mountain of myrrh **appears to him** to refer to **Mount Moriah**, where the temple stood, incense was offered and God's people worshiped.

Christ was present there until the shadows of Moses' law gave way to the dawn of the gospel and the rising of **the Sun of Righteousness**.

Though Christ, in his human nature, is absent from his earthly church until the heavenly day breaks, he remains **spiritually present with his people through his appointed ordinances**.

How beautiful believers become when they are justified through Christ's righteousness and adorned with spiritual graces!

Their thoughts, words and actions remain imperfect but are increasingly pure, showing hearts nourished by the gospel.

## Christ's love and protection (verses 8–15)

Christ's call to his church has two aspects.

**First, it is a command** to turn away from the world. Its mountains may appear pleasant, but they conceal **lions and leopards**—dangers for the soul.

**Second, it is a promise**: people will be gathered into the church from many places, and the Lord will finally deliver his people from persecution. For now they may dwell among lions (**Psalm 57:4**).

Christ's heart is with his church, where he has placed his treasure. He delights in its love toward him, shown both in the heart and in action.

The bride's perfume pictures **the gifts and graces of the Spirit**. Love and obedience please Christ more than sacrifices or incense.

Christ has clothed his people with **his righteousness** and given them holy joy and consolation. He walks in his garden, though unseen.

He has placed a protective hedge around it that the powers of darkness cannot break.

Believers' souls are like enclosed gardens containing **a well of living water**, the work of the Holy Spirit (**John 4:14; 7:38**).

The world does not know this saving fountain, and its enemies cannot corrupt it.

The fruits and spices picture believers and the graces growing within them. They are **planted**, not self-generated; precious and lasting beyond the fading flowers of this life.

When grace reaches glory, it will endure forever.

**Christ is the source that makes every garden fruitful.**

## The church asks for the Spirit's renewing work (verse 16)

The church prays for the Holy Spirit to make the garden of the soul fruitful.

Its spices represent the precious graces worked within believers. The Spirit acts like wind: **a north wind bringing conviction and a south wind bringing comfort**.

He awakens holy desires and produces both **the willing and the doing** of what is good.

The church invites Christ to enjoy the garden. Everything growing there already belongs to him.

Let him receive the honor of its fruit; may we enjoy the comfort of knowing that he accepts it.

A believer cannot rightly rejoice in spiritual fruit unless it contributes to **Christ's glory**.

Let us therefore remain separate from the world's sinful patterns, like a garden enclosed, and refuse to conform to them.
