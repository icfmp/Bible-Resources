# Revelation 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

## The sealed scroll and the Lamb (verses 1–7)

John saw a scroll of parchment in the hand of the One seated on the throne, closed with **seven seals**.

It represented God's purposes, soon to be revealed.

The designs and methods of divine Providence concerning the church and the world are **established and fixed**, yet remain hidden from created minds until God reveals them.

The seals would be opened **one after another**, not all at once, as the Lord unfolded his purposes until they were fully accomplished.

No creature could open or even read the scroll.

Only the Lord could do so.

People who have already seen something of God's glory long to see more, and those who know him wish to understand his will.

Yet even good people can be too eager to investigate the hidden mysteries of his government.

When answers are delayed, their eager curiosity may turn into sorrow.

John wept because he could not read God's secret decrees.

How many others should weep, rather, over their **ignorance of Christ's gospel**, on which eternal salvation depends!

We do not need to know in advance every event that may happen to us.

Both **eager speculation about future prosperity** and **advance knowledge of coming calamities** could make us less prepared for present duties, spiritual conflicts and the enjoyment of current blessings.

Nevertheless, through Scripture's promises and prophecies we may rightly seek to know the final outcome for believers and Christ's church.

**The incarnate Son has prevailed**, so we may learn everything we need to know.

Christ stands as **Mediator** between God and his ministers and people.

He is called **the Lion**, yet appears as **a Lamb who has been slain**.

The marks of his suffering show that he intercedes in heaven by virtue of the satisfaction he has made for sin.

The Lamb's **seven horns** signify perfect power to accomplish God's will, and his **seven eyes** signify perfect wisdom to understand that will and execute it effectively.

The Father entrusted his eternal purposes to Christ.

Christ gladly received them because he delights to reveal his Father's will.

He also gives the **Holy Spirit** to make God's truth and will known.

## All creation honors the Redeemer (verses 8–14)

The world has good reason to rejoice that God deals with human beings in grace and mercy **through the Redeemer**.

He rules not merely as our Creator but as our Savior.

The **harps** signify praise, while the **bowls of incense** represent the prayers of the saints.

Prayer and praise should therefore **always go together**.

Christ has redeemed his people from bondage to **sin, guilt and Satan**.

He has not merely bought their freedom.

He has given them extraordinary honor, making them **kings and priests**.

As kings, they are called to govern their own spirits and overcome both the world and the evil one.

As priests, they enjoy access to God and may offer spiritual sacrifices.

The worship offered to the Lamb shows unmistakably that **Jesus Christ is worthy of the same worship as the Father**, from all creatures and forever.

Blessed are those who will praise him in heaven and eternally bless the Lamb who set them apart for himself by his blood.

**Father, Son and Holy Spirit** deserve our highest worship.

Let every creature proclaim the Lord's greatness and adore his majesty.
