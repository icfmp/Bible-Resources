# 1 Kings 12 — Modern English

## Rehoboam answers the people harshly (verses 1–15)

Israel's tribes complained to Rehoboam about their burdens.

They did **not** complain about Solomon's idolatry or his turning away from God.

Their greatest grievance should have been his religious rebellion, but they hardly cared about that as long as they could live comfortably and avoid taxes.

People inclined to division can always find something to complain about.

Considering Scripture's account of the peace, wealth and prosperity Israel enjoyed under Solomon, Henry judges their accusations to have been false or greatly exaggerated.

Rehoboam followed the advice of the younger men.

Few rulers have been more blinded by pride and the desire for arbitrary power—nothing is more destructive.

Through his foolish reply, God's prior judgment was fulfilled.

God left Rehoboam to his folly and withheld the discernment that would have led to peace, so that the kingdom would be divided.

The Lord accomplishes his wise and righteous purposes even through human errors and sins.

People who lose the kingdom of heaven likewise throw it away through stubbornness and folly, as Rehoboam threw away his kingdom.

## The ten tribes rebel (verses 16–24)

The people spoke disrespectfully about David.

How quickly a good person's service to the public is forgotten!

Remembering that God governs even the losses and hardships that come to us, and that other people may be only the instruments involved, should keep us from plotting revenge.

Rehoboam and his people finally listened to the Lord's word.

When we know what God commands, we must submit even when it directly contradicts what we wanted.

If we possess God's favor, the whole universe cannot truly harm us.

## Jeroboam establishes false worship (verses 25–33)

Jeroboam distrusted God's providence.

He devised his own way to protect himself and his kingship, even when doing so required sin.

Practical disbelief in God's complete sufficiency lies beneath our departures from him.

He probably intended the new worship to honor **Jehovah, the God of Israel**.

Yet representing God in this way violated divine law and dishonored his majesty.

People might accept worship of the God of Israel through an image more readily than openly worshiping Baal.

But that compromise paved the way for later idolatry.

Blessed Lord, give us grace to honor your temple, appointed worship, house of prayer and Sabbaths.

Never let us imitate Jeroboam by installing a detestable idol in our hearts.

Be everything precious to us.

Rule in our hearts as our hope of glory.
