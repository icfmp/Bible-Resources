# 2 Kings 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## The Shunammite receives her land back (verses 1–6)

God rewarded the kindness the Shunammite woman had shown Elisha by caring for her during famine.

It is wise to recognize coming danger and, when we can do so lawfully, seek a place of safety.

After the famine she returned from the Philistines' territory.

That was not an appropriate place for an Israelite to remain longer than necessity required.

She had once been so securely established among her own people that she needed no one to speak for her to the king.

But life is uncertain.

People and things we depend on may fail, while unexpected people become our helpers.

Sometimes seemingly small events have large consequences.

The king had just been listening to Gehazi recount Elisha's works.

The woman's appearance confirmed what he had heard and helped persuade him to grant her request.

God thereby provided for someone whose life had already benefited from his miraculous care.

## Elisha speaks with Hazael; Ben-hadad dies (verses 7–15)

Affliction often changes people's feelings about God's ministers.

Those who once hated or despised them may come to value their advice and prayers.

Elisha did not learn Hazael's future crimes by studying his face.

**God revealed them to him**, bringing tears to his eyes.

Knowing more about what lies ahead can increase sorrow.

A person may honestly recoil from an evil act while natural conscience still restrains him, then later become reconciled to that very act.

People occupying low positions seldom understand how strong the temptations of power and prosperity can become.

If they later gain authority, they may discover that their own hearts are far more deceitful and wicked than they imagined.

Satan ruins people by assuring them they will recover and prosper, lulling them into false security.

Hazael's false report harmed the king, depriving him of the warning he needed to prepare for death.

It also injured Elisha's reputation, because it made the prophet appear dishonest.

**Henry does not claim certainty that Hazael murdered Ben-hadad.**

He says it is possible Hazael caused his death **without deliberately intending to do so**.

Hazael certainly behaved deceptively and later persecuted Israel.

## Jehoram's wicked reign in Judah (verses 16–24)

Scripture gives an overall account of Jehoram's wickedness.

His father had undoubtedly taught him true knowledge of the Lord.

But his father acted unwisely in arranging his marriage into Ahab's family.

No spiritual good could come from union with a household so devoted to idolatry.

## Ahaziah's wicked reign in Judah (verses 25–29)

Names do not determine a person's character.

Still, it was a bad sign for Jehoshaphat's family to borrow names from the house of Ahab.

Ahaziah's connection to that family helped bring about both his wrongdoing and his destruction.

Anyone choosing a spouse should remember that they may also be choosing **the future parent of their children**.

God's providence arranged for Ahaziah to be destroyed with Ahab's house when the measure of their sin was full.

People who willingly share in another's wrongdoing should expect to share the consequences.

May the changes, trouble and wickedness we witness in the world make us increasingly eager to receive salvation through Jesus Christ.
