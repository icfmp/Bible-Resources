# 2 Thessalonians 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle expresses confidence in the Thessalonians, and prays for them. | 1–5 |
| He charges them to withdraw from disorderly walkers, particularly from the lazy and busybodies. | 6–15 |
| And concludes with a prayer for them, and a greeting. | 16–18 |

## Pray for faithful gospel work and for one another (verses 1–5)

Christians who live far apart can still meet **at the throne of grace**.

Even when people can offer one another no other practical help, they may give and receive genuine, valuable service **through prayer**.

Opponents of gospel preaching and persecutors of faithful ministers act **unreasonably and wickedly**.

Many do not believe the gospel. We should not be surprised when such people become restless and malicious toward those who proclaim it.

**Sin itself is the greatest evil**, but there are other dangers from which we need God's protection.

His gracious promises encourage us to trust him.

When God has given his promise, **its fulfillment is certain**.

Paul was confident about the Thessalonians, but that confidence was founded upon **his confidence in God**, not on human reliability.

He prayed for **spiritual blessings**.

We are sinful and miserable because we so often set our affections upon **the wrong things**.

There is no genuine love of God without **faith in Jesus Christ**.

If through God's special grace we possess this saving faith, we should earnestly ask for grace to **obey the Lord without reservation**, loving God and patiently enduring for Christ's sake.

## Do honest work, correct the disorderly and never tire of doing good (verses 6–15)

Those who have received Christ's gospel must **live in ways appropriate to it**.

People who **were able to work but refused to work** should not be supported in their deliberate idleness.

Christianity does not endorse laziness.

Resources consumed by those unwilling to work are then unavailable to encourage **diligent people** or support **the sick and afflicted**.

Being faithful in our ordinary employment is also **a duty of our Christian calling**.

Some people wanted support while doing no work.

They also became **meddlesome and self-important**, interfering with other people's affairs.

They caused considerable harm.

Using religion as a disguise for idleness or any other sin is a **serious corruption of Christianity**.

A servant waiting properly for the return of the Master should be **doing the work the Master commanded**.

An idle mind is easily occupied by Satan and our own sinful desires.

The human mind is always active. If it is not doing good, it will often **turn toward evil**.

It is a rare and excellent combination to be **energetic in one's own work and quiet about other people's affairs**.

Those who refused to work peacefully were to be **corrected**.

Believers could withdraw familiar fellowship from them when necessary.

Yet the aim remained **their good**, and correction should take the form of **loving admonition**, not hatred.

God will be with those who continue in his ways.

**Do not grow tired of doing good**.

Continue faithfully until your task is finished.

The time for complete rest will come **in heaven**.

## Pray for peace and Christ's grace (verses 16–18)

Paul prayed for the Thessalonians, and we should seek the same blessings for ourselves and our friends.

He especially desired **peace with God**.

He prayed that they would enjoy that peace **always and in every circumstance**, using all the means of grace God provides.

We can wish nothing better for those we love than **God's gracious presence**.

If the Lord is with us, our location and the absence of other people cannot destroy our happiness.

Through **the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ** we hope to enjoy peace with God and his presence.

His grace supplies everything necessary for true safety and happiness.

However much grace we ask God to give other people, **there will always be enough for us too**.
