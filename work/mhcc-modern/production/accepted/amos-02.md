# Amos 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Judgments against Moab and Judah. | 1–8 |
| The ingratitude and ruin of Israel. | 9–16 |

## The sins of Moab and Judah (verses 1–8)

Evil desires in the heart become visible in many forms, but **God examines motives as well as conduct**.

People who treat others cruelly may expect to receive cruelty in return.

The surrounding nations were condemned for wrongs against human beings. **Judah's sin was dishonoring God**.

The people despised the Lord's law, and he justly abandoned them to deception.

The idolatry they inherited from their ancestors did **not excuse their wrongdoing**.

Some of the worst abominations and most grievous oppression have been committed by **people claiming to worship the Lord**.

Such conduct encourages others to become unbelieving and to turn toward idolatry.

## Israel forgets God's gifts and opposes his servants (verses 9–16)

We need frequent reminders of God's former mercies.

Those mercies make our later sins **more blameworthy**.

Israel had received help **for the soul**—means of grace that taught the people how to use earthly blessings well.

Those spiritual gifts were more valuable than the earthly blessings themselves.

Faithful ministers are a tremendous blessing, and **God is the One who raises them up**.

Sinners' own consciences can testify that God did not leave them without opportunities to learn from his grace.

Yet the people did everything possible to draw faithful believers away from him.

**Satan and his agents seek to corrupt young people**, especially through the promise of amusement, bodily pleasure and **drinking companions**.

Many promising young professing Christians have been ruined by drunkenness.

The Lord describes these sins, especially those of people professing his name, as **a burden to him**.

His patience may be exhausted, but **his power never is**.

People who reject God's word and add stubbornness to their sins will not escape judgment through wealth or strength.

We should humble ourselves before God for **ingratitude and unfaithfulness**.
