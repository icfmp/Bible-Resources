# Luke 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The centurion's servant healed. | 1–10 |
| The widow's son raised. | 11–18 |
| John the Baptist's inquiry concerning Jesus. | 19–35 |
| Christ anointed in the house of the Pharisee, The parable of the two debtors. | 36–50 |

## Jesus heals the centurion's servant (verses 1–10)

Servants ought to conduct themselves in ways that earn the affection and trust of those they serve.

Masters, in turn, have a particular duty to care for their servants during sickness.

We should still bring the illness of our families to Christ through **faithful and earnest prayer**.

Building places where God's people can gather for worship is a good work, showing love for both God and his people.

Jesus was pleased with the centurion's faith.

He always fulfills the expectations of faith that honors **his power and his love**.

The servant's healing was both **immediate and complete**.

## Jesus raises the widow's son (verses 11–18)

Jesus saw the widow following her son's body toward the grave and **had compassion on her**.

Consider his power over death itself.

His gospel call to everyone, and especially to young people, is to **rise from the dead and receive his light and life**.

When Christ restored the young man, the change became visible: he sat up.

If he has given us his grace, our conduct should reveal it.

The young man also began to speak.

Similarly, when Jesus grants spiritual life, he opens our lips in **prayer and praise**.

When divine power accompanying the gospel raises people spiritually dead in sin, we ought to glorify God and recognize his gracious visit to his people.

May we belong to this compassionate Savior and look with joy toward the day his voice will summon everyone from the grave.

May we share in **the resurrection of life**, not the resurrection of condemnation.

## John the Baptist asks about Jesus (verses 19–35)

Jesus' miracles displayed his power in the natural world.

But he added the evidence of his work in the kingdom of grace: **the gospel was preached to the poor**.

The ministry of John had already shown that Christ's kingdom was spiritual.

John prepared the way by calling people to **repentance and a reformed heart and life**.

Those who were moved neither by John's ministry nor by Jesus' own teaching were rightly condemned.

They mocked the different ways God graciously addressed them.

Many people ruin themselves by refusing to take the matters of their souls seriously.

Let us show ourselves to be **children of wisdom**, attending to God's word and reverencing the truths and good news that unbelieving people and Pharisees ridicule.

## The repentant woman and the two debtors (verses 36–50)

Only a broken heart can understand how precious Christ is and how glorious the gospel's mercy is.

Repentant sinners feel they can never express enough sorrow over their sins or enough gratitude for his kindness.

The self-satisfied are often offended that the gospel welcomes such people.

The Pharisee **Simon** noticed the woman's past reputation, but failed to rejoice over the evidence of her repentance.

No one can escape the coming judgment without **freely granted forgiveness**.

Jesus purchased that forgiveness with his blood and gives it to everyone who believes in him.

Through the parable of two debtors, Jesus led Simon to admit that someone forgiven a larger debt has reason for greater love.

**Sin is a debt**, and everyone is indebted to Almighty God.

Some are guilty of greater offenses than others.

But whether our debt is large or small, it is **more than we can repay**.

God is ready to forgive.

His Son purchased pardon for believers; the gospel promises it; and his Spirit assures repentant sinners of it and gives comfort.

Let us reject Simon's pride.

Relying on Christ alone, we should love and obey him more deeply and recommend him more earnestly to others.

The more we show **genuine sorrow for sin and love for Jesus**, the clearer the evidence that we have received forgiveness.

Grace transforms both the sinner's **standing before God through the complete forgiveness of sins** and the sinner's **heart and conduct through faith in Christ**.
