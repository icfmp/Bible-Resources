# Ezekiel 4 — Modern English

## Ezekiel acts out Jerusalem's siege (verses 1–8)

The prophet was commanded to **represent the siege of Jerusalem through signs**.

He lay on his left side for a number of days, understood by Henry as possibly corresponding to the years since idolatry had become established.

The prophet's portrayal of the city's coming destruction served one purpose:

**To show that sin was the cause provoking the ruin of a once-flourishing Jerusalem.**

## Ezekiel's restricted food pictures famine (verses 9–17)

The bread that sustained Ezekiel was to be made from **coarse grains and legumes mixed together**.

Such food was seldom used except during extreme scarcity.

Even that was to be eaten only **in a small amount**.

The sign represented the desperate shortage the Jews would suffer during the siege and captivity.

When Ezekiel objected, he did not say he had been raised delicately and had never endured hardship.

Instead, he appealed to his **conscientious obedience to the law**: he had never eaten food forbidden to him.

It will comfort us when hardship comes if our consciences testify that we have carefully avoided even **the appearance of evil**.

Consider the terrible ruin caused by sin and acknowledge that God's judgment is righteous.

The people had used abundance for **luxury and excess**.

Their punishment through famine corresponded to that misuse.

When people refuse to serve the Lord joyfully while they have everything they need, they may end up serving enemies **while lacking even the necessities of life**.
