# Genesis 33 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The friendly meeting of Jacob and Esau. | 1–16 |
| Jacob comes to Succoth and Shalem, He builds an altar. | 17–20 |

## Jacob and Esau are reconciled (verses 1–16)

Having committed his situation to God in prayer, Jacob continued on his way.

Whatever happens, nothing can truly harm the person whose heart is firmly fixed on trusting God.

Jacob bowed before Esau.

Humble, submissive behavior can do much to turn away anger.

Esau embraced his brother.

God holds every person's heart in his hands. He can turn it whenever and however he pleases.

It is never useless to trust God or to call upon him in trouble.

When someone's ways please the Lord, God can even make that person's enemies live at peace with him.

Esau received Jacob as his brother, and the two showed one another great tenderness.

Esau asked, “Who are these people with you?”

Jacob answered this ordinary question in a way that revealed his character: he was a man whose eyes were continually turned toward the Lord.

Even after his fear had passed, Jacob urged Esau to accept his gift, and Esau took it.

It is a good thing when faith makes people generous, warmhearted, and openhanded.

But Jacob declined Esau's offer to travel with him.

It is unwise to become too closely attached to powerful relatives who do not fear God.

They may expect us to join in their worldly pleasures, or at least quietly approve of them, even while they criticize or mock our faith.

Such relationships may become a trap for us, or those relatives may take offense at us.

If we truly know the value of our souls, we will risk losing everything rather than placing them in danger.

If we truly love Christ, we will risk losing everything rather than renouncing him.

Finally, notice Jacob's tender concern for his family and his flocks.

It reminds us of the good Shepherd of our souls, who gathers the lambs in his arms, carries them close to his heart, and gently leads those nursing their young (Isaiah 40:11).

As parents, teachers, and pastors, we should all follow his example.

## Jacob worships God at his new home (verses 17–20)

Jacob did more than speak words of thanks for the favor God had shown him.

He gave thanks through what he did.

He also maintained faith and worship in his family.

Wherever we have a tent, God should have an altar.

Jacob dedicated his altar to *El-Elohe-Israel*—“God, the God of Israel.”

In doing so, he honored the one living and true God.

He also honored the God of Israel as the God who had entered into covenant with him.

Israel's God is Israel's glory.

Blessed be his name! He is still the mighty God, the God of Israel.

May we praise his name and rejoice in his love throughout our pilgrimage on earth, and forever in the heavenly Canaan.
