# Hebrews 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The office and duty of a high priest abundantly answered in Christ. | 1–10 |
| The Christian Hebrews reproved for their little progress in the knowledge of the gospel. | 11–14 |

## Jesus fulfills the high priest's calling (verses 1–10)

A high priest must share **our human nature**. The need for such a priest shows that humans have sinned and cannot approach the holy God on their own.

But everyone who comes to God **through our great High Priest, Jesus Christ**, is welcome.

He tenderly intercedes for those who have strayed from the paths of truth, duty and happiness, and can lead them back from error and suffering.

Only someone **appointed by God** can expect divine help and blessing in performing this sacred office.

In his earthly life Jesus truly shared human weakness short of sin. He **hungered, suffered temptation and faced death**.

He taught us not merely that we should pray but that we should pray **earnestly and fervently**. How many of our prayers are dry and unfeeling, unlike those Christ offered with tears!

He was strengthened to bear the terrible suffering appointed to him.

**The only complete deliverance from death is to be carried through it.** Christ rose and was exalted, and he saves fully those who come to God through him.

Jesus also taught his people to learn **humble obedience** in the afflictions God permits.

We need correction to learn submission. His obedience in our nature encourages our own obedience and assures us that divine help and comfort are available amid temptation.

Having been perfected for his great mediatorial work, **Jesus is the source of eternal salvation to all who obey him**.

Are we among those people?

## Grow beyond spiritual infancy (verses 11–14)

**Dull or inattentive hearers** make gospel teaching difficult.

Even believers who possess some faith can be slow to understand and believe.

God rightly expects much from those who have received much.

To be inexperienced in the word of righteousness is to lack **practical spiritual experience**.

True Christian understanding includes a spiritual taste and delight in **the goodness, sweetness and excellence of the gospel**.

The joy found in experiencing God's grace and love in Christ is too great to express adequately.
