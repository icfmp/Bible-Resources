# Leviticus 19 — Modern English

## Original heading

**laws.**

## Holy conduct toward God and neighbor

This chapter includes a few ceremonial regulations, but most of its commands still bind us.

They explain and apply the Ten Commandments.

God calls Israel to holiness because Israel's God is holy (verse 2).

His people must separate themselves from worldly and sinful conduct and devote themselves completely to him.

This remains the law of Christ.

May the Lord bring our every thought into obedience to him!

**Honor your parents** (verse 3).

Children must respect and obey their parents from the heart.

They should value them, treat them respectfully, try to please them and ease their burdens.

**Worship God alone** (verse 4).

False gods deceive their worshipers and lead them toward misery.

The true God makes his people holy and happy.

Do not even turn your eyes toward idols, much less your hearts.

**Leave part of the harvest for the poor** (verse 9).

Acts of devotion must always be joined with acts of charity, according to our means.

We should not grasp at everything we have the right to claim, demanding the full extent of our rights in every situation.

**Be honest in every dealing** (verse 11).

Make sure that what you possess was obtained justly.

Dishonest gain cannot make a person truly or lastingly rich.

**Revere God's holy name** (verse 12).

Treat it seriously and respectfully.

**Do not keep what belongs to someone else** (verse 13).

This especially includes wages due to workers.

**Protect those who cannot defend themselves** (verse 14).

Guard their safety and reputation.

Do not harm anyone simply because that person cannot—or will not—retaliate.

Avoid even making a weaker brother or sister stumble.

Fear of God should restrain us from wrongdoing even when it cannot provoke human punishment.

**Judge impartially** (verse 15).

All those entrusted with authority must administer justice without favoritism.

Spreading malicious stories and sowing discord among neighbors is among the worst things a person can do.

**Correct your neighbor in love** (verse 17).

It is better to rebuke someone gently than to harbor hatred because that person has wronged you.

If we fail to correct our neighbor when we should, we become guilty ourselves; such silence is a form of hatred rather than love.

A loving friend can say, “I will do this person the kindness of pointing out the fault.”

**Love your neighbor as yourself** (verse 18).

Put aside malice and practice brotherly love.

We often forgive ourselves for wrongs we have committed, without loving ourselves any less.

We should show a similar readiness to forgive those around us.

We must often give up something we want for the good of another person.

**Reject occult practices** (verse 31).

Christians insult God when they seek predictions of the future, use charms or spells, or practice similar things.

Asking whether these activities are harmless shows serious ignorance of their spiritual danger.

**Respect older people** (verse 32).

Faith teaches us good manners and the obligation to honor those who deserve respect.

**Care for foreigners** (verse 33).

Foreigners, widows and orphans are objects of God's special concern.

Wronging them is dangerous and sinful.

God's grace is open to foreigners, and we should do what we can to commend his ways to them.

**Use honest weights and measures** (verse 35).

We must not choose only the commands we like.

Our aim should be to carry out the whole will of God.

The nearer our lives and dispositions come to his law, the more happiness we will find and bring to people around us, and the better we will adorn the gospel.
