# Jeremiah 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortations to repentance. | 1–5 |
| Judah more guilty than Israel. | 6–11 |
| But pardon is promised. | 12–20 |
| The children of Israel express their sorrow and repentance. | 21–25 |

## God invites the unfaithful to return (verses 1–5)

When we repent, it is good to remember **the particular sins we have committed**, including the places where we committed them and the people whose company encouraged them.

How gently the Lord had corrected his people!

When he receives repentant sinners, he acts as **God, not merely as a human being**.

Whatever you have said or done in the past, will you not **turn to him from this moment forward**?

Will not such grace overcome your stubbornness?

Now that pardon is announced, will you not accept its benefit?

Repentant people may expect the tender compassion of **a Father welcoming a returning prodigal**.

They approach him as **the Guide of their youth**.

Young people especially need guidance.

Those returning to God can take courage from the promise that **he will not remain angry forever**.

All his mercies throughout history encourage us in the same way.

What could be more desirable for a young person than to have **the Lord as Father and Guide**?

Parents should earnestly direct their children, day after day, to seek that blessing.

## Judah's guilt is greater than Israel's (verses 6–11)

When we consider the sins committed by people who have abandoned a profession of faith, and the consequences that followed, we find abundant reason to avoid their paths.

It is dreadful to be shown **more guilty than those who have already perished through their wrongdoing**.

But knowing that others were even more wicked will be **little comfort in everlasting judgment**.

## Pardon and restoration are promised (verses 12–20)

Notice how willing God is to forgive sin, and the blessings he has prepared for **the age of the gospel**.

These promises were proclaimed **toward the north**, addressing Israel's ten tribes who were captive in **Assyria**.

They are told how to return to God.

If we confess our sins, the Lord is **faithful and just to forgive**.

Henry also sees these promises reaching toward a future return of the Jews in later ages.

God graciously receives those who come back to him and, by his grace, calls them out **from among others**.

**The ark of the covenant** was not recovered after the exile.

The earlier dispensation was destined to pass away.

Henry connects this transition with the great increase of believers as **Gentiles and Israelites scattered among them** were brought into God's church.

The prophecy describes a happy condition for God's people.

The Lord can teach people everywhere to call him **Father**.

Yet no one can truly be his child without **a thorough change of heart and life**.

Without that renewal, we have no security against wandering away again.

## Repentance acknowledges that only God can save (verses 21–25)

Sin means turning aside into crooked paths.

At the root of every sin lies **forgetfulness of the Lord our God**.

By sin we bring trouble upon ourselves.

But God promises to **heal the backsliding** of those who return.

That healing comes through **his pardoning mercy, his peace that quiets the heart and his grace that renews us**.

Repentant people devote themselves to God.

They reject every hope of rescue that depends on anyone else.

They trust in the Lord alone, because **only he can save**.

These promises point to **the great salvation from sin accomplished by Jesus Christ**.

Those who repent acknowledge God's justice in their troubles and condemn their own wrongdoing.

They learn to call sin **shameful**, even the sins in which they once found the greatest pleasure.

They come to recognize sin as **death and ruin**, and understand that it is responsible for their misery.

People who harden themselves in sin will receive contempt and distress.

Those who conceal their sins will not prosper.

But **those who confess and forsake them find mercy**.
