# Isaiah 52 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Freedom announced through Christ's kingdom (verses 1–12)

The gospel declares freedom to those bound by fear.

Let people exhausted and weighed down by the burden of sin come to **Jesus Christ** for relief.

Shake off the dust of doubts and fears and loosen the chains that bind you.

Our Redeemer did not purchase salvation with **silver, gold or any perishable possession**.

He paid with **his own precious blood**.

When we consider how freely this salvation is offered and how much harm sin does even to our earthly peace, we will value Christ's redemption more highly.

Do we seek victory over **every sin**, remembering that God's glory requires holiness in every person who follows Christ?

The good news is **that the Lord Jesus reigns**.

Christ himself first announced these tidings.

His ministers now proclaim them.

As they keep themselves clean from the world's corruptions, they become welcome and beautiful messengers to those who receive their message.

Zion's watchmen could scarcely discern evidence of God's favor through the dark cloud of their afflictions.

But now the cloud is removed and they can plainly see the fulfillment of his promise.

Zion's ruined places will rejoice, and **the whole world will benefit**.

Henry applies this restoration to the salvation offered through Christ.

**Babylon is no place for Israelites.**

Likewise, anyone enslaved to sin and Satan should make use of the liberty Christ proclaims.

The people should depart **quickly and diligently**, wasting no time or lingering in captivity.

But they must not depart in **fearful, distrustful haste**.

Those walking in obedience are under God's special protection.

Faith in that care removes the need for panicked flight.

## The Messiah's suffering and exaltation (verses 13–15)

These verses begin a remarkable, detailed and faithful prophecy of **the Messiah's office, character and glory**.

Henry says its testimony has convinced some of the most stubborn unbelievers.

Christ is **Wisdom itself**.

The work of redemption reveals the wisdom of God in a mystery.

People who saw Jesus suffering might have thought that no one had ever appeared so miserable.

His sorrow seemed beyond every other sorrow.

Yet **God exalted him to the highest honor**.

Only Christ's gospel could reveal this saving work as God has now made it known.

Christ shed his blood for sinners **once**, but its saving power **continues**.

May those opposing him learn the wisdom of ending their opposition.

May they share in **the blood of sprinkling** and **the baptism of the Holy Spirit**, obeying Christ and praising his salvation.
