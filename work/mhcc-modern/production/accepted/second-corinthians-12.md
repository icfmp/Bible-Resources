# 2 Corinthians 12 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Paul's heavenly revelations (verses 1–6)

There is no serious doubt that Paul was speaking about **himself**.

He did not know exactly how his experience had occurred.

Perhaps heavenly realities were shown to him while his body remained in a trance, as had sometimes happened to earlier prophets.

Perhaps his soul was temporarily separated from his body and taken into heaven.

Or perhaps both his body and soul were caught up together.

**Paul himself did not know**, and Henry does not pretend to resolve the matter.

We are neither capable of understanding nor meant to know every detail of heaven's glorious life at present.

Paul did **not** try to publish a description of everything he heard.

Instead, he proclaimed the **teaching of Jesus Christ**.

That teaching is the foundation upon which the church rests and upon which our faith and hope must be built.

The vision should enlarge our expectations of future glory, but it should also make us content with **the ordinary means God has provided for learning his truth and will**.

## The thorn in the flesh and sufficient grace (verses 7–10)

Paul explained how God kept him humble after receiving extraordinary visions and revelations.

The Lord allowed a **thorn in the flesh** so that Paul would not become proud.

Scripture does not tell us what this thorn was.

Henry leaves open whether it was **a severe outward trouble or a powerful temptation**.

God can turn even the reproaches of enemies to good by using them to expose and restrain our pride.

When God loves us, he will not leave us to become proud beyond measure.

Spiritual burdens may be used to cure **spiritual pride**.

Paul called the thorn a **messenger of Satan**.

Satan meant it for evil, but **God overruled it for good**.

Prayer is a remedy for every wound and a source of help in every illness or trial.

When we face thorns in the flesh, we should devote ourselves to prayer.

If an answer is not given at the first or second request, we should **continue praying**.

Troubles teach us to pray. Troubles that remain can teach us **perseverance in prayer**.

God accepts the prayers of faith, but he does not always grant what we ask.

Sometimes he grants a request in judgment; at other times **he refuses a request in love**.

If he does not remove our trouble or temptation but gives us **sufficient grace**, we have no reason to complain.

Grace includes God's gracious favor toward us.

It is enough to illuminate and strengthen our souls, sustain our life and comfort us amid suffering.

**God's strength is made perfect in human weakness**.

Our weakness becomes the occasion for his grace to appear even more clearly.

When we know we are weak in ourselves, we are moved to Christ for strength and enjoy the supply he gives.

## Paul defends his concern for the Corinthians (verses 11–21)

We should defend the good reputation of faithful people when that reputation is unfairly attacked.

We owe particular gratitude to those whom God has used to bring **spiritual blessings** into our lives.

Paul's words demonstrate the character and intentions of **a faithful gospel minister**.

His chief aim was **to do good**.

He expressed concern that on his next visit he might find professing believers living in sins that required painful correction.

Even the failings of those under their care can deeply humble faithful ministers.

God may use those disappointments to humble ministers who would otherwise be tempted by pride.

The Corinthians' behavior showed how far the false teachers had drawn their followers away from godliness.

How grievous that such sins can be found among people professing the gospel!

Yet this happened even during the apostles' own lives and has happened far too often since.
