# 1 Corinthians 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Cautions against going to law in heathen courts. | 1–8 |
| Sins which, if lived and died in, shut out from the kingdom of God. | 9–11 |
| Our bodies, which are the members of Christ, and temples of the Holy Ghost, must not be defiled. | 12–20 |

## Settle disputes as brothers and sisters (verses 1–8)

Christians ought not to quarrel with one another, because they are **brothers and sisters in Christ**.

If they gave this relationship proper weight, many lawsuits, arguments and disputes would never begin.

**Where serious injury to ourselves or our families is involved, we may use lawful means to obtain justice**.

Paul's instruction should not be turned into an absolute ban on seeking legal redress.

Nevertheless, believers should have **a forgiving disposition**.

Whenever possible, they should refer disputes to trustworthy people among their fellow believers instead of bringing every disagreement into court.

Many disagreements are small enough to be settled readily if we first **conquer our own passions**.

Learn to bear with each other and yield where appropriate.

Even people of limited learning and experience may be able to settle quarrels if the parties are prepared to listen.

It is shameful when minor disputes among Christians become so bitter that no one among them can help reconcile them.

**Peace within one's own mind and tranquillity among neighbors are worth more than winning a case**.

When lawsuits occur between believers, something has already gone wrong in their relationships.

## Grace changes people who once practiced serious sins (verses 9–11)

Paul warned the Corinthians against numerous grave sins, including sins in which **some of them had formerly lived**.

This warning carried particular force because the Corinthians had become proud of their supposed knowledge and wisdom.

**All unrighteousness is sin**.

Any sin deliberately practiced and left unrepented—any sin allowed to reign—**shuts a person out of God's kingdom**.

Do not deceive yourselves.

People naturally flatter themselves with the idea that they can live in sin, die belonging to Christ and enjoy heaven.

But no one can **sow to the flesh and reap eternal life**.

Paul reminded the Corinthian Christians of the tremendous change **the gospel and God's grace** had produced in them.

**Christ's blood and the washing of regeneration** can remove all sin's guilt.

Our **justification** comes from the suffering and merit of Jesus Christ.

Our **sanctification** comes from **the Holy Spirit's work**.

These two graces are inseparably joined: **everyone made righteous before God is also made holy by God's grace**.

## Believers' bodies belong to Christ (verses 12–20)

Some Corinthians appear to have argued that **everything was lawful for them**.

Paul opposed this dangerous idea.

Christ has truly set believers free, and they should remain firm in that liberty.

But no Christian should willingly become **a slave to any bodily appetite**.

Our bodies belong to the Lord.

They are meant to serve as instruments of righteousness leading to holiness, **not instruments of sin**.

Jesus Christ's resurrection brought honor to his body.

The future resurrection will also bring glory to believers' bodies.

The hope of that resurrection should encourage Christians to refuse sexual immorality and other practices that dishonor the body.

The believer's soul is united to Christ through faith.

Therefore **the whole person becomes a member of his spiritual body**.

Some sins may be resisted through direct struggle, but **sexual immorality must be fled**.

Its destructive consequences have ruined many lives.

Its harm affects not only the body but often **the mind**.

Christ redeemed our bodies from deserved condemnation and hopeless slavery through **his atoning sacrifice**.

We must keep them clean as vessels suitable for our Master's use.

United to Christ as one spirit and **purchased at a price of immeasurable value**, we belong wholly to him.

May we spend the rest of our lives, until the final day and hour, **glorifying God with our bodies and our spirits, which are his**.
