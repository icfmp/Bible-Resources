# Joshua 17 — Modern English

## Manasseh's inheritance (verses 1–6)

Manasseh was one of Joseph's two tribes, yet its inheritance was divided into two parts.

Zelophehad's daughters now received the benefit of their godly zeal and prudent foresight.

Those who take care, while traveling through this world's wilderness, to secure a place among the saints in light will enjoy the comfort of that inheritance in the next world.

But those who neglect it now will lose that blessing forever.

Lord, teach us to believe and obey here, and grant us an inheritance among your saints in eternal glory.

## Manasseh's boundaries and the remaining Canaanites (verses 7–13)

The lands of **Manasseh and Ephraim** were closely connected.

Although each tribe had its own inheritance, their people could live among one another and show each other kindness.

They belonged to different tribes but were still one Israel, bound to love one another as brothers.

Yet they allowed the Canaanites to remain among them.

They did this against God's command, because it suited their own interests.

## Joseph's descendants request more land (verses 14–18)

Joshua held public office and did not favor his own tribe over any other.

He governed without partiality or favoritism. Everyone entrusted with public responsibilities should follow his example.

Joshua told Joseph's descendants that their allotted land would be sufficient if they were willing to **work and fight** for it.

People invent many excuses to avoid hard work.

One especially convenient excuse is to rely on influential or wealthy relatives who might provide what we want.

We may then press those relatives to use positions of trust unfairly on our behalf.

But it is far kinder to point out opportunities that are already within someone's reach and encourage that person to use them than to encourage laziness and extravagance.

True religion never excuses such behavior.

The rule remains that **those unwilling to work should not eat**.

Many of the things we claim we “cannot” do are really things our laziness has made us reluctant to attempt.

This happens particularly in our spiritual work and warfare.

Apart from Christ we can do nothing.

But instead of trusting him and acting, we may simply sit still and attempt nothing.

If we truly belong to Christ, he will stir us to make our best efforts and cry to him for help.

Then our territory will be enlarged (1 Chronicles 4:9–10), and our complaints will cease—or better, become joyful thanksgiving.
