# Isaiah 53 — Modern English

## The despised and rejected Messiah (verses 1–3)

Nowhere else in the Old Testament, according to Henry, is it prophesied **so plainly and fully that Christ must suffer before entering his glory**.

Yet even now many fail to recognize or acknowledge the divine power accompanying this word.

The authentic and supremely important announcement of **salvation for sinners through God's Son** is neglected.

The humble condition Christ willingly took, and the way he appeared in the world, did not match the ideas many Jews had formed of the Messiah.

They expected a figure arriving with pomp.

Instead, he grew up quietly and almost unnoticed, like a **young plant**.

He had none of the outward glory people would naturally expect.

His life was not merely humble in social position.

It was also **filled with sorrow**.

Because he was **made sin for us**, he endured the sentence to which our sin had exposed us.

Hearts ruled by worldly desires find nothing in Jesus that makes them seek a share in him.

And how many people still despise him through their treatment of his people or reject **his teaching and authority**!

## Christ suffers in our place for our sins (verses 4–9)

Here Scripture describes both **Christ's sufferings and their purpose**.

Our Lord Jesus suffered **for our sins, in our place**.

All of us have sinned and fallen short of the glory of God.

Sinners love their own particular sins and cling to the evil paths they have chosen.

Our sins deserve every grief and sorrow, even the most severe judgment.

We are saved from the ruin sin deserves because **our sins were laid upon Christ**.

This atonement was made for our transgressions.

**It is the only way of salvation.**

Our sins were the **thorns on Christ's head**, the **nails in his hands and feet**, and the **spear in his side**.

He was given up to death **because of our offenses**.

Through his suffering, Christ also purchased **the Holy Spirit and God's grace** for us.

By that grace, the corruption and sinful desires that afflict our souls can be put to death.

If he has taught us to value everything else as loss compared with him, we can endure our far lighter sufferings.

How can we fail to love **the One who first loved us**?

## The willing sacrifice is exalted and justifies sinners (verses 10–12)

Come and see how deeply Christ loved us!

**We could never have placed him in our position. He placed himself there willingly.**

He took away the sin of the world **by taking it upon himself**.

He willingly became subject to death, which is the wages of sin for us.

Consider the many graces and glories that follow his humiliation and belong to his exalted state.

Christ does not entrust the ultimate care of his family to anyone else.

God's purposes will certainly be accomplished.

Whatever is undertaken in accordance with his will will prosper.

Christ will see the result in **the conversion and salvation of sinners**.

Many are justified by him—those for whom he gave his life as a ransom.

We are **justified by faith**.

In this way God receives the greatest glory, free grace is honored, human pride is humbled and our eternal happiness is secured.

We must know and trust Christ as the One who **carried our sins** and rescued us from sinking beneath their weight by bearing it himself.

He has defeated the great enemies of his people: **sin and Satan, death and hell, the world and the flesh**.

Everything the Father intended the Redeemer to obtain will certainly belong to him.

He led captivity captive and received gifts **for human beings, to give them gifts**.

When we consider the Son of God's sufferings, we should remember the long record of our own transgressions.

Look upon Christ as suffering under **the burden of our guilt**.

Here is the firm foundation upon which a trembling sinner can rest.

We were **purchased by his blood**, and our lives display the glory of his grace.

For his people, Christ **continually pleads and prevails**, destroying the devil's works.
