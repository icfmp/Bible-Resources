# 1 Samuel 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Achish puts confidence in David, Saul's fear. | 1–6 |
| Saul consults a witch at Endor. | 7–19 |
| Saul's terror. | 20–25 |

## Achish trusts David; Saul becomes afraid (verses 1–6)

David faced a very difficult situation.

Refusing Achish's demand for military assistance would expose him to danger.

But if he promised help and then remained neutral—or turned against the Philistines—he would act ungratefully and betray their trust.

If he fought against Israel, he would sin gravely.

He seemed unable to escape without compromising his conscience.

Yet his ambiguous answer, intended to gain time, was not worthy of a true Israelite.

Trouble becomes terrifying to those who live in disobedience.

In his distress Saul inquired of the Lord.

But he did not approach in faith.

His divided heart made him unstable.

Saul had enforced the law against people who consulted familiar spirits (**Exodus 22:18**).

Some people appear zealous against wrongdoing only when they personally suffer from it.

They care little for God's honor and do not really hate sin because it is sin.

They condemn in others what they willingly practice themselves.

Saul tried to drive the devil from his kingdom while harboring envy and malice in his own heart.

How foolish to seek help from the very people he had tried to remove in obedience to God's law!

## Saul consults the medium at Endor (verses 7–19)

Once we depart from the plain path of duty, nearly every choice leads us farther astray and increases confusion and temptation.

Saul asked the woman to summon someone from the dead.

God had expressly forbidden such practices (**Deuteronomy 18:11**).

Whether it is genuine occult activity or merely a pretended one, the attempt to obtain help or knowledge through a creature that cannot be obtained from God in the path of duty is either malicious or deeply ignorant.

While Samuel lived, Scripture never describes Saul consulting him in difficult decisions.

He should have sought that counsel then.

Now Samuel was dead, Saul asked for him to be brought back.

Some people despise and persecute godly ministers while they live and wish to have them back only after their deaths.

Henry concludes that this episode cannot be explained as a **mere human trick or deception**.

The woman had no power to summon Samuel by her own will.

But God may have permitted Samuel's appearance in response to Saul's inquiry.

The woman's surprise and terror show that what happened was unusual and unexpected.

Saul had rejected Samuel's solemn warnings during Samuel's lifetime.

Now he hoped, in defiance of God's command, to receive encouragement or advice from him.

God may instead have allowed the departed prophet to appear in order to confirm his earlier judgment and announce Saul's approaching death.

The words **“You and your sons shall be with me”** establish only that they would be in the world of the dead.

They do not establish the spiritual or eternal state of Saul or his sons.

The event solemnly shows God permitting a departed prophet to confirm from beyond the grave what he had already declared on earth.

## Saul falls into despair (verses 20–25)

Anyone seeking help or comfort outside God's appointed ways will be disappointed as Saul was.

His terror led almost to despair, but not to humility.

He did not confess his sin.

He offered no sacrifices or prayers for pardon.

Nor does he appear to have considered the fate of his children and people or tried to escape the danger.

Instead, in stubborn despair, he moved toward destruction.

God has placed a few such examples in Scripture as warnings.

They teach us never to suppress conviction or despise the divine word.

But **any sinner who still has even one thought of genuine repentance must not suppose himself beyond hope**.

Let that person humble himself, seek the Lord's mercy and determine to continue seeking it throughout life.

Such a person will not seek God in vain.

- **7–19:** **Deuteronomy 18:11**; Saul failing to consult Samuel while alive; Henry's account **rejects ordinary human fraud but says the woman could not produce Samuel**, God possibly allowing a real appearance, meaning of **“with me” strictly the realm of the departed, not a judgment of salvation/damnation** — retained with interpretive caution.
- **20–25:** despair **without repentance, confession or prayer**, no concern for children/people, historical warning balanced by explicit **hope for every person still able to turn and seek God** — retained.
