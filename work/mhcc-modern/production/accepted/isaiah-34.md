# Isaiah 34 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's vengeance against the enemies of his church. | 1–8 |
| Their desolation. | 9–17 |

## Judgment against the enemies of God's church (verses 1–8)

Isaiah describes the Lord's wars, whose purposes are righteous and whose final outcome is certain.

The warning concerns **all nations**.

All have benefited from God's patience; those refusing to repent must also reckon with his judgment.

The fearful description of bloodshed should impress upon our minds the seriousness of divine justice.

Henry interprets **Idumea** as representing the peoples set against God's church, including what he calls **the kingdom of Antichrist**.

The full terror of the final day awaiting people who persist in opposing Christ is beyond human comprehension.

God has appointed the time when his church will be delivered and its enemies brought down.

Until that day, we should wait patiently and **not pronounce final judgments before the appointed time**.

Through Jesus Christ, the Lord can show mercy to believers without compromising righteousness.

In this way he glorifies his own name.

## The barrenness left by sin (verses 9–17)

Those who try to destroy God's church will never succeed in their ultimate purpose.

Instead they bring ruin upon themselves.

**What dreadful changes sin can cause!**

It can turn fertile countryside into wasteland and transform a crowded city into an uninhabited wilderness.

Let us compare everything we discover in **the book of the Lord** with the movements of Providence around us.

Then we will seek his kingdom and righteousness more diligently.

What the Lord commands through his mouth, **his Spirit will accomplish**.

As the predictions of Scripture are fulfilled, the evidence of its truth continues to grow, leading toward the time when these scenes of judgment give way to better days.

Henry interprets Israel as a figure of **Christ's church**, and the Edomites—Israel's bitter enemies—as figures of **the enemies of Christ's kingdom**.

God's Jerusalem may be reduced to ruins for a period.

But the enemies who persist in opposing his church face a desolation that lasts forever.
