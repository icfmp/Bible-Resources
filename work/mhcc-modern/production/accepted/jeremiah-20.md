# Jeremiah 20 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The doom of Pashur, who ill-treated the prophet. | 1–6 |
| Jeremiah complains of hard usage. | 7–13 |
| He regrets his ever having been born. | 14–18 |

## Pashhur becomes an object of terror (verses 1–6)

**Pashhur** struck Jeremiah and put him in the stocks.

The prophet remained silent until **God gave him a message**.

To confirm that message, Pashhur received the name **“Terror on every side.”**

It describes a person not simply suffering but **despairing**, not merely surrounded by danger but **terrified on every side**.

Wicked people may experience dreadful fear even where no genuine danger appears.

God can make even **the boldest sinner a terror to himself**.

People who refuse to hear God's prophets expose their faults may later be forced to hear those same accusations **from their own consciences**.

How miserable is someone whose conscience has made that person a constant terror!

Friends may abandon such a person.

God may leave a sinner alive in deep misery as **a visible reminder of his justice**.

## The Lord strengthens Jeremiah to keep serving (verses 7–13)

Jeremiah complained about the insults and injuries he endured.

But Henry notes a possible reading of verse 7:

**“You persuaded me, and I was persuaded; you were stronger than I and overcame me by the influence of your Spirit.”**

When we know that we are following the path of duty God has given us, we act weakly and foolishly if discouragement makes us wish **we had never begun the work**.

Jeremiah discovered that divine grace was powerful enough to keep him serving despite the temptation **to abandon his calling**.

Whatever wrongs others do to us, we must entrust judgment to **the God who says, “I will repay.”**

The prophet became deeply conscious of **God's presence**, his protection and the promises upon which he relied.

He therefore urged himself and others to **glorify the Lord**.

When God's people lay their concerns openly before him, he enables them to see his deliverance.

## Jeremiah laments the day of his birth (verses 14–18)

When grace wins a victory over temptation, it is right to feel ashamed of our foolishness and to marvel at God's kindness.

We should also be warned to guard our minds more carefully when another trial comes.

See how powerful a temptation Jeremiah faced—and how God's help enabled him to overcome it!

In his bitterness, Jeremiah became angry that **his first breath had not been his last**.

These lamentations were recorded **not to teach us to repeat the same wishes**, but to give us needed warnings.

People who think they stand securely must take care **lest they fall**.

We should pray daily:

**“Lead us not into temptation.”**

How frail, inconsistent and sinful we are!

How foolish and unnatural our wishes become when **discontent rules our hearts**!

Consider **Jesus Christ**, who endured the opposition of sinners against himself, so that we will not become weary or fainthearted during **our much smaller trials**.
