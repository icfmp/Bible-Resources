# 2 Kings 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elisha prophesies plenty. | 1, 2 |
| The flight of the Syrian army. | 3–11 |
| Samaria plentifully supplied. | 12–20 |

## Elisha promises abundant food (verses 1 and 2)

Human desperation gives God an opportunity to display his glory through saving power.

He often comes to his people's aid when their own strength is exhausted.

Unbelief greatly dishonors and displeases God.

It also deprives people of the blessings he intended for them.

Those who refuse to believe the promise of eternal life may see its fulfillment **at a distance**, but never enjoy it.

Yet even temporal deliverance and mercies will do sinners no lasting good unless the Lord's kindness leads them to repentance.

## The Syrian army flees (verses 3–11)

God can make the strongest heart tremble whenever he chooses.

If people refuse to fear him, he can make them afraid even at the shaking of a leaf.

Through Providence, the men with leprosy arrived after the Syrians had fled.

Their consciences warned them that it would be wrong to keep the discovery to themselves.

Ordinary human sympathy and fear of punishment restrain selfishness even in those without saving faith.

Such feelings help preserve order and kindness in society.

But those who have discovered **the unsearchable riches of Jesus Christ** possess a still greater reason to share good news promptly with others.

They do so from love for Christ, not merely from fear or self-interest.

That same love should move them to share earthly provisions generously with brothers and sisters in need.

## Samaria receives food (verses 12–20)

God supplied Israel in a way no one expected.

This encourages us to trust his power and goodness in our deepest difficulties.

We can rely upon God's promises, because not one of his words will fail.

The nobleman who had doubted Elisha saw the restored abundance.

That sight exposed the foolishness of his unbelief and silenced his doubts.

But **he did not eat the food he saw**.

People who expect God's promises to fail may rightly discover that the promises of the world disappoint them instead.

Let this chapter teach us how seriously the Lord regards distrust of his power, providence and word.

Earthly life and enjoyment are uncertain.

God's warnings of judgment are certain and will come upon those who remain guilty.

May the Lord help us discover whether we are exposed to his warnings or have an interest in his promises.
