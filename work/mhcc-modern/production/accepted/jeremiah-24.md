# Jeremiah 24 — Modern English

## Original source heading

**Good and bad figs represent the Jews in captivity, and those who remain in their own land.**

## Two baskets of figs

Jeremiah saw **two baskets of figs** set before the temple as **first-fruit offerings**.

The figs in one basket were **very good**.

Those in the other were **very bad**.

What creature is more degraded than someone who persists in wickedness?

And who is more valuable than a truly godly person?

This vision was intended to **encourage those already in exile**, assuring them that they would have a happy return.

It also warned and humbled the proud people who **remained in Jerusalem**, telling them that they would experience miserable captivity.

The **good figs** represent the faithful captives.

We cannot determine **God's love or hatred simply from the outward circumstances before us**.

Sometimes early suffering proves to be **for the best**.

When a child receives correction promptly, the correction may have a better effect.

The exile itself was intended for the people's good.

**God's good purposes never fail**.

Affliction convinced them of sin, humbled them under his hand, loosened their attachment to the world, taught them to pray and led them away from sin—especially from **idolatry**.

God promised to acknowledge these people as his own **even in captivity**.

The Lord recognizes everyone who truly belongs to him, whatever their outward condition.

He promised to protect them during trouble and to bring them to a glorious deliverance **at the proper time**.

When suffering is sanctified to us, we can trust that it will **end for our good**.

The people would return to God **with their whole hearts**.

Then they could once again acknowledge him as their God, pray to him and hope for his blessings.

The **bad figs** represented **Zedekiah and his party who remained in the land**.

They would be removed for their harm and become despised among nations.

God possesses many kinds of judgment.

People who escape one may still meet another, until they are brought to repentance.

The prophecy certainly found fulfillment **in Jeremiah's time**.

But Henry adds that **the Spirit of prophecy may also have been looking ahead** to the later scattering of unbelieving Jews among the nations.

Those who desire blessing from God should ask him to give them **a heart that truly knows him**.
