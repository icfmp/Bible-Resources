## Christ is born (verses 1–7)

The fullness of time had come.

God sent his Son, **born of a woman and born under the law**.

The circumstances of Jesus' birth were extraordinarily humble.

He was born where travelers lodged, as a temporary guest in the world. His brief earthly stay teaches us to regard ourselves as sojourners here too.

Through sin, humanity had become like an abandoned newborn—helpless and without hope. Christ entered a condition resembling ours.

He knew how reluctant people are to live in humble homes, wear ordinary clothing or eat simple meals.

He knew how parents desire to decorate and indulge their children, how readily poor people envy the rich, and how often rich people look down upon the poor.

Yet when we look in faith at **the Son of God made human and lying in a manger**, our vanity, ambition and envy are challenged.

We can hardly contemplate that scene properly and still make earthly greatness the object of our desires for ourselves or our children.

## Angels announce the birth to shepherds (verses 8–20)

Angels announced the Savior's birth, but they were sent to humble, devout and hardworking shepherds.

These shepherds were carrying out their ordinary occupation, watching their sheep.

Faithful work does not put us beyond the reach of God's gracious visits. We may remain close to him while carrying out an honest calling.

The glory of this work belongs to God: **Glory to God in the highest**.

All God's works display his glory, but the redemption of the world displays his glory in the highest way.

God's goodwill toward humanity, made known through the coming of the Messiah, brought peace into this world.

Here **peace** means all the good that flows to us because Christ took our nature upon himself.

An innumerable company of angels confirmed this welcome message.

The shepherds hurried to the place.

Having found what they were told to expect, they spread the good news about the child who was **the Savior, Christ the Lord**.

Mary carefully kept and considered everything that happened.

These events strengthened her devotion.

We, too, would avoid many errors in judgment and behavior if we pondered more deeply what God has revealed about his Son.

The announcement remains good news for us: **a Savior, Christ the Lord, has been born**.

## Jesus is circumcised and presented in the temple (verses 21–24)

Our Lord Jesus **was not born in sin**.

Unlike sinners, he did not need the death of a corrupt nature or renewal toward holiness that circumcision symbolized.

For him, the ordinance anticipated his coming **perfect obedience to the whole law**, through suffering and temptation, even to death on our behalf.

After **forty days**, Mary went to the temple to offer the sacrifices appointed for purification.

Joseph also presented the holy child Jesus.

As a firstborn son, he was to be dedicated to the Lord and redeemed according to the law.

Let us similarly present our children to the Lord who gave them to us, asking him to rescue them from sin and death and make them holy to himself.

## Simeon prophesies about Jesus (verses 25–35)

The same Spirit who sustained Simeon's hope now brought that hope to joyful fulfillment.

Those who desire to see Christ should seek him where he makes himself known.

Simeon confessed that the child he held was **the Savior himself—the salvation God had appointed**.

He was ready to leave the world.

How small this world appears to someone who holds Christ and beholds salvation!

Simeon's words show the comfort of a godly person's death.

Such a person departs **at peace with God, at peace in conscience, and at peace with the thought of death**.

Those who have welcomed Christ can welcome death.

Joseph and Mary marveled at what was said about Jesus.

Simeon's prophecy also gave them reason to **rejoice with reverent trembling**.

Jesus, his truth and his people continue to be spoken against.

His teaching becomes a test that reveals human character.

Some people's hidden good desires are exposed when they embrace him.

Other people's hidden corruption is revealed through their opposition to him.

Human beings will be judged according to their heart's response to Christ.

Jesus would be a **suffering Savior**, and his mother would suffer too because of her close relationship and love for him.

## Anna speaks about Jesus (verses 36–40)

There was much wrong in the religious community of that period, but God had not left himself without witnesses.

**Anna** lived at the temple or attended it constantly.

She was devoted to prayer and served God in everything.

People to whom Jesus has been revealed have good reason to thank the Lord and tell others about him, as she did.

The examples of Simeon and Anna should encourage older believers.

Their gray hair is a crown of honor when they continue in the way of righteousness.

Before our lips are silenced by death, let us use them to praise our Redeemer.

It was fitting for Christ to be made like his brothers and sisters in every respect.

He passed through infancy and childhood as other children do, yet **without sin** and with clear signs of his divine nature.

By the Spirit of God all his powers functioned perfectly.

Other children have foolishness bound up in their hearts, as their actions and words reveal. Jesus, by contrast, was **full of wisdom**.

Everything he said and did was appropriate and wise, far beyond his years.

While other children reveal the corruption of fallen nature, he displayed only **the grace of God**.

## Jesus speaks with teachers in the temple (verses 41–52)

Children honor Christ when they attend public worship.

Jesus' earthly parents remained for **all seven days of the festival**.

It is good to stay to the end of worship rather than hurry away from holy duties.

Those who have lost the comfort of fellowship with Christ and the assurance of belonging to him should ask **where, when and how** they lost these blessings and retrace their steps.

They should seek him where he has promised to make himself known.

Mary and Joseph found Jesus in the temple, where teachers of the law instructed their students.

He listened, asked questions and answered questions with remarkable wisdom.

Young people should seek the truth of God, attend the teaching of the gospel and ask their elders and teachers thoughtful questions that will deepen their understanding.

Those who seek Jesus sorrowfully can find him again with greater joy.

Jesus explained that he must be in **his Father's house, attending to his Father's business**.

All God's children should likewise make the Father's work their first concern, letting other matters yield to it when necessary.

Yet although Jesus was the Son of God, he **submitted to his earthly parents**.

How can ordinary people justify disobedience to their parents when the divine Son obeyed his?

We may fail to understand human speech because it is unclear. But we must not dismiss God's words as though they were unreliable.

What first seems difficult may later become plain.

Even the greatest and wisest can learn from this extraordinary Child that true greatness includes **knowing our proper position, fulfilling our calling and refusing pleasures that conflict with our duties**.
