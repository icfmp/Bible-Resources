# Jeremiah 29 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Two letters to the captives in Babylon; In the first, they are recommended to be patient and composed. | 1–19 |
| In the second, judgments are denounced against the false prophets who deceived them. | 20–32 |

## Live faithfully while waiting in Babylon (verses 1–7)

**God's written word is just as truly inspired as his spoken word**.

A zealous servant of the Lord will use every means available to help people who are **far away as well as nearby**.

Writing is especially useful for that purpose.

The invention of **printing** made it still easier for many people to receive the knowledge of God's word.

The letter showed that God **had not abandoned the exiles**, even though their disobedience had brought his correction upon them.

If they continued to fear the Lord, they could still live **comfortably in Babylon**.

In every condition of life, wisdom and duty require us not to abandon the comfort we **do** possess simply because we lack everything we **wish** to possess.

The exiles were instructed to seek **the welfare of the country where they were held captive**.

As long as Babylon's king protected them, they should live **quiet, peaceful, godly and honest lives** under his authority.

They must wait patiently for God to bring deliverance **at his appointed time**.

## God's good purposes are fulfilled in his time (verses 8–19)

Be careful when choosing which prophets to listen to.

Do not mistake people who share your preferences for prophets sent by God, or your own imaginings and dreams for **divine revelations**.

False prophets flatter people who enjoy being flattered.

Their hearers speak kindly to them so that **the prophets will tell their hearers what they want to hear**.

God promised the exiles would return after **seventy years**.

Henry notes that this period must be counted from **the first deportation, not the final one**.

The return would fulfill **God's good word** to them.

God's purposes are firm.

Human beings may not even know what they truly want, but the Lord is never uncertain.

At times we fear that all his plans for us are against us.

Yet for those who belong to him, even what appears evil will ultimately serve **their good**.

He gives them not the outcomes imagined by **their fears**, nor the wishes entertained by **their fantasies**, but **the future promised to their faith**—which is best for them.

When God pours out **a special spirit of prayer**, that is a hopeful sign that he is approaching a people in mercy.

His promises are intended **to encourage prayer**.

He has never told sincere seekers to seek him in vain.

But those who remained in Jerusalem would be destroyed despite the assurances given by false prophets.

The reason repeatedly stated also explains the final ruin of impenitent sinners:

**God called them, but they refused to hear his word**.

## False prophets are judged for their lies (verses 20–32)

Jeremiah announced judgment upon false prophets who had deceived the Jews in Babylon.

Lying is wrong.

Lying **to God's people in order to give them false confidence** is worse.

But pretending that one's own lies are **spoken by the God of truth** is worst of all.

These teachers flattered others in their sins because they could not condemn those sins without condemning **themselves**.

Even hidden wrongdoing is known to God.

A day will come when he brings **every concealed work of darkness into the light**.

**Shemaiah** urged the priests to persecute Jeremiah.

How deeply hardened people become when they imagine their possession of power gives them **the right to do harm**!

The exiles were already in terrible bondage because of their contempt toward God's messengers.

Yet under that discipline, some continued sinning even more.

**Suffering alone does not cure sin.** The Lord's grace must work with correction if it is to renew a heart.

Those who reject the blessing of God's word, as Shemaiah did, deserve to lose its benefit.

Many charges raised against active Christians in every age amount to little more than this:

**They urge people to attend to their true interests and duties, and to wait for God's promises in his appointed way.**
