# Jeremiah 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The glory of the Jews should be marred. | 1–11 |
| All ranks should suffer misery, An earnest exhortation to repentance. | 12–17 |
| An awful message to Jerusalem and its king. | 18–27 |

## The ruined linen belt (verses 1–11)

The prophets often taught through **visible signs**, and here God explains the sign himself (**Jeremiah 13:9–11**).

Israel had been like the belt that clung closely to its owner.

God had caused the people to cling to him through **the law he gave them, the prophets he sent and the blessings he bestowed**.

But through idolatry and sin, they had buried themselves in foreign soil and become entangled with other nations.

They were now so corrupted that, like the ruined belt, **they were no longer useful**.

If we are proud of **learning, power or outward privileges**, God is just in allowing them to wither.

Our minds need to awaken to guilt and danger.

Yet no warning will truly change us without **the Holy Spirit's influence**.

## The jars of judgment and a call to repent (verses 12–17)

Just as jars are made to hold wine, so the people's sins had made them **vessels prepared for divine wrath**.

They would be filled with judgments until those judgments led them to **destroy one another**.

Jeremiah begged them to **give glory to God** by confessing sin, humbling themselves in repentance and returning to his service.

If they refused, they would be carried into foreign lands, still enveloped in the darkness of **idolatry and wickedness**.

Anyone with a compassionate heart is moved by suffering, whether witnessed or only anticipated.

But a godly heart should grieve especially over **the afflictions of the Lord's flock**.

## No one can cleanse a sinful nature without God (verses 18–27)

The message was addressed to **King Jehoiakim and his queen**.

Their suffering would be great.

Why had it come upon them?

Because they had persisted stubbornly in sin.

People cannot change the natural color of their skin by their own efforts.

Henry uses that biblical image to express how **morally impossible it is for unrenewed sinners to reform themselves by their own strength**.

**Sin is the soul's corruption and disfigurement**.

We were formed in it and cannot free ourselves through our own power.

But **Almighty grace can accomplish what no human being can**.

Neither inherited corruption nor long-established habits of sin can prevent **God's Spirit, who creates new life**, from transforming a person.

The Lord asks Jerusalem whether she is determined to refuse cleansing.

If someone enslaved by sin feels that changing deeply rooted desires is as impossible as changing human nature, that person should **not despair**.

What is impossible for human beings is possible **with God**.

Let us seek help from **the One mighty to save**.
