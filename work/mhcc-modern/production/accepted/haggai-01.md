# Haggai 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Haggai reproves the Jews for neglecting the temple. | 1–11 |
| He promises God's assistance to them. | 12–15 |

## The people put off rebuilding God's house (verses 1–11)

Consider the sin of the Jews after they returned from captivity in Babylon.

People working for God may be driven away from their task by difficulty or opposition.

But when that happens, they should return to the work.

The Jews did not say they would **never** build the temple.

They said, **“Not yet.”**

In the same way, people rarely say they will never repent, reform their conduct or seek God.

They merely say, **“Not yet.”**

Thus the great purpose for which they were brought into this world remains unfinished.

We often misunderstand the difficulties that arise while doing our duty.

We treat them as excuses to stop, when God intended them to **test our courage and faith**.

The people neglected God's house because they wanted more time and money for their private affairs.

God made the consequences correspond to their sin.

They tried to avoid poverty by failing to rebuild the temple, but through that neglect he brought poverty upon them.

Many worthwhile tasks have been planned and then abandoned because people thought the time was not right.

Believers lose opportunities to do good.

Sinners delay attending to their souls until the opportunity is gone.

If we work only for perishable food and possessions, we risk losing the fruits of our labor.

But work performed **in the Lord for what lasts to eternal life** will not be wasted.

If we want to enjoy earthly blessings for as long as they are good for us, we must have **God as our Friend** (see also **Luke 12:33**).

When our earthly affairs are frustrated or disappoint us, we should ask whether we have neglected our duties toward God and our souls while caring more about our own interests than **Christ's interests**.

Some say they cannot afford to contribute to religious or charitable work while spending **ten times as much** on unnecessary comforts and improvements for themselves.

They misunderstand their own best interests.

They are careful to decorate their houses while **God's temple within their hearts lies in ruins**.

Everyone therefore needs to examine the heart honestly, considering their spiritual condition.

We will be held accountable for our sins.

Our duty is to do what God commands.

Yet people often inspect everyone else's conduct closely while remaining careless about their own.

Having neglected a duty in the past is no reason to keep neglecting it.

If the Lord takes pleasure in a work well done, we should take pleasure in doing it.

Let everyone who has postponed returning to God **return wholeheartedly while there is time**.

## God promises his presence as they resume building (verses 12–15)

The people returned to God by obeying his command.

When we listen to God's messengers, we should honor **the One who sent them**.

The word of the Lord succeeds when, by his grace, he stirs people's hearts to obey it.

**God's power makes us willing** to do his will.

When he has work to accomplish, he will find people fit for it or prepare them himself.

Each person contributed according to ability, acting out of regard for the Lord as their God.

Those who have wasted time should work to redeem it.

The longer we have delayed through folly, the more urgently we ought to begin.

God met their renewed obedience with mercy.

Those who work for him may take courage from the promise **that he is with them**.

If God is for us, who can be against us?

Let this promise stir us to diligence.
