# Psalm 56 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David seeks mercy from God, amidst the malice of his enemies. | 1–7 |
| He rests his faith on God's promises, and declares his obligation to praise him for mercies. | 8–13 |

## David seeks God's mercy (verses 1–7)

**“Be merciful to me, O God.”**

This request includes every blessing we seek at the throne of grace. If God grants us mercy, nothing further is needed to make us truly happy.

It also expresses the best ground for our prayers: **not our merit, but God's free and abundant mercy**.

Surrounded by danger and difficulties, we may flee toward that mercy and trust it.

David's enemies were too strong for him unless God intervened.

He resolved to make God's promises the subject of praise. We have good reason to do the same.

We should not place our trust in **human strength** when it is on our side, nor become terrified by that strength when directed against us.

The sin of wicked people will never make them truly secure.

Who knows how powerful God's righteous judgment can be or how far it will reach?

## God's care for every sorrow (verses 8–13)

The prolonged and severe trials endured by many faithful believers should teach us patience under our smaller difficulties.

Yet we easily complain and become discouraged over little sorrows.

David comforted himself with the knowledge that God saw every injury and every grief.

God has **a bottle and a book for his people's tears**—tears shed over sin as well as over suffering.

He notices them with tenderness.

Every genuine believer can say, **“The Lord is my helper; I will not fear what anyone can do to me.”**

Human power is limited to what God allows.

David's vows were not an oppressive burden. They marked him as God's servant, restrained him from harmful actions and guided him in duty.

Promises of thanksgiving rightly accompany prayers for mercy.

When God saves us from committing sin or removes its guilt through pardon, he has delivered our souls from death, **the wages of sin**.

The good work God begins he will carry through to completion.

David hoped to be kept even from **the appearance of evil**.

We should seek rescue from sin and outward difficulty so that we may **serve God more faithfully and without fear**.

When his grace has saved us from spiritual death, he will bring us to heaven, where we will walk before him in light forever.
