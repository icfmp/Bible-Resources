## The disciples gather grain on the Sabbath (verses 1–8)

When the disciples plucked grain, they acted within God's law (**Deuteronomy 23:25**). They had little food and were content with that modest provision.

The Pharisees did not object because the grain belonged to another person; they objected because it was the Sabbath.

Christ came to free his followers from the Pharisees' **unscriptural human rules**, not merely their moral corruption, and he defended what the disciples did.

The strongest person's desires must not be indulged at the expense of the weakest person's genuine needs.

Work that is **necessary** is lawful on the Sabbath. The day's rest should **promote**, not prevent, worship.

Reasonable food and care for health are proper. But forcing servants to stay home and turning a household into a scene of haste and disorder in order to prepare an indulgent feast for visitors is a very different matter.

Henry explicitly reproves such conduct, even among people who profess faith.

The Sabbath was instituted **for human good** (**Deuteronomy 5:14**). No law should be interpreted in a way that defeats its own purpose.

Christ is **Lord of the Sabbath**. Both the day and the appropriate work of that day belong to him.

## Jesus heals a withered hand (verses 9–13)

Works of mercy are lawful and fitting on the Lord's day.

There are useful acts beyond formal worship: **visiting the sick, feeding the poor, helping those needing urgent assistance and teaching young people to care for their souls**.

Such things must come from love and generosity, with humility and self-denial, and are acceptable to God (**Genesis 4:7**, as cited in this secondary witness).

As with other miracles, this healing also has a spiritual meaning.

By nature our hands are spiritually withered; we cannot produce good works through our own power.

Christ alone gives life to the dead soul.

He works within us **both the desire and the ability to obey**.

His command comes with the promise of grace through his word.

## The Pharisees plot against Jesus (verses 14–21)

The Pharisees looked for a charge that would allow them to put Jesus to death.

Knowing their plan and that his appointed time had not yet come, Christ withdrew.

His character and conduct fulfilled the prophet's description with extraordinary exactness.

We may confidently entrust ourselves to so kind and faithful a Friend.

He does not **break the bruised reed** but strengthens it.

He does not **extinguish the weakly smoking wick** but encourages its flame.

Let us abandon quarrelsome and angry argument and receive one another as Christ has received us.

Encouraged by his gentleness, let us pray that his Spirit will rest upon us and help us follow his example.

## Jesus delivers a man oppressed by a demon (verses 22–30)

A soul held captive by Satan is spiritually **blind** to God's truth and **unable to speak properly in prayer**.

Through unbelief the devil closes the eyes; through temptation he keeps the lips from praying.

The more people admired Christ's miracle, the more urgently the Pharisees tried to discredit him.

But if Satan were helping Jesus to expel demons, his own kingdom would be fighting itself and could not stand.

And if the Pharisees claimed Jesus acted through the devil's prince, they had no valid reason to say their own followers' exorcisms used a different power.

There are two great opposed interests in the world.

Where the **Holy Spirit casts out evil through conversion**, leading sinners to faith and obedience, God's kingdom has come.

Anyone who refuses to welcome or assist this gracious change opposes Christ.

## Blasphemy against the Holy Spirit (verses 31 and 32)

Jesus first gives a gracious assurance that **all kinds of sin may be pardoned on gospel terms**.

He also teaches his followers to be willing to forgive insulting words directed against themselves.

Humble, conscientious believers sometimes fear that they have committed the unforgivable sin.

Yet people who have approached that sin most closely seldom feel troubled about it.

**People who sincerely repent and believe the gospel have not committed the unpardonable sin.**

Repentance and faith are special gifts of God. He would not give them to someone whom he had absolutely determined never to pardon.

Even the fear of having committed this sin can be a hopeful sign that the fearful person has not committed it.

A trembling, contrite sinner has this witness within.

## Words reveal what fills the heart (verses 33–37)

Our speech reveals our character, just as an accent may reveal the country we come from.

**The heart is the spring; our words are the streams.**

If the source is polluted, its waters will also be polluted.

Only **the grace of God**, like salt used to heal a spring, can renew the heart, guide the tongue and cleanse corrupt conversation.

A wicked person's heart holds a store of evil desires.

From this store come words and deeds that dishonor God and injure neighbors.

We should continually watch ourselves, ensuring that our speech suits the character of a Christian.

## The demand for a sign and the danger of empty reform (verses 38–45)

Christ welcomes holy desires and faithful prayers.

But those who ask with wrong motives may ask and receive nothing.

God gave signs to people such as **Abraham and Gideon** who wanted their faith strengthened, while refusing signs demanded as an excuse for unbelief.

The great proof that Christ is Messiah is **his resurrection from the dead by his own power**, the sign of Jonah.

As Jonah was preserved for three days and nights in the great fish and then emerged alive, so Jesus would be in the grave and then rise.

The repentant Ninevites would shame those who refused repentance.

The queen of Sheba would shame those who refused to believe Christ, even though she had less light and greater difficulties.

Jesus' warning about the returning evil spirit pictures the condition of the **Jewish church and nation**.

It also applies to everyone who experiences some outward moral reform **without genuine conversion**.

An unclean spirit may leave a person's life temporarily.

If Christ does not dwell in the heart to keep it out, it finds a place cleaned by outward reform but still ready to obey sinful suggestions.

The person may become a stronger enemy of truth than before.

Every heart is a dwelling place of evil unless through faith in Christ it becomes **a temple of the Holy Spirit**.

## Jesus identifies his true family (verses 46–50)

Christ taught clearly, familiarly and in ways his listeners could understand.

His mother and brothers were outside asking to speak with him, when it would have been better for them to be inside listening to his teaching.

Those closest to opportunities for grace often neglect them most, assuming they will always have another chance.

Yet tomorrow is not ours to command.

Friends and relatives may unintentionally interrupt our work or distract us with earthly concerns when our souls need attention.

Jesus was fully devoted to the work his Father gave him. Lesser duties did not lead him to neglect the greater.

This does **not** authorize disrespect toward parents or unkindness to relatives under the pretext of religion.

It means that lesser obligations must yield when a higher duty is presently required.

Let us cling to Christ and regard every genuine Christian, whatever that person's worldly station, as **the brother, sister or mother of the Lord of glory**.

Love, respect and serve them for his sake and after his example.
