# Joshua 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Joshua recalls God's goodness (verses 1–14)

We should never consider our work for God finished until our lives are finished.

If God gives us more years than we expected, as he gave Joshua, it is because we still have work to do.

Anyone seeking to have the mind of Jesus Christ should be glad to give a final testimony to the Savior's goodness.

We should tell everyone around us how deeply God's undeserved kindness obligates us to him.

Israel gathered in solemn worship.

Joshua addressed the people in God's name and as his messenger.

His address had **two parts: teaching and application**.

In the first, Joshua recalled the great things God had done for Israel and for the generations before them.

Then he applied that history of mercy by urging the people to fear and serve the Lord out of gratitude, so that his favor might continue among them.

## Joshua calls Israel to choose and renew the covenant (verses 15–28)

God's people must serve him with willing minds.

**Love is the only genuine principle** from which acceptable service to God can spring.

The Father seeks worshipers who worship in spirit and truth.

Our natural, fleshly minds are hostile to God and cannot perform such spiritual worship.

We therefore **must be born again**.

Many people are content with outward religious forms, which they regard as imposed duties rather than a delight.

Joshua told the people to choose whom they would serve.

He did not mean that serving God was an optional matter of indifference.

He set the issue clearly before them and declared his own decision, whatever others might do.

Those traveling toward heaven must be willing to go against the current.

They must not simply follow the majority, but follow the example of the best.

No one can live faithfully in any position without serious attention to religious duties in family life.

Israel responded, “We also will serve the Lord.”

Joshua's example had a powerful influence because he had been such a blessing to the people.

People with great influence can do much good when they are zealous for true faith.

Joshua led them to express a sincere determination to cling to God.

But they needed to abandon every confidence in their own strength.

Without God's help, their resolutions would fail.

After they had deliberately chosen to serve God, Joshua bound them to that decision through a solemn covenant.

He erected a memorial.

In this moving way, he took his final leave of them.

If they later perished through rebellion, they would bear responsibility for their own blood.

The house of God, the Lord's table and even the walls and trees before which we have made solemn promises could testify against us if we turn away from him.

Yet we may trust God to put his reverence within our hearts so that we will not abandon him.

Only God can grant saving grace.

Nevertheless, he blesses our efforts to encourage others to serve him.

## Joshua dies; Joseph's bones are buried (verses 29–33)

Joseph died in Egypt but commanded that his bones should not finally remain there.

He wanted them brought to the promised land once Israel had entered its rest.

Joshua also died and was buried, as was Eleazar the high priest.

Even the most useful servants of God eventually die, having served their generation according to his will.

One after another, they fall asleep and their bodies decay.

But Jesus, who completed his earthly life with a saving work far greater than that of Joshua or Joseph, **rose from the dead and did not undergo corruption**.

Those whom the Lord has redeemed will inherit the kingdom prepared for them from the foundation of the world.

In amazement at Jesus' grace, they will declare:

**To him who loved us and washed us from our sins by his own blood, and made us kings and priests to God his Father—to him be glory and dominion forever and ever. Amen.**
