## The Sabbath and appointed times (verses 1–3)

This chapter establishes holy times, including many already mentioned elsewhere.

The annual festivals brought the whole nation together at the sanctuary.

Yet even these great gatherings were no reason to give them more importance than the Sabbath.

On the Sabbath, the people were to cease from ordinary worldly work.

The rest pictured freedom from sin and the rest believers find in God.

The Lord's Sabbaths were to be observed faithfully not only in public worship but also at home, by individual families and by families gathered together.

Keeping God's Sabbath in the home would make it beautiful, strong and secure. It would help sanctify, strengthen and dignify family life.

## Passover and the firstfruits (verses 4–14)

The Passover feast lasted seven days.

These were not meant to be idle days filled with games, as Henry says many people who call themselves Christians spend their religious holidays.

Offerings were made to God, and the people were to use the time for prayer, praise and godly reflection.

The sheaf of firstfruits pointed to our Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead as the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.

Henry notes that Jesus rose on the very day when this offering of firstfruits was made.

The law also teaches us to honor the Lord with our possessions and the firstfruits of all our increase (Proverbs 3:9).

Israel could not eat the new grain before first offering God's portion.

We should likewise begin with God: at the beginning of every day, every meal, every undertaking and every business.

Seek God's kingdom first.

## Pentecost and generosity toward the poor (verses 15–22)

The Feast of Weeks recalled the giving of the law fifty days after Israel departed from Egypt.

It also pointed ahead to the coming of the Holy Spirit fifty days after Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us.

On that day, the apostles presented to God the firstfruits of the Christian church.

The command concerning Pentecost was joined to a repeated instruction to leave gleanings in the fields for the poor.

People who truly recognize God's mercy to them will show mercy to the poor without resentment.

## Trumpets and the Day of Atonement (verses 23–32)

The trumpet blasts pictured gospel preaching, summoning people to turn from sin and receive Christ's salvation.

That salvation was pictured by the Day of Atonement.

The trumpet also called the people to rejoice in God and to live as strangers and pilgrims in the world, a lesson pictured by the Feast of Tabernacles later in the same month.

At the beginning of the year, this sound urged the people to wake from spiritual laziness, examine their lives and change their ways.

The Day of Atonement followed nine days afterward.

They were given time to prepare through sincere and serious repentance so that the day might truly bring them atonement.

Humbling ourselves over sin and making peace with God require the fullest attention of both heart and mind.

On that day the Lord spoke peace to his people.

Therefore they were to set aside ordinary work so that they could hear his voice of comfort and joy more clearly.

## The Feast of Tabernacles (verses 33–44)

During this feast, the people remembered their life in temporary shelters during the wilderness journey.

They also remembered how their ancestors had lived in tents in Canaan.

These memories reminded them of their origins and their deliverance.

The festival may also have pictured Christ dwelling on earth in human nature.

It certainly pictured the believer's life here: living as a stranger and pilgrim, with home and heart above, where the Savior is.

After seven days in temporary shelters, people would value the comfort of their own houses more deeply.

Those who live in ease and plenty benefit from occasionally learning what hardship feels like.

Harvest joy should make us rejoice still more in God.

Everything on earth belongs to him.

Whatever benefits we enjoy should therefore bring him glory, especially when his mercy has brought a task or season to completion.

These festivals were appointed **in addition to** the ordinary Sabbaths and voluntary offerings.

Extraordinary acts of service never excuse us from our regular duties.
