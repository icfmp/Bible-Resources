# 1 Corinthians 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The plain manner in which the apostle preached Christ crucified. | 1–5 |
| The wisdom contained in this doctrine. | 6–9 |
| It cannot be duly known but by the Holy Spirit. | 10–16 |

## Paul's plain preaching of Christ crucified (verses 1–5)

**Jesus Christ—his person, offices and sufferings—is the heart of the gospel**.

He should be the main subject of every gospel minister's preaching.

This does **not** mean leaving out other parts of God's revealed truth and will.

Paul proclaimed **the whole counsel of God**.

Few people understand the fear and trembling experienced by faithful ministers who recognize their own weakness.

They know how inadequate their abilities are and feel their responsibility deeply.

When ministers simply proclaim **Christ crucified**, any genuine saving success must come from **God's power accompanying his word**.

Through that power, sinners believe and receive salvation.

## God's deep wisdom revealed in Christ (verses 6–9)

Those who receive the gospel as divine truth and examine it under the Holy Spirit's light see more than the outward historical facts about Jesus' crucifixion.

They recognize **God's deep and wonderful plan of wisdom**.

This is the mystery now revealed to the saints (**Colossians 1:26**).

It had been hidden from the pagan world and only dimly pictured by Old Testament types and distant prophecies.

Now **the Spirit of God** has disclosed it.

Jesus is **the Lord of glory**, a title too exalted for any merely created being.

Many people would not oppose God's saving work if they truly understood his wisdom in redemption.

God has prepared blessings for those who love and wait for him that bodily senses cannot discover and human teaching cannot fully convey.

We cannot yet imagine their greatness.

We must receive these promises as they are revealed to us **in Scripture**.

## The Holy Spirit enables spiritual understanding (verses 10–16)

God reveals true wisdom through **his Holy Spirit**.

This helps demonstrate the divine authority of Scripture (**2 Peter 1:21**).

It also bears witness to **the deity of the Holy Spirit**.

He knows and searches all things, even **the depths of God**.

No one can know God's inner purposes except his Spirit, who is **one with the Father and the Son** and reveals divine mysteries to the church.

Henry sees here testimony to **the Holy Spirit's true deity and distinct personhood**.

The apostles were not governed by worldly principles.

They received revelation of these truths through God's Spirit, together with the Spirit's saving impact upon them.

They communicated them in **plain language taught by the Holy Spirit**, instead of relying upon artificial rhetoric or manipulative human eloquence.

The natural person, even when regarded by the world as wise, does not receive **the things of God's Spirit**.

Pride in worldly reasoning can oppose spiritual truth as strongly as the crudest sensuality.

A mind sanctified by God recognizes the beauty of holiness.

This does **not** destroy its ability to reason about ordinary natural affairs.

But the unregenerate person does not understand the principles, pleasures and activity of divine life.

Only the spiritual person receives from God the knowledge of his saving will.

How little people understand of God's mind through merely natural powers!

The apostles received the Spirit's help to disclose it.

**The mind of Christ, and God's mind as revealed in Christ, are made known in the Holy Scriptures**.

Christians are privileged to receive that revelation through the Spirit, experience his sanctifying work within and bear good fruit in life.
