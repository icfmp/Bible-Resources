## Jesus teaches his disciples (verses 1 and 2)

No one can find true happiness in this life or in the next without seeking it **from Christ, according to the teaching of his word**.

He showed his listeners which evils they should hate and which good things they should eagerly seek and practice.

## The eight blessings (verses 3–12)

Our Savior described **eight kinds of blessed people**.

Together, they show the principal graces that should characterize Christians.

**First, the poor in spirit are blessed.**

Such people respond humbly to humble circumstances.

They are lowly in their own estimation.

They recognize their spiritual need, grieve over their guilt and eagerly desire **a Redeemer**.

The kingdom of grace belongs to them now, and the kingdom of glory awaits them.

**Second, those who mourn are blessed.**

This is godly sorrow that produces sincere repentance, watchfulness, humility and continual dependence on **God's mercy through Jesus Christ**.

It also leads people to seek the Holy Spirit's continuing work of cleansing away sin.

Heaven is the Lord's mountain of joy, but our path toward it passes through **a valley of tears**.

God himself will comfort those who mourn in this way.

**Third, the meek are blessed.**

Meek people quietly submit to God, bear insults without retaliation and respond gently or remain silent when appropriate.

Through patience they maintain possession of **their own souls**, even when they can scarcely hold on to anything else.

Meekness often promotes comfort, safety and even outward well-being in this life.

**Fourth, those who hunger and thirst for righteousness are blessed.**

Here righteousness represents all spiritual blessings.

These blessings were purchased through **Christ's righteousness** and are assured by God's faithfulness.

We should desire them as earnestly as hungry and thirsty people desire food and water.

Not every desire for grace is itself a work of grace.

But this kind of spiritual longing is **created by God**, who will not abandon the work of his hands.

**Fifth, the merciful are blessed.**

We must not merely endure our own suffering patiently.

We should do everything possible to relieve other people's misery.

We must care for their **souls** as well as their circumstances, pitying those trapped in sin and seeking to rescue them like **burning brands pulled from the fire**.

**Sixth, the pure in heart are blessed, for they will see God.**

Holiness and happiness are united in this blessing.

Our hearts must be purified **through faith** and reserved for the Lord.

Let us pray, **“Create a clean heart in me, O God.”**

Only the pure are able to see God and rejoice in his holiness.

Heaven would not be happiness to an impure heart.

As God cannot approve sin, those determined to remain in sin cannot delight in his purity.

**Seventh, the peacemakers are blessed.**

They love peace, seek it and work to remain quiet and respectful.

They protect peace from being broken and seek to restore it when it has been lost.

If those who make peace are blessed, **woe to those who break it**.

**Eighth, those who are persecuted for righteousness are blessed.**

This blessing is especially characteristic of Christianity and receives more explanation than the others.

There is nothing about suffering that **earns merit before God**.

Yet God will ensure that those who lose anything for his sake—even life itself—will not finally lose by serving him.

How different Christ's teaching is from the world's!

The world admires the proud, fashionable, rich, powerful and victorious.

May the Lord show us mercy, acknowledge us as his children and bring us into his kingdom.

With that hope, we can face humble or painful circumstances with joy.

## Salt and light (verses 13–16)

Humanity, lying in ignorance and evil, resembled a vast heap ready to decay.

Christ sent his disciples to act as **salt**, preserving and seasoning the world through their teaching and way of life.

If they fail in that calling, they become like salt that has lost its flavor.

A person may profess Christ while remaining without grace.

No other message or means can make that person useful apart from true saving change.

Our light must shine through **good works visible to other people**.

What passes privately between God and our souls should remain private.

But conduct that is naturally public should match our Christian profession and be worthy of respect.

Our goal must always be **God's glory**, not our own reputation.

## Christ fulfills and confirms God's law (verses 17–20)

No one should imagine that Christ allows his people to take **God's holy law** lightly.

No sinner participates in Christ's **justifying righteousness** without repentance.

The mercy announced by the gospel makes a believer more deeply aware of sin, not less.

God's law becomes the Christian's guide for duty, in which the believer delights.

Anyone who deliberately permits disobedience while claiming to follow Christ—or teaches others to do so—cannot be a genuine disciple, regardless of public status.

Everyone entering God's kingdom needs **Christ's righteousness imputed through faith alone**.

But **a renewed heart** also produces a comprehensive change in attitudes and actions.

## Anger and reconciliation: the sixth commandment (verses 21–26)

Some Jewish teachers had reduced the command against murder to a prohibition of **the outward act of killing**.

They had neglected its spiritual meaning.

Christ revealed the command's true scope, by which we will be judged and should therefore live.

**Reckless anger is murder in the heart.**

Our brother or neighbor includes every human being, however lowly their station, because all people were made of **one blood**.

**“Raca”** expresses contempt flowing from pride.

**“You fool”**, used spitefully, expresses hatred.

Malicious slander and condemnation are like slow, secret poison.

Christ warns that what people dismiss as trivial will be brought before God's judgment.

We must protect Christian love and seek peace with everyone.

If we have injured another person, we should confess wrongdoing, humble ourselves and **make or offer appropriate amends**, whether the harm was done by speech or conduct.

We should do so promptly.

Unreconciled hostility hinders our fitness to participate in holy worship.

Preparing for worship provides a fitting opportunity for **self-examination**.

This also teaches our need to be reconciled **to God through Christ**.

While we live, we are still on our way to his judgment seat.

After death it will be too late.

Because life is uncertain and the matter is so serious, we must seek peace with God without delay.

## Purity and fidelity: the seventh commandment (verses 27–32)

Victory over corrupt inward desires may require painful struggle.

Yet **it must be pursued**.

Every spiritual resource has been given to save us **from** sin, not to keep us comfortable **in** it.

Every sense and ability should be guarded against occasions of wrongdoing.

Those who lead others toward temptation, including through deliberately provocative appearance or other behavior, bear responsibility for their influence.

People accept painful medical treatments to preserve bodily life.

What should they be willing to endure to protect **the eternal welfare of their souls**?

God's commands are expressions of tender mercy.

His Spirit supplies the grace and consolation needed to obey them.

## Truthfulness and oaths: the third commandment (verses 33–37)

Henry does **not** understand Christ to forbid every solemn oath.

Oaths taken in court or on other proper occasions may be lawful **when made reverently and truthfully**.

But oaths used needlessly in ordinary conversation are sinful.

So are verbal formulas intended to escape the responsibility of swearing truthfully while still pretending to invoke God.

The more dishonest people become, the less an oath restrains them.

The more truthful people become, the less they need oaths to be believed.

Christ does not prescribe exact words to use in every affirmation or denial.

He requires **consistent truthfulness**, making unnecessary oaths needless.

## Refusing retaliation (verses 38–42)

We should be willing to endure injuries that can rightly be borne **for the sake of peace**, entrusting our affairs to the Lord.

Christians should avoid needless quarrels and fighting.

Someone may object that human nature cannot endure an insult without answering it.

But **flesh and blood cannot inherit God's kingdom**.

Those governed by right principles will enjoy more peace and comfort than those ruled by offended pride.

## Love even your enemies (verses 43–48)

Some Jewish teachers interpreted “neighbor” as referring only to people of their own **nation, religion or group**, whom they already regarded as friends.

Jesus commands genuine kindness toward **everyone**.

We should especially care for other people's souls.

We must **pray for our enemies**.

Many people return kindness for kindness.

Christ calls his disciples to **return good for evil**.

That reveals a nobler principle than the ordinary conduct of the world.

Others greet and embrace only people from their own party or opinion.

We must not limit our respect so narrowly.

Christians ought to desire and pursue **maturity and completeness in grace and holiness**, following the example of the heavenly Father (**1 Peter 1:15–16**).

More is rightly expected from Christ's followers than from others.

Let us ask God to enable us to show, through our lives, that we truly are his children.
