# 1 Samuel 9 — Modern English

## Saul searches for his father's donkeys (verses 1–10)

Saul readily went to look for his father's lost donkeys.

His willingness to obey his father was worthy of praise.

His servant suggested that, since they were near **Ramah**, they should consult Samuel.

Wherever we go, we should make good use of opportunities to learn from wise and godly people.

Many would seek advice from a servant of God if one happened to be nearby, but would never go out of their way to obtain spiritual wisdom.

People feel losses of money or property keenly and make great efforts to recover them.

Yet how little effort they devote to the salvation of their souls—and how quickly they grow weary of that search!

If ministers could guarantee people a profitable business or wealth, their advice would be sought more eagerly than it is now.

They teach people how to escape eternal misery and receive eternal life, yet many prefer to learn their **fortune rather than their duty**.

Samuel did not need the visitors' payment.

He would not have refused advice merely because they brought no gift.

The offering expressed respect for his office and followed the custom of presenting gifts to people in authority.

## God reveals Saul's arrival to Samuel (verses 11–17)

Even the young female servants of the town could tell Saul's party where to find the prophet.

They knew about the coming sacrifice and that Samuel would have to attend it.

There is real benefit in living among people who value God and his worship.

We should always be ready to help those seeking one of God's messengers.

Although the Lord had granted Israel's request for a king out of displeasure, he nevertheless chose a man who could lead them and deliver them from the Philistines.

God acted graciously in hearing his people's cry.

## Samuel receives Saul (verses 18–27)

Samuel was not jealous of Saul or hostile toward him.

Instead, he was the first to honor him.

Samuel spoke with Saul on the flat rooftop that evening and again early the next morning.

Henry believes Samuel likely convinced Saul that God had selected him to govern Israel and made clear his own willingness to step aside.

How different God's purposes for us may be from our own plans!

Saul may be the only person who literally went looking for donkeys and found a kingdom.

But many others have traveled or moved house seeking wealth or pleasure and unexpectedly encountered people through whom God led them toward salvation.

Someone seemed to know the secrets of their lives and hearts, and they began to pay serious attention to the Lord's word.

If this has happened to us, we should not grieve that our original earthly plans failed.

God has given us—or is preparing to give us—something far better.
