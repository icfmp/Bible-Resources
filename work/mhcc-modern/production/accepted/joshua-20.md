# Joshua 20 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The law concerning the cities of refuge. | 1–6 |

## Israel appoints the cities of refuge (verses 1–6)

Once the Israelites had settled in the inheritance promised them, God reminded them to set apart cities of refuge.

The purpose and symbolic meaning of those cities had already been explained in Numbers 35 and Deuteronomy 19.

God's spiritual Israel has, and will continue to have, not only rest in **Christ and heaven**, but also a refuge where people can find safety.

These cities pictured the relief the gospel provides for repentant sinners.

In our Lord Jesus Christ, those who flee to him find protection from the law's curse and God's wrath (Hebrews 6:18).

## The six refuge cities (verses 7–9)

The cities on both sides of the Jordan were positioned so that, from any part of the country, a person could reach one within half a day's journey.

God is always a refuge near at hand.

All six were Levite cities.

This arrangement was a kindness to fugitives. Someone who could not go up to the Lord's house would nevertheless have God's servants nearby to teach, pray and help make up for the lack of regular public worship.

Some interpreters also find spiritual meaning in each city's name:

**Kedesh** means *holy*: our Refuge is the holy Jesus.

**Shechem** means *shoulder*: the government rests on his shoulder.

**Hebron** means *fellowship*: believers are brought into fellowship with Jesus Christ our Lord.

**Bezer** means *fortification*: he is a stronghold for everyone who trusts in him.

**Ramoth** means *high* or *exalted*: God has exalted Christ at his right hand.

**Golan** means *joy* or *exultation*: in him all the saints are justified and will rejoice in his glory.
