# Jeremiah 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## The potter, the clay and God's righteous rule (verses 1–10)

As Jeremiah watched the potter at work, God impressed **two great truths** upon his mind.

**First**, the Lord possesses the authority and power **to shape and dispose of kingdoms and nations as he chooses**.

He may deal with us according to his wisdom.

For clay to argue with the potter would be absurd—and so is our disputing against God's right to rule.

**Second**, God always acts according to **settled principles of goodness and justice**.

When judgment is approaching, we may be sure that sin has brought it upon us.

But **sincere conversion and turning away from sin** can prevent the threatened judgment—whether for **individuals, families or nations**.

## Sinful liberty is the worst slavery (verses 11–17)

Sinners call unrestrained living **freedom**.

Yet slavery to sinful desires is **the most dreadful form of slavery**.

Judah had abandoned God for idols.

A thirsty person who reaches a cool and refreshing stream would not ordinarily abandon that reliable water for an uncertain supply elsewhere.

But Israel abandoned **the ancient paths established by God's law**.

Instead of walking along the safe highway, the people chose a path where they were bound to stumble.

Such is the way of idolatry and wrongdoing.

It left them miserable and their land desolate.

Earthly troubles can be endured if God **continues to favor us through them**.

But if he is displeased and refuses his help, we are undone.

Multitudes turn from **the Lord and his Christ** and leave the ancient paths to follow religious ways invented by themselves.

What will they do **on the day of judgment**?

## Jeremiah's enemies plot against him (verses 18–23)

When Jeremiah called the people to repent, they plotted against him instead of obeying.

Sinners treat **the great Intercessor** in a similar way.

They speak against Christ and in that sense **crucify him afresh**, even while **his blood pleads on their behalf in heaven**.

But the prophet had performed the duty God gave him toward the people.

That consciousness of duty would be **a source of rejoicing in a time of trouble**.
