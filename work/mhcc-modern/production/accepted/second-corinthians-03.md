# 2 Corinthians 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The preference of the gospel to the law given by Moses. | 1–11 |
| The preaching of the apostle was suitable to the excellency and evidence of the gospel, through the power of the Holy Ghost. | 12–18 |

## The gospel brings greater glory than the ministry of the law (verses 1–11)

Humble, spiritually minded people dislike even the appearance of praising themselves or seeking human applause.

Faithful ministers find their greatest joy and the best testimony to their work in the **changed hearts and lives of the people they serve**.

The Corinthians themselves were evidence of Christ's work through Paul's ministry.

Christ's law was written upon their hearts, and his love had been poured into them.

This message was not engraved on stone tablets, as the law given through Moses had been.

It was written on **living, softened human hearts** (Ezekiel 36:26).

Henry draws an important distinction: the hearts are **fleshy**, meaning soft and receptive, not **fleshly**, meaning governed by sinful appetites.

The **Holy Spirit's creative and renewing power** softened those hearts so that they could receive Christ's law.

All the glory belongs to God.

Our entire dependence is upon him, so **all the praise** must be his as well.

The **letter kills**, but the **Spirit gives life**.

The written law exposes sin and announces its curse and death.

But merely possessing the outward words or forms of the **gospel** also leaves a person unchanged when the Holy Spirit does not give life through them.

The old covenant ministry revealed human guilt and God's just anger.

The gospel reveals grace and **Emmanuel, God with us**.

It shows the righteousness God provides through faith and explains the mercy and forgiveness available **through Jesus Christ**, leading to eternal life.

The glory of this gospel far exceeds that of the Mosaic dispensation, as a brighter light outshines a lesser one.

But even the New Testament becomes merely a **dead letter** when treated only as a religious system or external form, without dependence on the Spirit's power to awaken and transform the soul.

## The veil is removed and believers are transformed (verses 12–18)

Ministers of the gospel should speak **plainly and clearly**.

Believers living under the old covenant saw only partial and passing glimpses of the Savior's glory.

Unbelievers often stopped at the visible ceremonies and did not look beyond them.

But the gospel openly calls us to **believe, love and obey**.

The message of **Christ crucified** has been stated as clearly as human language can express it.

Under the law, a veil lay upon people's hearts.

That veil is removed by the biblical teaching concerning Christ when God converts a person to himself.

The ignorance that once obscured the heart is lifted.

Those who believe and enjoy the gospel receive wonderful freedom: they can now delight in walking according to God's commandments.

They see the glory of the Lord without the former veil.

Christians should cherish these privileges and make full use of them.

But we must not be satisfied with knowing doctrines without experiencing **the transforming work of the Spirit**.

His work makes us increasingly like the character and purpose of Christ's glorious gospel and unites us more closely to our Lord and Savior.

Through Scripture we behold Christ as **in a mirror**.

Just as reflected light causes a face to shine, looking upon the Lord by faith changes believers so that his glory begins to shine in their lives.
