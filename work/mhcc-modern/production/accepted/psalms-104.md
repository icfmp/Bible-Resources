# Psalm 104 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's majesty in the heavens, The creation of the sea, and the dry land. | 1–9 |
| His provision for all creatures. | 10–18 |
| The regular course of day and night, and God's sovereign power over all the creatures. | 19–30 |
| A resolution to continue praising God. | 31–35 |

## The glory of the Creator (verses 1–9)

Everything we see calls us to praise and bless the Lord, who is great.

His **eternal power and divine nature** are evident in what he has made.

God is light, and there is no darkness in him.

**The Lord Jesus, the beloved Son, is the Light of the world**.

## God's provision for every creature (verses 10–18)

As we consider the provision made for every living creature, we should notice how their existence offers **natural praise to God**.

Yet human beings, so forgetful and ungrateful, enjoy the largest share of their Creator's kindness.

Do not overlook the **spiritual blessings** pictured by these provisions: the fruitfulness of the church through grace, **the bread of eternal life**, **the cup of salvation**, and **the oil of gladness**.

If God provides for creatures of lesser strength and understanding, surely he will remain a refuge to his people.

## Day, night, work, resurrection and Providence (verses 19–30)

Praise God for the continuing pattern of day and night.

People who wait for darkness in order to commit wicked acts resemble wild animals that emerge at night, keeping company with the **fruitless works of darkness**.

God hears the needs expressed by creatures according to their nature, including ravenous beasts.

How much more will he graciously understand the language of his own people, even **weak, broken groanings that cannot be put into words**?

Each day's work should be performed during the time given for it.

We should take up our responsibilities each morning and continue until evening.

Rest will come when night arrives, when no one can work.

The psalmist marvels at God's creation.

Human art often reveals roughness when examined closely.

But God's natural works appear even more detailed and orderly the closer we look.

Everything is **made in wisdom**, answering the purpose for which it was created.

Each spring suggests **the resurrection**, when new life seems to rise out of the ruin of the old world.

But human beings live beyond bodily death.

When God takes away breath, **the soul enters another condition**, and the body will rise again either to **glory or misery**.

May the Lord send his Spirit to **make our souls new in holiness**.

## Creation's fading glory and the greater work of redemption (verses 31–35)

Human glory fades, while **God's glory endures forever**.

Creatures change, but their Creator never changes.

If contemplating the beauty of creation brings such delight to the soul, how much more wonderful is **the work of redemption** to an enlightened mind!

Only through that work can a sinner find sound grounds for **confidence and joy in God**.

He sustains everything with pleasure, governs everything and rejoices in his works.

May our souls, transformed by grace, keep reflecting upon him and praising his name.
