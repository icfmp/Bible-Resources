# Mark 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ despised in his own country. | 1–6 |
| The apostles sent forth. | 7–13 |
| John the Baptist put to death. | 14–29 |
| The apostles return, Five thousand fed by a miracle. | 30–44 |
| Christ walks on the sea, He heals those that touch him. | 45–56 |

## Jesus is rejected in his hometown (verses 1–6)

Jesus' neighbors tried to turn others against him by calling him **the carpenter**.

Henry judges it probable that Jesus had worked in that trade alongside his earthly father.

By doing so, Christ honored **craftspeople and all who earn their living through honest manual labor**.

His followers should be content with knowing that they do good, even when they receive no praise.

How much the inhabitants of Nazareth lost through stubborn prejudice against Jesus!

May divine grace deliver us from unbelief that turns Christ into a source of judgment rather than life.

Following his example, let us teach the gospel's way of salvation even to **people in poor cottages and humble rural communities**.

## Jesus sends the apostles to preach (verses 7–13)

The apostles knew their weakness and expected no worldly advantage.

Yet they obeyed their Master and went forward depending on **his strength**.

They did not entertain people with interesting but useless curiosities.

They told listeners to **repent of sin and turn to God**.

Christ's servants may hope that their preaching will bring many people from spiritual darkness to God and that souls will be healed through **the power of the Holy Spirit**.

## Herod orders John's execution (verses 14–29)

Herod feared John while he was alive.

He feared him even more after his death.

He had obeyed **many** of John's instructions, but obedience to many commandments is not enough.

We must regard **all God's commands** as binding.

Herod respected John's preaching until it challenged his relationship with Herodias.

Many people welcome sound preaching so long as it does not disturb **their favorite sin**.

It is better for sinners to persecute faithful ministers now than to curse those ministers eternally for having concealed the truth.

God's ways are beyond our complete understanding.

Yet he will never be unable to reward his servants for what they lose or suffer for his sake.

Death could not finally surprise John, a faithful and holy man.

His enemies' apparent triumph was short-lived.

## The disciples return; five thousand are fed (verses 30–44)

Ministers should do or teach nothing they would be ashamed to report to **their Master**.

Jesus noticed both the fears of some disciples and the exhausting efforts of others.

He provided rest for the tired and refuge for those who were afraid.

The crowds came to receive **the spiritual food of his word**.

After teaching them, he ensured that they had bodily food as well.

If Christ and his apostles accepted simple and modest living, we should be willing to do so.

This miracle demonstrates that Jesus came not only to **restore spiritual life** but also to **preserve and nourish** it.

There is sufficient provision in him for everyone who comes.

Only people filled with **self-importance** leave Christ empty-handed.

Although Jesus could supply limitless bread, he taught the disciples **not to waste God's gifts**.

Many people lack even ordinary food.

We ourselves may one day need what we carelessly throw away today.

## Jesus walks on water and heals those who come (verses 45–56)

The church often resembles a ship at sea, tossed by storms and lacking comfort.

Christ may be **for us**, even while the wind and waves are against us.

The consolation for believers is that **their Master is on heaven's mountain, interceding for them**.

No obstacle can prevent his coming to help his people at the appointed time.

He quieted the disciples' fears by revealing his identity.

Our fears often vanish when mistaken ideas are corrected, particularly mistaken ideas about Christ.

If the disciples have Jesus among them, everything is well.

We fail to understand Christ's present works because we forget what he has already done.

If Christian ministers could now cure every bodily disease, crowds would rush to them.

How sad that so many people are more concerned about **the health of their bodies** than the salvation of their souls!
