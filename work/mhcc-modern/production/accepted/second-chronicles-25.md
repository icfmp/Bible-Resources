## Amaziah's reign begins (verses 1–13)

Amaziah was not an enemy of true religion, but he was **a lukewarm and indifferent friend**.

Many people do what is right without doing it with their whole hearts.

Rash decisions create painful work for repentance.

Yet Amaziah deserved honor for obeying God's command when it required sacrifice.

A firm belief that **God is fully able to uphold us in duty and make up for losses suffered in serving him** makes his commands easier to bear.

When we must surrender something for God and his worship, we can be content knowing he can provide something much better.

Sinners who have been convinced intellectually but do not possess genuine faith often object when obedience requires self-denial.

Like Amaziah, they ask, **“But what shall we do about the hundred talents?”**

What if keeping the Sabbath means losing good customers?

What if obedience costs a profitable business opportunity or the world's friendship?

People often try to quiet their consciences by claiming that forbidden practices are necessary.

The answer remains the same: **the Lord can give you far more than that**.

Even in this life, he can make up for what is surrendered for his sake.

## Amaziah worships Edom's idols (verses 14–16)

After defeating the Edomites, Amaziah worshiped the gods who had failed to protect the people he conquered.

What absurdity!

If people seriously considered how powerless the things they turn to are, they would not abandon the Lord and harm themselves.

The prophet's rebuke was so just that Amaziah could not answer it, except by ordering the prophet to stop speaking.

Sinners may feel relieved when they silence those correcting them.

But people who refuse to hear correction are becoming ready for destruction.

## Amaziah challenges Israel and is humiliated (verses 17–28)

Few proud rulers have been more completely humbled than Amaziah by **Jehoash, king of Israel**.

Pride brings a person low (**Proverbs 29:23**).

It goes before destruction and rightly produces it.

Everyone who exalts himself will be humbled.

Someone who rushes into a quarrel may find himself humiliated and at a loss when his opponent answers him (**Proverbs 25:8**).

What are we when we try to establish our **own righteousness** or justify ourselves before the Most High God?

We resemble contemptible thistles imagining themselves majestic cedars.

Temptations and sinful desires are like wild beasts that trample such proud pretensions into the dust.

A person's destruction can often be traced to the moment the person turned away from the Lord.
