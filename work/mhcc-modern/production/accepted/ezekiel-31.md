# Ezekiel 31 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The glory of Assyria. | 1–9 |
| Its fall, and the like for Egypt. | 10–18 |

## Assyria's glory is pictured as a cedar (verses 1–9)

When we see others fall into **sin and ruin**, we should be warned against pride and false confidence.

The prophet presents an example of a ruler whose greatness resembled that of the king of Egypt.

**Assyria** is compared with a magnificent **cedar tree**.

Those who rise above others often become objects of **envy**.

But the blessings of **the heavenly paradise** are never spoiled in this way.

The greatest security any created thing can offer is like the **shadow of a tree**: narrow and fragile protection.

Let us therefore seek our safety with **God**, where we truly can rest.

We must recognize **his hand** in the rise of powerful people, rather than envy them.

Though worldly prosperity may appear firmly established, its security is only an appearance.

## Assyria's fall warns Egypt (verses 10–18)

The king of Egypt resembled Assyria's king in greatness.

He also resembled him in **pride**, and would resemble him in **ruin**.

His own sin caused his downfall.

We lose no earthly comfort that we have not already forfeited many times through sin.

When powerful rulers fall, they often draw many others into destruction with them, just as many powerful rulers have fallen before them.

Their downfall should warn us to remain **humble**.

Look how low Pharaoh would be brought!

What would remain of his splendor and pride?

It is better to be **a lowly tree of righteousness**, bearing fruit for **God's glory and other people's good**.

The wicked may flourish like a tall cedar or a spreading bay tree, but soon they disappear and their place can no longer be found.

Let us observe the upright person: **the end of that person is peace**.
