# 1 Timothy 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Directions as to the elder and younger men and women. | 1, 2 |
| And as to poor widows. | 3–8 |
| Concerning widows. | 9–16 |
| The respect to be paid to elders. Timothy is to take care in rebuking offenders, in ordaining ministers, and as to his own health. | 17–25 |

## Correct others with respect and gentleness (verses 1 and 2)

We should respect people according to **their age and responsibilities**.

When someone younger has acted wrongly, correction should never be given from a desire **to find fault**.

It should be motivated by a desire **to help that person grow**.

Great meekness, wisdom and care are required when correcting anyone, even when correction is clearly deserved.

## Care for widows and needy family members (verses 3–8)

Honor widows who are truly without support.

Provide practical relief and assistance for their needs.

When parents become poor and their children possess the means to help, those children have a duty to support them **as fully as they can**.

Widowhood can bring deep loneliness and distress.

But widows should trust the Lord and continue **in prayer**.

Anyone who lives entirely for worldly pleasure is **spiritually dead while still physically alive**.

How many professing Christians live in that condition, even into old age!

Men and women who refuse to support needy relatives **effectively deny the faith they profess**.

If they spend on sinful indulgence what should have been used to maintain their households, their conduct is **worse than that of unbelievers**.

A professing Christian who embraces corrupt ideas or practices can bring greater dishonor upon the faith than someone who has never claimed to believe it.

## Faithful charity and suitable church service (verses 9–16)

Anyone appointed to public church service must be **free from justified accusations of wrongdoing**.

Some people may be worthy recipients of charitable help without being suitable for **an official ministry role**.

Those wishing to receive mercy in adversity should be willing to **show mercy in prosperity**.

People who are eager to perform good work generally prove faithful when entrusted with responsibility.

Those who become idle rarely remain merely inactive.

They often begin interfering in the affairs of neighbors and **sowing discord among fellow believers**.

Christians should relieve destitute relatives whenever possible.

That allows the church to direct its shared resources toward **those who are completely abandoned and have no relatives able to help them**.

## Honor faithful ministers and judge without favoritism (verses 17–25)

Churches must provide adequate **material support for ministers**.

Those who labor diligently in teaching and leading God's people deserve **special honor and esteem**.

That support is their rightful due, just as an employee deserves payment for work performed.

Paul solemnly instructed Timothy **not to show favoritism**.

We must continually guard against **participating in other people's sins**.

Keep yourself pure—not only by avoiding similar misconduct, but also by refusing to **approve, encourage or assist wrongdoing** by others.

Paul also instructed Timothy to care for **his physical health**.

We should neither make our bodies **masters that control our lives** nor treat them as **slaves to be abused**.

Instead, care for them so that they remain as useful as possible for **serving God**.

Some sins are visible to everyone long before judgment.

Others remain concealed for a time.

Yet the Lord will bring **hidden acts and the true purposes of every heart** into the light.

Remembering that day, let everyone fulfill their responsibilities—whether public or private, high or low—so that **God's name and teaching are never disgraced through our conduct**.
