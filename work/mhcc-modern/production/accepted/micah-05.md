# Micah 5 — Modern English

## Christ's birth in Bethlehem and his kingdom (verses 1–6)

After announcing how greatly **David's royal family** would be brought low, Micah gave a promise about **the Messiah and his kingdom** to strengthen God's people's faith.

The prophecy reveals **Christ's eternal existence as God** and his work as **Mediator**.

His birth would take place in **Bethlehem**.

The Jews understood that location as the Messiah's birthplace (**Matthew 2:5**).

Christ's government brings happiness to his people.

They may enjoy **security and rest under his care**.

The promised protection from the Assyrians also represents the protection Christ gives **the gospel church and individual believers** against **the powers of darkness**.

**Christ is our Peace as Priest**, because he atones for sin and reconciles us to God.

**Christ is our Peace as King**, because he conquers our enemies.

Therefore our souls may rest confidently in him.

Christ will also provide **human instruments of protection and deliverance**.

Those who attempt to destroy God's church frequently bring destruction upon themselves.

Henry sees these promises as possibly encompassing **the past successes of gospel preaching**, **the future spread of the gospel** and **the downfall of powers opposing Christ**.

He calls this **perhaps the most important single prophecy in the Old Testament**, because it reveals the Messiah's personal character and the way he would be made known to the world.

It distinguishes **his birth as a human being** from **his eternal divine existence**.

Henry also understands it to predict Israel's **temporary rejection and final restoration**, and universal peace in the latter days.

Meanwhile, let us trust **our Shepherd's care and power**.

If he allows enemies to attack, he will provide the assistance we need.

## The remnant of Israel and its victories (verses 7–15)

The remnant of Israel converted to Christ during the early Christian period was scattered among many nations **like drops of dew**.

These believers became instruments by which many additional people were brought into **spiritual worship**.

For people who rejected or opposed salvation, however, they became **like lions**.

Their message condemned those who persisted in unbelief.

God promised not only **the reform of the Jews** but also **the purification of the Christian church**.

Likewise, in our own spiritual struggles, we can expect victory when we depend simply upon **the Lord our salvation**, worship him and serve him diligently.
