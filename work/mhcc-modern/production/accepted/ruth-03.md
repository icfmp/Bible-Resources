# Ruth 3 — Modern English

## Naomi advises Ruth about marriage (verses 1–5)

Marriage ought to provide as much lasting rest and security as anything can provide in this world.

It should establish a lifelong relationship and settle a person's affections.

That is why marriage should be entered into **seriously**, with earnest prayers for God's guidance and blessing and with careful attention to his commands.

Parents should advise their children thoughtfully on this important matter, seeking their spiritual good.

Always remember: **what is best for us is what is best for our souls**.

Naomi's advice may seem strange to modern readers, but it reflected the laws and customs of Israel.

Henry argues that Naomi would not have recommended it if the action had carried the appearance of wrongdoing.

Ruth had now joined the true faith.

The laws and customs of Israel gave her a legal claim involving **Boaz as a possible family redeemer**.

Widows customarily asserted such claims (Deuteronomy 25:5–10).

But this conduct is **not recorded for imitation in other times**, nor should it be judged as though the ancient custom were the same as today's.

Had the proposal been wrong, Henry believes Ruth's virtue and good judgment would have led her to refuse it.

## Boaz responds to Ruth's request (verses 6–13)

Something improper in one country or period is not necessarily improper in another.

Boaz, as a judge of Israel, could explain what Ruth should do.

He could also determine whether he possessed the right of redemption and what steps and ceremonies were necessary for marriage to him or to another relative.

His conduct deserves the greatest praise.

He did not exploit Ruth or treat her with contempt because she was a poor foreign widow.

Nor did he suspect her of evil intentions.

He spoke respectfully of her as a virtuous woman and made her a promise.

As soon as morning came, he sent her away with a gift for Naomi.

Boaz's promise was **conditional**: a nearer relative had the first right of redemption.

## Ruth returns home to Naomi (verses 14–18)

Ruth had done everything appropriate for her to do.

Now she needed to wait patiently for the result.

Boaz had accepted responsibility and would deal with the matter carefully.

Believers have even stronger reason to entrust their worries to God, because he has promised to care for them.

Our strength may sometimes be to **remain quiet and wait** (Isaiah 30:7).

This history encourages us to come by faith to **the feet of Jesus Christ**.

He is our close Relative, having taken our human nature.

He possesses the right to redeem us.

We should seek his guidance and ask, “Lord, what do you want me to do?” (Acts 9:6).

Christ will never condemn us for asking him such a question.

Let us also desire and seek this same spiritual rest for our children and friends, so that all may be well with them.
