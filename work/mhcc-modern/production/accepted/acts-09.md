# Acts 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| The conversion of Saul. | 1–9 |

## Saul encounters the risen Jesus (verses 1–9)

Saul was so deeply mistaken that he believed **attacking Christ's name** was service to God.

Persecution had become as natural to him as breathing.

Yet we should never despair that regenerating grace can convert even the greatest sinner, or that the Lord's mercy can forgive even terrible sin.

It is a clear sign of divine favor when God's grace within us or Providence around us **prevents us from carrying out sinful plans**.

Saul saw **the Righteous One** (see **Acts 22:14; 26:13**).

How near the unseen world is to us!

God only needs to lift the veil, and realities appear beside which even the most impressive earthly things seem insignificant.

Saul surrendered without conditions, asking what Jesus wanted him to do.

Christ's self-revelation humbles sinners and brings down their proud opinion of themselves.

For **three days**, Saul ate nothing. God allowed this period of spiritual anguish to continue.

His sins stood before his conscience, and he felt unable to understand his spiritual condition.

When people truly recognize their guilt, they cast themselves entirely upon the Savior's mercy and ask how he would have them live.

God directs the humbled sinner.

He often leads people toward the joy and peace of faith through serious conviction and sorrow over sin.

Yet those who sow in tears will reap in joy.

## Ananias visits Saul; Saul preaches Jesus (verses 10–22)

A good work had begun in Saul when he knelt before the Lord, asking what he must do.

Jesus never abandons someone brought sincerely to that place.

Behold the proud Pharisee, merciless oppressor and daring blasphemer **praying**!

The same wonder can take place today in the life of an unbeliever or notorious sinner.

Such prayer is different from outward recitation.

Before conversion, Saul had **said prayers**; now **he really prayed**.

Regenerating grace creates a praying heart.

Just as a living person must breathe, a spiritually living Christian must pray.

Even experienced disciples may hesitate before God's commands.

Ananias had trouble believing Saul was truly changed.

Yet God delights in surpassing our narrow expectations, transforming people we considered fit only for judgment into **objects of his mercy**.

The Spirit's teaching removes the scales of **ignorance and pride**.

A converted sinner becomes a new creation, wanting to commend Jesus Christ, **the anointed Son of God**, even to former associates.

## Saul faces persecution and joins the disciples (verses 23–31)

Anyone beginning to walk with God should expect trials.

Yet the Lord knows how to rescue his servants and make a way through temptation.

Saul's conversion is a strong evidence of Christianity's truth.

But **the event itself could not convert a hostile human heart**.

Only God's power to make a person new can produce true faith.

Believers can be too suspicious of people they previously distrusted.

Caution is reasonable in a deceptive world, but we must also practice **charity** (**1 Corinthians 13:5**).

God brings genuine believers into fellowship with his people and may let them testify before those who once saw their hatred of the gospel.

Christ directed Saul to leave Jerusalem and be sent to **the Gentiles** (**Acts 22:21**).

The Lord's witnesses cannot be killed before their appointed testimony is complete.

Persecution subsided for a time.

Believers walked uprightly, enjoyed the Holy Spirit's comfort and grew as others joined them.

They lived through the Spirit's consolation not only in hardship but also in **rest and prosperity**.

Those who walk carefully are most able to walk joyfully.

## Peter heals Aeneas (verses 32–35)

All genuine Christians are **saints**, or God's holy people.

That name belongs not only to famous figures such as Peter and Paul but to **every sincere believer in Christ**.

Jesus chose patients whose conditions were hopeless by ordinary means to show how helpless fallen humanity is.

When we were without strength, Christ sent his word to heal us.

Peter claimed no personal power.

He directed **Aeneas** to look to Jesus for the healing.

The truth that Christ alone gives the power for holiness does **not** excuse us from acting.

Although Jesus makes us well, we must **rise and use the strength he gives**.

## Dorcas is raised to life (verses 36–43)

Some people speak beautifully about charity while doing very little.

**Tabitha, also called Dorcas, was known for good works rather than impressive talk**.

Even Christians without money to give can practice charity through **their hands and their feet**—working, visiting and helping others.

The best praise is the testimony of a person's own deeds.

It is ungrateful to receive kindness and then fail to recognize it.

While depending entirely on Christ's fullness for our own salvation, we should also long to be **full of good works**, bringing glory to him and benefit to his people.

People like Dorcas make the gospel attractive through their practical conduct.

Henry contrasts her useful service with people who devote their lives to outward appearance, dress and vanity.

God's power accompanied Peter's word, and Dorcas returned to life.

When God awakens dead souls to spiritual life, an early sign is that **the eyes of their understanding open**.

This miracle shows that the Lord can restore what we have lost and govern all events for the good of those who trust him and the honor of his name.

## Editorial review — Acts 9

**Complete CCEL English witness directly checked:** [CCEL, Acts 9](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xxxvi.ix.html). Released `MHCC 0.1.1` remains authoritative for exact source-native SWORD content/links, independent editorial QA and reader validation.

- **1–9:** Saul as zealous but gravely mistaken, **renewing grace for greatest sinners**, Providence blocking evil, **Acts 22:14; 26:13**, **three days** of conviction and surrender.
- **10–22:** shift from **saying prayers to praying**, living faith necessarily praying, Ananias's understandable doubt, Spirit removing ignorance and pride, Saul preaching the Son of God.
- **23–31:** Saul's conversion powerful witness but **not itself regenerating others**, caution plus **1 Corinthians 13:5** charity, Gentile mission (**Acts 22:21**), Spirit's comfort in **peace and trouble**.
- **32–35:** **saints = all genuine believers**, Christ's sole healing power **alongside human duty to act**.
- **36–43:** Dorcas's deeds rather than talk, **charity available without money**, vanity contrast, life as opened eyes, Christ's providential care.
