# Jeremiah 1 — Modern English

## Jeremiah is called to speak God's word (verses 1–10)

Jeremiah received his call to prophetic service when he was still young.

He was appointed to speak not only to **the Jews**, but also to **the neighboring nations**.

Through the book he remains a prophet whose warnings concern **the whole world**.

It would be well for everyone to take those warnings seriously.

The Lord who formed us knows the particular duties and purposes for which he intends us.

But unless he makes us holy through **his Spirit who creates new life**, we will not be fit for his holy service on earth or his holy happiness in heaven.

We should think humbly of ourselves.

Young people should remember their inexperience and not presume to undertake tasks beyond their abilities.

Yet an awareness of our weakness and inadequacy should make us approach our work **with humility**, not cause us to **draw back when God calls**.

Those commissioned to deliver messages from God must not fear **human opposition**.

Through a sign, the Lord gave Jeremiah the gift needed for the task.

God's message must be delivered **in God's own words**.

Whatever worldly advisers or politicians believe, the true security of a kingdom is ultimately determined by **God's purpose and word**.

## The almond tree, boiling pot and promised protection (verses 11–19)

God showed Jeremiah the approaching destruction of **Judah and Jerusalem by the Chaldeans**.

The **almond tree**, which blossoms earlier in spring than other trees, indicated **how soon the judgment would come**.

God also showed where the approaching destruction would originate.

Jeremiah saw a **boiling pot**, a picture of Judah and Jerusalem thrown into terrible turmoil.

The pot's opening, or the face of the furnace beneath it, pointed **toward the north**.

From that direction would come the fire and the fuel that would consume Judah.

Northern powers would unite.

The cause of these judgments was **Judah's sin**.

Jeremiah was to declare **the whole counsel of God**, not merely the parts that pleased his listeners.

**Reverence for God is the best remedy for fear of people.**

It is better to have every human being against us than to have **God as our enemy**.

Those who know that God is with them do not need to fear anyone who opposes them.

Let us pray for the willingness to give up our private interests whenever necessary, and for steadfastness so that nothing draws us away from duty.
