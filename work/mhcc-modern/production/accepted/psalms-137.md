# Psalm 137 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews bewail their captivity. | 1–4 |
| Their affection for Jerusalem. | 5–9 |

## Refusing to turn Zion's songs into a mockery (verses 1–4)

The Jews' enemies had carried them captive into a foreign land.

They then added insult to their grief by demanding laughter and songs.

That cruelty was also **profanity**, because their captors wanted to hear **the songs of Zion**.

We should not comply with the demands of those who mock what is holy.

The captives were not merely saying they were too sad to sing. Their objection was that **these were the Lord's songs**, which they dared not offer as entertainment among idolaters.

## Remembering Jerusalem and entrusting vengeance to God (verses 5–9)

We naturally think about what we truly love.

Those who rejoice in God make Jerusalem their joy for his sake.

The Jewish exiles resolved never to lose that affection.

During suffering, we should recall with godly sorrow the mercies we forfeited and the sins through which they were lost.

Henry's text then contains a sentence apparently missing words concerning **temporal advantages and Christian profession**. This must be collated before any supposed reconstruction is accepted.

The psalm's severe ending is not permission for **private revenge**. On the contrary, Henry says explicitly that vengeance belongs to the Lord.

People who rejoice in others' suffering, especially the calamities of Jerusalem, will not escape judgment.

When praying for the promised success of God's church, we recognize that its enemies' designs must fail. This is not the same as seeking personal vengeance.

Above all, remember that our hope of reaching **the heavenly Jerusalem** rests entirely on **God's grace and Christ's finished salvation**.

## Editorial review — Psalm 137

**Entire secondary English witness directly inspected:** [CCEL, Psalm 137](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xx.cxxxvii.html). Released source `MHCC 0.1.1` remains authoritative for exact native text, markup and independent acceptance.

- **5–9:** Jerusalem remembered in love, **sins and lost mercies**, explicit ban on **personal vengeance**, judgment of enemies, salvation only through **Christ's finished work** — retained.

**Secondary textual defects:** CCEL says scoffers are not to be **“compiled”** with (presumably “complied”); also prints the incomplete clause “If temporal advantages ever render a profession, the worst calamity has befallen him.” No invented reconstruction is treated as Henry's authentic wording.
