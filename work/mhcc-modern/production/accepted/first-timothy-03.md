## The character required of an overseer (verses 1–7)

When a man desires the **pastoral office** from genuine love for Jesus Christ and concern for human souls, and is willing to deny himself and endure hardship by devoting his life to that work, **he desires a good work**.

Such an ambition deserves approval **if the person also possesses the required qualifications**.

A minister should give others as little just reason for complaint as possible.

Otherwise his behavior may bring disgrace upon **the office he holds**.

He ought to be **sober, self-controlled and moderate** in his actions and in his enjoyment of the created comforts God provides.

Scripture connects **sobriety and watchfulness** because each supports the other.

The families of ministers should be **good examples to other families**.

Beware of pride!

Pride was the sin that **turned angels into devils**.

A minister should have **a good reputation among neighbors**, with no justified scandal arising from his past conduct.

To encourage ministers who serve faithfully, Christ has made this gracious promise:

**“I am with you always, to the end of the age”** (Matthew 28:20).

He will equip his ministers for their duties, sustain them through difficulties, comfort them amid their work and reward their faithfulness.

## The integrity required of deacons (verses 8–13)

Deacons were first appointed to **distribute the church's charitable assistance and manage its affairs**.

Henry notes that some among the early deacons also became **pastors or evangelists**.

Deacons therefore received an important **public trust**.

They must be **serious, prudent and dependable**.

We should not entrust a public responsibility to anyone until that person has demonstrated fitness **for the work involved**.

People closely associated with ministers also need to live in a way **consistent with Christ's gospel**.

## The church proclaims the mystery of godliness (verses 14–16)

The church is **God's household and dwelling place**.

It upholds and displays **Scripture and Christ's teaching**, much like a pillar holding up a public proclamation.

When a church abandons its role as **pillar and foundation of the truth**, believers may and should leave it.

**Loyalty to God's truth must come first**.

The great mystery of godliness concerns **Jesus Christ**.

He is God who **became human and appeared in human flesh**.

God was pleased to reveal himself to the human race **by his Son taking human nature**.

Although Christ was accused of being a sinner and put to death as a criminal, he was **raised by the Spirit**, vindicated against every false charge.

Angels attended and served him, because **he is their Lord**.

The Gentiles welcomed the gospel even while many among Israel rejected it.

Remember that **God was revealed in the flesh** to take away our sins, redeem us from all lawlessness and purify a people for himself **eager to perform good works**.

These magnificent teachings must be made visible through **the Spirit's fruit in our conduct**.
