## Christ stands before Pilate (verses 1–14)

They bound Jesus.

We should often remember our Lord's bonds, remembering the One who was bound **for us**, and reckon ourselves bound with him.

By handing over their King, the religious leaders effectively handed over the kingdom of God. By their own action, it would be taken from them and entrusted to another nation.

Jesus answered Pilate directly but did not answer the witnesses. Their accusations were known to be false, and even Pilate was convinced of their falsehood.

Pilate hoped to appeal from the priests to the people, expecting the crowd to rescue Jesus from the religious leaders' demands.

But the priests stirred up the people still more. They cried for Jesus to be crucified.

We should judge people and events by their real character and by **God's word**, not by popular rumors.

No person ever received such disgraceful treatment as Jesus, the only perfectly wise, holy and excellent man ever to walk the earth.

Contemplating his mistreatment should give us a strong sense of humanity's wickedness and hostility to God and teach us to hate the sinful motives displayed by his persecutors.

## Jesus is led out to be crucified (verses 15–21)

Jesus encountered death in one of its most terrifying forms: the death reserved for the worst criminals.

The cross brought shame.

Because sin had dishonored God, Christ made satisfaction by accepting the greatest disgrace human nature could bear.

Crucifixion was also regarded as a **cursed death under the law** (**Deuteronomy 21:23**).

Roman soldiers mocked Jesus as a king, just as the high priest's servants had mocked him as prophet and Savior.

Can a Christian be proud of splendid purple or scarlet clothing when such garments were used to mock Christ?

He wore the crown of thorns that **we deserved**, so that we might wear the crown of glory that **he earned**.

Sin made us liable to everlasting shame and contempt. To deliver us, Jesus freely submitted to shame and contempt.

He was led out among evildoers, though he himself had committed no sin.

The suffering of the meek and holy Redeemer continually teaches believers, who should never become weary of considering it.

After seeing what Jesus endured, how can I, a guilty sinner, indulge angry complaints, reproaches or threats when I experience harm or hardship?

## Jesus is crucified (verses 22–32)

The place of Jesus' crucifixion was called the place of a skull, the customary place of execution. He was counted among transgressors in every respect.

When we look at Christ crucified, remember what was written above him: **he is a King**.

We must therefore submit ourselves to him as his true subjects.

Two thieves were crucified beside him, with Jesus in the middle, to deepen his humiliation.

Yet Scripture had foretold that he would be counted among transgressors because **he was made sin for us**.

Passersby insulted him.

They demanded that he come down from the cross before they would believe. But when he gave an even more convincing sign by **rising from the grave**, they still did not believe.

Anyone who truly understands the salvation purchased by the Son of God's suffering and death will seek it earnestly.

The first hope of pardon and eternal life produces thanksgiving, and the sins that brought the Lord of glory to the cross produce godly sorrow.

## Christ dies (verses 33–41)

Thick darkness covered the land **from noon until three in the afternoon**.

The people were trying to extinguish the **Sun of Righteousness**.

The darkness signified the deep cloud over Christ's human soul when he offered himself for sin.

Jesus did not complain here that his disciples had forsaken him, but that **his Father had forsaken him**.

Especially in this suffering he was made sin for us.

Paul could rejoice even when describing his life as a sacrifice poured out in the service of the saints (**Philippians 2:17**).

But being offered **as a sacrifice for sinners' sins** was something incomparably greater.

At the instant Jesus died, the temple veil tore from top to bottom.

For unbelieving Jews, Henry takes this as an alarming sign of judgment upon their temple order and nation.

For believers, it declares the opening of **a new and living way into God's holiest presence through Jesus' blood**.

The centurion seems to have been deeply moved by the confidence with which Jesus had spoken to God as Father and entrusted his spirit to him.

Rightly understanding the crucified Christ reconciles believers to the thought of dying. They long to see, love and praise the Savior who was pierced and wounded to rescue them from the coming judgment.

## Jesus is buried (verses 42–47)

We now stand beside the burial of our Lord.

May we, by grace, be united to him in the likeness of his burial.

**Joseph of Arimathea** was waiting for God's kingdom.

Those who hope to share that kingdom's blessings must be ready to acknowledge Christ's cause even when it seems utterly defeated.

God raised up Joseph to serve him at this moment.

Providence also ensured that Pilate investigated carefully whether Jesus had actually died. This removed any plausible claim that Jesus had merely survived the crucifixion.

Pilate gave Joseph permission to take the body down and bury it.

Some women watched where Jesus was laid because they intended to return after the Sabbath and anoint his body; there had been no time beforehand.

The exact location of the tomb mattered especially because **Jesus would rise again**.

He will not abandon those who trust and call upon him.

Death has lost its sting for the believer. It will soon end their sorrows, as it brought an end to the Savior's earthly sufferings.
