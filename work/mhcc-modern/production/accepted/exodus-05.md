# Exodus 5 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Pharaoh increases Israel's burdens (verses 1–9)

God will acknowledge his people, though they may be poor and despised, and he will find the right time to defend their cause.

Pharaoh treated everything he heard with contempt.

He did not know Jehovah, fear him, or love him. So he refused to obey him.

His pride, ambition, greed, and political shrewdness made his heart hard and led him toward destruction.

Moses and Aaron had made a reasonable request.

They asked only to travel three days into the wilderness for a good purpose: “We will offer sacrifices to the Lord our God.”

Pharaoh's answer was deeply unreasonable.

He accused the people of idleness, as though this were why they wanted to worship.

He twisted the truth to create an excuse for adding to their burdens.

Even now, people sometimes criticize their neighbors for spending a few hours away from ordinary work in the service of God, while making no objection to others spending twice as long in sinful pleasures.

Pharaoh's order was cruel.

Moses and Aaron themselves were to be made to carry burdens.

Persecutors often take pleasure in humiliating ministers and making their lives hard.

The Israelites had to produce the usual number of bricks without being given the usual straw to mix with the clay.

If they met the quota, the work would crush them. If they failed, they would be punished.

## The Israelites suffer, and Moses appeals to God (verses 10–23)

The Egyptian taskmasters were extremely harsh.

How much we need to pray that God will deliver us from wicked people!

The Israelite foremen were right to complain to Pharaoh, but he only mocked them.

Satan's malice has often tried to make the worship and service of God appear suitable only for people who have nothing else to do, as though faith were merely the business of the idle.

But worship is a duty even for those who have the busiest lives.

People who faithfully serve God may be condemned as idle by others, yet they will escape God's judgment on the truly lazy servant.

The Israelites should have humbled themselves before God and acknowledged the shame of their own sin.

Instead, they quarreled with the very men whom God had sent to deliver them.

Moses returned to the Lord.

He knew that his words and actions had followed God's instructions, so he brought the matter before God.

Whenever our obedience to God leads us into confusion or difficulty, we should go to him and open our situation to him in earnest prayer.

Disappointments in God's work must not drive us away from God.

Instead, we should consider carefully why he has allowed them.
