# Matthew 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The temptation of Christ. | 1–11 |
| The opening of Christ's ministry in Galilee. | 12–17 |
| Call of Simon and others. | 18–22 |
| Jesus teaches and works miracles. | 23–25 |

## Jesus is tempted by the devil (verses 1–11)

Immediately after Jesus was declared to be **the Son of God and the Savior of the world**, he was tempted.

Great spiritual privileges and special evidence of God's favor do not exempt anyone from temptation.

But when the Holy Spirit assures us that we are God's adopted children, that assurance answers the evil spirit's accusations.

**Christ was directed into the struggle**.

If we carelessly presume upon our own strength and invite temptation, we provoke God to leave us to ourselves.

Other human beings experience temptation through their own sinful desires (**James 1:14**).

But **Jesus had no corrupt nature**, and therefore his temptation came from the devil, not from inward sinful desire.

Satan is subtle, malicious and daring, but he can be resisted.

Christ's experience comforts us: **being tempted is not itself sin when the temptation is refused**.

In that case it is suffering, not guilt.

Satan's aim in every temptation was to lead Christ into sin against God.

**First**, he tempted Jesus to distrust his Father's goodness and care.

Satan often attacks when outward circumstances are difficult.

People in distress must be especially watchful.

Jesus answered the devil by appealing to **what Scripture says**.

This is our example.

No need, however pressing, gives us permission to use sinful means of providing for ourselves.

The Lord can and will provide through the means he appoints.

**Second**, Satan tempted Jesus to presume upon his Father's protection in a dangerous act.

Both **despair and presumption** are extremely dangerous, especially in spiritual matters.

Satan has no objection to attacking us in holy places.

Indeed, places of religious honor and worldly advancement can create particular opportunities for pride.

Those who rise high can become especially exposed to the enemy's assaults.

Satan knew enough Scripture to quote it.

A person's head may be full of biblical facts and the mouth full of religious expressions, while the heart remains hostile to God.

Satan omitted part of the promise he quoted.

Stepping outside our duty forfeits any claim that the promise authorizes our recklessness.

The secondary CCEL source places a reference to **Deuteronomy 8:3** near this point, which must be collated with the published native text rather than silently relocated.

God's promises remain firm, but **they do not authorize us to continue in sin so that grace may increase**.

**Third**, Satan offered Jesus the world's kingdoms and their glory in exchange for idolatrous worship.

Worldly splendor is one of the most attractive traps for careless people.

Jesus rejected the evil offer at once: **“Go away, Satan!”**

Some temptations are so obviously wicked that we must not deliberate over them, but reject them immediately.

When the devil is resisted, he flees.

A heart that pauses to negotiate is almost defeated.

What can anyone gain by possessing the whole world while losing their soul?

After the temptation, **Christ received help and comfort**.

This encouraged him in his mission and encourages us to trust him.

He knows from experience what it is to suffer temptation and receive timely help.

Therefore he not only **sympathizes with tempted believers** but comes to their aid when they need it.

## Christ begins preaching in Galilee (verses 12–17)

God acts justly in withdrawing the gospel and opportunities for grace from people who despise them and push them away.

Christ will not remain where he is unwelcome.

People without him live in darkness.

They may even sit contentedly in it, choosing ignorance rather than light.

When the gospel enters a place or a human heart, it is like the arrival of **daylight**.

Light reveals what is hidden and shows the way forward; so does the gospel.

**Repentance belongs to gospel preaching.**

Not only the strict John the Baptist but also the gracious Jesus himself proclaimed repentance.

The need for it has not changed.

Henry says the kingdom of heaven was not considered to have arrived in its full form until **the Holy Spirit was poured out after Christ's ascension**.

## Jesus calls fishermen to follow him (verses 18–22)

When Jesus began preaching, he also began gathering disciples.

They would first hear his teaching and later preach it.

They would witness his miracles and afterward testify to them.

He went not to **Herod's court** or to the chief priests and elders in Jerusalem, but to **fishermen beside the Sea of Galilee**.

The power that called Peter and Andrew could also have converted Annas and Caiaphas, because nothing is impossible with God.

But Christ chose those considered foolish by the world to put worldly wisdom to shame.

**Diligence in honest work pleases Christ** and does not hinder holiness.

Idleness exposes people more readily to Satan's temptations than to God's call.

It is encouraging to see young people caring for their parents and fulfilling their duties.

When Christ comes, it is good to be found **working faithfully**.

We should ask ourselves two questions.

**Do I belong to Christ? Am I fulfilling the calling God has given me?**

These men had followed Jesus earlier as ordinary disciples (**John 1:37**).

Now they were called to leave their occupations and follow him more fully.

Everyone who follows Christ must be willing to give up anything **when he commands it**.

This encounter shows the power of Jesus' gracious calling.

**He speaks, and the work is done**.

## Jesus teaches and heals many (verses 23–25)

Wherever Jesus went, he confirmed his divine mission through miracles.

The healings pictured the healing power of his teaching and the Holy Spirit's work through it.

We do not now ordinarily see Christ heal bodies miraculously in that way.

But when **medicine** brings recovery, the Lord deserves our thanks just as fully.

Jesus healed every kind of sickness and disease.

No condition was too severe or difficult for him to cure with a word.

Henry points particularly to three forms of suffering.

**Paralysis** was extreme bodily weakness.

**Lunacy**, as Henry's historical term, described severe disorder of the mind.

**Demonic possession** was an affliction involving both bodily and mental misery.

Christ healed all three.

By restoring bodily health, he showed that his greatest mission was to cure **the diseases of the soul**.

Sin is the soul's sickness, suffering and torment.

Jesus came to **take away sin and heal the soul**.

These details were restored in this *modern manuscript from the complete CCEL Concise witness*, **not invented** or silently inserted into the original module. The same unusual Deuteronomy 8:3 reference remains in the complete CCEL text and requires pinned-native collation.

- **1–11:** temptation immediately after baptism, Christ **without corrupt nature** contrasted with **James 1:14**, temptation **not sin if resisted**, **three distinct tests** (distrust, presumption, idolatrous glory), **Scripture against Satan**, danger of worldly advancement, timely succor and Christ's sympathy — retained from **complete CCEL**.
- **12–17:** light of gospel vs willing darkness, both **John and Jesus preaching repentance**, Spirit after ascension as full kingdom event — retained.
- **18–22:** fishermen rather than court, diligent employment/parental duty, **John 1:37**, readiness to leave work at Christ's command — retained.
- **23–25:** miracles as signs of deeper healing, medicine as providential gift, distinct **paralysis/lunacy/possession** with period language not silently rewritten in source, **soul's disease of sin** — retained.
