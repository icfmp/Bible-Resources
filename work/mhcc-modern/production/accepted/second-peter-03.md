# 2 Peter 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The design here is to remind of Christ's final coming to judgement. | 1–4 |
| He will appear unexpectedly, when the present frame of nature will be dissolved by fire. | 5–10 |
| From thence is inferred the need for holiness, and stedfastness in the faith. | 11–18 |

## Scoffers deny Christ's promised return (verses 1–4)

Christians should have their purified minds awakened to active and joyful holiness.

In the gospel age scoffers will arise who make light of sin, mock salvation through Jesus Christ and ridicule his promised return.

A central teaching of faith concerns what God has **promised**, not what human beings can verify through past experience.

Scoffers will keep attacking that promise until Christ returns.

They see no dramatic change and therefore refuse to fear God (**Psalm 55:19**).

They assume that because God has not previously done something, **he cannot do it or never will**.

## God's patience and the coming judgment (verses 5–10)

If the scoffers remembered God's judgment in the **flood**, they would not ridicule the warning of another world-wide judgment.

The same word that governed the ancient world has announced that the present heavens and earth will pass through **fire**.

This event is certain because **God is truthful and powerful**.

Human beings see an immense difference between a day and a thousand years. But all past, present and future are continually before God.

A delay of a thousand years is no more to him than our postponing something for an hour.

We struggle to understand eternity and wrongly imagine God must experience time as we do.

What people call slow fulfillment is actually **divine patience**.

It gives God's people time to grow in knowledge, holiness, faith, patience and good works, glorifying him by what they do and endure.

Everyone will give account of actions done in the body, whether good or evil.

Therefore live humbly, examine yourselves often and take final judgment seriously—even while others behave as though accountability would never come.

**Christ will come unexpectedly**, when people feel secure.

Splendid palaces and everything in which worldly people seek happiness will pass through judgment.

God's fire will consume what human sin has corrupted and refine what belongs to his work.

How foolish to make earthly possessions our ultimate portion when everything visible is passing away!

Seek happiness **beyond this world**.

## Pursue holiness while growing in the knowledge of Christ (verses 11–18)

The certainty of Christ's return calls us to **complete and universal holiness**, not a minimal standard.

Genuine knowledge of Christ changes a person's life.

Believers await **new heavens and a new earth**, freed from present corruption and sin.

Only those clothed in **Christ's righteousness** and sanctified through **the Holy Spirit** will dwell there.

God is faithful to his promises.

Those whose sins are pardoned and who are reconciled to him are secure. Seek peace with **all people**, together with holiness.

Do not expect to face the Lord in peace if you spend the present day idle and careless.

We have work to complete before he calls us home.

The delay in his appearing should move us to faithful service, not impatience or moral carelessness.

Proud and corrupt people distort difficult passages of Scripture to suit their own views.

But the misuse of **Paul's letters** is no reason to discard them or any other part of the Bible. Human sin can twist every good divine gift.

When Scripture contains difficult teachings, prepare yourselves to understand them by **obeying what is already clear**.

Self-denial, distrust of our own pride and submission to Christ's authority are necessary if we are to embrace all gospel truth.

Reject human opinions that conflict with God's revealed law.

Those swept away by error lose their own steadfastness.

To remain firm, keep growing in **faith, virtue, knowledge and every grace**.

Seek not only to know more about Jesus Christ, but to **become more like him and love him more deeply**.

Such knowledge leads to thanksgiving and to praise that begins now and will continue forever.
