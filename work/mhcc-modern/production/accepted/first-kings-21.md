# 1 Kings 21 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Ahab covets Naboth's vineyard. | 1–4 |
| Naboth murdered by Jezebel. | 5–16 |
| Elijah denounces judgments against Ahab. | 17–29 |

## Ahab covets Naboth's vineyard (verses 1–4)

Naboth may once have been pleased to own a vineyard close to the royal palace.

But that location proved fatal to him.

Possessions often become traps, and living near powerful people can be dangerous.

Discontent is a sin that punishes itself, making people miserable through their own restless desires.

It also produces itself.

Discontent does not arise simply from outward circumstances, but from the mind.

**Paul was content in prison; Ahab was discontent in a palace.**

Ahab enjoyed every pleasure of Canaan, a beautiful and fruitful country.

He possessed the wealth of a kingdom, the luxuries of a royal court and the honor and authority of a throne.

Yet none of those things satisfied him without Naboth's vineyard.

Sinful desires continually expose us to distress.

People determined to complain can always find some cause for dissatisfaction, however comfortable their lives may be.

## Jezebel has Naboth murdered (verses 5–16)

A wife ought to be a suitable helper to her husband.

But a cunning, unscrupulous and beloved spouse who acts as Satan's agent can bring terrible consequences.

Few rulers have ever given more wicked orders than those Jezebel sent to the leaders of Jezreel.

**Naboth was to be murdered under the appearance of religion**.

People have sometimes used religious language to cover even the most horrible wrongs.

The murder would also be made to appear lawful through the formal procedures of justice.

The episode reveals the cruelty of the wicked and Satan's influence among those who disobey God.

We should entrust our lives and earthly comforts to the Lord, because **innocence does not always protect people from harm in this world**.

Yet we may rejoice that all wrongs will finally be put right at the day of judgment.

## Elijah announces judgment on Ahab (verses 17–29)

The apostle Paul lamented being **sold under sin** (Romans 7:14), like a helpless captive opposed to his own bondage.

Ahab was different.

**He willingly sold himself to sin**, choosing its dominion and loving it.

Jezebel encouraged him to continue in wrongdoing.

Elijah confronted Ahab and exposed his sin.

A person has reached a miserable condition when he regards God's word as his enemy.

It is even more desperate when he considers ministers his enemies simply because they tell him the truth.

Ahab adopted the outward clothing and behavior of a repentant person.

But his heart remained proud and unchanged.

He showed only what others could see.

His repentance was external, not genuine.

Nevertheless, his outward humiliation brought a delay in the announced judgment.

Let this encourage everyone who **truly repents and sincerely believes the gospel**.

If God granted a reprieve to a merely outward and partial penitent, surely a sincere believer may hope to go home **justified**, not merely reprieved.
