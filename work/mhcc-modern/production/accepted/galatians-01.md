# Galatians 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle Paul asserts his apostolic character against such as lessened it. | 1–5 |
| He reproves the Galatians for revolting from the gospel of Christ under the influence of evil teachers. | 6–9 |
| He proves the Divine authority of his doctrine and mission; and declares what he was before his conversion and calling. | 10–14 |
| And how he proceeded after it. | 15–24 |

## Christ appointed Paul; grace and peace come from God (verses 1–5)

Paul was appointed an apostle directly by **Jesus Christ**, and therefore also by **God the Father**, who shares the Son's divine nature and appointed him to be Mediator.

Grace includes both God's loving favor toward us and the good work he performs **within us**.

Peace includes the inward comfort and outward wellbeing that are truly necessary for us.

Both blessings flow from the Father as their source **through Jesus Christ**.

Notice the order: **grace first, then peace**. Genuine peace cannot exist without grace.

Christ **gave himself for our sins** to make atonement for us. God's justice required that sacrifice, and Jesus accepted it willingly.

Consider the infinite cost paid for our sins!

Sin is so destructive that nothing less than the gift of **the Son of God** could put it away.

We ought to understand how dreadful sin is.

Pay special attention to the words **“for our sins.”**

Our sinful nature would prefer to make itself worthy through its works before coming to Christ.

It wants the healthy person, not the person who needs a physician, to be the Savior's patient.

But Christ died not only to rescue us from God's wrath and the curse of his law.

He also came to free us from the corrupt practices and habits to which we are naturally enslaved.

Those who have not been delivered from this present evil world **through the Holy Spirit's sanctifying work** have no basis for assuming that Christ's blood has saved them from its condemnation.

## Paul warns against another gospel (verses 6–9)

Anyone seeking a different route to heaven from the gospel of Christ is tragically deceived.

Paul rebuked the Galatians because they were abandoning **justification through Christ alone**.

Yet he corrected them with tenderness, recognizing that false teachers had deliberately led them astray.

When we reprove others, we must be faithful to truth while seeking to restore them **gently**.

Some teachers wanted to put **works of the law** in the place of **Christ's righteousness**.

By doing so, they corrupted Christianity itself.

Paul solemnly declared a curse upon anyone who tried to establish such a false foundation.

Other so-called gospels may flatter human **self-righteousness** or make room for **worldly desires**.

Either way, they are Satan's inventions rather than Christ's gospel of grace.

We must hold two truths together.

Rejecting God's **moral law as a rule for Christian living** dishonors Christ and destroys true religion.

But trusting our supposedly good works **to justify us before God** is equally fatal for anyone who persists in that false hope.

Be zealous for good deeds, but never put them **where Christ's righteousness alone belongs**.

And do not teach ideas that could lure others into such a dangerous deception.

## Paul's message came by revelation from God (verses 10–14)

Paul's aim in preaching was to bring people into obedience **to God, not to human authorities**.

He would never change Christ's teaching to gain people's favor or avoid their hostility.

In matters of eternal truth, we must not fear human disapproval or seek approval through merely clever speech.

Paul received the gospel through **revelation from heaven**.

Unlike many Christians, he had not been led toward Christ merely by upbringing or education.

## Christ's grace transformed Paul's life (verses 15–24)

God wonderfully brought Paul to **the knowledge of and faith in Jesus Christ**.

Everyone who is genuinely converted is called through divine grace.

Conversion is the work of God's power, acting within the heart.

It benefits us little for Christ merely to be **shown to us outwardly** unless he is also **revealed within us**.

Once Paul received this call, he prepared to obey immediately.

He did not pause to protect his wealth, public reputation, comfort or even his life.

The churches rejoiced when they heard about his conversion.

What cause for thanksgiving whenever God's people learn that a former opponent has been brought to faith!

Even believers who have never met such a person should glorify God for his **power and mercy in salvation** and for all the service that new believer has already rendered or may yet render to Christ's cause.
