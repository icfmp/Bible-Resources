# 1 Samuel 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David spares Saul's life. | 1–7 |
| David shows his innocence. | 8–15 |
| Saul acknowledges his fault. | 16–22 |

## David refuses to kill Saul (verses 1–7)

God placed Saul within David's reach.

But this was an opportunity for David to demonstrate **faith and patience**.

God had promised David the kingdom.

He had **not** commanded David to kill the king.

David argued strongly against harming Saul, speaking both to himself and to the men accompanying him.

Sin is something that should alarm us.

We must resist every temptation toward it.

David was not satisfied merely with refusing to do wrong himself.

He also restrained the people around him from doing it.

By doing so, he repaid good for evil to the man who had repaid his good with evil.

All who call themselves Christians should follow this pattern: never be overcome by evil, but **overcome evil with good**.

## David explains his innocence (verses 8–15)

David had been falsely accused of seeking Saul's life.

He showed that God had given him the opportunity to harm Saul, but he refused to do so **on principle**.

He declared that he would never take private vengeance.

When people wrong us, we can trust the Lord to put matters right.

Even if justice does not come in this life, it will come at the judgment of the last day.

## Saul is moved but remains unchanged (verses 16–22)

Saul seemed overwhelmed by David's kindness.

But many people grieve over their sins without truly repenting.

They weep bitterly yet remain attached to the very sins they mourn.

God fulfilled the promise David had trusted: he would bring David's righteousness into the light (**Psalm 37:6**).

People who carefully preserve a good conscience can entrust their reputation to God.

Sooner or later, he will make even hardened opponents recognize the people he loves.

Saul and David parted peacefully.

Yet Saul was **convinced, not converted**.

He was ashamed of his envy but still carried bitterness in his heart.

He was frustrated because, after finally finding David, he could not bring himself to kill him as planned.

Malice may appear dead while merely sleeping, and may later awaken with doubled strength.

Whether God protects us by restraining an enemy's hands or by changing that person's heart so that the enemy will not harm us, **the deliverance comes from him**.

It is evidence of his love and a pledge of his salvation, for which we should be grateful.
