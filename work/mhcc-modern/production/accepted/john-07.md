# John 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ goes to the feast of tabernacles. | 1–13 |
| His discourse at the feast. | 14–39 |
| The people dispute concerning Christ. | 40–53 |

## Jesus goes to the Feast of Tabernacles (verses 1–13)

Jesus' brothers or relatives were disappointed when they realized following him offered **no clear prospect of worldly advancement**.

People without true faith sometimes offer advice to those serving God, but they generally recommend whatever promises **immediate personal advantage**.

The people held conflicting opinions about Jesus' teaching and miracles.

Those who thought him a deceiver spoke openly and boldly.

Many who regarded him favorably were unwilling to admit their views publicly because they feared criticism or persecution.

Something similar occurs throughout church history.

Opponents of the gospel loudly condemn its ministers while some who respect those ministers remain silent to avoid reproach.

## Jesus teaches publicly at the feast (verses 14–39)

### Jesus' doctrine comes from the Father (verses 14–24)

Every faithful minister can humbly adopt Jesus' declaration in an appropriate sense: **the message preached is not something invented by the minister**.

True doctrine comes from **God's word**, taught by the Spirit.

Amid the world's disputes, anyone willing to do God's will can discover whether teaching comes from the Lord or from merely human ideas.

Those determined to hate the truth risk being abandoned to deadly errors.

Jesus showed that healing someone on the Sabbath was as consistent with the day's purpose as performing an outward religious ordinance.

He called his critics to judge according to **the spiritual meaning of God's law**, not outward appearances.

When considering another person, we should look beyond social status and external impressions to **real character and the Holy Spirit's gifts and graces**.

### Jesus declares his divine origin (verses 25–30)

Jesus told his hearers plainly that their assumptions about his origin were mistaken.

He had been **sent by God**, who remains faithful to every promise.

Jesus also declared that, despite their religious claims, **they did not truly know God**.

His claim to know the Father in a unique way provoked hostility.

They attempted to arrest him, yet were unable to carry out their plan.

**God can restrain human hands without changing human hearts.**

### Some believe but fear to speak (verses 31–36)

Jesus' teaching persuaded many people that he really was the promised Messiah.

Yet they lacked courage to acknowledge their conviction.

Christ's faithful people are **in the world but not of it**.

They may feel oppressed or tired by hostility here.

It is a comfort that their stay in this world is brief.

The days of earthly life and the opportunities for grace do not last forever.

People who now despise the Savior's help may desperately wish for it when suffering and judgment overtake them.

Many debated Jesus' sayings without understanding them.

Their fulfillment would ultimately show what he meant.

### Jesus offers living water through the Spirit (verses 37–39)

On the last day of the Feast of Tabernacles, Jewish worshipers traditionally drew water and poured it out before the Lord.

Henry says Jesus was **possibly alluding to this ceremony**.

Anyone longing for lasting happiness must come to Christ and submit to his guidance.

Spiritual thirst means a deep desire for blessings the world cannot provide.

The water Jesus offers signifies **the sanctifying and comforting work of the Holy Spirit**.

His consolation flows abundantly and continually, like a great river.

It is powerful enough to overcome doubts and fears.

Christ possesses inexhaustible fullness: **grace upon grace**.

The Spirit dwelling and working within believers becomes like a spring of living water, sending streams into the surrounding world.

The water refreshes and cleanses.

Henry says believers should **not expect the apostles' extraordinary miraculous gifts**, but may and should ask for the Spirit's ordinary and even more valuable sanctifying influence.

These blessings have flowed from the glorified Redeemer throughout the generations and into distant countries.

May we eagerly make them known to others.

## The crowds and leaders disagree about Jesus (verses 40–53)

Jesus' enemies' malice often resisted reason itself.

Sometimes the reason their violent plans failed was difficult to explain through ordinary human causes.

No one has ever spoken with **the wisdom, power, grace, clarity and gentleness of Jesus**.

Yet some who are briefly restrained by his words and speak well of him lose those convictions and return to sin.

People too often let worldly fashion and social pressure decide matters of eternal importance.

They may effectively choose condemnation rather than endure public disapproval.

God's wisdom frequently chooses people the world despises, while human folly rejects those God has chosen.

The Lord sometimes raises up weak and fearful disciples and uses them to frustrate the designs of his enemies.
