# Hosea 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An exhortation to repentance. | 1–3 |
| Israel's instability and breach of the covenant. | 4–11 |

## Return to the Lord (verses 1–3)

People who joined together to abandon God and draw one another into sin should **join together to return to him**.

That will bring **glory to God and good to themselves**.

During suffering, we are strengthened and encouraged to repent by maintaining **right thoughts about God's goodness and purposes**.

Deliverance from distress can be like **life restored from death**.

God promises to revive his people, and the assurance of his mercy should draw them back.

Henry also sees a further reference here to **the resurrection of Jesus Christ**.

He marvels that a prophecy concerning the church's deliverance would point toward **salvation through Christ**.

In his reading, the resurrection confirms that Jesus is the promised One and that believers need **no other Savior**.

The promised blessing is **eternal life through knowing God**.

God's returning favor is as certain as **morning following a dark night**.

He comes like **the early and late rain**, refreshing the ground and making it fruitful.

The grace of God in Christ is both rains: it **begins and sustains the work of spiritual fruitfulness**.

Just as the Redeemer rose from the grave, he will revive **the hearts and hopes of everyone who trusts him**.

Even a small ray of hope from God's word promises increasing **light, comfort, cleansing and fruitful grace**.

## Israel's passing repentance (verses 4–11)

Under suffering, Israel and Judah sometimes appeared willing to repent.

But their apparent goodness vanished like **a morning cloud or early dew**.

They soon returned to their former corruption.

That is why the Lord sent such serious warnings through his prophets.

God's word will be **the death either of sin or of the sinner**.

The Lord desires **mercy rather than sacrifice** and knowledge of himself that produces **holy reverence and love**.

This exposes the folly of people who trust external religious ceremonies to compensate for **lack of love toward God and other people**.

As **Adam broke God's covenant in paradise**, so Israel broke its national covenant despite the many blessings it had received.

Judah too had become ready for judgment.

May the Lord establish **his reverence and kingdom within our hearts**, never abandon us to ourselves and not permit temptation to overcome us.
