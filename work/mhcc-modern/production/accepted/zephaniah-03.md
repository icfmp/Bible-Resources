# Zephaniah 3 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Further reproof for sin (verses 1–7)

The holy God hates sin most severely when it is committed by those who live nearest to him and enjoy the greatest spiritual privileges.

A people living in sin already occupies a miserable position and will face misery if they do not repent.

Judah possessed signs of God's presence and many opportunities to know his will.

These privileges gave its people the strongest reasons to obey him.

Yet they continued in disobedience.

How sad it is that human beings often put more energy into wrongdoing than believers put into doing good!

## Mercy, purification and the humble remnant (verses 8–13)

Henry understands this prophecy as looking toward **the proclamation of the gospel**, even when judgment would fall upon the Jewish nation.

The gospel's purifying teaching—the pure language of the Lord's grace—would teach people to speak and live with **humility, repentance and faith**.

Clean and godly everyday conversation is itself a good thing.

Henry also sees here a promise of the church's purity and happiness in later days.

The Lord will remove boasting.

He will leave people nothing in which to glory except **Jesus Christ**, whom God has made their **wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption**.

Humility about sin and gratitude toward the Redeemer will make real believers upright and sincere, even when other people profess religion without living accordingly.

## Future joy and restoration (verses 14–20)

After promises that sin will be removed come promises that suffering will be removed.

When the cause of trouble is taken away, the resulting distress can finally cease.

**What makes God's people holy will make them happy.**

The promises given to a purified people find their complete accomplishment in the gospel.

Henry understands these verses principally as referring to **the future conversion and restoration of Israel** and the glorious days to follow.

He expects the church to experience great peace, comfort and blessing in those times.

**He will save**.

Jesus fulfills the meaning of his name, for he saves his people from their sins.

Before those promised days, believers may experience great sorrow and suffer disgrace.

But the Lord will rescue even the weakest believer and honor genuine Christians where they were once despised.

In Henry's interpretation, the same act of mercy would gather scattered Israel and lead its people back to their land.

Then God's Israel would receive enduring honor.

**Only the fulfillment of events will fully explain the language of the prophecy.**

The righteous have many troubles, but they can rejoice in God's love.

How deeply we should honor and rejoice in the Lord when we hear such gracious words!

If we are now unable to take part in his appointed worship, we rightly feel the loss.

But one day we will be gathered into his heavenly temple.

The believer's happiness and glory will be **complete, unchanging and eternal** when earthly sorrows have ended.
