# Genesis 16 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Sarai gives Hagar to Abram. | 1–3 |
| Hagar mistreats Sarai. | 4–6 |
| The Angel commands Hagar to return, promises her a son, and Ishmael is born. | 7–16 |

## Sarai gives Hagar to Abram (verses 1–3)

Sarai had stopped expecting to bear a child herself.

She suggested that Abram take another wife, whose children could be counted as hers.

Hagar was her enslaved servant, and under the custom of that time, Hagar's children would belong to Sarai.

But Sarai made this proposal without asking the Lord for counsel.

Unbelief was at work, and God's almighty power had been forgotten.

The result was a bad example and the beginning of many troubles.

Every relationship and situation in life brings some difficulty or burden that we must bear.

A large part of practicing faith is patiently accepting such trials, waiting for God's appointed time, and using only the means he has appointed to remove them.

Dangerous temptations can come disguised as reasonable and attractive plans.

Human wisdom, when governed by sinful desires, takes us away from God's way.

We could avoid much trouble if we sought God's counsel through his word and prayer before acting in doubtful matters.

## The conflict between Sarai and Hagar (verses 4–6)

Abram's unhappy marriage to Hagar quickly brought serious trouble.

When we leave the path of duty, we have ourselves to blame for the guilt and grief that follow.

We see it plainly here.

People who are ruled by passion often quarrel with others about matters for which they themselves are responsible.

Sarai had given Hagar to Abram. Yet she cried out, “May the wrong done to me be upon you!”

Words prompted by pride and anger are never wise.

Those who loudly and confidently appeal to God are not always in the right.

Rash accusations and appeals to divine judgment often reveal guilt and a weak cause rather than innocence.

Hagar, for her part, forgot that she had first provoked the trouble by despising her mistress.

When people suffer because of their own faults, they ought to bear it patiently (1 Peter 2:20).

## The Angel meets Hagar and sends her back (verses 7–16)

Hagar had left her proper place and abandoned her duty.

When the Angel found her, she was wandering farther and farther from the right path.

It is a great mercy when conscience or providence stops us from continuing along a sinful road.

“Where have you come from?”

The question urged Hagar to consider that she was fleeing her responsibilities and leaving behind the privileges she had enjoyed in Abram's household.

It is a blessing to live in a family where God is worshipped. Those who enjoy that blessing should remember its value.

“Where are you going?”

Hagar was heading toward sin and danger.

If she returned to Egypt, she would return to the worship of false gods. She would also face the dangers of the wilderness.

Thinking carefully about who we are often reminds us of what we ought to do.

Remembering where we came from shows us our sins and foolishness.

Thinking about where we are going reveals the danger and misery ahead.

Anyone who has left the place of duty should hurry back, however humbling that return may be.

The Angel spoke in God's own name and said, “I will.”

That declaration shows that this Angel was the eternal Word and Son of God.

Hagar could only marvel at the Lord's mercy.

How could someone as unworthy as she was have received such a gracious visit from him?

Her attitude changed.

She returned, and her changed conduct softened Sarai's heart, so that Sarai treated her more gently.

May we always live with this truth firmly in mind: **“You are the God who sees me.”**

## Chapter-level editorial audit

Reviewed against the [original Concise commentary for Genesis 16](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc/mhcc.ii.xvi.html). `MHCC 0.1.1` is the pinned derivative source; no textual changes are documented in Genesis 16.

- **1–3:** Sarai's inability to expect children, the historical slave-wife arrangement and ownership, lack of prayer, unbelief, burdens in each relation, patient waiting and avoiding sinful means, plausible-looking temptations and need for word/prayer—retained.
- **4–6:** household trouble, assigning fault to others, Sarai's anger despite her own decision, rash appeals to God, Hagar's earlier contempt, 1 Peter 2:20—retained.
- **7–16:** Hagar's flight, restoring grace through conscience/providence, both questions about past and future, spiritual family blessing, Egypt/idolatry/wilderness, prompt return, the Angel's “I will” and Henry's explicit identification with **the eternal Son**, Hagar's wonder and changed conduct, divine omniscience—retained.
