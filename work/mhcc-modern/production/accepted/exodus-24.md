# Exodus 24 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## The covenant is confirmed with blood (verses 1–8)

God and Israel entered into a solemn covenant.

Its solemnity pointed toward the covenant of grace between God and believers through Jesus Christ.

As soon as God set Israel apart as his own people, he gave them his written word to govern their life.

He has continued to govern his people through his word ever since.

God's covenants and commands are just in themselves and intended for our good.

The more thoughtfully we consider them and the more fully and clearly they are explained, the more reason we see to accept them and obey.

Blood from the sacrifice was sprinkled on the altar, the book and the people.

Neither the people themselves, their moral obedience nor their religious services could be accepted by a holy God without the shedding and application of blood.

Every blessing God gave them was an act of mercy.

He intended to treat them kindly.

In the same way, a sinner who trusts in Christ's blood can offer obedience that is willing and acceptable to God.

## The elders behold God's glory (verses 9–11)

Israel's elders saw the God of Israel.

They received a glimpse of his glory.

Whatever they saw could not be represented in an image or picture.

Yet they saw enough to know that God truly was with them.

Scripture describes only what was beneath his feet.

Sapphires formed the pavement under them.

May we also keep all the wealth of this world beneath our feet, rather than setting it in our hearts.

Believers now see the glorious justice and holiness of God much more clearly in the face of Jesus Christ than they saw them even through terrifying conviction of sin.

Through the Savior they can enjoy communion with the holy God.

## Moses ascends into the cloud (verses 12–18)

A cloud covered the mountain for six days.

It was a sign of God's special presence there.

Moses trusted that the One who had called him upward would also keep him safe.

The very attributes of God that terrify the wicked are a cause of joy to believers who approach him with humble reverence.

Through faith in the atoning Sacrifice, we hope to receive an honor greater than anything Moses experienced on earth.

Now we see only dimly, as though through a mirror.

But when God appears, we will see him face to face.

That vision will not last for a few days.

It will continue throughout eternity, with joy that remains bright and may grow brighter still.
