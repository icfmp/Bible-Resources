# Psalm 51 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist prays for mercy, humbly confessing and lamenting his sins. | 1–6 |
| He pleads for pardon, that he may promote the glory of God and the conversion of sinners. | 7–15 |
| God is pleased with a contrite heart, A prayer for the prosperity of Zion. | 16–19 |

## Confession and plea for mercy (verses 1–6)

After becoming convinced of his sin, David poured out his heart to God in prayer for **mercy and grace**.

Where else can children who have wandered return but to **the Lord who alone can heal them**?

Under divine instruction, David described the movements of his repentant heart. Genuine penitents are not ashamed to acknowledge repentance. His words also teach others how to confess and pray.

Although David had done and suffered much in God's cause, he relied **only upon God's infinite mercy** for pardon and peace.

Christ's blood, applied to the conscience, removes transgression and reconciles us not only to God but also to ourselves.

A true believer wants **every debt of sin forgiven and every stain cleansed**.

A hypocrite secretly reserves some favored sin and wants it spared.

David's sense of guilt was so deep that it continually filled him with sorrow and shame.

His deliberate sin had been **against God**, whose truth sinners deny and whose trust they betray.

True repentance traces actual sins back to **the fountain of original corruption**.

David confessed the sinful inclination present from childhood—the attraction toward evil and reluctance toward good that burden renewed believers and ruins the unconverted.

Yet he hoped God would receive him with grace.

God desires **truth in the inward parts**.

Where sincerity is found, God gives wisdom.

Those who genuinely seek to obey will be taught their duties, while expecting all spiritual good from **divine grace overcoming a corrupt nature**.

## Cleansing, new life and restored praise (verses 7–15)

David prayed to be purified with **hyssop**—an image of **Christ's blood applied to the soul through living faith**, just as water of purification was sprinkled by a bundle of hyssop.

Christ's blood is called the **blood of sprinkling** (**Hebrews 12:24**).

If that cleansing blood washes us, we are truly clean (**Hebrews 10:2**).

David did not ask to feel comfort before receiving cleansing.

First the bitter root of sorrow—sin—must be removed.

Then he could ask for lasting peace created by God, so that the bones broken by conviction might rejoice.

He asked God to hide his face from his sins, and blot them from his book, as sunlight disperses a cloud.

But the believer wants **renewal in holiness as much as the joy of salvation**.

David now recognized the uncleanness of his heart and knew he could not change it by personal power.

He therefore begged the Lord to **create a clean heart within him**.

He had grieved the Holy Spirit through sin and feared losing the Spirit's gracious presence more than anything else.

He asked that comfort might return.

Sin can make a person doubt participation in salvation, which undermines joy.

David therefore prayed to be upheld by the Spirit, lest he fall into further sin or despair.

The Spirit is **free**: he acts freely, and makes willing service possible.

The greater our joy in duty, the more steadily we perform it.

This is the freedom contrasted with slavery (**Galatians 5:1**), the Spirit of adoption speaking peace to the heart.

Because God is the **God of salvation from sin**, we may pray for deliverance not only from sin's guilt but also from its dominion.

When our lips are opened by mercy, they should sing of **God's forgiving grace**.

## The sacrifice of a broken heart (verses 16–19)

Anyone truly aware of sin's danger would give much to be forgiven.

Yet human beings cannot make satisfaction for their sins through gifts or sacrifices.

God receives acts of devotion only as expressions of love and duty, not as the price of pardon.

The work of grace in a true penitent produces **a broken spirit and a contrite heart**, tender and responsive to God's word.

May we all possess such hearts!

God graciously accepts contrition rather than treating outward burnt offerings as sufficient.

Yet **a broken heart is accepted only through Jesus Christ**; there is no genuine repentance separated from faith in him.

People despise what is broken, but God will not reject a truly contrite heart.

That heart does not pay for sin; only grace receives it.

Those who have suffered spiritually learn to pity and pray for others in similar distress.

David also feared that his sin might bring judgment upon **the city and kingdom**.

Even personal trouble and a wounded conscience must not make a gracious soul indifferent to **the church's welfare**.

May all the redeemed continually rejoice that through **Christ's blood** they have forgiveness according to his abundant grace.
