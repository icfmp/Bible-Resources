# 2 Samuel 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David's messengers ill-treated by Hanun. | 1–5 |
| The Ammonites defeated. | 6–14 |
| The Syrians defeated. | 15–19 |

## Hanun humiliates David's messengers (verses 1–5)

**Nahash** had been an enemy of Israel, yet he had treated David kindly.

David therefore decided to repay that kindness with gratitude.

Even if a Pharisee gives charity for prideful motives, so that God will not reward the giver, the person receiving that help should still be thankful.

People who bear hostility toward their neighbors often refuse to believe that those neighbors could wish them well.

Even the best-intentioned action may be twisted into an insult by someone who loves nobody but himself.

Faithful people should not be surprised to have their motives misrepresented in this way.

Love does not suspect evil without reason.

According to the customs of the time and place, Hanun treated David's ambassadors with extreme contempt.

David showed real concern for his humiliated servants.

We should not allow unjust accusations or insults to crush our spirits.

Such reproaches will eventually fade and reflect shame upon their authors.

An unfairly damaged reputation can recover, just as the ambassadors' beards would grow again.

God will bring the righteousness of his servants into the light.

We should wait for him patiently (**Psalm 37:6–7**).

## The Ammonites are defeated (verses 6–14)

Those who oppose the **Son of David** both provoke and initiate conflict with him.

God has forces at his disposal to answer those who defy his judgment (**Isaiah 5:19**).

They will discover that no one can harden his heart against God and prosper.

Those who fight under Christ's leadership should strengthen one another in spiritual warfare.

We should omit nothing that belongs to our duty, regardless of the result.

When we have acted conscientiously, we may leave the outcome with God in peace, trusting that he will save in his own way and time.

## The Syrians are defeated (verses 15–19)

After being defeated once, the Syrians made a new attempt.

A beaten cause may revive while any strength remains within it.

The enemies of the Son of David behave in the same way.

But this victory fulfilled God's promise to Abraham (**Genesis 15:18**) and repeated to Joshua (**Joshua 1:4**) that Israel's boundaries would reach the Euphrates.

This teaches us the danger of helping people who have deliberately set themselves against God.

When they fall under his judgment, their allies will fall with them.
