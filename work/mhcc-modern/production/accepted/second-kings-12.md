## Jehoash repairs the temple (verses 1–16)

It is a great kindness to young people, especially to those born into positions of influence, to have advisers who teach them to do what is right in the Lord's sight.

Those who are willing to receive good counsel act wisely.

The temple needed repairs, and Jehoash ordered them to be made.

The king showed zeal for the work.

God expects people with authority to use it in support of true worship, in correcting wrongs and repairing what has fallen into decay.

Jehoash first placed the priests in charge, expecting them to care deeply about the temple.

Yet little effective work was accomplished until the **twenty-third year** of his reign.

A different arrangement was then adopted.

When public funds are distributed faithfully, people become willing to contribute them generously.

Even while collecting what was needed to repair the temple, the people did not take away the priests' regular support.

**Those serving in the temple must not be left hungry under the pretext of repairing its walls.**

The people entrusted with the money handled their responsibilities carefully and honestly.

They did not buy ornaments until the necessary repairs were finished.

This teaches us to give priority to essential needs in our spending and to handle public money as carefully as our own.

## Jehoash is killed by his servants (verses 17–21)

Consider Jehoash's character and the lessons his life offers.

His beginning was so promising, but the conclusion was deeply sorrowful.

It should prompt us to examine any decline in our own spiritual lives.

If we know Christ as the foundation of faith and hope, let us desire above all to know Christ.

May the work of the blessed Spirit be evident in our souls.

May we see and feel our need of Jesus and seek him earnestly in all the fullness and grace suited to us.

In this way our souls will turn away from dead works to serve the living and true God.
