# Proverbs 26 — Modern English

## Verse 1 — Honor must be fitting

It is inappropriate to grant honor to people unfit or unworthy to receive it.

## Verse 2 — A baseless curse cannot injure

A curse spoken without cause does no more lasting harm than a **bird flying over someone's head**.

## Verse 3 — Correction suited to character

Every creature must be treated according to its nature. Careless and wicked people often refuse persuasion or reason. Human beings are naturally untamed, but **God's grace changes some hearts**.

## Verses 4 and 5 — Answer fools with discernment

Choose how to respond according to the person and circumstances, addressing the conscience in whatever way may best end the dispute.

## Verses 6–9 — Foolishness misuses wise sayings

Fools are unfit to carry important messages or receive great honors. Even wise proverbs lose their value when handled or applied foolishly.

## Verse 10 — Two possible meanings of divine judgment

Henry allows **two readings**: the Creator will deal with sinners according to guilt, or powerful human rulers should disgrace and punish wicked conduct. Neither possibility should be silently replaced by the other.

## Verse 11 — Returning to sin

A dog returning to its own filth is a revolting image of sinners who repeatedly return to their vices (**2 Peter 2:22**).

## Verse 12 — The danger of self-conceit

Some people know very little yet are extraordinarily proud of it. They resemble those who think their **spiritual condition is sound when it is desperately wrong**.

## Verse 13 — Imaginary dangers and real duties

The lazy person fears work and invents dangers. It is foolish to avoid a real duty through imagined difficulties, especially **duties of faith**.

## Verse 14 — Movement without progress

Love of bodily ease produces spiritual sickness. Some religious people resemble a door turning on its hinges: they perform outward exercises but make **no progress toward heaven** because the world and flesh hold them fast.

## Verse 15 — Laziness refuses even nourishment

The idle person may leave bed but still accomplish nothing. People neglecting duty often claim they cannot perform it. The spiritually lazy refuse to feed their souls on **the Bread of Life** or draw near in prayer for promised blessings.

## Verse 16 — Labor in the Lord is not wasted

People who work diligently in spiritual service know they serve **a good Master**, and their labor will not be in vain.

## Verse 17 — Avoid meddling in others' quarrels

Interfering without necessity in other people's disputes is a way of deliberately exposing ourselves to temptation.

## Verses 18 and 19 — Do not excuse harm as a joke

People who sin as a joke must **repent seriously** or their wrongdoing will destroy them.

## Verses 20–22 — Gossip sustains quarrels

Conflict can inflame families and communities. **Whispering and malicious backbiting** often kindle and feed the fire.

## Verse 23 — The disguise of an evil heart

A wicked heart covered by attractive speech resembles a broken piece of pottery coated with the waste of silver.

## Verses 24–26 — Beware flattering deceit

Be cautious of pleasing words unless the speaker's character is trustworthy. **Satan spoke attractively to Eve**; trusting his flattering temptations was ruinous.

## Verse 27 — Schemes rebound upon their makers

Plotting evil is hard work, like digging a pit or rolling a heavy stone, but the plotter is often preparing the danger **for himself**.

## Verse 28 — Two kinds of destructive lies

Henry distinguishes **slandering lies**, whose harm is plain, from **flattering lies**, which destroy secretly. A wise person may have more reason to fear a flatterer than a slanderer.
