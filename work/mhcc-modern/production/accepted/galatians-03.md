# Galatians 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Galatians reproved for departing from the great doctrine of justification alone, through faith in Christ. | 1–5 |
| This doctrine established from the example of Abraham. | 6–9 |
| From the tenor of the law and the severity of its curse. | 10–14 |
| From the covenant of promises, which the law could not disannul. | 15–18 |
| The law was a school master to lead them to Christ. | 19–25 |

## Christ crucified and the Spirit received by faith (verses 1–5)

The Galatians' departure from the gospel was especially unreasonable because **Christ crucified had already been clearly preached among them**.

They had also participated in the **Lord's Supper**, where his suffering and its meaning had been set before them.

Had they received the Holy Spirit by keeping the Mosaic law or by any meritorious works of their own?

No.

They received him by **hearing and believing the message of justification through faith in Christ alone**.

God's favor and approval had been demonstrated through that message.

How foolish to reject the ministry and doctrine through which God had already blessed them!

Sadly, people often turn from **Christ crucified**, the very heart of Christianity, toward empty distinctions, moral instruction without the Savior or unsupported religious speculations.

Satan works through different people and methods to blind minds so they will not trust **the crucified Redeemer**.

Where are the Spirit's fruits most clearly produced—where salvation is taught by **works of law**, or where it is taught by **faith in Christ**?

Henry answers confidently: among those who proclaim **justification by faith**.

## Abraham was accepted by faith; Christ bore the curse (verses 6–14)

Paul used **Abraham's example** to prove the gospel doctrine the Galatians had abandoned.

Abraham trusted **God's word and promise**.

Through that faith he was regarded by God as righteous.

When Scripture is said to foresee something, the Holy Spirit who inspired it is the One who foresaw.

Abraham was blessed by **believing God's promise**, and others receive that blessing in the same way.

Let us study what Abraham trusted, the nature of his faith and the effects it produced.

No one escapes the law's curse through obedience, because its curse falls upon sinners and **all have sinned**.

A lawbreaker already stands under condemnation and cannot look to the same law as a way of earning acquittal.

The righteous are those rescued from death and wrath and brought into **life under God's favor**.

This happens **through faith**, not meritorious works.

Justification by faith was not an invention of the Christian era.

It was taught long before Christ's coming and has always been **the only way sinners have been justified**.

Though the law cannot free us from its own curse, faith in Christ can.

Jesus redeemed us by **being made a curse for us**.

Henry explains that Christ was **made sin as a sin offering**, not himself made sinful, and was placed for a time under divine punishment—**not separated from God**.

His suffering warns sinners to flee God's righteous wrath more strongly than the law's threats alone.

If the Father did not spare his Son when he bore our sins, how can the unrepentant expect to escape judgment?

Yet from the cross Christ freely invites sinners to **take refuge in him**.

## God's covenant promise was not cancelled by the law (verses 15–18)

The covenant God made with Abraham was not cancelled when the law came through Moses.

God's promise was given to **Abraham and his Seed**.

It continues through Christ, who lives forever, and through those who belong to him by faith.

This helps us understand the difference between **the law's promises** and **the gospel's promises**.

The law addresses each individual person under its requirements.

The gospel's promises are made **first to Christ**, and then through him to those who are joined to Christ by faith.

We must distinguish **promise and law**, not only as abstract teachings but also in how they shape our desires and our lives.

If the promise is made dependent upon legal merit, it is turned back into law.

Keep Christ continually before your eyes as the sure defense of faith against any attempt to rely upon **human righteousness**.

## The law reveals sin and points toward the promise (verses 19–22)

If God's promise already provided salvation, why was the law added?

Even Israel, the people chosen for special privileges, remained sinful.

The law did not introduce a second route to justification.

Instead, it revealed **the seriousness of sin** and showed sinners their need for God's promise.

It pointed toward **Jesus Christ**, through whom alone sins could be forgiven and people made righteous.

The promise came directly from God.

The law was given through the ministry of angels and through a mediator, **Moses**.

It could not therefore have been intended to cancel the earlier promise.

A mediator acts between parties, not merely for one against the other.

The law's great purpose was that, recognizing their guilt and inability to achieve righteousness by lawkeeping, sinners might believe in Christ and receive **the promised blessing through faith**.

God's holy, just and good law, which remains the standard of moral duty, **cannot be an enemy of Christ's gospel**.

When correctly understood, it supports the gospel's purpose.

## The law leads sinners to Christ as a teacher leads a child (verses 23–25)

The ceremonial law could not itself give **saving knowledge and life**.

Its practices, especially **sacrifices**, pointed forward to Christ so that sinners might be justified **through faith**.

In this sense the law was like a servant charged with taking a child to school.

It led people to **Christ**, the Teacher who reveals the true way of justification and salvation.

The gospel gives believers a much clearer knowledge of divine grace and mercy than the older order did.

Many people remain like prisoners in a dark dungeon, content in their sins and blinded by Satan through worldly pleasures and interests.

When the sinner awakens, the true danger becomes clear.

God's **grace and mercy** now appear as the only hope.

The Holy Spirit often uses **the law's warnings** to awaken a conscience and reveal the need for Christ.

The sinner is moved to rely upon **Christ's suffering and merits** for justification by faith.

Then the law, under the Spirit's instruction, becomes **a loved rule of duty and a standard for daily self-examination**.

Used in this way, it leads the believer to depend **more completely**, not less completely, upon the Savior.

## Through faith, believers become God's children in Christ (verses 26–29)

Genuine Christians enjoy privileges far greater than those experienced under the old covenant.

They are no longer treated like distant servants but received as **children of God**.

Having accepted Jesus Christ as Lord and Savior and relying upon him for justification and salvation, they receive the status of sons and daughters.

But **no outward form or profession**, by itself, secures these privileges.

Anyone who does not have **the Spirit of Christ** does not truly belong to him.

In baptism, believers **put on Christ** and openly profess themselves his disciples.

Baptism points toward being joined with **Christ's death and resurrection**, so that believers die to sin and live in newness and holiness.

But putting on Christ according to the gospel is not merely **outward imitation**.

It requires **new birth and complete inward change**.

The God who makes believers heirs will provide for them.

Therefore let us carry out the duties entrusted to us and commit every other care into his hands.

Our greatest concern should be **heaven**.

This world's things are small by comparison.

The heavenly city of God is the inheritance of his children.

Above all, seek to be assured of that inheritance.
