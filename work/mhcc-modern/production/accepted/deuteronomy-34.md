# Deuteronomy 34 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Moses views the promised land from mount Nebo. | 1–4 |
| The death and burial of Moses, The mourning of the people. | 5–8 |
| Joshua succeeds Moses, The praise of Moses. | 9–12 |

## Moses views Canaan from Mount Nebo (verses 1–4)

Moses had shown no desire to abandon his work before it was completed.

But once his work was finished, he showed no unwillingness to die.

God had made clear that Moses would not enter Canaan.

Yet the Lord also promised that he could see the land.

He gave him a view of the whole good country.

Believers sometimes receive a similar glimpse, through grace, of the happiness and glory prepared for them.

God may reserve the brightest discoveries of his kindness for his people near the end of life, to strengthen them as they die.

Those who die believing in Christ and hoping for heaven can leave this world joyfully.

## Moses dies and Israel mourns (verses 5–8)

Moses obeyed God's command to die as willingly as the other commands he had received, though it might seem harder than any of them.

In this he resembled our Lord Jesus.

Yet there was a profound contrast.

Moses died peacefully, with honor, and without severe physical suffering.

Jesus died on a shameful and agonizing cross.

Moses died **at the mouth of the Lord**, according to God's command.

God's servants, when all their other work is done, must finally die.

They should be prepared to return home whenever their Master calls (Acts 21:13).

No one knew the place where Moses was buried.

If the soul is at rest with God, the place where the body lies matters comparatively little.

Moses' bodily strength had not failed.

His mind remained clear and active, and his memory was as strong as ever.

Henry regards this as a reward for his service and remarkable meekness.

Israel mourned solemnly for him.

But even after great losses, we must not allow grief to consume us.

If we hope to go joyfully to heaven, why should we approach the grave with nothing but sorrow?

## Joshua succeeds Moses; Christ is greater than both (verses 9–12)

Moses brought Israel to the borders of Canaan and then died.

This pictured a deeper truth: **the law makes nothing perfect** (Hebrews 7:19).

It brings sinners into the wilderness of conviction but cannot bring them into the settled rest and peace of the heavenly Canaan.

That work belongs to **Joshua**, whose name is the same as the name of our Lord Jesus.

Joshua was a picture of him.

What the law could not accomplish, Jesus accomplished (Romans 8:3).

Through him we receive peace of conscience now and everlasting rest in heaven.

Moses was greater than every other Old Testament prophet.

Yet Jesus was greater than Moses by far more than Moses surpassed the prophets who came before or after him.

Notice the likeness between Israel's redeemer and the Redeemer of humanity.

Moses was sent by God to free Israel from cruel bondage.

He led the people out and overcame their enemies.

He became their deliverer, lawgiver and judge, bringing them to the very edge of the promised land.

Jesus came to rescue us from slavery to the devil and restore us to freedom and happiness.

He confirmed every moral command God had first given.

But he also writes the law, not upon stone tablets, but upon the living tablets of our hearts.

He will judge humanity.

God has appointed a day when Christ will expose the secrets of every person and reward or punish each one with perfect justice.

Because he is so much greater than Moses, we who follow Christ should obey him faithfully and live consistently with the holy faith we profess.

May God graciously enable us all to do so!
