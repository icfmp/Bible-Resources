# 1 Samuel 10 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Samuel anoints Saul (verses 1–8)

The sacred anointing used for Israel's king pointed forward to **the Messiah, the Anointed One**.

Jesus Christ is King of the church and the great High Priest we confess.

He received the Holy Spirit without measure, far above Israel's former priests and rulers.

Samuel gave Saul signs that would happen that same day to confirm his calling.

The first place Samuel directed him to was an ancestor's tomb.

Saul needed to remember his mortality.

Even with a crown waiting for him, he must remember the grave where every earthly honor becomes dust.

From Samuel's time onward, there seem to have been schools or meeting places where godly young people learned spiritual truth.

Saul would feel strongly moved to join them.

He would be **changed into another man**.

The Spirit of God has power to transform people wonderfully.

Yet Henry distinguishes a temporary change in Saul's conduct from the new birth.

By praising God among his people, Saul became another man.

**Whether he became a genuinely new man in the saving sense remains doubtful.**

## The signs are fulfilled; Saul prophesies (verses 9–16)

Every sign Samuel gave came true exactly.

God granted Saul **another heart**, a different attitude for his new task.

Yet outward devotion and an immediate change of manner must not be trusted too readily.

Saul among the prophets was still Saul.

His anointing was kept private.

He allowed God to carry forward the work through Samuel and waited to see how the matter developed.

## Saul is publicly chosen as king (verses 17–27)

Samuel told Israel that it had rejected God that day.

At first Saul was reluctant to accept a position of power.

Later, once he possessed it, he would be unwilling even to consider surrendering it.

He hid himself when the people sought him.

It is good to recognize our unworthiness and weakness before accepting an important duty.

But we should not go to the opposite extreme and refuse the work that God and his church have entrusted to us.

Most of the people responded with indifference.

Saul returned quietly to his own house.

A group of men accompanied him because God had moved their hearts to support his authority.

Whenever a human heart inclines toward what is right, it is because God has touched it.

**One touch is enough when it comes from him**.

Others despised Saul.

This division pictures different responses to our exalted Redeemer.

Some submit to Jesus Christ and follow him wherever he leads, because the Lord has changed their hearts and made them willing.

Others despise Christ and ask, “How can this man save us?”

They are offended by him and will face judgment if they persist.
