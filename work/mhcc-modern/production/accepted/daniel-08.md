## The ram, goat and little horn (verses 1–14)

God showed Daniel the future destruction of kingdoms that were as powerful in their time as Babylon.

If we could see the political changes that will occur after our deaths, we would be less disturbed by the changes we witness during life.

Henry identifies the **ram with two horns** as **the empire of the Medes and Persians**, the second empire.

The **goat** that overcame it represents **Alexander the Great**.

Alexander died at about **thirty-three years old**, while still in full strength.

His death exposed the emptiness of worldly splendor and power.

Neither can make a person truly happy.

People may dispute the causes of a prosperous conqueror's death, but the great First Cause had no more work for him to accomplish and therefore brought his life to an end.

Henry takes the **four great horns** arising after the first as **Alexander's four principal generals**.

A little horn grew into a great persecutor of God's church and people.

Henry suggests this points to what he calls **the “Mohammedan delusion”**, meaning **Islam**, which he believed had spread and at one time almost destroyed the Christian faith.

This is **his polemical historical identification**, not an identification explicitly stated in the vision.

God is just when he removes sacred privileges from people who despise and profane them.

He may teach people the value of worship **through its absence** when they refused to value it while they possessed it.

Daniel was told that the period of suffering had **a fixed limit**, but was not told **when it would begin**.

To understand God's mind, we must turn to **Christ**, in whom are hidden **all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge**.

They are hidden **for us**, not withheld from us.

Henry acknowledges **considerable difficulty in determining the precise time** and expects the end of that suffering not to be distant.

God will cleanse his church at the time that brings him glory.

**Christ died to cleanse his church**, and he will present her blameless to himself.

## The vision is explained (verses 15–27)

Henry understands the figure appearing as a man before Daniel to be **the eternal Son of God**.

He commanded the angel **Gabriel** to explain the vision.

Daniel's distress and astonishment at the suffering shown to him strengthen Henry's interpretation that **a long period of calamity** lay ahead for his people and the church.

When the vision ended, Daniel was instructed to **keep it private for the time being**.

He did so and **returned to his ordinary duties**.

As long as we live in this world, we have work to do.

Even those whom God has most honored should not believe themselves above everyday responsibilities.

The joy of fellowship with God must not draw us away from **the duties of our callings**.

Instead, we should remain with God **while carrying them out**.

Those entrusted with public responsibilities must fulfill them honestly.

Amid doubts and discouragement, genuine believers can look forward to a good outcome.

Let us learn to settle our minds and attend faithfully to the work appointed to each of us **in the church and in the world**.
