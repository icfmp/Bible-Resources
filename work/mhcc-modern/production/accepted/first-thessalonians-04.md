# 1 Thessalonians 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortations to purity and holiness. | 1–8 |
| To brotherly love, peaceable behaviour, and diligence. | 9–12 |
| Not to sorrow unduly for the death of godly relations and friends, considering the glorious resurrection of their bodies at Christ's second coming. | 13–18 |

## Pursue holiness in body, mind and will (verses 1–8)

It is not enough merely to **remain in the faith of the gospel**.

We must **grow in the work that genuine faith produces**.

Christ's commandments provide the rule for our conduct.

God's will is that believers receive **renewal through the Holy Spirit**, grow in holiness and faithfully perform their assigned responsibilities.

Pursuing this holiness requires careful restraint over **bodily appetites and senses**, as well as over the thoughts, choices and desires that can direct them toward sin.

God calls no one into his family so that the person may live an unholy life.

Rather, he teaches and enables his children **to walk before him in holiness**.

Some people treat moral commands lightly because they hear them from **human ministers**.

But these are **God's commands**, and despising them means **despising God himself**.

## Love one another, live quietly and work faithfully (verses 9–12)

When we see a good quality in another person, we should **acknowledge and commend it**, helping that person grow still more.

Everyone genuinely taught by God learns **to love fellow believers**.

The Holy Spirit's teaching is greater than the teaching of human beings.

Human instruction is useless without God's inward work.

Even people already known for love and other virtues need to **grow in them and persevere until the end**.

It is especially desirable to cultivate a **peaceful spirit and calm manner of life**.

Satan works to stir up conflict, and our own hearts are too willing to become agitated.

We should therefore make it our purpose **to live quietly**.

People who meddle continually in other people's affairs often have little peace themselves and create great disturbances among their neighbors.

They often neglect the related command **to work diligently in their own responsibilities**.

Christianity does not remove us from ordinary employment and household duties.

It calls us to be **faithful and diligent within them**.

Laziness can cause serious poverty and hardship.

Those who work diligently at appropriate tasks often earn what they need and find honest satisfaction in their labor.

## Hope in the resurrection brings comfort in bereavement (verses 13–18)

These verses comfort **family and friends of believers who die**.

Grieving the death of loved ones is entirely appropriate.

We may weep for **our loss**, even while their death is **their gain**.

Christian faith does not forbid natural affection, and saving grace does not erase our human feelings.

But we should resist becoming overwhelmed with grief in the manner of those who have **no hope of a better life**.

Death is unfamiliar, and we know little through ordinary experience about what follows it.

Yet we have reliable assurance of **the resurrection and the return of Jesus Christ**.

These truths relieve the fear of death and bring genuine comfort when Christian friends die.

All God's people will meet again and **never be separated**.

But the chief happiness of heaven is **being with the Lord himself**: seeing him, living with him and enjoying him forever.

We should encourage one another during seasons of sorrow rather than weakening each other's courage or increasing despair.

The resurrection and the Lord's return offer many truths with which we can give **real comfort**.

Yet the thought of appearing before God's judgment seat is comforting only for those who have reason to know **their sins have been forgiven**.

The Holy Spirit must bear witness within them, and their hearts must be **renewed and purified** so that they love God and sincerely seek his honor.

We cannot regard ourselves as safe unless this is true of us, or at least **we earnestly desire this renewing work**.
