# 2 Samuel 20 — Modern English

## Sheba rebels against David (verses 1–3)

One trial follows another for God's people, for their good, until they reach the place where sin and sorrow are forever removed.

Angry people arguing with one another often misunderstand or twist each other's words.

Proud people insist that everything go their way or else refuse to give any assistance.

The favor of the crowd is not dependable.

Consider how quickly the cries of **“Hosanna to the Son of David”** changed into **“Crucify him! Crucify him!”**

## Joab murders Amasa (verses 4–13)

Joab murdered **Amasa** brutally.

The more deliberate planning involved in a sin, the worse it is.

Joab readily sacrificed the interests of both the king and the kingdom to satisfy his own desire for revenge.

How could someone guilty of murder bring himself to pursue a traitor?

How could he enter danger so boldly when his conscience was weighed down with such guilt?

His conscience had become hardened and unfeeling.

## Sheba finds refuge in Abel (verses 14–22)

A city that knowingly shelters a traitor exposes itself to judgment.

The human heart fares no better when it protects rebellious sinful desires that will not allow **Christ to reign**.

A wise woman acted prudently.

She satisfied Joab's demand and saved the city from destruction.

Wisdom is not restricted by social position or sex.

Nor does it consist merely in knowing many complicated things.

It consists in understanding how to act when a problem arises, how to prevent harm and how to secure a good outcome.

Many disasters could be prevented if the parties involved made an effort to understand one another and clear up mistaken assumptions.

The condition for peace here was to surrender **the traitor**.

Henry sees a picture of how God deals with the soul when it is troubled by conviction.

**Sin is the traitor.**

A cherished sinful desire is the rebel.

If we part with that desire and cast away the wrongdoing, we can find peace.

There is no peace with God while we insist on protecting rebellion against him.

## David's officers (verses 23–26)

This passage describes David's court after he regained his throne.

It is good when competent people are appointed to carry out public duties.

Let everyone who receives responsibility seek to carry it out faithfully, as a servant of the **Son of David**.
