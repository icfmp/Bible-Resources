# Joel 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's judgments in the latter days. | 1–8 |
| The extent of these judgments. | 9–17 |
| The blessings the church shall enjoy. | 18–21 |

## Judgment upon the nations (verses 1–8)

Henry understands this passage as foretelling **the restoration of the Jews** and **the final victory of true religion** over its enemies.

It recalls the contempt with which Jewish people have often been treated and the low value placed upon their lives.

No one has hardened the heart against **God or his church** and enjoyed lasting prosperity.

## The day of judgment (verses 9–17)

The prophecy challenges **all who oppose God's people**.

There will be no escape from divine judgment.

In that day, unrepentant sinners will be cut off from every source of **comfort and joy**.

Henry notes that many prophets predict **the final victory of God's church** over those who oppose it.

The same day will be **terrible for the wicked but joyful for the righteous**.

Those who belong to Christ have reason to rejoice in **their Strength and Redeemer**.

The **acceptable year of the Lord**, so full of mercy for some, will be a day of extraordinary judgment for others.

Everyone who remains apart from Christ should awaken and **flee from the wrath to come**.

## The church's future blessings (verses 18–21)

God will pour out his blessings abundantly, and **the gospel will spread swiftly to the farthest regions of the earth**.

The prophet speaks through **meaningful symbols**.

A day is coming when everything wrong will finally be **made right**.

The source of these abundant blessings is **God's own house**, from which the streams flow.

**Christ is that Fountain**.

His suffering, merit and grace **cleanse, refresh and make people spiritually fruitful**.

The grace of the gospel flowing from Christ will reach **the Gentile world**, even the most distant lands.

It will produce the **fruits of righteousness** among them.

All the good we enjoy now—and all we hope to enjoy forever—flows from **the Lord's house above, his heavenly temple**.
