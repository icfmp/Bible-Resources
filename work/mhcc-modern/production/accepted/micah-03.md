# Micah 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The cruelty of the princes, and the falsehood of the prophets. | 1–8 |
| Their false security. | 9–12 |

## Cruel rulers and dishonest prophets (verses 1–8)

People cannot expect to live wickedly and yet enjoy lasting prosperity.

They may experience the same kind of harm they have done to others.

How rarely do **uncomfortable but wholesome truths** reach the ears of those with high rank and public authority!

People who mislead others are preparing **humiliation for themselves**.

Micah possessed a burning love for God and for people's souls.

He cared deeply about **God's glory, their salvation and the destruction caused by sin**.

The difficulties he encountered did not drive him away from his calling.

His strength came **not from himself**, but because he was **filled with the Spirit of the Lord**.

People who act honestly can also act **boldly**.

Those who come to hear God's word should be willing to have their sins pointed out.

They should receive correction graciously and with gratitude.

## The false confidence of leaders (verses 9–12)

Zion's walls are no reason to honor people who built them by **shed blood and injustice**.

**Human sin does not accomplish God's righteousness**.

Even acts that are outwardly useful become hateful to God and to people when they are done for **dishonest profit**.

Faith rests upon **the Lord as the soul's foundation**.

Presumption merely leans upon God as a support when it is convenient, attempting to make him serve private interests.

Claiming that the Lord dwells among us does not protect us from judgment when his presence has **not restrained our evil conduct**.

Micah announced that Zion would be **plowed like a field** because of its leaders' sin.

Henry says the prophecy was fulfilled **at the destruction of Jerusalem by the Romans** and remained evident in his own day.

The historical interpretation belongs to Henry.

When sacred places are polluted with sin, God may rightly bring them to ruin.
