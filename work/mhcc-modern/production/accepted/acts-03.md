# Acts 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A lame man healed by Peter and John. | 1–11 |
| Peter's address to the Jews. | 12–26 |

## Peter heals a man who could not walk (verses 1–11)

The apostles and first Christians continued to attend the temple's times of prayer.

Peter and John seem to have received divine direction to perform this miracle upon a man **more than forty years old**, who had been unable to walk since birth.

In the name of **Jesus of Nazareth**, Peter commanded him to stand and walk.

If we want to help spiritually helpless people, we likewise must act **in Christ's name and through his power**.

We call sinners to rise and walk in holiness **through faith in him**.

What a delightful thought: every faculty of our fallen nature that has become powerless through sin can be healed **by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth**.

What holy joy will we experience when God's Spirit strengthens us to enter the courts of his house!

## Peter addresses the crowd (verses 12–26)

### The source of the miracle (verses 12–18)

Notice the difference between Jesus and the apostles when performing miracles.

Jesus spoke with **almighty authority** and did not refuse the honor due to his divine miracles.

The apostles, by contrast, referred every miracle to Christ and rejected honor for themselves except as **unworthy instruments of his power**.

This reveals Jesus' unity and equality with the Father, while the apostles remained weak and sinful human beings wholly dependent upon him.

Useful servants must therefore be deeply humble.

**Not to us, Lord, but to your name belongs the glory.**

Every crown must be placed at Christ's feet.

Peter showed the crowd the seriousness of its guilt in rejecting Jesus, but did not intend to drive sinners into anger or despair.

Those who reject Christ act in ignorance, but that **does not excuse their wrongdoing**.

### Repentance and times of refreshing (verses 19–21)

Everyone seeking forgiveness and the comfort of Christ's pardoning love must be urgently called to **repentance**.

Blessed are those who have experienced the refreshing assurance that forgiven sins bring.

The Holy Spirit did not reveal the precise dates and periods of every future event.

Such matters remain obscure and are not necessary for faithfulness.

When sinners recognize their wrongdoing, they call upon God for pardon.

To those who repent, turn to Christ and believe, **times of refreshing come from the Lord's presence**.

During our earthly period of testing, the glorified Savior remains physically out of sight; we are called to **live by faith**.

### Moses' prophecy and the blessing of turning from sin (verses 22–26)

Peter cited the words of **Moses**, the prophet whom the Jewish hearers most honored and in whose name some were tempted to reject Christianity.

Moses' warning exposed the terrible consequences of refusing to hear Christ.

Jesus came to bring a blessing.

He also sent his Spirit as the great blessing of redemption.

**Christ blesses us by turning us away from our wrongdoing and saving us from our sins**.

By nature, people cling to sin. Divine grace changes that affection so we not only leave sin behind but **come to hate it**.

No one should imagine happiness is possible by continuing in wrongdoing when God teaches that the blessing consists in being **turned away from evil**.

It is not enough to seek rescue merely from the **punishment** sin deserves.

Anyone who truly understands the gospel also desires to be rescued from **sin itself**.

And no one should expect such a change apart from faith in and reception of **Jesus Christ, the Son of God**, our wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption.
