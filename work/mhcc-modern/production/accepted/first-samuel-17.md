# 1 Samuel 17 — Modern English

## Goliath challenges Israel (verses 1–11)

Human beings depend entirely on God in everything.

When he withdraws his help, even the bravest and most determined people find themselves without courage or strength. Everyday experience confirms this truth.

## David arrives at the camp (verses 12–30)

Jesse had no idea what would happen when he sent his son to the army at such a critical moment.

But God wisely orders human activities and circumstances to accomplish his own purposes.

When most people have grown formal and lukewarm in religion, anyone willing to go farther or risk more for God's cause is likely to be accused of pride and ambition.

Such criticism may come especially from close relatives, like **Eliab**, or careless superiors.

This tested David's gentleness, patience and perseverance.

Although reason and justice were on his side, he did not repay insult with insult. A gentle reply turned away his brother's anger.

**Conquering his own temper was a greater honor than defeating Goliath.**

People who undertake important public service should expect to be misunderstood or opposed, even by those whose encouragement and assistance they had anticipated.

They must humbly keep working despite enemies' threats and friends' suspicions.

## David offers to fight Goliath (verses 31–39)

David had come straight from caring for sheep that very morning.

Yet this young shepherd had more courage than Israel's mighty warriors.

God often sends useful counsel and accomplishes great things through people whom the world considers weak or foolish.

Just as David answered his brother's anger with gentleness, he answered Saul's fear with **faith**.

When he tended sheep, David had been careful and tender toward them.

This reminds us of Christ, the good Shepherd, who not only endangered himself for the sheep but gave his life for them.

Past deliverances should encourage us to trust God now and act boldly in our duty. The One who delivered before is still able and willing to deliver.

Saul gave David permission to fight.

But David was not comfortable wearing Saul's armor, which he had never used.

God arranged even this, so that David's victory would be seen clearly as the fruit of **faith**, not impressive equipment.

A tool is not valuable to us merely because it is excellent in itself. It must be **suitable for the person and the work**.

Saul's fine coat and strong armor could not help David when they did not fit him.

But **faith, prayer, truth, righteousness, the whole armor of God and the mind of Christ** are needed by every servant of God, whatever that servant's calling.

## David goes out to meet Goliath (verses 40–47)

Foolish self-confidence and presumption lead to destruction.

David's answer displayed extraordinary humility, faith and devotion.

He expected God to give him victory.

He was content to appear poorly armed and outwardly insignificant, so that everyone would know the victory belonged to the Lord alone.

## David defeats Goliath (verses 48–58)

Human life is frail and uncertain, even when someone believes himself fully protected.

How quickly death can come through a very small thing!

Let the strong never boast of their strength, nor the armed of their weapons.

God resists the proud and brings shame upon those who defy him and his people.

No one who stubbornly sets himself against God will ultimately prosper.

The account encourages us to serve God's cause with courage and unwavering trust in him.

Every follower of the Lamb must enter a conflict against an enemy **more formidable than Goliath**.

The devil still challenges God's people.

But resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

Go forward with David's faith, and the powers of darkness will not stand against you.

How often, however, Christians suffer defeat through the unbelief still found in their own hearts!
