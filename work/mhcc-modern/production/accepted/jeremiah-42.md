# Jeremiah 42 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Johanan desires Jeremiah to ask counsel of God. | 1–6 |
| They are assured of safety in Judea, but of destruction in Egypt. | 7–22 |

## Johanan asks Jeremiah to seek God's counsel (verses 1–6)

The commanders sought Jeremiah's help when it suited their purposes.

Whenever we face difficult or uncertain choices, we should **look to God for direction**.

We may pray in faith for **the Spirit's wisdom within our hearts** and guidance through **God's Providence**.

But we do not genuinely desire to know God's will unless we have already resolved to **obey what he reveals**.

Many promise to do whatever the Lord commands while inwardly hoping that his answer will **flatter their pride** and **leave their favorite sins untouched**.

Something will eventually expose the true condition of their hearts.

## God's answer commands them to remain in Judah (verses 7–22)

If we wish to know God's will in doubtful circumstances, we must **wait as well as pray**.

He is ready to show mercy again to those he has corrected.

He rejects no one who trusts his promises.

His word gives enough encouragement to calm even the **unreasonable fears** that keep his people from their duties.

Whatever loss or hardship obedience may appear to bring, God's promises provide for it.

He will protect and deliver those who **trust and serve him**.

It is folly to leave the place where our duty lies merely because we meet trouble there, especially when doing so means abandoning **the land God had set apart**.

The very dangers we hope to avoid through sin are often brought upon us **by the sin itself**.

This applies to ordinary life as well.

Someone may hope to escape human suffering by moving elsewhere. Yet the troubles common to humanity will follow wherever that person goes.

Those who make solemn promises to God while deliberately hiding their intentions deserve serious rebuke.

**Their conduct reveals more than their words.**

We do not always know what is truly good for us.

What we most eagerly desire and cling to may prove harmful—or even fatal.
