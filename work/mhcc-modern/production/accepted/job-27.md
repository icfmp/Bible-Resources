# Job 27 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job protests his sincerity. | 1–6 |
| The hypocrite is without hope. | 7–10 |
| The miserable end of the wicked. | 11–23 |

## Job maintains his sincerity (verses 1–6)

Job's friends finally let him speak without interruption.

He continued with gravity and useful instruction.

Job trusted in both **the justice of his cause** and **the goodness of his God**.

He willingly entrusted his case to him.

Yet Job did not show proper reverence when he said God had denied him justice and afflicted his soul.

Holding firmly to our integrity, while resolving not to give our conscience reason to accuse us, frustrates the evil one's designs.

## The hypocrite has no secure hope (verses 7–10)

Job considered the position of hypocrites and wicked people deeply miserable.

Even if an insincere profession of religion brought them advantages throughout life, and they held on to false confidence until death, **what good would that do when God required their souls?**

The more genuine comfort we find in religion, the more firmly we cling to it.

People who find no delight in God are readily drawn away by the pleasures of this life and overcome by its difficulties.

## The final ruin of the wicked (verses 11–23)

Job's friends had described the worldly suffering of wicked people as always matching the severity of their sins.

Job considered that **even if that does not happen**, the outcome after death is dreadful.

He therefore presented a truer view of the matter.

For a godly person, death is like a **favorable wind carrying a ship toward a heavenly homeland**.

For an unrepentant sinner, it is like a **storm driving the person toward destruction**.

During life, such a sinner benefits from God's patient mercy.

But death ends that period of patience, and judgment follows.

When the Lord brings someone down in judgment, there is no fleeing from or resisting his anger.

People who refuse now to flee to **the open arms of divine grace** will not later escape divine judgment.

What good is it to gain the entire world and lose one's own soul?
