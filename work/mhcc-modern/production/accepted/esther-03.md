# Esther 3 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

| Haman seeks to destroy the Jews. | 1–6 |

## Haman seeks to destroy the Jews (verses 1–6)

Mordecai refused to show Haman the reverence demanded of him.

The Jewish faith forbade giving any human being honors that resembled idolatrous worship, especially a wicked person like Haman.

Human beings naturally tend toward idolatry: **self is our favorite idol**, and we enjoy being treated as though everything belonged to us.

Faith does not abolish good manners. On the contrary, it teaches us to give appropriate honor to those who deserve it.

Yet someone belonging to Zion must regard a wicked person like Haman as contemptible, not only inwardly but in judgment and conduct (**Psalm 15:4**).

True believers cannot obey decrees or follow fashions that violate God's commands.

They must **obey God rather than human beings**, leaving the consequences in his hands.

Haman became furious.

His plot reflected the spirit of the murderer from the beginning, whose hatred toward Christ and the church rules those who follow him.

## Haman obtains a decree (verses 7–15)

Anyone who knows the human heart and human history should understand how a ruler could agree to a proposal so cruel and contrary to his own interests.

We should be thankful for mild and just government.

Haman tried to discover, through his superstition, an **auspicious day** for the massacre he intended.

God's wisdom used that folly to accomplish his own purposes.

The lot Haman cast delayed the date of the planned killing and thereby helped bring about his defeat.

The episode displays God's particular Providence in human affairs and his care for the church.

Haman feared that the king might be troubled by what he had approved, so he encouraged him to continue drinking.

People often try to drown their convictions and harden their own hearts and others' hearts through intoxication.

Everything seemed to be moving toward Haman's goal.

Yet God can allow sinners to progress far in their plans before his unseen, almighty power turns them back.

Even the strongest attack against Jehovah is vain.

If Haman had destroyed the Jewish people, how could God's promises and prophecies about **the world's Redeemer** have been fulfilled?

**God's everlasting covenant would have had to fail first.**
