# Mark 9 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## The transfiguration (verses 1–13)

Jesus predicted that his kingdom would soon be displayed in power.

The transfiguration gave his disciples **a glimpse of that kingdom**.

It is good to be removed from the distractions of the world and alone with Christ.

How much better will it be to enjoy Christ's glory in heaven with all the saints!

But even joyful experiences can create a temptation: when we are comfortable, we may stop thinking about the needs and difficulties of other people.

The Father publicly acknowledged Jesus as **his beloved Son**.

God accepts him and is willing to accept us through him.

We should therefore acknowledge Jesus as our beloved Savior, accept him and give ourselves to **his government**.

Christ does not abandon the soul merely because its experiences of joy and comfort have faded.

Jesus then explained the prophecy about **Elijah**, especially fitting in light of the poor treatment **John the Baptist** had received.

## Jesus delivers a child from an evil spirit (verses 14–29)

The child's father complained that the disciples had been unable to cure him.

Jesus instead directed attention toward **the lack of faith** involved.

Much is promised to those who believe.

A hard heart can be softened, spiritual diseases can be healed, and people conscious of their weakness can persevere to the end.

Those who grieve over unbelief should seek **Christ's grace to overcome it**.

His grace is sufficient.

Everyone Christ heals is healed effectively.

Satan does not leave willingly those whom he has enslaved for a long time.

When he can no longer deceive or destroy someone, he may still attempt to fill the soul with fear and turmoil.

The disciples must not assume every task in Christ's service will be equally easy.

Some difficult work requires **more than ordinary effort**.

## Jesus corrects the apostles' pride and intolerance (verses 30–40)

Jesus' suffering was approaching.

It would be terrible for even demons to treat him in such a way.

It is all the more astonishing that **human beings should abuse the Son of Man who came to redeem them**.

But Christ never spoke of his death without also speaking of **his resurrection**, which would remove his disgrace and should have lessened the disciples' sorrow.

Many people remain ignorant because **they are ashamed to ask questions**.

Jesus explained his love and grace clearly, but the disciples still struggled to understand.

We will have to answer for our speech and discussions, especially arguments about **which of us deserves the greatest honor**.

People most humble and self-denying resemble Christ most closely and will receive his warmest approval.

Jesus illustrated this with a child: welcoming such a child in Christ's name is welcoming Christ himself.

The disciples wanted to silence a person who was successfully helping sinners through the name of Jesus but was **not working with their own group**.

People today can make the same mistake.

Jesus corrected them.

Anyone truly bringing sinners to **repentance, faith in Christ and holy conduct** is demonstrating that the Lord is at work.

We should not reject genuine gospel service merely because the minister belongs to another circle of disciples.

## Sin is worse than suffering (verses 41–50)

Jesus repeatedly spoke of the wicked person's **worm that never dies and fire that is never extinguished**.

Henry understands the never-dying worm as **the remorse of conscience and painful self-awareness** that persist in judgment.

Enduring severe hardship, suffering and self-denial during this short life is immeasurably better than enjoying every worldly pleasure for a time and suffering eternally afterward.

Like offerings presented in sacrifice, we must be **seasoned with salt**.

Our sinful desires and inclinations must be subdued and put to death **by the Holy Spirit**.

People who possess the **salt of grace** should show that a living principle of grace truly governs their hearts.

It will drive out corrupt tendencies that offend God or trouble their consciences.
