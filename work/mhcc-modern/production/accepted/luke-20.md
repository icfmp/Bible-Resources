# Luke 20 — Modern English

## Jesus' authority is challenged (verses 1–8)

Some people claim to be investigating the evidence for revelation and the truth of the gospel, when they are really looking for excuses to continue in unbelief.

Jesus responded to the priests and scribes with a simple question about **John's baptism**.

Ordinary people could have answered it.

They knew John's ministry came from heaven and aimed at spiritual rather than worldly ends.

When people bury the knowledge already given to them, it is right that they should be denied further understanding.

The religious leaders refused to acknowledge what they knew.

Jesus therefore rightly declined to explain his own authority to people who would not accept the divine origin of John's mission.

## The tenants of the vineyard (verses 9–19)

Jesus addressed the parable to people determined to reject his authority despite abundant evidence for it.

How many still resemble those who killed the prophets and ultimately crucified Christ!

Their hostility to God springs from a desire to live according to sinful passions **without being restrained**.

Everyone privileged to possess God's word should use that blessing faithfully.

The judgment will be terrible for those who reject **the Son**.

It will also come upon those who claim to honor him but fail to produce the fruits of obedience in due season.

The hearers recognized that the punishment in the parable was just.

Yet they could not bear to hear its application to themselves.

Sinners often persevere in a destructive path even while fearing its final consequences.

## Paying taxes and giving God what belongs to him (verses 20–26)

People plotting against Christ and his gospel cannot hide their motives from him.

Jesus refused to be trapped into the false choice his questioners presented.

He exposed their dishonest intention without giving them a statement that would allow them to turn either the governor or the people against him.

Wisdom from heaven can guide those who sincerely teach God's way around traps laid by malicious people.

It also clarifies **our duties toward God, civil rulers and other human beings**.

By faithfully fulfilling those duties, we prevent opponents from making justified accusations against us.

## Jesus answers the Sadducees about resurrection (verses 27–38)

People seeking to undermine God's truth often create artificial difficulties and then treat them as decisive objections.

We mislead ourselves and misrepresent Christ's teaching when we imagine **the unseen future world must operate exactly like the visible present world**.

There is a world we see now, and a world still unseen.

Consider both carefully and give your greatest attention to the one that matters most.

Believers will share in **the blessed resurrection of the dead**.

We cannot fully imagine or describe their future happiness (**1 Corinthians 2:9**).

Those who enter their Lord's joy are entirely occupied with him.

Where holiness is perfect, the restraints needed against sin here are no longer necessary.

God called himself **the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob**.

He was fully sufficient for them (**Genesis 17:1**) and their exceeding great reward (**Genesis 15:1**).

Yet their earthly lives did not receive everything embraced in those promises.

There must therefore be another life in which God fulfills **the full extent of his covenant commitment** to them.

## Jesus silences the scribes (verses 39–47)

Some scribes praised Jesus' answer about resurrection.

Yet his question about the Messiah silenced them.

**Christ as God is David's Lord; Christ as man is David's descendant.**

Jesus warned that scribes who exploited **poor widows** would face severe judgment.

They also misused religious practices, especially **prayer**, to conceal worldly and wicked schemes.

Hypocritical devotion adds another sin to wrongdoing.

Let us ask God to keep us from pride, ambition, greed and every other evil.

May he teach us to seek the honor that comes **from him alone**.
