# Genesis 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Noah, his family and the living creatures enter the ark; the flood begins. | 1–12 |
| Noah is shut inside the ark. | 13–16 |

| All living creatures outside the ark perish. | 21–24 |

## Noah enters the ark and the flood begins (verses 1–12)

God's invitation to Noah was full of kindness. It was like a loving father calling his children indoors when he sees night approaching or a storm gathering.

Noah knew that the ark would be his refuge, yet he did not enter until God commanded him to do so. What comfort there is in knowing that God goes before us at every step!

Noah had worked hard to build the ark; now that very ark would keep him alive. Whatever we do in faith and obedience to God's commands will bring us comfort, sooner or later.

God's invitation to Noah reminds us of the gospel's invitation to needy sinners. Christ is the ark in whom alone we can be safe when death and judgment draw near.

God's word says, “Come.” His ministers say, “Come.” The Spirit says, “Come—come into the ark!”

Noah was counted righteous not because of any righteousness of his own, but because he was an heir of the righteousness that comes through faith (Hebrews 11:7).

He believed God's revelation of a Savior and sought and expected salvation through him alone. Thus he was justified by faith and received the Spirit, whose fruit is every kind of goodness. Whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him.

After 120 years, God gave the people another seven days to repent. But they wasted those days just as they had wasted all the years before.

Only one week remained—one more Sabbath in which to consider what would bring them peace.

Those who have neglected their souls through long years of health, treating death as something far away, often remain just as careless during their last days of sickness, even when death is close. Their hearts have been hardened by sin's deceitfulness.

By faith in God's warning, Noah prepared the ark because a flood was coming. By that same faith, he entered the ark when God warned him that the flood would come very soon.

On the very day Noah was safely settled inside, the fountains of the great deep burst open.

The earth contained waters which, at God's command, sprang up and flooded it. Similarly, our bodies contain fluids that, when God permits, can become the sources of deadly diseases.

The windows of heaven opened too, pouring onto the earth the waters above the firmament—that is, in the air.

Ordinary rain falls in drops. But the rain that came then was unlike any rain before or since. For forty days and nights it fell without stopping or easing, over the whole earth at once.

God exercised his almighty power in a special way to bring about the flood. It is therefore useless and presumptuous to think that human wisdom can fully explain how he did it.

## God shuts Noah inside the ark (verses 13–16)

Even fierce and dangerous animals became gentle and manageable in the ark. Yet when that occasion passed, they remained the same kinds of creatures as before. Being in the ark had not changed their natures.

So it is with hypocrites in the church. They may outwardly conform to the rules of that ark, but their hearts remain unchanged. Sooner or later their true character will appear.

God continued to care for Noah. He shut the door to keep Noah safe inside and to keep everyone else outside for good. God has not told us exactly how he shut it.

Noah's safety in the ark illustrates many of our duties and privileges under the gospel. The apostle speaks of it as a picture of Christian baptism (1 Peter 3:20–21).

Our great duty, in obedience to the gospel call, is to enter by living faith in Christ the way of salvation God has prepared for sinners.

Those who enter the ark should seek to bring as many others as possible, through sound teaching, loving persuasion, and good example. There is room in Christ for everyone who comes.

God placed Adam in Paradise but did not shut him in, so Adam cast himself out. When God placed Noah in the ark, however, he shut him in.

Likewise, when God brings a soul to Christ, its salvation is secure. Its safety does not rest in its own keeping, but in the Mediator's hands.

Yet the door of mercy will soon be shut to those who now treat it lightly. Knock now, while the door may still be opened (Luke 13:25).

## The waters rise over the earth (verses 17–20)

The flood continued to rise for forty days. The waters rose so high that they covered the tops of even the highest mountains by more than twenty feet.

No place on earth is high enough to put human beings beyond the reach of God's judgments. God's hand will find every one of his enemies (Psalm 21:8).

As the waters rose, the ark rose with them. The very waters that destroyed everything else carried the ark safely upward.

What means death leading to death for unbelievers becomes life leading to life for the faithful.

## All living creatures outside the ark perish (verses 21–24)

Every man, woman, and child in the world outside the ark died in the flood. We can easily imagine the terror that seized them.

Our Savior tells us that until the very day the flood came, people were eating and drinking (Luke 17:26–27). They were deaf and blind to every divine warning.

Death overtook them while they lived that way. They realized their folly when it was too late.

We may suppose they tried every possible means of saving themselves, but all their efforts failed.

Those who are not found in Christ, the true Ark, are certainly ruined—ruined forever.

Consider this terrible judgment. Who can stand before the Lord when he is angry?

Unless sinners repent, their sin will eventually bring them to ruin. The righteous God knows how to bring destruction upon the ungodly world (2 Peter 2:5).

How dreadful will the day of judgment and the destruction of ungodly people be!

How happy are those who belong to Christ's family and are safe with him. They can look forward without terror and rejoice that they will triumph when fire consumes the earth and everything in it.

We are quick to imagine that something about ourselves or our circumstances will make us exceptions.

But if we neglect, refuse, or misuse the salvation Christ offers, our imagined advantages will not save us. We will perish along with the unbelieving world.
