# Ezekiel 21 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The ruin of Judah under the emblem of a sharp sword. | 1–17 |
| The approach of the king of Babylon described. | 18–27 |
| The destruction of the Ammonites. | 28–32 |

## The sword of judgment approaches Judah (verses 1–17)

This passage explains the parable at the end of the previous chapter.

The Lord announced that **Jerusalem and the whole land** would be cut down.

Everyone would learn that the judgment came from **his decree against a wicked and rebellious people**.

Those who proclaim God's dreadful judgment on sinners should make it clear that they **do not delight in the day of suffering**.

**Christ's example teaches us to weep over the people whose destruction we must announce.**

Whatever instruments the Lord chooses to execute judgment, he gives them the strength required for their appointed task.

The sword flashes with terror before those against whom it is drawn.

To some it is a **sword**; to the Lord's people it is a **rod of discipline**.

God is serious about this sentence, and the prophet must show the same seriousness in proclaiming it.

## Nebuchadnezzar is directed toward Jerusalem (verses 18–27)

Through the Spirit of prophecy, Ezekiel foresaw **Nebuchadnezzar marching from Babylon**.

The king would decide his direction by **divination**.

Yet the Lord's wise purposes governed the outcome.

God would overthrow Judah's government until the arrival of **the One whose right it is to rule**.

Henry sees this as pointing beyond the immediate events to the continuing upheavals of the Jewish nation and the changes of earthly states and kingdoms.

These would prepare the way for **the Messiah's kingdom throughout the earth**.

The Lord secretly directs events toward his wise designs.

Even amid the most terrifying warnings of judgment, we hear a word of **mercy**, pointing toward the One through whom mercy comes to sinful people.

## Ammon's false confidence ends in destruction (verses 28–32)

The Ammonites' diviners falsely promised victory.

The nation would ultimately lose its power and disappear from memory.

Let us be thankful that God has appointed us to **serve as instruments of mercy**.

We should use our understanding and abilities **to do good**, and keep away from those whose only skill is **causing destruction**.
