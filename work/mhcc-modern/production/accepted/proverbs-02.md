# Proverbs 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Promises to those who seek wisdom. | 1–9 |
| The advantages of wisdom. | 10–22 |

## Seeking the wisdom God freely gives (verses 1–9)

Those who earnestly seek wisdom from heaven will never regret the effort they have made.

**The gift is free**, but that does not remove the need for **diligence in seeking it** (**John 6:27**).

Those who seek will find; those who ask will receive.

Who receives this blessing?

The righteous, upon whom **God's image is renewed**, an image that includes true righteousness.

When we depend upon God and seek wisdom from him, he will enable us to **walk in the paths of justice**.

## Wisdom protects the heart and conduct (verses 10–22)

Those who are truly wise avoid evil company and evil practices.

When wisdom governs us, it does not merely fill the mind with information.

**It enters the heart**.

It protects against both the corruption already within us and temptation arriving from outside.

Sin's ways are dark, unsafe and joyless.

How foolish to leave the bright and peaceful paths of uprightness for such roads!

Some people delight in both **committing sin** and **watching others sin**.

A wise person avoids their company.

He also avoids people who encourage lustful actions that defile the body, **the living temple**, and fight against the soul.

These are dangers that distress godly people and give thoughtful parents reason to be concerned for their children.

The suffering of others should serve as a warning.

Our Lord Jesus warned against sinful pleasures by telling us of **the everlasting sufferings that may follow them**.

People caught in these traps rarely free themselves easily.

Sin's deceit hardens the heart and blinds the understanding.

Henry notes that many readers also understand these warnings **figuratively**, applying them to idolatry or to making the soul a servant of the body by pursuing forbidden objects.

The righteous and wicked alike will eventually leave this earth.

But the earth serves **very different purposes** for them.

For an unrepentant sinner, it is **all the heaven that person will ever possess**.

For the righteous, it is **a place to prepare for heaven**.

Does it not matter to us whether we share the wicked person's end or the everlasting joys promised to believers?
