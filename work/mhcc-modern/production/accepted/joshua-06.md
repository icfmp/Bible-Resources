# Joshua 6 — Modern English

## The siege of Jericho (verses 1–5)

Jericho was determined not to submit to Israel. The city was strongly fortified, both by its position and by human defenses, and its gates were shut.

But the people were foolishly strengthening themselves against the Almighty. Their hardened hearts would bring about their destruction.

God had determined that Israel would take Jericho, and quickly. The people were not commanded to prepare an ordinary military siege.

By this unusual method, the Lord honored the ark as the visible sign of his presence. He taught Israel that every victory would come from him.

The people's faith and patience were tested and strengthened.

## Israel marches around the city (verses 6–16)

Wherever the ark went, the people followed.

Likewise, ministers should sound the trumpet of the everlasting gospel, proclaiming freedom and victory and encouraging Christians in their spiritual conflict.

God's promised deliverances must be sought **in his way** and awaited **at his time**.

At last, when the command came, the people shouted, and Jericho's walls fell.

This was a **shout of faith**: they believed God's promise that the walls would fall.

It was also a **shout of prayer**: they cried to heaven for help, and help came.

## Jericho falls, and Rahab's family is spared (verses 17–27)

Jericho became a solemn and dreadful offering to God's justice against a people who had filled up the measure of their sins.

The One who had given the inhabitants life as their Creator now carried out the sentence their sins had deserved.

Rahab was not destroyed with those who did not believe (Hebrews 11:31).

Her relatives were spared with her. In this Henry sees a picture of the salvation that comes to a household through faith in Christ (Acts 16:31).

They were rescued like burning pieces of wood snatched from the fire.

The choice before us is equally serious: will we belong to Rahab's company or to the people of Jericho?

Our portion depends on whether we receive or reject the sign of salvation—**faith in Christ working through love**.

Let us remember the weight of that choice and act accordingly.

Where God's curse rests, no one can escape its consequences. It brings ruin beyond remedy.
