# Psalm 13 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The psalmist complains that God had long withdrawn. He earnestly prays for comfort. He assures himself of an answer of peace.**

## From long sorrow to confidence in mercy

At times God hides his face, and his children are left uncertain about their relationship with him.

They feel this **more deeply than any outward hardship**.

Yet believers often burden themselves with anxieties beyond what is necessary.

**The bread of sorrow** may become their daily food.

Our own Master, Jesus Christ, was **a Man of Sorrows**.

During long afflictions, a common temptation is to imagine that suffering will never end.

People who have been without joy for a long time may begin losing hope as well.

Let our complaints **drive us to prayer**, not away from it.

Nothing is more devastating to the soul than feeling deprived of God's favor.

Nothing is more reviving than the return of that favor.

The sudden, joyful changes recorded in the Psalms are remarkable.

In **verse 4**, everything seems full of gloomy despair.

But in **verse 5**, the psalmist rises above his fears and throws himself entirely upon the mercy and care of his **divine Redeemer**.

See the power of faith and the goodness of approaching God.

When we bring our troubles to the throne of grace and leave them there, we can depart as **Hannah** did, no longer looking sad (**1 Samuel 1:18**).

The psalmist's faith rested upon **God's mercy**, not upon any merit of his own.

Once he trusted that mercy, his heart rejoiced in salvation.

Joy and peace come through believing.

He became so confident of God's promised salvation that he spoke as though it were already accomplished.

Believers may pray in the same way, abandoning every hope except **God's mercy through the Savior's blood**.

Sometimes their burdens lift suddenly.

At other times relief comes slowly.

When comfort returns, they recognize that many earlier fears were unnecessary and confess that the Lord has dealt generously with them.
