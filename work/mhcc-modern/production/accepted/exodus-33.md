# Exodus 33 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Lord refuses to go with Israel. | 1–6 |
| The tabernacle of Moses removed without the camp. | 7–11 |
| Moses desires to see the glory of God. | 12–23 |

## God withdraws the assurance of his presence (verses 1–6)

Even when God forgives his people, he must teach them what their sin deserved.

His command to go forward as they were expressed his displeasure powerfully.

He promised to keep his covenant with Abraham by giving the people Canaan.

Yet he withheld the signs of his presence with which he had previously blessed them.

The Israelites mourned over their sin.

Of all sin's painful results, the one true penitents fear and grieve most is the departure of God's presence.

Canaan itself would not be a pleasant land without the Lord.

Those who had gladly surrendered their ornaments to support idolatry could surely lay aside ornaments as a sign of grief and shame for it.

## Moses pitches the tent outside the camp (verses 7–11)

Moses took the tabernacle and pitched it outside the camp.

This appears to have been a temporary building used for worship and for hearing disputes among the people.

The Israelites watched Moses as he went to it.

They longed to be restored to peace with God and were deeply concerned about what would happen next.

The pillar of cloud had withdrawn from the camp when the people defiled it through idolatry.

Now it returned.

If our hearts go out to meet God, he will graciously come to meet us.

## Moses asks for God's presence and glory (verses 12–23)

Moses pleaded earnestly with the Lord.

Through Christ's intercession we receive more than rescue from destruction.

We also receive the promise of everlasting happiness.

Notice how Moses pleaded.

We find grace in God's sight when we have received grace in our hearts that directs and strengthens us to do our duty.

Moses spoke as someone who dreaded the thought of going forward without God's presence.

God's gracious promises and past kindness should not merely strengthen our trust.

They should also make us more earnest in prayer.

And notice how Moses prevailed.

His intercession foreshadowed Christ's, which continues forever on behalf of all who come to God through him.

Christ's intercession does not depend on any merit in those for whom he prays.

Moses also asked to see God's glory, and even that request was heard.

A complete revelation of that glory would have overwhelmed Moses himself.

Human beings are too lowly and unworthy, too weak to bear it, and too guilty not to fear it.

Only God's merciful revelation of himself through Jesus Christ can be endured by us.

Yet the Lord granted Moses enough to satisfy him abundantly.

God's goodness is his glory.

He would have us know him more by the glory of his mercy than by the glory of his majesty.

The rock gave Moses a suitable place from which to see God's goodness and glory.

The rock at Horeb pointed to Christ, the Rock of refuge, salvation and strength.

Happy are those who stand upon this Rock!

The cleft in it may also picture Christ as struck, crucified, wounded and slain.

The account then reminds us that even our knowledge of God through Christ is incomplete in this life.

Compared with the sight of him that awaits us in heaven, it is like seeing only the back of someone who has passed by.

The clearest revelation of God's glory, grace and goodness in Christ is reserved for the life to come.
