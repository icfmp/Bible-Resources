# Genesis 44 — Modern English

## Joseph tests his brothers' love for Benjamin (verses 1–17)

Joseph wanted to discover how his brothers now felt about Benjamin.

If they still envied and hated Rachel's other son as they had hated Joseph, or if they still showed the same lack of concern for their father Jacob, their behavior would now reveal it.

When the cup was found in Benjamin's sack, they had an excuse to leave him behind as a slave.

But we cannot judge what people are now only by what they used to be, or predict what they will do simply by what they have done.

Joseph's steward accused them of ingratitude: they were repaying kindness with wrongdoing.

He also accused them of foolishness. Why steal a cup used every day, which would quickly be missed and carefully sought?

Henry explains that the steward's accusation can be understood in two ways.

Joseph might have had a particular attachment to the cup, so that he would search thoroughly for it if it disappeared.

Or Joseph might have left it at their table deliberately to see whether they were honest.

The brothers placed themselves at Joseph's mercy and acknowledged that God's judgment was righteous.

Perhaps they were thinking of their old wrong against Joseph and believed that God was now calling them to account for it.

Even when people wrong us and we suffer unjustly at their hands, we must still acknowledge that God is righteous and that he brings our sins to light.

## Judah pleads for Benjamin (verses 18–34)

Even if Joseph had been a complete stranger to the family, as Judah supposed, Judah's powerful appeal could hardly have failed to move him.

But neither Jacob nor Benjamin needed an intercessor to persuade Joseph to love them. He already loved them.

Judah now remained faithfully at Benjamin's side in his distress.

Much later, that loyalty was repaid when the tribe of Benjamin remained with the tribe of Judah after the other tribes had abandoned it.

When the apostle discusses Christ's mediation, he notes that our Lord descended from Judah (Hebrews 7:14).

Christ not only interceded for transgressors; he became their **Surety**, showing tender concern for both his Father and his brothers and sisters.

Jesus, the great fulfillment of what Joseph foreshadows, humbles and tests his people even after they have enjoyed some of his kindness.

He brings their sins back to mind so that they may truly repent, show their repentance, and recognize how much they owe to his mercy.
