# Matthew 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Multitudes follow Christ. | 1 |
| He heals a leper. | 2–4 |
| A centurion's servant healed. | 5–13 |
| Cure of Peter's wife's mother. | 14–17 |
| The scribe's zealous proposal. | 18–22 |
| Christ in a storm. | 23–27 |
| He heals two possessed with devils. | 28–34 |

## The crowds follow Jesus (verse 1)

This verse concludes the preceding account of Jesus' sermon. Those to whom Christ has revealed himself want to know still more of him.

## Jesus cleanses a man with leprosy (verses 2–4)

The man approached Jesus and worshiped him as someone possessing **divine power**.

His healing teaches us both **whom** to approach for help and **how** to come.

We may not know what God intends for our bodily illnesses, but we can always be confident of his wisdom and mercy.

No guilt is so great that **Christ's blood cannot atone for it**; no sinful corruption is so strong that his grace cannot overcome it.

To be cleansed, we must appeal to his pity. We cannot claim his mercy as a debt, but must ask for it humbly as a gift.

Those who seek Christ by faith for mercy and grace can trust his willingness to grant the spiritual help they truly need.

Afflictions are blessed when they lead us to Christ and move us to seek salvation from him.

Those whom he has cleansed from the spiritual leprosy of sin should be willing to tell faithful ministers of their condition, so that those ministers can advise, comfort and pray for them.

## Jesus heals a centurion's servant (verses 5–13)

The centurion was a **Roman soldier and a Gentile**, yet he was a godly man. No occupation or position excuses unbelief or sin.

He was concerned for his servant. We should likewise care about the spiritual sickness of our children and those under our responsibility, bringing their needs to Christ through prayer and faith.

The centurion showed deep humility. Christ's grace makes humble people still more humble.

He also showed extraordinary faith. **The less we trust our own worthiness, the more fully we can trust Christ.**

He acknowledged Christ's divine authority over people and nature, just as an officer commands those under him.

We should become obedient servants of God, going and coming according to his word and Providence.

The Son of Man often finds little faith, and therefore little fruit, among people who profess to belong to his kingdom.

An outward profession may earn the title **children of the kingdom**. But if we rest on that title without living faith, we will be cast out.

The servant received physical healing; the centurion received Christ's approval for his faith.

Believe and receive according to Christ's promise.

Healing of the soul is both an effect and an evidence of our share in **Christ's atoning blood**.

## Jesus heals Peter's mother-in-law (verses 14–17)

Peter was married and yet was an apostle.

Jesus' kindness to his wife's family shows his approval of marriage.

Henry explicitly argues here against **the Roman church's prohibition on ministers marrying**, pointing to Peter himself.

Peter's mother-in-law lived with his family, illustrating the duty of kindness toward relatives.

In spiritual healing, **Scripture speaks the word** and **the Spirit supplies the touch**, moving the heart and hands.

Ordinarily people recovering from a fever remain weak for a time.

Jesus' healing was above natural causes: the woman immediately regained enough strength to serve others.

News of his miracles spread, and many sick people came.

He healed everyone brought to him, however poor the patient or serious the disease.

Our bodies are exposed to many illnesses and troubles. The gospel's announcement that Christ **bore our sicknesses and carried our sorrows** gives deeper comfort than all the writings of philosophers.

Let us not resent the labor, cost or inconvenience involved in helping others.

## The cost of following Jesus (verses 18–22)

The scribe was **too quick to promise**.

His offer to follow Christ looked determined, but many religious resolutions arise from sudden emotion without careful consideration and produce no lasting obedience.

One might have expected Jesus to welcome him enthusiastically, since a scribe could apparently bring more public influence than a group of fishermen.

But Christ knew his heart.

The man's proposal appears to have been driven by **worldly ambition or love of gain**.

Jesus warned him that the Son of Man had nowhere to lay his head; anyone following him should expect no greater worldly comfort.

Henry judges it probable that the scribe went away.

Another man was **too slow to act**. He wanted to bury his father first and afterward serve Jesus.

Honoring and burying a father is a good duty, but **it was not the duty Christ had assigned this man at that moment**.

When Christ calls, even affection for the closest relatives and duties ordinarily proper must yield to the higher duty he now commands.

An unwilling heart always finds an excuse.

Christ called this man directly: **Follow me**. Henry expects that Christ's effective call gave him strength to obey and that he did follow.

The contrast is between the scribe's self-confident **“I will follow you”** and Christ's effective **“Follow me.”**

We are brought to Jesus through **the power of his call**, not by our own resolve (**Romans 9:16**).

## Jesus calms the storm (verses 23–27)

Those who travel by sea and face danger there can take comfort in trusting and praying to a Savior who experienced the sea and its storms.

Everyone journeying with Christ through this world must expect storms.

Jesus shared our human nature in everything except sin. He grew tired and slept.

His sleep also tested the disciples' faith.

When they became frightened, they came to their Master.

A troubled soul may experience passions and temptations rising like waves, while God seems silent and distant.

It may cry, **Lord Jesus, do not remain silent or I am lost!**

True faith can still be weak. Christ's followers sometimes torment themselves with fears about present trouble and imagined future disasters.

A violent storm of doubt and fear may give way to a wonderful calm through **the Spirit of adoption**.

The disciples had never seen a storm become completely quiet in an instant.

The One who did this can do anything.

We may therefore trust him in every outward or inward storm (**Isaiah 26:4**).

## Jesus casts out demons (verses 28–34)

The demons have no share in Christ as Savior and cannot expect his saving benefits.

How great is God's mysterious love for fallen humanity when fallen angels are not offered the same saving help (**Hebrews 2:16**)!

The demons had to acknowledge Jesus' greatness, though they had no part in him.

They rejected his authority, but they could not escape him as **Judge**.

Every human being must also face him.

Satan and his servants can act only as far as Christ permits.

They cannot enter even **a herd of pigs** without his permission, and they must leave when he commands.

God sometimes allows their destructive activity for wise and holy purposes.

The devil drives people toward sins they have already resolved to avoid and know will bring shame and grief.

It is miserable to be held captive by him.

Many people prefer their **pigs to the Savior**.

They do not want Christ or his word within their hearts because his teaching would overthrow the animal-like desires they insist on feeding.

Those who repeatedly tell the Lord to depart from them must fear hearing the final sentence commanding **them** to depart from him.
