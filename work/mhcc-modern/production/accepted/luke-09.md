# Luke 9 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Jesus sends the twelve apostles (verses 1–9)

Jesus sent out his twelve disciples, who had by then learned enough from him to begin instructing others.

They were not to seek people's respect through an impressive outward appearance.

They should go **as they were**, without anxiety over worldly provisions.

The Lord Jesus is the source of all power and authority, and every creature is ultimately subject to him.

If he accompanies his ministers' preaching with power to release sinners from Satan's bondage, he will also provide for their needs.

When **truth and love** come together in this way yet God's message is rejected, those who despise it have no excuse. Their rejection becomes testimony against them.

**Herod's guilty conscience** made him think John might have risen from death.

He wanted to see Jesus. Why did he not simply go to him?

Henry suggests it was **probably pride** or a desire to avoid another person who would rebuke his sins.

Because Herod delayed, his heart grew harder.

When he finally saw Jesus, he treated him with the same prejudice as others (**Luke 23:11**).

## Jesus feeds the crowd (verses 10–17)

People followed Jesus, even when it was inconvenient.

He nevertheless gave them what they truly needed.

He taught them about **God's kingdom** and healed the sick.

Then with **five loaves and two fish** he fed **five thousand men**.

The Lord will not let people who fear and faithfully serve him lack any good thing.

When we receive the comforts of ordinary life, we should acknowledge that they come from God.

We are not worthy of them.

We owe both these gifts and the pleasure we find in them to **Christ's mediation**, through which the curse has been removed.

Christ's blessing can make a little accomplish much.

He fills every hungry soul and satisfies it from the goodness of his house.

There were leftovers.

**Our Father's house contains bread enough, and more than enough.**

Christ's resources are not narrow or limited.

## Peter confesses Jesus; the disciples must deny themselves (verses 18–27)

What comfort that Jesus is **God's Anointed One**!

This means he was both appointed to be the Messiah and fully equipped for his mission.

Jesus explained his approaching suffering and death.

His disciples were not to seek to prevent his suffering. They were to prepare for their **own suffering as his followers**.

We frequently meet crosses on the road of duty.

We must not deliberately bring suffering upon ourselves.

But when a cross is placed upon us in the course of obedience, we must **take it up and follow Christ**.

Our true welfare depends on the condition of our **souls**.

A body cannot be happy in eternity if its soul is miserable.

Yet the soul may be truly blessed even when the body endures serious pain and oppression here.

We must never be ashamed of Christ or his gospel.

## The transfiguration (verses 28–36)

Christ's transfiguration gave the disciples a glimpse of the glory in which he would return to judge the world.

It encouraged them to suffer for him.

**Prayer is a transforming duty**. It can make a person's face shine.

Even when glorified on the mountain, Jesus was willing to speak about **his coming suffering and death**.

During our greatest moments of earthly joy, remember that we have **no permanent city here**.

How greatly we need to pray for enlivening grace!

The disciples were drowsy, but later awoke fully enough to witness what happened and report it.

People who propose building earthly tents for glorified saints in heaven have misunderstood the event.

## Jesus delivers a child from an evil spirit (verses 37–42)

The child's condition was distressing.

He was oppressed by an evil spirit.

Henry regards such afflictions as more dreadful than sickness arising only from natural causes.

Satan brings terrible harm wherever he gains influence.

How blessed are those who can come to Christ!

He can do what even his disciples cannot.

With **one word**, Jesus healed the child.

When our own children recover from illness, it is comforting to receive them as restored by Christ's hand.

## Jesus corrects the disciples' ambition (verses 43–50)

Jesus plainly predicted his suffering, but the disciples resisted understanding him because it did not fit their expectations.

He used a **young child** as an example of **simplicity and humility**.

What greater honor could someone receive in this world than to be welcomed as a messenger of God and Christ, with the Father and Son receiving that welcome as directed to themselves?

The twelve disciples had an unusually strong reason to expect other believers to identify with their group.

Yet Jesus warned them not to forbid people who were acting in his name merely because **they did not follow with the Twelve**.

People may be faithful followers of Christ and accepted by him **even when they are not part of our own fellowship**.

## Jesus rejects the disciples' mistaken zeal (verses 51–56)

The disciples failed to understand the Samaritan villagers' conduct.

Their refusal to receive Jesus and his followers reflected **national prejudice and sectarian hostility**, rather than the kind of opposition to God's worship involved in the judgment of Ahaziah and Elijah.

The Samaritans had not assaulted or harmed them.

The cases were therefore very different.

The disciples also failed to understand that **the gospel's distinctive miracles were to be works of mercy**.

Most importantly, they failed to recognize what was actually moving their own hearts: **pride and worldly ambition**.

It is easy to boast about our zeal for the Lord while in reality pursuing our own interests, injuring others and accomplishing harm rather than good.

Jesus exposed and corrected those motives.

## Following Jesus without delay (verses 57–62)

One person eagerly offered to follow Jesus, but seems not to have considered the cost.

To follow Christ we must let go of dreams of worldly greatness.

We cannot sincerely combine Christian discipleship with making earthly advantage our governing aim.

Another seemed willing to follow, but asked for a short delay.

Jesus himself had called this person.

True religion teaches kindness, family responsibility and honoring our parents.

Yet **family duties must not be made excuses for neglecting obedience to God**.

A third wanted time to speak with friends and arrange affairs at home.

His earthly concerns occupied more of his heart than they should.

He was placing himself within reach of a temptation that could divert him from following Jesus.

No one can attend effectively to one task while continually distracted by another.

Those who begin God's work must determine to continue.

**Looking back leads to drawing back, and drawing back leads to destruction.**

Only those who persevere to the end will be saved.
