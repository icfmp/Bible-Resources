# Proverbs 23 — Modern English

## Verses 1–3 — Restrain appetite

God's limits upon our appetites are intended to protect us from injuring ourselves.

## Verses 4 and 5 — Do not make wealth your goal

Do not become someone whose whole purpose is getting rich. Worldly possessions cannot satisfy the soul, and even what we grasp firmly **cannot be held for long**.

## Verses 6–8 — Avoid insincere hospitality; welcome God's feast

Do not make yourself dependent upon people whose welcome is insincere. But when God invites us to **his own feast**, we can safely receive the **Bread of Life** and delight in his provision (**Isaiah 25:6; 55:2**).

## Verse 9 — Know when silence is appropriate

We ought to speak about God's truth whenever there is a fitting opportunity, but when someone plainly refuses to listen, wisdom sometimes requires silence.

## Verses 10 and 11 — God protects the fatherless

Fatherless children receive God's special care. **He is their mighty Redeemer**, fully able to defend them.

## Verses 12–16 — Teach and lovingly correct children

A parent urges a child to give serious attention to **Scripture**, corrects wrongdoing with prayer while trusting God's blessing, and encourages obedience by showing what will bring genuine good. What joy parents have when children follow this faithful teaching!

## Verses 17 and 18 — Do not envy temporary prosperity

The believer's hope will not be disappointed. Both present trials and the apparent prosperity of sinners are drawing toward their end.

## Verses 19–28 — Give the whole heart to God

The gracious Savior purchased pardon and peace and calls us with a loving parent's affection to listen, become wise and follow his guidance. Young people should heed godly parents: **when the heart is directed, the steps follow**. **Buy the truth and never sell it**, not even for pleasure, honor or wealth. God requires the **whole heart**, not a portion divided between himself and the world. Follow Scripture, Providence and faithful examples. Guard particularly against drunkenness, which dulls the mind, and sexual immorality, which takes away the heart due to God and is difficult to escape once welcomed.

## Verses 29–35 — The self-destruction of drunkenness

Avoid even the beginnings of alcohol's sinful allurements. Excess brings strife, impurity, insolence, uncontrolled speech and dulled conscience. A drunken person may face mortal danger yet feel secure, like someone sleeping atop **a ship's mast**. Even while judgment approaches, the person may insist, **“I will seek it again.”** What sensible person would sell themselves to a habit that risks dying senseless and waking under judgment? Henry understands Wisdom's words as **Christ addressing sinners**.
