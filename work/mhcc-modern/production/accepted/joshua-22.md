# Joshua 22 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Reuben and Gad, with the half tribe of Manasseh, dismissed to their homes. | 1–9 |
| They build an altar of testimony, The congregation offended thereat. | 10–20 |
| The answer of the Reubenites. | 21–29 |
| The children of Israel satisfied. | 30–34 |

## The eastern tribes return home (verses 1–9)

Joshua dismissed these tribes with sound counsel.

Receiving a command is useless unless we obey it. We cannot obey rightly without paying careful attention.

Above all, we must **love the Lord our God** as the best of all beings and the best of friends.

As far as love governs the heart, we will continually seek to follow his ways, even when those ways are narrow and difficult, and to keep every one of his commands.

In every circumstance, we must purpose from the heart to cling to the Lord and serve him and his kingdom among people, with all our heart and soul.

This good counsel applies to everyone. May God give us grace to accept it!

## The eastern tribes build an altar and cause concern (verses 10–20)

The tribes living east of the Jordan wanted to remain connected to Israel's worship.

At first their new altar seemed like an attempt to establish a rival to the altar at Shiloh.

God is jealous for the worship he has appointed, and we should be concerned about anything that resembles or leads to idolatry.

Corruption in religion is best confronted at its beginning.

But the Israelites' **prudence in investigating their concern** was no less commendable than their zeal to protect worship.

Many bitter disputes could be prevented or quickly resolved if people would first inquire into the alleged offense.

Remembering great sins committed earlier should make us watchful against sin's beginnings: the path of sin slopes downward.

We must warn a neighbor who does wrong, rather than allow that person to continue uncorrected (Leviticus 19:17).

The western tribes even offered to welcome their brothers to settle in the land where the Lord's tabernacle stood.

That generous offer expressed the true spirit of Israel.

## The eastern tribes explain the altar (verses 21–29)

The eastern tribes received their brothers' criticism patiently.

They gave their explanation with humility and solemnity, doing everything possible to satisfy the others.

They spoke God's name with reverence.

Their brief confession of faith removed the suspicion that they intended to worship other gods.

We should always speak of God seriously and pause with reverence when mentioning his name.

A careless expression such as “God knows” may misuse his name.

But God truly does know everything, including our deepest thoughts and intentions.

In every religious act, it matters above all that we are sincere before him.

Yet we should also let other people recognize that sincerity by its fruits, especially when those who misunderstand us are genuinely concerned for God's glory.

The tribes rejected the evil purpose they were accused of and clearly explained the altar's true intention.

People who have received the benefits of God's appointed worship naturally want to preserve those blessings for their children.

They should take every appropriate step to ensure that their descendants are recognized as part of God's people.

**Christ is the great Altar** who makes every gift acceptable.

The clearest evidence that we belong to him is the work of his Spirit in our hearts.

## Israel accepts the explanation and peace is restored (verses 30–34)

It was good that both sides wanted peace as well as faithfulness to God.

Religious disagreements can become especially fierce and difficult to resolve when wisdom and love are lacking.

Proud, irritable people may refuse to withdraw an accusation even when clear evidence shows they were wrong.

Israel did not act in that spirit.

The western tribes recognized their brothers' innocence as a sign of God's presence.

We should be glad when fears about another Christian's faithfulness prove unfounded and we discover that the person genuinely loves God and seeks holiness.

The altar was named **Ed**, meaning *witness*.

It testified to the tribes' determination to preserve pure worship, and it would stand as a warning to their descendants if they later abandoned the Lord.

How happy it would be if all professing Christians learned from Israel's example!

We need zeal and steadiness for God's truth **together with fairness, gentleness, willingness to understand, readiness to explain and readiness to accept a brother's explanation**.

May the Lord increase the number who preserve the Spirit's unity through the bond of peace.

May grace and comfort increase among everyone who sincerely loves Jesus Christ.
