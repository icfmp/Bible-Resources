# 2 Samuel 23 — Modern English

## David's final words (verses 1–7)

David's final testimony deserves serious attention.

Those who have spent many years enjoying God's goodness and finding wisdom in his ways should bear witness to the truth of his promises as their earthly lives draw to a close.

David declared that **God had inspired his words**.

The Holy Spirit spoke through him.

Like other holy men, he spoke and wrote as the Spirit moved him.

David had many failures to confess.

He had neglected duties and sometimes acted wrongly.

Yet he found comfort in the Lord's **everlasting covenant** with him.

Above all, David meant the covenant of mercy and peace.

He received it as a sinner who believed in the promised Savior, accepted God's promised blessing and surrendered himself to serve the Lord as someone redeemed by grace.

Believers will enjoy these covenant blessings forever.

God the Father, Son and Holy Spirit will receive eternal glory through their salvation.

Forgiveness, righteousness, grace and everlasting life are secured as God's gifts through Jesus Christ.

Christ possesses limitless riches of grace and every blessing for people who seek salvation through him.

David knew enough of the holy law to understand the greatness of his own sin.

He therefore knew how deeply he needed Christ's salvation.

The covenant was **all his salvation** and therefore **all his desire**.

Compared with this hope, every earthly thing became less attractive.

David was ready to surrender earthly things or leave them behind at death so that he could enjoy perfect happiness with God (**Psalm 73:24–28**).

Yet he grieved that sinful desires remained powerful within him and that his own faith, hope and love were often weak.

He would readily have admitted that his carelessness and lack of watchfulness had contributed to these weaknesses.

Nevertheless, the hope of being made perfect in heavenly glory encouraged him as he approached death.

## David's mighty men (verses 8–39)

David once longed for water from the well at Bethlehem.

His desire seems to have been a moment of weakness.

Thirsty and remembering the water he had drunk there when he was young, he spoke without sufficient thought.

His mighty men were eager to risk their lives at even a slight suggestion from their king.

How much more eagerly should we seek to please **the Lord Jesus**, obeying his will as revealed through Scripture, the Holy Spirit and his providence!

But David refused to drink that water.

He poured it out to the Lord as a drink offering.

In this way he opposed his own foolish desire, disciplined himself for indulging it and showed that sober reflection could correct his rashness.

He understood the need for self-denial.

David treated that water as especially precious because his men had risked their blood to obtain it.

How much more highly should we value the blessings purchased for us through the **blood of the Savior**!

Let us not neglect so great a salvation.
