# Job 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Bildad reproves Job. | 1–4 |
| Ruin attends the wicked. | 5–10 |
| The ruin of the wicked. | 11–21 |

## Bildad rebukes Job again (verses 1–4)

Bildad had previously given Job some useful counsel and encouragement.

Now his speech contained only rebukes and predictions of destruction.

Because Job would not agree that he was wicked, Bildad wrongly concluded he was excluding God's Providence from human affairs.

## The wicked fall into snares (verses 5–10)

Bildad described the misery of wicked people.

Much of his teaching is true when understood as a warning: **sin is a dreadful condition, and without repentance it leads to destruction**.

But Bildad thought he could easily apply those warnings to Job.

That conclusion was neither safe nor just.

Angry debaters frequently classify their opponents as enemies of God and draw false conclusions from important truths.

Bildad pictured the wicked person as an animal trapped in a snare or a criminal brought into custody.

From the beginning Satan has been both a murderer and a robber.

He sets traps for sinners wherever they go.

If he makes people sinful like himself, he also makes them miserable like himself.

He hunts for their precious lives.

A wicked person's own transgression becomes a trap, and God prepares judgment for that wrongdoing.

See how sinners run into traps they themselves have made possible.

## Bildad warns of the wicked person's end (verses 11–21)

Bildad described judgment awaiting wicked people in the world to come, a judgment sometimes anticipated in this life.

Sin leads to fear and everlasting confusion.

The terrors of a guilty conscience can be foretastes of that end, as seen in **Cain and Judas**.

The death of an unrepentant sinner is miserable, even if the person once lived confidently.

At death, every earthly support fails.

How blessed are the saints, and how indebted to **Jesus Christ**!

Through him death's terror has been so transformed that death becomes a servant and even a friend to the believer.

Bildad described the destruction of wicked people's households and children.

Those who want their family's true welfare should fear destroying it through sin.

God's judgments may continue affecting a wicked person's reputation and family after death.

Henry sees this as evidence of a deeper eternal judgment.

**The memory of the righteous brings blessing, while the wicked person's name decays** (**Proverbs 10:7**).

May these warnings encourage sinners to flee from coming wrath, which human strength, cunning and wealth cannot escape.

But **Jesus lives forever to deliver everyone who trusts him**.

Take courage, suffering believers.

Your sorrow will last only a little while.

Your beloved Savior will see you again, your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you.
