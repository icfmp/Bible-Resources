# Esther 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The success of the Jews. | 1–19 |
| The feast of Purim in remembrance of this. | 20–32 |

## The Jews are delivered (verses 1–19)

The enemies of the Jews expected to defeat them under the earlier royal decree.

Had those enemies not tried to harm God's people, they would not themselves have suffered.

The Jews acted together and strengthened one another.

We should likewise remain firm **in one spirit and with one mind**, standing together against the spiritual enemies who seek to rob us of **faith, a possession worth more than life**.

The Jews refused to enrich themselves through plunder.

This honored their faith by showing that they sought only their own preservation.

In every circumstance God's people should be compassionate and **free from selfish motives**.

They may wisely decline advantages they could lawfully claim.

The Jews celebrated **the day after** their work ended.

Whenever God grants a great mercy, we should respond promptly with thanksgiving.

## The Feast of Purim is established (verses 20–32)

The continued observance of Jewish festivals publicly testifies to the truth of the Old Testament Scriptures.

Henry argues from that truth that the promised Messiah has already come and that **Jesus of Nazareth alone fulfills the promise**.

Purim was established through public authority, yet under God's guidance by the Holy Spirit.

The name **Purim** comes from a Persian word meaning **“lot.”**

That name reminded the Jews how the Almighty used even pagan superstition to carry out his purposes.

When we remember God's mercies, we should also remember the fears and distress from which he delivered us.

We must not forget personal mercies and thereby lose their comfort or withhold the glory owed to God.

May he teach us to rejoice with a holy joy that anticipates and prepares us for heaven's happiness.

Every act of divine goodness toward us creates a fresh obligation to **do good to others**, especially to those in greatest need.

Above all, our redemption through Christ calls us to mercy and generosity (**2 Corinthians 8:9**).
