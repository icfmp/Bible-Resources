# Numbers 32 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The tribes of Reuben and Gad request an inheritance on the east of Jordan. | 1–5 |
| Moses reproves the Reubenites and Gadites. | 6–15 |
| They explain their views, Moses consents. | 16–27 |
| They take possession of the land to the east of Jordan. | 28–42 |

## Reuben and Gad ask for land east of the Jordan (verses 1–5)

The tribes of Reuben and Gad wanted the land Israel had recently conquered to become their own inheritance.

Two ordinary human temptations may have influenced them: **the lust of the eyes** and **the pride of life**.

They valued their own convenience more highly than the good of the whole community.

Many people still do something similar.

They pursue what benefits them personally rather than what advances the cause of Jesus Christ.

They allow worldly advantage to satisfy them, instead of seeking the heavenly Canaan toward which God's people are traveling.

## Moses rebukes their request (verses 6–15)

The proposal suggested that they had little regard for Canaan, distrusted God's promise and wished to avoid the dangers of conquering the land.

Moses was rightly angry.

God's people should never remain comfortable and unconcerned while their brothers and sisters face difficult or dangerous work.

That duty applies both to the needs of the whole community and to those of individuals.

Moses reminded Reuben and Gad of the fatal consequences of Israel's earlier unbelief, when their fathers had also been close to entering Canaan.

If people seriously considered how sin ends, they would be far more afraid to begin it.

## The tribes promise to assist their brothers (verses 16–27)

Moses' plain rebuke achieved a good result.

When their sin and its danger were shown to them, the tribes responded to their duty without resentment or pointless argument.

Everyone should care about the well-being of others as well as personal interests.

The law of love requires us, when needed, to work, take risks and suffer for one another.

The men of war promised to go fully armed before Israel into Canaan and not return until the conquest was finished.

Moses accepted their proposal.

But he warned them that breaking their promise would be a sin **against the Lord**, not merely a wrong against the other tribes.

God would call them to account.

“Be sure your sin will find you out.”

Sooner or later wrongdoing catches up with the person who committed it.

Our concern should be to **find out our sins now**—to recognize, repent of and abandon them—before they come to find us in judgment.

## The tribes settle east of the river (verses 28–42)

The tribes built up their cities—that is, they repaired what was already there.

They also changed their names.

Henry thinks the old names may have been connected with idolatry and therefore deserved to be forgotten.

Selfishness, the desire to seek our own interests instead of those of Christ, is a common and dangerous attitude.

Someone who truly believes the gospel, recognizes God's goodness, is constrained by Christ's love and is made holy through the Holy Spirit cannot remain indifferent to the spread of faith or the spiritual welfare of others merely from love of comfort or fear of hardship.

Let your light shine before other people, so that they see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.
