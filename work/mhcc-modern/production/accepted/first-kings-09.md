# 1 Kings 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's answer to Solomon. | 1–9 |
| The presents of Solomon and Hiram. | 10–14 |
| Solomon's buildings, His trade. | 15–28 |

## God answers Solomon's prayer (verses 1–9)

After Solomon completed and dedicated the temple, the Lord warned him against pride and called him to reverent fear.

Even after all the service we can offer, **our standing before God remains the same**.

Nothing we do can purchase permission to sin.

A genuine believer would never desire such permission.

The believer would rather be corrected by God than continue comfortably and prosperously in wrongdoing.

## Solomon and Hiram exchange gifts (verses 10–14)

Solomon gave **twenty cities** to Hiram.

Hiram was displeased with them.

If Solomon wanted to please him, it would be better to cooperate with him in **trade**, as the two kings eventually did.

See how God's providence suits different parts of the earth to people's varied needs and dispositions.

He also suits people to the lands where they live.

Through this arrangement, human beings depend upon one another and work together for their common benefit.

## Solomon's prosperity and trade (verses 15–28)

This passage further describes Solomon's greatness.

He began in the right order.

He built **God's house first**, and only after finishing it did he undertake his own palace.

Then the Lord blessed his other building projects with success.

Let devotion come first, profit afterward, and pleasure last.

When we make real sacrifices for God's glory and for other people's good, the work may also bring benefit to us.

Canaan, the holy land and glory of all lands, had **no gold of its own**.

This reminds us that the most valuable production is often what sustains human life—our own lives and our neighbors'—rather than what brings luxury.

Canaan produced those necessary goods.

Solomon gained much wealth through commerce.

Yet he recommended a **better trade**, one available even to the poorest people.

Wisdom is better than silver, and its profit is greater than fine gold (**Proverbs 3:14**).
