# Isaiah 62 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's care of his church and people. | 1–5 |
| The office of ministers in preaching the gospel. | 6–9 |
| Every hinderance shall be removed from the way of salvation. | 10–12 |

## Christ's unchanging love for his church (verses 1–5)

Here the **Son of God** assures his church of his enduring love and his intercession through all her trials and difficulties.

She will receive **a new and pleasant name**, different from any name she had before.

Before the gospel was preached widely, hardly anyone seemed sincerely concerned about the true condition of religion in the world.

But by his grace, God has produced something in his church that makes her **his delight**.

Let that love move us toward **holiness**.

If the Lord rejoices over us, we should rejoice in **serving him**.

## Ministers and believers persevere in prayer (verses 6–9)

People who profess to belong to God must also be **people of prayer**.

The Lord is not irritated by earnest pleading, as human beings may be.

He himself tells us to continue crying out and **give him no rest**.

Jesus' parable of persistent requesting teaches the same principle (**Luke 11:5–6**).

When God pours out **a spirit of prayer** on a people, it is a hopeful sign that he is approaching them with mercy.

Notice how uncertain our earthly comforts are.

Notice also God's kindness in granting **plenty**, together with **peace to enjoy it**.

May we delight in the gatherings of God's people so that we may experience **the consolation of his Spirit**.

## The way is prepared for salvation (verses 10–12)

God will make a way for **Christ's salvation**.

Every barrier will be removed.

Christ brings with him the reward of **comfort and peace**.

But the path before him requires **humiliation and reformation**.

Those he saves will be called **the holy people** and **the redeemed of the Lord**.

Holiness brings honor and beauty to a person or a place.

It makes God's people desirable and loved.

Many historical events may already have fulfilled parts of this promise and served as pledges of **more glorious times still to come**.

Scripture repeatedly shows the close connection between the blessing of **the Jews** and that of **the Gentiles**.

Jesus Christ will finish his work.

**He never abandons anyone whom he has redeemed and sanctified**.
