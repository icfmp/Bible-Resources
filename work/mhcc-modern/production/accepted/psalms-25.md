# Psalm 25 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Confidence in prayer. | 1–7 |
| Prayer for remission of sins. | 8–14 |
| For help in affliction. | 15–22 |

## Confidence in prayer (verses 1–7)

When we worship God, we should lift **our souls** toward him.

Anyone who waits upon God with faith and hopes in him with confidence will never have reason to be ashamed of that trust.

Even the most mature believer still needs and desires **God's instruction**.

If we sincerely want to know our duty and are determined to do it, we can trust the Lord to guide us.

David was especially eager for forgiveness.

When God pardons sin, Scripture says that he remembers it no more. This describes **complete remission**.

Our claim upon forgiveness and every needed good must rest on **God's goodness, not ours; on his mercy, not our merit**.

We should make that appeal while recognizing our unworthiness and trusting the riches of grace.

How vast is the mercy that can cover forever the sins and follies of a youth lived without God and without hope!

Blessed be the Lord: **the blood of the great Sacrifice** can cleanse every stain.

## God guides and forgives sinners (verses 8–14)

We are all sinners, and **Christ came into the world to save sinners**, teach them and call them to repentance.

We judge the reliability of a promise by the character of the One making it. That is why we can depend on God's promises.

All the Lord's paths—his **promises and providential dealings**—are mercy and truth.

Through every circumstance, even severe affliction, his people may discover his mercy and the fulfillment of his word.

When they reach the end of their journey, they will understand more clearly that every path was merciful and true.

Humble people distrust themselves, want to be instructed and seek divine guidance.

God directs them by **the written word**, enabling them to find rest for their souls in the Savior.

Even when the body is sick and hurting, the soul may remain at peace in God.

## Help during suffering (verses 15–22)

David ended as he began: looking to God and expressing his dependence and longing.

It is good to **hope and wait quietly** for the Lord's salvation.

If God turns toward us in favor, it matters little who turns away.

David appealed to the integrity of his dealings with his enemies.

He remained a sinner before God, but his conscience testified that he had not wronged those accusing him.

The Lord would finally give Israel rest from its enemies.

In heaven, God's people will be perfectly redeemed from every trouble.

Blessed Savior, you have graciously taught us that without you we can do nothing.

Teach us how to pray, how to approach you in your appointed way and how to lift our whole hearts and desires toward you, for **you are the Lord our righteousness**.
