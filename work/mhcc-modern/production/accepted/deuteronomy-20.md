# Deuteronomy 20 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortation and proclamation respecting those who went to war. | 1–9 |
| Peace to be offered, What cities were to be devoted. | 10–20 |

## Israel's soldiers must trust God (verses 1–9)

In wars fought according to God's command, Israel could rely on his help.

The Lord, not the people's weapons or numbers, was to be their confidence.

In this respect their battles pictured the Christian's spiritual warfare.

Men unwilling to fight were to be released from the military campaign.

Sometimes their reluctance arose from family or other personal circumstances.

God did not want his service performed by people dragged into it against their will.

“Your people will offer themselves willingly” (Psalm 110:3).

Those who run the Christian race and fight the good fight of faith must set aside everything that makes them reluctant to do their duty.

People who were weak or afraid could also return home.

One reason was to prevent their fear from spreading to their fellow soldiers.

We must not adopt the fears of people who are afraid (Isaiah 8:12).

## Peace offered and the limits of ancient warfare (verses 10–20)

Israel received laws about warfare against other nations.

Henry finds in the offer of peace a picture of God's gracious way of dealing with sinners.

He proclaims peace and urges them to be reconciled to him.

This also teaches us how to treat other people.

Even if others want war, we should want peace.

The ancient law included a different provision concerning towns within the territory promised to Israel, where idol worshipers were not to remain and lead the people astray.

Henry emphasizes an essential distinction: **these regulations do not govern Christians' conduct today**.

**Christ's law of love**, not Israel's ancient rules for conquest, is our rule.

The horrors of war bring anguish to a compassionate heart whenever we remember them.

They demonstrate humanity's wickedness, Satan's power and God's just judgment on a sinful world.

More dreadful still is the condition of those fighting against their own Maker, refusing even the fitting service of praise and worship.

Their destruction is certain.

Christians need not fear the number or power of their spiritual enemies, or their own weakness.

The Lord saves his people.

But anyone whose heart still clings to this world or fears Christ's cross and the conflict should not imagine the spiritual battle can be fought halfheartedly.

Even during a siege, Israel was forbidden to destroy fruit trees unnecessarily.

God cares for human welfare better than we care for it ourselves.

His commands protect our necessities and comforts, while uncontrolled appetite and passion destroy them.

Jewish interpreters understand this law to forbid deliberate waste of any kind.

Every creature of God is good.

We should not refuse his gifts, but we should never abuse them.

The things we carelessly destroy today may be exactly what we need tomorrow.
