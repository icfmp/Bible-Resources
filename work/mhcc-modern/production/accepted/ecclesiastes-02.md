# Ecclesiastes 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Pleasure, wealth and magnificence cannot satisfy (verses 1–11)

Solomon soon discovered that laughter and pleasure were vanity.

Loud, glittering entertainment does little to make a person genuinely happy.

The many things people attempt in order to find satisfaction in the world—and the way they keep moving from one to another—resemble **a feverish person's restless tossing**.

When Solomon realized the folly of giving himself to wine, he turned to the expensive amusements and entertainments of rulers.

Poor people reading the description may be tempted to envy him or resent their own circumstances.

But the remedy is to listen to the wealthy man's **own judgment** on those pleasures.

They were all **vanity and vexation of spirit**.

The same experiences would bring us the same disappointing result.

Having food and clothing, let us learn to be **content**.

Solomon retained his great human knowledge and understanding throughout these experiments.

Yet worldly pleasure, when separated from spiritual blessings, leaves the mind still longing for more and never truly satisfied.

Happiness does not come from the social position or circumstances we occupy.

Final blessedness comes **only through Jesus Christ**.

## Human wisdom cannot make a person finally happy (verses 12–17)

Solomon found that knowledge and prudence are better than ignorance and foolishness.

Nevertheless, human learning and wisdom cannot give eternal happiness.

The greatest scholar who dies without knowing **Jesus Christ** is lost just as surely as the person who never received an education.

What value will worldly praise have for the body in the grave or the soul under judgment?

And those whose spirits have been made perfect in heaven have no need of it either.

If this world were all we had, these things might cause us to despair of earthly life as nothing but **vanity and vexation of spirit**.

## Use this world's gifts according to God's will (verses 18–26)

Our hearts are often reluctant to give up their expectation of finding something truly great and lasting in the created world.

Solomon eventually learned to surrender that expectation.

Even wealthy people live in **a valley of tears**.

It is foolish to become a slave to worldly pursuits when they can provide nothing greater than support for the body.

At best we can enjoy **a moderate and cheerful use of earthly things**, suited to our circumstances and position.

But we should also **take joy in honest work**.

The things God gives us should help us fulfill our ordinary responsibilities diligently and cheerfully.

This contentment is **God's gift**.

Wealth can be either a blessing or a curse, depending on whether a person has a heart that uses it wisely.

God gives those he accepts joy and satisfaction **in knowing and loving him**.

But sinners who make the world their entire portion find labor, sorrow, emptiness and vexation—then ultimately leave the wealth they pursued to other people.

Let sinners reflect seriously upon their end.

Seeking a lasting inheritance **in Christ's love and the blessings that follow from it** is the only way to enjoy even present life in a truly satisfying way.
