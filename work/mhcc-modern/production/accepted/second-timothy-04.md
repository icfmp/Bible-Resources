# 2 Timothy 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle solemnly charges Timothy to be diligent, though many will not bear sound doctrine. | 1–5 |
| Enforces the charge from his own martyrdom, then at hand. | 6–8 |
| Desires him to come speedily. | 9–13 |
| He cautions, and complains of such as had deserted him; and expresses his faith as to his own preservation to the heavenly kingdom. | 14–18 |
| Friendly greetings and his usual blessing. | 19–22 |

## Preach the word despite resistance (verses 1–5)

People may grow weary of **Christ's plain gospel** and prefer entertaining stories to clear, searching preaching.

They reject sermons that speak directly to their consciences and are relevant to their lives.

Those who truly care for human souls must remain watchful, accept the painful consequences of faithful ministry and use every opportunity to make the pure gospel known.

## Paul's nearing death and his crown (verses 6–8)

The blood of Christian martyrs is **not an atoning sacrifice**. Yet their death can be an offering that acknowledges God's grace and truth.

For a good person, death is release from the prison of this world and departure to enjoy the world to come.

Paul had kept **the faith and the gospel's doctrines**, as both Christian and minister.

What comfort to be able to say this near the end of life!

The crown awaiting believers is a **crown of righteousness purchased through Christ's righteousness**. They have not yet received it, but it is securely reserved for them.

Believers may rejoice even amid poverty, pain, illness and death. But those who neglect the duties belonging to their calling weaken the evidence of their faith and may approach death troubled by uncertainty.

## Come soon; bring books and necessary things (verses 9–13)

Love of the present world often causes people to abandon Christ's truth and ways.

Although Paul was guided by divine inspiration, **he still wanted his books**.

As long as we live, we still need to learn.

The apostles did not despise human means of meeting life's needs or of gaining further instruction.

Let us thank God for the writings of wise and faithful Christians from earlier generations, and use our reading so that **our progress becomes evident to others**.

## Human supporters fail; the Lord does not (verses 14–18)

False brothers and sisters may be as dangerous as open enemies.

People willing to oppose someone like Paul deserve caution.

The Christians in Rome had eagerly greeted Paul when he arrived (**Acts 28**). But when standing with him appeared dangerous, **they all deserted him**.

Though God could justly hold their failure against them, **Paul prayed for their forgiveness**.

He was delivered from the lion's mouth, which Henry identifies as possibly **Nero or one of his judges**.

If the Lord stands beside us, he will strengthen us through trials and dangers. **His presence more than compensates for the absence of everyone else**.

## Farewell and blessing (verses 19–22)

Nothing more is needed for true happiness than **the Lord Jesus Christ present with our spirits**. All spiritual blessings are united in him.

The finest prayer we can make for friends is that Christ will dwell with their spirits, make them holy, save them and eventually receive them to himself.

Many who believed as Paul did are already before God's throne praising their Lord. May we follow their example.
