# 2 Chronicles 26 — Modern English

## Uzziah's prosperous reign (verses 1–15)

As long as Uzziah sought the Lord and paid attention to true religion, God made him prosper.

Only those whom God makes prosperous truly prosper, because success comes from him.

Many people can testify that they prospered while they sought God and attended to their duties, but difficulties multiplied when they turned away.

God does not continually bless laziness or withhold his blessing from diligence.

No one who sincerely seeks him does so in vain.

Uzziah became famous throughout the neighboring countries.

Having a good reputation before **God and godly people** is true honor.

He did not delight in warfare or excessive amusement, but loved **agriculture**.

## Uzziah tries to burn incense (verses 16–23)

Earlier kings had sinned by **abandoning God's temple and burning incense at idolatrous altars**.

Uzziah's sin was different: he entered God's sanctuary and tried to burn incense on **God's own altar**.

How difficult it is to avoid one extreme without falling into the opposite!

Pride lay at the root of his wrongdoing.

Instead of lifting up God's name in gratitude for everything he had received, Uzziah lifted up **his own heart to his own harm**.

The desire for forbidden knowledge and honors beyond one's proper place often comes from pride.

Our prayers, like incense, must be placed by faith into the hands of **Jesus Christ our great High Priest**, or we cannot expect God to accept them (**Revelation 8:3**).

Uzziah argued with the priests but could not prevail against his Creator.

He was afflicted with leprosy and lived apart from society until death.

His punishment corresponded to his sin.

Because he sought unlawful honor, God humbled and disgraced him.

Those who covet forbidden honors may lose the honors lawfully available to them.

Adam reached for knowledge from the forbidden tree and thereby lost access to the tree of life.

Let everyone who reads this acknowledge the Lord's righteousness.

If God sees fit to set aside even useful and prosperous people, like broken vessels, and raises up others to take their place, they should willingly lay aside worldly concerns and devote their remaining days to preparing for death.
