# Leviticus 26 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

| Threatenings against disobedience. | 14–39 |
| God promises to remember those that repent. | 40–46 |

## Promises given for obedience (verses 1–13)

This chapter reinforces the laws given through Moses.

God promises rewards for obedience and warns of punishment for disobedience.

As long as Israel honored God's worship, Sabbath and sanctuary as a nation, and did not turn toward idols, the Lord promised continuing earthly benefits and religious privileges.

These promises particularly concerned life in the present world.

Yet they also pictured the spiritual blessings secured to believers through Christ in the covenant of grace.

Henry identifies seven blessings.

**First, abundance.** The land would produce plentiful fruit. Every good and perfect gift comes from the Father of lights.

**Second, peace.** God would protect his people. Those who dwell in him are safe.

**Third, victory.** God could save them in war whether their army was large or small.

**Fourth, increase in population.** In the spiritual picture, the church of the gospel would become fruitful.

**Fifth, God's favor.** This is the source of every real good.

**Sixth, signs of God's presence through appointed worship.** If we want his ordinances to remain among us, we must hold fast to them.

**Seventh, the grace of the covenant.** All the blessings are brought together in God's promise to be their God and make them his people.

Every covenant blessing rests upon redemption.

God had purchased Israel as his own people.

He would not cast them off until they first rejected him.

## Warnings about disobedience (verses 14–39)

After the blessings God placed curses before his people.

These were warnings about what would make them miserable if they refused to obey.

Henry identifies two leading paths to ruin.

The first is **despising God's commands**.

People who first reject his rules may eventually reject his covenant altogether.

The second is **despising God's correction**.

If suffering does not teach them obedience, God himself will stand against them, which is the deepest cause of their misery.

All creation will become hostile toward them as God's judgments are sent upon them.

The warnings are detailed prophecies.

The Lord knew Israel's later rebellions and what would follow from them.

The judgments include earthly consequences.

People who refuse to part from sin when God commands them will be separated from it through his judgments.

Those who cling to their sinful desires will suffer enough from them.

The judgments also include spiritual consequences affecting the mind and conscience.

The people will find no acceptance with God and will continually be terrified by guilt.

It is just for God to leave arrogant sinners, who deliberately presume upon his mercy, without any sense of pardon.

Only free grace prevents us from wasting away under the sins into which we were born and which we have continued to commit.

## God remembers those who repent (verses 40–46)

Among individuals in Israel, prosperity and suffering did **not** always correspond directly to a person's obedience or wrongdoing.

But the people had a unique **national covenant**.

Under it, national obedience was linked to national prosperity, and national disobedience brought national judgment.

Wickedness can ruin any nation, especially where God's word and the light of the gospel are available.

Sooner or later, sin brings both shame and destruction to a people.

May we humble ourselves for our sins now and turn away the approaching storm before it breaks.

May God grant that, while time remains, we attend to the things that bring lasting peace with him.
