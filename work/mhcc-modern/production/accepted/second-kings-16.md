## Ahaz's wicked reign (verses 1–9)

Ahaz's days were few and filled with evil.

Those whose own consciences condemn them will turn almost anywhere for help in trouble rather than turn to God.

His sin became its own punishment.

It is common for people to try to escape the difficulties created by one sin through committing another.

## Ahaz copies a foreign altar (verses 10–16)

Until this time God's altar had remained in its appointed place and had been used for worship.

Ahaz replaced it with another.

Human beings have a natural inclination toward some form of religion, and that inclination is not easily extinguished.

But unless it is governed by **God's word and Spirit**, it can produce foolish superstition or detestable idolatry.

At best, it may quiet a guilty conscience through meaningless ceremonies.

People who reject true faith have often been especially willing to believe ridiculous falsehoods.

## Ahaz damages the temple (verses 17–20)

Ahaz treated the Sabbath with contempt, opening the way for many other sins.

He did this to please the **king of Assyria**.

People who once had ready access to the Lord's house but turn away from it to please their neighbors are rapidly moving toward ruin.
