# Proverbs 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter-level introductory comment

Throughout Proverbs, we should look for a meaning beyond each passage's first and most immediate application. **Henry sees that deeper meaning in Jesus Christ**, the Wisdom so often spoken of throughout this book.

## Verse 1 — Children and their parents

Parents' happiness depends greatly upon their children. This gives children and parents alike strong reasons to fulfill their duties faithfully.

## Verses 2 and 3 — The soul's real needs

A righteous person may be poor, but the Lord will not let that person lack what is necessary for **spiritual life**.

## Verse 4 — Diligence in the Lord's service

People who are **fervent in spirit, serving the Lord** are likely to become rich in **faith and good works**.

## Verse 5 — Use opportunities wisely

Those who waste their opportunities deserve rebuke, for they are neglecting chances to prepare both for **this life and the life to come**.

## Verse 6 — True blessing

The blessings resting upon faithful people are not empty appearances: they are **real blessings**.

## Verse 7 — The difference that outlasts death

Both righteous and wicked people must die, but their souls face **vastly different conditions**.

## Verse 8 — Wisdom put into practice

A person who is wise in heart **acts upon the knowledge** received.

## Verse 9 — The exposure of deceit

People who disguise their intentions and continually evade the truth will eventually be **found out**.

## Verse 10 — Craftiness does not excuse sin

Cleverness and cunning cannot make wrongdoing innocent.

## Verse 11 — Words that bring life

A good person speaks to **teach, encourage and correct** others.

## Verse 12 — Love maintains peace

Where hatred rules, every small event produces quarrels. By patiently **bearing with one another**, we preserve peace and harmony.

## Verse 13 — Foolishness invites correction

Those who persist foolishly in wicked ways are preparing **their own punishment**.

## Verse 14 — Store up useful knowledge

We should treasure all knowledge that may be useful, so it will be ready when we need it. We gain this wisdom through **reading, listening to God's word, meditation, prayer and faith in Christ**, whom God makes wisdom for us.

## Verse 15 — The mistaken confidence of rich and poor

Both rich and poor people may misunderstand their material circumstances. **Wealth can expose people to many dangers**. Someone with little may nevertheless live comfortably with contentment, a good conscience and faith.

## Verse 16 — Work that leads to life

A righteous person may own nothing beyond what hard work produces. Yet that labor **leads to life**.

## Verse 17 — Receive correction

A traveler who has lost the way and refuses directions toward the right road will **remain lost**.

## Verse 18 — Nothing can be hidden from God

It is especially foolish to imagine anything can be concealed from God. Malice is no less evil.

## Verse 19 — Restrain excessive speech

Those who speak too much often say many things they should not. Restraining our words is **wisdom** and helps preserve our peace.

## Verses 20 and 21 — The words of the righteous

The speech of the righteous is sincere, free from the impurities of deceit and evil design. Godly conversation becomes **spiritual nourishment for people in need**. Foolish people perish because they lack **heart or thoughtful understanding**, as the Hebrew expression indicates.

## Verse 22 — Wealth without sorrow

The truly desirable kind of wealth brings neither anguish in enjoying it, despair when it is lost, nor guilt through misuse. What comes from **God's love** is accompanied by **God's grace**.

## Verse 23 — Harm is no game

Only the foolish and wicked take pleasure in harming others or enticing them into sin.

## Verse 24 — The hope of the righteous

The righteous person's greatest desire for **eternal blessings** will be fulfilled.

## Verse 25 — The brief prosperity of sinners

The apparent success of unrepentant sinners resembles **a whirlwind**: it soon exhausts itself and vanishes.

## Verse 26 — A lazy worker brings distress

Just as **vinegar irritates the teeth** and **smoke stings the eyes**, a lazy person causes frustration and trouble to an employer.

## Verses 27 and 28 — Reverence and lasting hope

Who desires life? Fear God. He gives his people all the life necessary in this world and **eternal life** in the next. Their hope is therefore lasting.

## Verse 29 — The believer grows stronger

Those who trust God increase in **faith**, and obedience becomes increasingly delightful to them.

## Verse 30 — Earth is not the wicked person's permanent home

Wicked people might wish to remain on earth among their idols forever, but they cannot. They must **die and leave those idols behind**.

## Verses 31 and 32 — Speech that benefits others

A good person speaks with wisdom for other people's benefit. The wicked speak things displeasing to God and offensive to those around them; this is both their sin and their ruin. The righteous are **preserved by God's power**, and nothing can separate them from **God's love in Christ Jesus**.
