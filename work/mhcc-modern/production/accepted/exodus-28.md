## Aaron and his sons are appointed as priests (verses 1–5)

Until this time, the heads of households had served as priests and offered sacrifices.

Now the office was restricted to Aaron's family, and that arrangement continued until the coming of the gospel.

The priests' special clothing distinguished them from the people.

But it also represented the holy conduct that should be the **glory and beauty** of everyone who ministers in God's name.

Without such a life, both the minister and the ministry will be despised.

These garments also pointed to the glory of divine majesty and the beauty of perfect holiness.

Those qualities make Jesus Christ our great High Priest.

Under the gospel, our own adornment should not consist chiefly of gold and costly clothing.

It should be the garments of salvation and the robe of righteousness.

## The ephod (verses 6–14)

The high priest wore a richly made ephod as his outer garment.

Other priests wore plain linen ephods.

The high priest's ephod was a short sleeveless garment, fastened closely around the body with a belt.

Its shoulder pieces joined together by precious stones set in gold.

One stone was placed on each shoulder, engraved with the names of Israel's children.

This pictured Christ, our High Priest, who carries his people before the Lord as a continual memorial.

Henry compares the ephod with Christ's seamless coat, woven in one piece from the top.

He also speaks of the golden bells associated with this garment: their value and pleasant sound represent the good profession believers make.

The pomegranates represent the good fruit they bear.

## The breastplate; the Urim and Thummim (verses 15–30)

The high priest's finest ornament was his breastplate, a beautifully worked piece of cloth.

Each tribe's name was engraved upon a precious stone set into it.

These stones showed how precious and honorable God's people are in his sight.

Even the smallest and poorest tribe was represented by a jewel on the high priest's breast.

Likewise, every believer is dear to Christ, no matter how little other people value that believer.

The priest carried the tribes' names on both his shoulders and his breast.

This reminds us of the **strength and love** with which Jesus intercedes for his own.

He carries them with almighty power and holds them close with tender affection.

What comfort this gives us whenever we approach God!

The **Urim and Thummim** were placed in the breastplate.

Through them, God's will was made known in difficult or doubtful matters.

Their names mean **light** and **integrity**, Henry explains.

Many different ideas have been proposed about what they actually were.

He considers it most likely that they were the twelve precious stones in the breastplate.

But Christ is now our divine Oracle.

In these last days, God reveals himself and his will to us through his Son (Hebrews 1:1–2; John 1:18).

Christ is the true Light, the faithful Witness and the Truth itself.

From him we receive the Spirit of Truth, who guides us into all truth.

## The robe and the golden plate (verses 31–39)

The robe worn beneath the ephod reached to the knees and had no sleeves.

Aaron had to minister in the garments God appointed.

We must serve the Lord with holy fear, remembering that in ourselves we deserve death.

A golden plate was fastened to Aaron's forehead.

On it were engraved the words **“Holiness to the Lord.”**

This reminded him that God is holy and that priests must be holy and wholly dedicated to him.

The declaration appeared openly on Aaron's forehead.

In the same way, our belonging to God should be openly professed.

The letters were engraved like those on a seal.

They were meant to be deep and lasting—not merely painted on, to be washed away.

Our holiness to the Lord must be just as durable and real.

Christ is our High Priest.

Through him our sins are forgiven and no longer counted against us.

Only because of Christ are our persons and actions pleasing to God.

## Clothing for Aaron's sons (verses 40–43)

The priests' garments pointed to the righteousness of Christ.

Without that righteousness, we will appear before God bearing our own guilt and will die.

Blessed is the one who watches and keeps his garments (Revelation 16:15).

And blessed be God that he has given us a High Priest of his own choosing.

Christ has been appointed and set apart for his work.

The glory of his divine majesty and the beauty of his perfect holiness equip him fully for his great office.

Happy are we if the spiritual meaning of the law teaches us how perfectly suited he is to our need.

We cannot approach a holy God or find acceptance except through him.

We receive no light, wisdom or perfection apart from Christ.

We possess no true beauty or glory except through becoming like him.

Let us take courage from our High Priest's power, love and compassion.

We may come boldly to the throne of grace, where we will receive mercy and find grace for the help we need.

## Editorial verification — Exodus 28

**Secondary witness:** [CCEL, *Concise Commentary*, Exodus 28](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.iii.xxviii.html). **MHCC 0.1.1** remains the source of record; exact native unit/markup/reference checks are pending.

- **1–5:** family heads formerly serving as priests; Aaronic restriction until the gospel; garments showing **character and holiness as ministerial glory/beauty**, Christ's high-priestly majesty and the believer's robe of righteousness — retained.
- **6–14:** costly short ephod, lesser priests' linen, sleeveless belt/shoulder stones and names carried as Christ's memorial, **seamless coat**, golden bells as profession and pomegranates as spiritual fruit — retained.
- **15–30:** tribal jewels precious even for the poorest, Christ's **strength on shoulders and tender love on breast**, Urim/Thummim **light and integrity**, alternative conjectures with Henry's **tentative** preference for the twelve stones, Christ revealing God with **Hebrews 1:1–2; John 1:18**, and the Spirit of Truth — retained.
- **31–39:** under-robe, prescribed priestly clothing, holy fear, forehead plate, **permanent engraved “Holiness” versus washable paint**, forgiveness and acceptance only through Christ — retained.
- **40–43:** Christ's righteousness necessary for acceptance, **Revelation 16:15**, his appointed perfect priesthood and tender invitation to the throne of grace — retained.

**Original textual caution:** Henry's **6–14** discussion locates the golden bells with “this ephod,” whereas the biblical clothing description places bells on the **robe** later in the chapter.
