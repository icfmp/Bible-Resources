# Acts 7 — Modern English

## Stephen defends himself from Israel's history (verses 1–50)

### Abraham, Joseph and the promises of God (verses 1–16)

Stephen had been accused of blaspheming God and abandoning Israel's religion.

He therefore showed that he was a true descendant of Abraham and valued the history and promises of God's people.

The promise to Abraham advanced slowly toward fulfillment.

That slow development revealed its **spiritual meaning**, looking beyond earthly Canaan toward **the heavenly country**.

God was with Joseph in his troubles.

Through his Spirit he gave Joseph personal comfort and favor in the eyes of those around him.

Stephen reminded the hearers of Israel's humble beginnings, correcting their pride in the nation's later greatness.

He recalled the patriarchs' envy of Joseph.

The same hostility toward God's appointed deliverer could now be seen in the rejection of **Christ and his servants**.

The patriarchs' desire for burial in Canaan showed faith looking toward an everlasting home.

When long-established religious customs have been distorted, it is wise to return to their original meaning.

To learn the nature and fruits of **justifying faith**, study Abraham, the father of believers.

His call reveals the **power and freedom of divine grace** and what conversion truly means.

Outward religious forms and distinctions count for little compared with separation from sinful ways and wholehearted dedication to God.

### The childhood and early work of Moses (verses 17–29)

Do not despair when God's promises appear slow to unfold.

Times of suffering often become **times when the church grows**.

God may be preparing deliverance precisely when his people face their darkest hour.

Moses was exceedingly beautiful before God.

The beauty most precious in God's sight is **holiness**.

He was preserved wonderfully in infancy because God intended to employ him in an important work.

If God guarded the infant Moses, how much more surely will he protect **the purposes of his beloved Son Jesus** against those opposing him!

Stephen's enemies insisted upon the honor of Moses and his law to discredit Jesus.

But if they had not deliberately resisted the truth, they could have seen that Jesus delivers from **a slavery worse than Egypt's bondage**.

Human beings may prolong their own troubles, yet God will care for his servants and carry out his merciful plans.

### Moses meets God and leads Israel (verses 30–41)

No one should imagine God can act only in a particular location.

He can lead his people into **a wilderness** and speak comfort to them there.

The bush burned without being consumed.

It pictured Israel enduring Egypt's furnace of affliction without destruction.

Henry also regards it as a possible type of Christ's incarnation, in which **divine and human natures** are united.

The deaths of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob did not end God's covenant relationship with them.

Jesus himself used that continuing covenant to prove the **future life** (**Matthew 22:31**).

Abraham died, yet God remains his God; therefore Abraham lives.

The gospel has brought life and immortality into clear light.

Moses, as Israel's deliverer, was an important **type of Christ**.

God hears the sighs of his persecuted people and acts from compassion.

Israel's rescue from Egypt pictured Jesus descending from heaven for our salvation.

The very Jesus whom Stephen's audience rejected was appointed by God as **Prince and Savior**, just as the Israelites once rejected Moses.

Calling Moses an instrument does not dishonor him.

Jesus infinitely surpasses him.

Indeed, Stephen honored Moses by declaring his prophecy of Christ fulfilled.

God, who had given Israel ceremonies through Moses, had the authority to change those ceremonies through **his Son**.

Yet Israel had wanted to return to slavery rather than obey Moses.

Likewise many refuse Christ because they love this present evil world and their own works.

### Idolatry and God's true dwelling place (verses 42–50)

Stephen reminded the people of their ancestors' idolatry.

God permitted them to pursue false worship as judgment for having first abandoned him.

It did not dishonor God when **the tabernacle gave way to the temple**.

Nor did it dishonor him when the earthly temple gave way to **spiritual worship**; eventually the spiritual order will lead to everlasting worship in heaven.

The whole universe belongs to God.

He is present everywhere and fills creation with his glory.

He therefore does not need a building to contain him or reveal that he is divine.

Heaven is his throne and earth his footstool.

None of our services enriches the One who created everything.

After **the human nature of Christ**, the dwelling place most precious to God is **a broken and spiritually renewed human heart**.

## Stephen confronts his accusers (verses 51–53)

Stephen seems to have been preparing to explain that temple service must give way to worship **in spirit and truth**.

He recognized that his listeners would not receive the argument.

With wisdom, courage and power from the Holy Spirit, he rebuked their hostility.

When clear reasoning only hardens opponents of the gospel, they must still be warned about **their guilt and danger**.

Their stubbornness resembled the resistance of their ancestors.

Fallen human hearts contain a fleshly principle that **resists the Holy Spirit**.

But Henry also teaches that among God's chosen people this resistance is overcome by grace at the appointed time.

The gospel now came through the Holy Spirit, not merely through angels.

Yet Stephen's opponents refused both God's law and his gospel.

Their guilty consciences were disturbed, but instead of repentance and prayer, they sought relief by **killing the person who exposed their sin**.

## Stephen is killed while praying (verses 54–60)

Nothing gives greater comfort to a dying believer or encouragement to a suffering believer than seeing **Jesus at God's right hand**.

Through faith we too can look toward him there.

Stephen prayed **two brief prayers** as he died.

Jesus is truly God, the One to whom we may pray and in whom we may place our confidence in life and death.

Those who have trusted him during life may find comfort in him at death.

Stephen also prayed for his persecutors.

Their wrongdoing was dreadful, but if they would honestly acknowledge and repent of it, God would not finally hold it against them.

Stephen's death came suddenly and violently.

Yet Scripture says **he fell asleep**.

He prepared himself for death with the calmness of someone going to bed.

He will awaken on the morning of **the resurrection**, entering the Lord's presence, where joy is complete and pleasures endure forever.
