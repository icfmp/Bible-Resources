# Revelation 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A pause between two great periods. | 1–3 |
| The peace, happiness, and safety of the saints, as signified by an angel's sealing 144,000. | 4–8 |
| A song of praise. | 9–12 |
| The blessedness and glory of those that suffered martyrdom for Christ. | 13–17 |

## God's people are sealed before the coming judgments (verses 1–8)

In the Bible's figurative language, the **four winds blowing together** signify terrible and widespread destruction.

But the destruction is delayed.

Seals are used to identify the possessions belonging to a particular owner.

This seal represents **the witness of the Holy Spirit impressed upon believers' hearts**.

The Lord would not allow his people to enter their trials before they had been marked as his, so they might be prepared for the conflicts before them.

The seal is placed upon the **forehead**.

It can be seen by both friends and enemies, although believers themselves recognize it only as they look steadily into **the mirror of God's word**.

The number of those sealed, **144,000**, may represent the remnant God has preserved.

Although God's church is a small flock compared with the ungodly world, it is in fact a great society, and the Lord will make it greater still.

The universal church is represented here **under the figure of Israel**.

## A great multitude gives praise to God and the Lamb (verses 9–12)

After the first believers who came to Christ, the converted **Gentile nations** followed them.

Together they joyfully acknowledged that their salvation came from **God and the Redeemer**.

When we worship, we draw near to God, but we can do so **only through Christ**.

Without a Mediator, sinners could not approach the throne.

The worshipers' **white robes** signify justification, holiness and victory.

Their **palm branches** are the emblems carried by conquerors in triumph.

So every faithful servant of God will finally appear after fighting the good fight of faith and finishing the appointed course.

The redeemed will openly and fervently praise **both the Father and the Son** for their great salvation.

We can already see what heaven's work will be.

Let us begin it on earth, devoting our hearts increasingly to thankful praise and longing for the place where **both our praise and our happiness** will be perfect.

## The martyrs' heavenly rest and comfort (verses 13–17)

Faithful Christians deserve respect and careful attention.

Anyone who wishes to learn should not be ashamed to ask someone able to explain a matter.

The way to heaven leads through many trials.

But even the greatest suffering cannot separate Christ's people from the love of God.

Indeed, affliction can make heaven more welcome and its glory more precious.

The redeemed do not become pure through **the blood they themselves shed as martyrs**.

Only **the blood of the Lamb** cleanses sinners from their guilt and makes their robes white before God.

Other blood stains; Christ's blood alone cleanses.

In heaven they are happy in their service.

Heaven is a state of **service without suffering**, and **rest without idleness**—a delightful rest filled with praise.

They once experienced sorrow and shed tears because of sin and suffering.

But God, with the tender care of a father, will wipe every tear away.

Remembering this should sustain Christians under their present troubles.

Every redeemed person owes eternal happiness entirely to **sovereign mercy**.

God's worship is their delight; his presence and favor complete their joy.

They cannot imagine any greater happiness.

May all his people come to him for needed grace and offer him all praise and glory.
