# Isaiah 64 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The church prays that God's power may be manifested. | 1–5 |
| A confession of sin, and afflictions bewailed. | 6–12 |

## The church pleads for God to reveal his power (verses 1–5)

God's people long for him to act visibly **for them and among them**, so that everyone will recognize his power.

This prayer also applies to **Christ's second coming**, when the Lord himself will descend from heaven.

They appeal to what God has done before and to the kind purposes he has announced for his people.

They need not fear that his promises will disappoint them **by failing to come true**, because his promises are sure.

Nor will they be disappointed **by their fulfillment**, because what God provides is completely sufficient.

The happiness of his people rests upon what he has **planned and is preparing for them**—and upon the way he is preparing **them** for that blessedness.

It rests on all that God has already done and everything he will yet do.

If we believe this, how could we consider anything too great to expect from **his truth, power and love**?

The happiness God promises is **spiritual**, beyond the full comprehension of unaided human understanding.

He stands ready to show grace.

Notice the fellowship between **a gracious God and a soul renewed by grace**.

We should conscientiously fulfill everything the Lord requires of us.

God meets those who seek to walk in his ways.

This reveals his willingness and eagerness to do them good.

He has been rightly angry with us for our sins.

But that anger is temporary for his reconciled people, whereas **his favor is life**, continuing and sustaining their salvation.

## Confession, helplessness and appeal to the Father (verses 6–12)

In affliction, God's people confess and mourn their sins.

They admit that they have no claim upon his mercy.

Sin is the hateful thing that God opposes.

Whatever outward goodness our deeds seem to possess, **if we offer them as a way of earning God's acceptance**, they are only **rags**.

They cannot cover our guilt.

Indeed, they become like **filthy rags**, making our condition worse.

Even the few good works that really have excellence because they are **fruits of the Holy Spirit** remain imperfect and stained by the way we carry them out.

They too must be washed in **the fountain opened for sin and uncleanness**.

It is a dangerous sign when people stop praying.

To pray is to take hold, by faith, of God's promises of good will.

We plead those promises, cling to him and earnestly ask him **not to leave us** or to return to us when we feel his absence.

The people had brought their trouble on themselves through foolishness.

Sin withers sinners, like something blighted, before the **wind of their own wrongdoing** carries them away.

When they made themselves unclean, it was no surprise that God regarded them with displeasure.

Yet even in their foolishness, poverty and disgrace they could say:

**“You are still our Father.”**

The anger they endured was the correction of **a Father who could be reconciled to them**.

They expected relief from him alone and entrusted their cause to him.

They did not ask, **“Lord, do not rebuke us at all.”**

Correction might be necessary.

Instead, their plea was, **“Do not rebuke us in your displeasure.”**

They described their pitiable condition.

See the ruin sin can bring upon a people!

An outward profession of holiness provides no protection against it.

The people did not tell God exactly what to say or do.

They asked him to speak for **their comfort and relief**.

How few sincerely call upon the Lord or stir themselves up to cling to him!

He may delay the answer to prayer.

But he will finally answer those who call upon his name and hope in his mercy.
