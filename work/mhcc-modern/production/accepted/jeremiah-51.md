## Babylon's coming destruction (verses 1–58)

The details of this prophecy are interwoven and repeated.

Babylon possessed enormous treasures, but neither its **waters nor its wealth** could keep it safe.

Judgment would arrive when the Babylonians least expected it.

Wherever we are—even in the deepest trouble or far from home—we should **remember the Lord our God**.

At moments of greatest fear or greatest hope, remembering him is especially necessary.

Henry connects the grief caused by Babylon's downfall with the lament over **“New Testament Babylon” in Revelation 18:9,19**.

He understands the ruin of systems supporting **idolatry, unbelief and superstition** as clearing the way for a revival of true godliness.

In his prophetic application, a great power characterized by **anti-Christian tyranny, idolatry, superstition and persecution of true Christians** is as certainly doomed to judgment as ancient Babylon.

This is Henry's interpretation of the later biblical imagery, not a new identification of a particular modern group.

Many people would then mourn their sins and seek the Lord.

The lost sheep of Israel's house would return to **the Good Shepherd's fold** and no longer wander away.

The exact fulfillment of ancient prophecies strengthens our faith in **all God's promises and the prophecies of Scripture**.

## The written prophecy is sent to Babylon (verses 59–64)

**Seraiah** was entrusted with taking the prophecy to Babylon and reading it to his fellow Jews in captivity.

Through faith they could look beyond the power oppressing them, foresee its end and be encouraged.

The world offers impressive sights and flattering promises, but Scripture announces that its proud splendor will not last.

The scroll was to be **thrown into the Euphrates**.

Henry connects that sign to the downfall of **New Testament Babylon in Revelation 18:21**.

Those who finally sink under God's wrath and curse sink forever.

Babylon and every power that opposes Christ will ultimately fall and not rise again.

Let us hope in God's word and **wait quietly for his salvation**.

Then we will witness the judgment of wickedness **without sharing in its condemnation**.
