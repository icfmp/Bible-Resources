## John the Baptist's ministry (verses 1–14)

The purpose of John's ministry was to turn people **away from their sins and toward the Savior**.

He did not preach loyalty to a sect or party, but a profession of repentance.

**Baptism with water** was the outward sign.

John taught that repentance was necessary for the forgiveness of sins. Washing with water represented the **inward cleansing and renewal of heart** that accompanies or results from genuine repentance, as well as a public profession of it.

John fulfilled the prophecy of **Isaiah 40:3**.

Preparing the way for the gospel in someone's heart means bringing down proud thoughts, submitting them to Christ and removing what obstructs the coming of his grace.

John gave warnings and practical instructions.

Fallen humanity has become a brood of vipers, corrupt and hostile even toward one another.

The only escape from coming judgment is **repentance**, shown by a changed way of life.

Neither religious profession nor membership in the visible people of God is of any value without holiness of **heart and conduct**.

Such privilege only makes judgment more serious if we do not produce fruit worthy of repentance.

John addressed different kinds of people according to their circumstances and occupations.

Anyone claiming repentance must show it through a reformed life appropriate to their responsibilities.

The gospel calls us to **mercy rather than empty sacrifice**, doing every possible good and treating everyone justly.

The same principle that makes a person refuse dishonest gain also leads that person to **restore what was gained by wrongdoing**.

John gave soldiers particular instructions suited to the temptations of their work.

His answers both explained immediate duties and **tested the sincerity** of those asking.

No one can genuinely receive salvation through Christ without repentance, and the marks of that repentance can be seen in such actions.

## John bears witness to Christ (verses 15–20)

John denied being the Messiah, but strengthened people's expectation of the long-promised Savior.

John could exhort them to repent and declare God's forgiveness upon repentance.

He could not **produce repentance in their hearts or grant remission himself**.

What an example of speaking highly of Christ and humbly of ourselves!

John baptized only with water as a sign of the need for cleansing.

**Christ baptizes with the Holy Spirit.**

Jesus gives the Spirit to cleanse the heart, not only as water removes outward dirt but as **fire purifies metal**, burning away its impurities and making it fit for a new form.

John preached affectionately, pleading with his hearers.

He preached practically, awakening them to their duties and showing them what to do.

He spoke in language ordinary people could understand.

He preached evangelically, always directing his hearers toward **Christ for both righteousness and strength**.

He preached abundantly, withholding nothing from the counsel of God.

Yet his public ministry was abruptly stopped while he was still useful.

John rebuked Herod's many sins, and Herod imprisoned him.

People who mistreat faithful servants of God increase their own guilt.

## Jesus is baptized (verses 21 and 22)

Jesus did **not** confess sin as the other people did, because he had no sin to confess.

Yet he prayed, as they did, remaining in fellowship with his Father.

Henry observes that the three heavenly testimonies to Jesus' identity were given while he was praying or soon afterward (**Luke 9:35; John 12:28**).

The **Holy Spirit** descended upon him in a visible form like a dove.

A voice came from heaven, from **God the Father**, bearing witness to **God the Son**.

His baptism thus displayed the truth of **the Holy Trinity—three persons in the one Godhead**.

## The genealogy of Jesus (verses 23–38)

Matthew's genealogy shows Jesus as **Abraham's descendant**, through whom every family on earth would be blessed, and as heir to **David's royal throne**.

Luke's genealogy traces the line back to **Adam**, showing Jesus as the promised offspring of the woman who would crush the serpent's head.

Henry describes **Heli as Mary's father rather than Joseph's**, presenting this as an explanation of the apparent differences between the two Gospel genealogies.

Other learned interpreters have proposed explanations of those differences.

But our salvation does not depend on our ability to solve every genealogical difficulty. Such difficulties do **not** overthrow the divine authority of the Gospels.

Adam is called **the son of God** in the sense that he was God's offspring by creation.

Christ is both **Son of Adam and Son of God**, qualified to be the Mediator between God and the descendants of Adam and to bring those descendants into God's family through himself.

All flesh descended from the first Adam is like grass that dries and fades.

Those who receive the Spirit of life from **the second Adam** possess the eternal blessedness announced in the gospel.
