# John 4 — Modern English

## Jesus leaves Judea for Galilee (verses 1–3)

Jesus focused chiefly on **preaching the gospel**, a more excellent task than administering baptism (**1 Corinthians 1:17**).

He honored his disciples by giving them responsibility for baptizing.

We learn that the benefit of a sacrament does **not depend upon the particular human hand administering it**.

## Jesus speaks with the Samaritan woman (verses 4–26)

The Jews and Samaritans despised one another.

Jesus traveled through Samaria on his way from Judea to Galilee.

We should avoid unnecessary exposure to temptations. When duty requires us to pass through danger, we should not linger there.

Jesus grew tired on his journey.

This shows **he was truly human**.

He had accepted the burdens of fallen human life in order to become a curse for us, though he himself was without sin.

He was poor and traveled on foot.

When tired, he sat beside a well without a comfortable couch to rest upon.

Should we complain when our circumstances resemble the poverty and fatigue the Son of God willingly accepted?

Jesus asked a woman for water.

She was surprised because he did not share the hatred of his countrymen toward Samaritans.

Moderation and kindness across party divisions can astonish people.

Jesus used the opportunity to teach her about divine things.

He exposed **her ignorance, her sin and her need of a Savior**, bringing her to conversion.

The **living water** means **the Holy Spirit**.

The coming Messiah had been promised through this image in the Old Testament.

The Spirit's grace and comfort satisfy a thirsty soul that understands its deepest needs.

The woman took Jesus' figurative words literally.

Water from Jacob's well could provide satisfaction only briefly.

Every earthly comfort eventually leaves us thirsty again.

But whoever receives God's Spirit and the gospel's consolations will receive enough to satisfy the soul completely.

The worldly heart remains occupied with worldly aims.

The woman initially wanted this water merely to avoid the trouble of visiting the well.

Sinful thinking is ingenious in escaping conviction.

Jesus then brought the truth directly home to her conscience by exposing **her present way of life**.

She recognized his prophetic authority.

The ability of Jesus' word to search the heart and reveal hidden sin demonstrates his divine authority.

Our arguments about religious locations would be less heated if we remembered how temporary such questions are.

The **object of worship remains God as Father**, but controversies about a particular worship location would no longer determine where God accepts worshipers.

Reason suggests arranging suitable and orderly places to meet.

Yet no place possesses greater acceptance before God simply by its location.

Jesus acknowledged that the **Jewish people had the true worship and scriptural knowledge of God**: **salvation is from the Jews**.

Other nations received God's saving message through them.

But the system of worship established under the old covenant was itself approaching fulfillment and conclusion.

God would be known as Father by believers **from every nation**.

True worship is the soul communing with God **under the Holy Spirit's influence**.

The prayers, requests and thanksgiving of an upright heart, moved by spiritual love, are worship that honors and pleases him.

The woman preferred to postpone the question until the Messiah arrived.

Jesus answered plainly: **“I who speak to you am he.”**

He revealed himself exceptionally clearly to a woman who was both a Samaritan and a social outsider.

No past sins prevent anyone from being received by Christ when they humble themselves and believe in him as **the Savior of the world**.

## The woman's testimony reaches Samaria (verses 27–42)

The disciples were surprised to see Jesus talking with a Samaritan woman.

Yet they recognized that he must have a good reason for doing so.

When his words or providence confuse us, we can rest assured that what he does is wise and good.

Two things especially impressed the woman.

**First**, Jesus knew the thoughts, words and actions of human beings.

**Second**, his word had confronted her secret sins with power.

She told other people about the very details she might have been expected to keep hidden.

A knowledge of Christ gained through **true conviction of sin** is most likely to be sound and saving.

The townspeople came to hear Jesus.

He used the occasion to describe doing his Father's will as **food**, a task performed with joy as well as diligence.

He compared his work to **harvesting**.

The coming harvest is planned and awaited.

Once it arrives, everyone must work while the opportunity remains, because harvest time is brief and cannot be recovered after it passes.

God sometimes employs the weakest and most unexpected people in accomplishing great work.

Through one poor woman, Jesus spread saving knowledge throughout a town.

The townspeople's faith grew in three ways.

**Its content** grew: they believed Jesus to be **the Savior of the whole world**, not only of the Jews.

**Its certainty** grew: they became convinced he truly was the Christ.

**Its foundation** grew: they now believed because **they had heard him themselves**, not only because of the woman's report.

## Jesus heals an official's son (verses 43–54)

The boy's father held an honorable position, but wealth and status did not protect his child from sickness.

Even the greatest people must approach God as beggars in need.

The father persisted in seeking help.

At first his faith was weak: he doubted whether Jesus could heal from a distance.

We find it difficult to believe that **time and place cannot limit Christ's knowledge, love or power**.

Jesus gave him a reassuring answer.

When he speaks life to a soul, **his word itself gives life**.

The man returned home, showing real confidence in what Jesus had said.

He did not rush home that night in panic; his mind rested in the promise.

His servants met him with news of the child's healing.

Good news comes to those who hope in God's word.

Comparing Jesus' deeds with his promises strengthens faith.

The miracle brought not only health to the child but **saving faith to the household**.

A personal experience of Christ's word can establish his authority firmly within a soul.

His whole family believed, and Jesus became precious to them.

The knowledge of Christ continues to spread through families, bringing **spiritual health and salvation**.
