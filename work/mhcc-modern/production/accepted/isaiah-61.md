# Isaiah 61 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Messiah, his character and office. | 1–3 |
| His promises of the future blessedness of the church. | 4–9 |
| The church praises God for these mercies. | 10, 11 |

## The Spirit-anointed Messiah proclaims good news (verses 1–3)

The prophets sometimes received the **Holy Spirit** to teach them what to say and move them to proclaim it.

But Jesus Christ possessed the Spirit **always and without measure**.

As a man, he was thus perfectly equipped for the work the Father had appointed him to accomplish.

People who are materially poor are often more ready to receive the gospel (**James 2:5**).

Yet the gospel benefits us only when we receive it **with humility and meekness**.

To those who are **poor in spirit**, Christ announced good news, as when he pronounced blessing upon the meek.

**His atoning satisfaction has been accepted.**

Sin's dominion holds us under Satan's power.

But the Son is ready to make us free **by his Spirit**.

And if the Son makes us free, **we are free indeed**.

Christ came to destroy sin and Satan, and he triumphed over them through **his cross**.

Those who stubbornly refuse these invitations will be dealt with as enemies.

Christ was appointed to be **a Comforter**, and he truly is one.

He comforts all who mourn and look **to him rather than to the world** for relief.

His purpose is that believers should abound in the fruits of righteousness, like branches planted and tended by God.

God's mercy, Christ's atonement and the gospel of grace do not benefit people who remain proud and self-sufficient.

The Holy Spirit must humble them and show them their true character and needs.

Only then will they recognize their need of **the Friend and Savior of sinners**.

To people humbled before God, Christ's message is truly glad tidings.

## The restored people become God's servants (verses 4–9)

These promises first address Jews returning from captivity.

But they also reach everyone whom grace rescues from **spiritual bondage**.

An unholy soul resembles **a ruined city with broken walls** or **a destroyed house**.

Through the power of Christ's gospel and grace, that soul is restored to become **a dwelling place for God through the Spirit**.

God's grace can teach us a holy detachment from worldly concerns.

Our hands may remain busy in worldly duties, while our **hearts are not entangled in them** but remain wholly devoted to God and his service.

Henry connects that detachment with the picture of others carrying out the plowing and vineyard work.

Everyone whom the Lord sets free, **he also calls to serve**.

Serving God is **perfect freedom and the highest honor**.

All believers have been made **kings and priests to God**.

They should therefore live in ways worthy of that calling.

Those who have the Lord as their portion have received an excellent inheritance and should rejoice in it.

The fullness of heavenly joy will more than compensate for everything suffered or given in service.

God loves **truth and justice** and therefore hates every act of injustice.

Even an offering made to God **cannot justify robbery**.

Indeed, theft committed under the pretense of making a religious offering is especially hateful.

May the children of godly parents demonstrate the fruit of good instruction, their parents' prayers and the Lord's blessing through the lives they lead.

## The redeemed rejoice in salvation (verses 10 and 11)

Only those who are covered **now by Christ's righteousness** and renewed by **the sanctifying Holy Spirit** can finally wear the garments of salvation.

Those blessings will spread through future generations as fruit rises from the earth.

With reliable abundance and to the benefit of humanity, God will cause **righteousness and praise to grow**.

The good news will spread far away, until Christ's salvation is announced **to the ends of the earth**.

Let us pray earnestly that the Lord will cause this righteousness to grow among us.

It is the true excellence and glory of **Christian faith and profession**.
