# Deuteronomy 30 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

## Mercy promised to people who return (verses 1–10)

Henry sees here a promise of mercy for Israel in the latter days.

He connects these verses with the warnings given in the previous two chapters and interprets their fulfillment through the Roman destruction of Jerusalem and Israel's long dispersion.

He also understands them as promising a future event that had **not yet** taken place in his time.

He expects Jewish people to be converted to faith in Christ.

Many interpreters, he notes, also expect a later return to the land of Canaan.

This is **Henry's interpretation of prophecy**, faithfully reported rather than newly asserted as an established prediction by the editor.

The language of this chapter is, in large part, unconditional promise rather than merely a conditional offer.

The Lord himself promises to **circumcise the people's hearts**.

When regenerating grace removes corruption and divine love replaces love of sin, people truly repent, turn to God and obey him.

The Lord then delights in doing good to them.

Their change is not merely outward or a change in opinions.

It reaches into their hearts.

It produces hatred of sin and wholehearted love for God, reconciled to them through Jesus Christ.

The people Henry wrote about were far from that condition.

But so were the people who helped kill Jesus before they were converted in one hour on the day of Pentecost.

In the day of God's power, a nation can be born in a day.

He will fulfill his purpose at his own time.

As a **conditional** promise, the passage also applies to individuals and peoples everywhere.

Even the worst sinners who repent and are converted will receive pardon and be restored to God's favor.

## The command is near (verses 11–14)

God's command is not too high for ordinary people to understand.

Its meaning is not reserved for scholars.

It has been written plainly, so that even someone passing by can read it.

It is in people's mouths, in their ordinary language, available for reading and discussion with their children.

It has been made accessible even to those with little education.

This is especially true of the gospel of Christ, to which the apostle Paul applies these words.

God's word is close to us—and Christ is revealed in that word.

If we believe in our hearts that Jesus is the promised Messiah, and openly confess that faith with our mouths, we have Christ with us.

## Life and death are set before the people (verses 15–20)

What appeal could be more moving or leave a deeper impression?

Everyone desires life, goodness and happiness, and hopes to escape death, evil and misery.

The Lord has shown great compassion by revealing through his word the path that leads to everlasting happiness.

If people refuse that path, the fault is theirs.

The matter can be stated simply.

Love and serve God, and you will find life and happiness.

Turn away from him, reject his service and follow false gods, and you will bring ruin on yourselves.

Since humanity's fall, there has never been **more than one way to heaven**.

The Old and New Testaments reveal the same way, although the New Testament makes it clearer.

Moses spoke of the same way of being accepted by God that Paul later explained more fully.

Paul spoke of the same obedience that Moses had expounded in greater detail.

Both Testaments bring the true and good way close to us and make it known.
