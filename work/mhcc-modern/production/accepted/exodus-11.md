# Exodus 11 — Modern English

## God's final instructions (verses 1–3)

While Moses was still standing before Pharaoh, God secretly revealed what was about to happen.

This enabled Moses to warn Pharaoh of the last and most terrible judgment before leaving him.

Israel's slavery was almost over.

The Egyptians who had abused the Israelites in their labor would have preferred to send them away with nothing.

But God made sure the workers would not lose the wages of their toil.

He commanded them to request payment when they departed, and it was given to them.

God will vindicate those who have suffered injustice and have quietly entrusted their cause to him.

Those who endure patiently will not finally lose by doing so.

The Lord gave Israel favor in the Egyptians' eyes by showing how greatly he himself favored them.

He changed the Egyptians' feelings toward the people they had oppressed, moving them to show compassion.

God honors those who honor him.

## God announces the death of Egypt's firstborn (verses 4–10)

All the firstborn in Egypt would die at once.

This had been the first plague threatened, but it would be the last to take place.

See how slow God is to become angry!

The judgment was announced, and its time was fixed.

Every firstborn would fall into the sleep of death, not quietly, but amid the cries that would wake whole families at midnight.

The prince was not too powerful to be touched, nor was the slave working at the mill too lowly to be noticed.

While angels struck the Egyptians, not even a dog would bark against Israel's children.

This anticipated the difference that will appear on the great day between God's people and his enemies.

If people understood the difference God makes—and will make for all eternity—between those who serve him and those who do not, they would not treat religion as something unimportant.

They would not approach it so carelessly.

After delivering God's message, Moses left Pharaoh in great anger at his stubbornness, even though Moses was the meekest man on earth.

Scripture has foretold that many who hear the gospel will refuse to believe, so that their unbelief need not surprise or discourage us (Romans 10:16).

Let us never think less of Christ's gospel because others despise it.

Pharaoh's heart remained hardened, but he was forced to retreat from his arrogant demands until Israel was completely freed.

Likewise, God's people will find growing freedom from the enemy of their souls as they resist him in Jesus Christ's strength.

Every struggle against him through the blood of the Lamb, and every desire to become more like the Lamb and to love him more deeply, will be rewarded by greater release from his power.
