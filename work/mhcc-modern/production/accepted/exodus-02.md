# Exodus 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Moses is born, and exposed on the river. | 1–4 |
| He is found, and brought up by Pharaoh's daughter. | 5–10 |
| Moses slays an Egyptian, and flees to Midian. | 11–15 |
| Moses marries the daughter of Jethro. | 16–22 |

## The infant Moses is placed by the river (verses 1–4)

Notice how God's providence works.

Just when Pharaoh's cruelty reached its height, with the order to drown Hebrew infants, the deliverer was born.

While people plotted the destruction of the church, God was preparing its salvation.

Moses' parents saw that he was a beautiful child.

Living faith can take encouragement from even the smallest indication of God's favor.

Hebrews 11:23 tells us they hid Moses by faith.

They trusted God's promise that Israel would be preserved.

Faith in God's promises moves us to use lawful means to seek his mercy.

Our responsibility is to do our duty; the outcome belongs to God.

Faith lifts us above the fear of human beings.

After three months, when they could hide the child no longer, they placed him in a basket made of reeds near the river and sent his sister to watch him.

If a mother's frail love took such care, what must the love and compassion of God be like, when they are as limitless as he is?

Moses was never more securely protected—not even later, when Israel surrounded his tent in the wilderness—than when he lay helpless and alone beside the water.

Neither river nor Egyptian could harm him.

When we seem most abandoned and helpless, God may be especially near.

## Pharaoh's daughter rescues Moses (verses 5–10)

Look at the place where the great Moses lay as an infant: a little basket among the reeds at the water's edge.

If he had remained there long, he would have died.

But Providence led Pharaoh's daughter to that very place and moved her to pity the helpless child.

She dared to show compassion when others did not.

We should often remember and praise God's care for us in our earliest years.

Pharaoh was cruelly trying to destroy Israel.

Yet Pharaoh's own daughter took pity on a Hebrew baby.

Without knowing what she was doing, she preserved the man who would deliver Israel.

She even provided him with a good nurse: his own mother.

Through Moses' sister, the mother was brought back to care for him.

Moses was raised as the son of Pharaoh's daughter.

Many people born in poverty and obscurity are raised to prominence through surprising acts of Providence, so that the world may see that God reigns.

## Moses defends his people and flees (verses 11–15)

Moses boldly identified himself with God's people.

Hebrews 11 shows that he acted in faith, fully prepared to leave the honors, wealth, and pleasures of his high rank among the Egyptians.

By God's grace he shared in faith in Christ, the faith that overcomes the world.

He was willing not only to risk everything but to suffer for Christ's sake, because he was convinced Israel was God's people.

Henry understands Moses' killing of the Egyptian as an act carried out by **special authorization from heaven**, and he explicitly says that this must **not** become a rule for other cases.

Moses rescued an oppressed Israelite.

He also tried to settle a dispute between two Hebrews.

The rebuke he gave still has value.

May it not also be addressed to people whose fierce disputes divide and weaken the Christian church?

They forget that they are brothers and sisters.

The person in the wrong quarreled with Moses.

Anger at correction is often a sign of guilt.

People do not realize how much they harm themselves when they reject faithful rebukes and despise those who give them.

Moses might have been tempted to say, “If this is how the Hebrews behave, I will return to court and be Pharaoh's daughter's son again.”

But we must not allow the foolishness or bad temper of some professing believers to turn us against God's ways and God's people.

Moses had to flee to Midian.

God directed even this flight toward wise and holy purposes.

## Moses helps Reuel's daughters and marries (verses 16–22)

Moses found refuge in Midian.

Although he had been educated at court, he readily helped Reuel's daughters water their flocks.

He loved to act justly and to defend those he saw being wronged.

Everyone should do the same, as far as he is able.

Moses also loved doing good.

Wherever God's providence places us, we should want to be useful and look for ways to help.

When we cannot do all the good we would like, we should still do whatever good we can.

Moses gained the favor of the leader of Midian, who gave him one of his daughters in marriage.

They had a son named *Gershom*, meaning “a stranger there,” to keep alive the memory of the country where Moses had lived as a stranger.

## God hears Israel's cries (verses 23–25)

Israel's enslavement continued, even though the killing of Hebrew infants had stopped.

Sometimes God allows wicked people to lay a heavy burden on the righteous for a very long time.

At last the Israelites began to turn their thoughts toward God in their suffering.

When God inclines and enables us to cry out to him for deliverance, that is often a sign that he is drawing near to rescue us.

He heard their groaning and showed that he had noticed their distress.

God remembered his covenant, which he never forgets.

He acted because of his promise, not because they had earned deliverance.

He looked upon the Israelites.

Moses had looked upon them with pity, but now God looked upon them and acted to help.

His attention was fixed on Israel, and he was about to reveal his favor.

God is always a very present help in trouble.

Take courage, then, all who know their own guilt and bondage and are looking to him for deliverance.

God in Christ Jesus looks upon you as well.

He joins his loving invitation to the Redeemer's promise: “Come to me, all who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest” (Matthew 11:28).
