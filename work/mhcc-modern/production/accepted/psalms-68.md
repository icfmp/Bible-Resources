# Psalm 68 — Modern English

## God's greatness and tender compassion (verses 1–6)

No one has hardened the heart against God and found lasting prosperity.

The Lord is **his people's joy**. Let them rejoice when they come before him.

God receives his existence from no one; he is the source of every created being.

Yet he has freely committed himself through **promise and covenant** to bless his people.

We should praise him for mercy and compassion.

He continually cares for the afflicted and oppressed.

Repenting sinners are helpless and exposed, even more so than fatherless children. God receives them into **his own family** and makes them sharers in its blessings.

## God's remarkable care for Israel (verses 7–14)

Fresh experiences of mercy should awaken memories of earlier mercies.

When God leads his people into a wilderness, he goes before them there and will also lead them out.

He sustained Israel in the wilderness and later in Canaan. The **daily manna** seems especially relevant to this passage.

These provisions also picture the nourishment of God's spiritual Israel.

**The Spirit of grace and the gospel of grace** are the generous rain that strengthens the Lord's inheritance and makes it fruitful.

Christ comes like showers watering the earth.

The record of Israel's victories looks forward to **the exalted Redeemer's triumph over death and hell** for all who belong to him.

Israel's suffering in Egypt's brick kilns seemed wretched.

Its later glory in Canaan under David and Solomon seemed wonderful by contrast.

So those once enslaved by Satan become honorable when **justified and sanctified through Christ**.

When they enter heaven, the last remnants of sin disappear.

They become like the **wings of a dove covered in silver and its feathers shining gold**.

Through complete salvation, those once filthy with guilt become **white as snow**.

## The ascended Christ bestows gifts (verses 15–21)

Henry understands these verses as speaking especially of **Christ's ascension**, as Paul applies them in **Ephesians 4:8**.

Through the purchase of his death, Christ received the gifts necessary for sinners' conversion and believers' salvation.

He continually distributes these gifts, even among people formerly rebellious, so that the Lord may live among them as **Friend and Father**.

As Jesus said, the Father gave him authority over all people to give eternal life to those entrusted to him (**John 17:2**).

Christ came to a rebellious world **not to condemn it, but that the world might be saved through him**.

He is Savior and Benefactor to his willing people, but a consuming fire to those who persist in rebellion.

His gifts are so many and weighty that God can be said to **load us with benefits**.

He will not offer merely temporary comforts as our inheritance: **he himself will be the God of our salvation**.

Christ removes death's sting for believers when they die and grants complete victory over death when they rise again.

He will strike down the pride of the enemy and **crush the serpent's head**.

## Christ's victories for his people (verses 22–28)

The victories God gave David over Israel's enemies pictured **Christ's victories** for himself and everyone trusting him.

Those who take him as their Savior can regard him as their God and King acting for their good, answering their prayers, especially through his **word and appointed ordinances**.

The Messiah's kingdom will gain the submission of rulers and learned people across the world.

Henry notes that **verse 28** seems to address the king.

Yet its words may be applied to the Redeemer, his church and every sincere believer.

We pray: **Lord Jesus, complete the undertaking you began for us by finishing your good work within us**.

## The nations invited into the church (verses 29–31)

The psalm gives an urgent invitation to those still outside God's church.

Some submit because fear and conscience compel them, as Providence exposes the foolishness of their opposition and draws them to make peace with the church.

Others submit **willingly**.

Henry associates both responses with the language of **verses 29 and 31**.

The beauty and goodness of serving God, and of the gospel that went out from Jerusalem, are sufficient reasons to invite sinners from every nation.

## All power belongs to God (verses 32–35)

All who worship in God's holy places should admire and adore him **with reverence and godly fear**.

The God of Israel gives his people strength and power.

Only **through Christ strengthening us** can we do what he calls us to do.

We must therefore give him every part of the glory, thanking him humbly for enabling our work and for graciously accepting what his own hands accomplish within us.
