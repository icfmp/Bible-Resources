# Matthew 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ foretells the destruction of the temple. | 1–3 |
| The troubles before the destruction of Jerusalem. | 4–28 |
| Christ foretells other signs and miseries, to the end of the world. | 29–41 |
| Exhortations to watchfulness. | 42–51 |

## Jesus foretells the temple's destruction (verses 1–3)

Jesus predicted the complete destruction of the temple.

Remembering that even the greatest earthly beauty and splendor will decay helps us avoid valuing them too highly.

The most beautiful human body will become food for worms.

The most magnificent building will eventually become ruins.

Jesus asked whether the disciples saw all these things.

We should look at worldly things in such a way that we **see through them to their inevitable end**.

On the Mount of Olives he began explaining events concerning **Jerusalem's destruction** and, beyond that, matters reaching toward **the end of the world**.

## Warnings before Jerusalem's destruction (verses 4–28)

The disciples asked **when** these things would happen and **what signs** would precede them.

Jesus did not give a straightforward answer to the timing question.

He responded extensively to the request for signs.

Henry reads the prophecy as first concerning near events: Jerusalem's destruction, the ending of the old Jewish religious order and state, the calling of Gentiles and the gospel kingdom's spread.

The prediction also looks ahead toward the **general judgment**, more specifically as its conclusion approaches.

Christ's teaching was intended **more to make disciples watchful than to satisfy curiosity**.

It prepared them to respond wisely instead of providing an exhaustive schedule of events.

That is the kind of understanding of the times God's people should desire.

Jesus warned against false teachers, wars and upheaval among nations.

Henry regards the wars that followed rejection of Christ as a warning about refusing the gospel's offer of peace.

Those who will not hear messengers of peace may instead hear the noise of war.

Nevertheless, a heart fixed upon God can remain peaceful during public disorder.

It is contrary to Christ's desire that his disciples live continually overwhelmed by fear.

If earthly judgment is terrible, eternal judgment for those who persistently reject the Savior is far worse.

**The greatest earthly troubles are only a beginning of sorrows** by comparison.

Jesus also promised that some would endure faithfully to the end.

The gospel would be preached throughout the world, accomplishing its appointed purpose before the end.

Christ foretold dangers that would accompany Jerusalem's fall.

The warning would guide and comfort his disciples.

If God opens a way of escape, they should **use it**.

Refusing ordinary measures of safety would be **testing God, not trusting him**.

During public distress, prayer is especially appropriate, though it is always in season.

We may submit to whatever God sends while still **praying that suffering may be prevented or lessened**.

It is particularly difficult when emergencies prevent believers from enjoying worship and Sabbath rest.

Jesus promised that, for the sake of the elect, the days of calamity would be **shortened**.

God may use enemies to accomplish his purposes but still limit their destructive intentions.

The gospel's spread is compared to lightning: powerful and visible rather than a hidden, local secret.

Henry also understands the eagle imagery in relation to **Roman armies and their eagle standards**, seeing judgment against a corrupt nation.

He further says these warnings apply to **Christ's final judgment** (**2 Thessalonians 2:1**).

We should therefore make our calling and election sure and refuse to be deceived by false claims.

## Jesus foretells his second coming (verses 29–41)

The prophets sometimes described future events as near in order to emphasize their certainty and importance.

Jesus announced his own return and the extraordinary change that will make **all things new**.

At his first coming he was rejected and opposed.

At his second he will be seen in glory.

Sooner or later every sinner will mourn.

Repentant sinners mourn now over their sins as they look to Christ and eventually reap joy.

Those refusing repentance will see the One they pierced and mourn in despair.

God's elect are scattered through many nations and places.

On the day they are gathered, **not one will be missing**, nor will distance prevent any from entering heaven.

Henry interprets Jesus' words about **“this generation” (verse 34)** as affirming that **the Jewish people would continue to exist as a distinct people through history until the final judgment**.

That interpretation is recorded as Henry's eschatological reading, not as an established conclusion about the verse's meaning.

People plan confidently for generations of earthly success without preparing for the certain and overwhelming event of Christ's return.

That day will overthrow every human plan opposed to God.

It will be unexpected, as the Flood was to people in Noah's time.

These warnings apply first to temporal judgments—particularly Jerusalem's imminent judgment—and second to **the final judgment**.

Before the Flood people continued their daily activities in careless confidence.

They did not understand, nor did they believe, until judgment came.

If we recognized how soon earthly possessions and comforts will disappear, we would cling to them far less.

Delaying thought of judgment does not delay judgment itself.

The coming of Christ will be sudden.

Men and women will be engaged in ordinary work.

Then everything else will be forgotten, and the question will become urgent: **Am I prepared to meet the Lord?**

The final judgment's significance for the whole world resembles the significance of **death for each individual**.

## Jesus commands watchfulness (verses 42–51)

Watching for Christ's coming means maintaining the attitude and conduct we would willingly let our Master find us practicing.

We know our lives on earth are short.

We do not know that we will live a long time, much less when the final judgment will arrive.

His coming will be joyful for those prepared and dreadful for those unprepared.

Someone may claim to be Christ's servant while remaining unbelieving, greedy, ambitious or devoted to pleasure.

Such a person cannot expect acceptance merely from an outward profession.

Those who choose the world as their ultimate portion here face final loss in the world to come.

May our Lord find us ready.

May he declare us blessed and present us to the Father **washed in Christ's blood, purified by his Spirit and prepared for the inheritance of the saints in light**.
