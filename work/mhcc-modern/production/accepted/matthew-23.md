# Matthew 23 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jesus reproves the scribes and Pharisees. | 1–12 |
| Crimes of the Pharisees. | 13–33 |
| The guilt of Jerusalem. | 34–39 |

## Jesus rebukes religious pride (verses 1–12)

The scribes and Pharisees taught Moses' law and required obedience to it.

But Jesus charged them with **religious hypocrisy**.

We can judge only what we see outwardly; **God examines the heart**.

The Pharisees broadened their **phylacteries**—small cases or scrolls containing four passages from the law, worn upon the forehead and left arm.

Henry identifies the passages as **Exodus 13:2–10; 13:11–16; Deuteronomy 6:4–9; 11:13–21**.

Making the cases larger was intended to suggest greater devotion than that of other people.

God had instructed Israel to wear **fringes on their clothing** as reminders of being his distinct people (**Numbers 15:38**).

The Pharisees enlarged those fringes to appear more religious.

**Pride** was the sin they cherished most, and Jesus regularly opposed it.

It is right for learners to respect faithful teachers of God's word.

It is wrong for teachers to demand honors and become proud when they receive them.

Christ's genuine disciples are uncomfortable with being placed above everyone else.

Such pride contradicts Christianity's spirit.

Yet when we look around the visible church, can we say this humble attitude is universal?

Henry warns that something of this anti-Christian pride appears **in every religious community and in our own hearts**.

## Jesus exposes the Pharisees' sins (verses 13–33)

The scribes and Pharisees opposed Christ's gospel and thereby hindered the salvation of souls.

Refusing Christ ourselves is terrible.

Preventing others from coming to him is still worse.

An outward display of devotion can conceal enormous wickedness.

**Pretended piety will be judged as doubled guilt**.

The Pharisees diligently sought converts **to their own party**, pursuing personal honor and advantage rather than God's glory or the good of souls.

They repeatedly made religious duties give way to their worldly profit.

They were exacting about smaller aspects of the law while neglecting **its weightier duties**.

Jesus did not condemn their care to avoid even small sins.

If swallowing a **gnat** is wrong, it is right to strain it out.

He condemned their inconsistency in meticulously avoiding the gnat while **swallowing a camel**, committing far greater wrongs.

Though they appeared devout, they lacked true sobriety and righteousness.

Our true condition is what we are **inside**.

Outward incentives may make a person's public conduct respectable while the inward life remains corrupt.

But when the heart and spirit are renewed, **a renewed life follows**.

We must therefore begin our reform with ourselves.

The Pharisees' righteousness resembled **the decorations of a grave**, or clothing placed upon a lifeless body.

It was only display.

Sinners deceive themselves by following the sins popular in their own generation while imagining they would have courageously opposed the sins of earlier generations.

We may claim that, if we had lived when Jesus was on earth, we would never have rejected him.

Yet **Christ, through his Spirit, word and ministers, may still receive contempt from us today**.

God is just when he allows persistently rebellious people to follow their own chosen desires and experience their consequences.

Jesus reveals the real character of those who oppose him.

## Jesus mourns over Jerusalem (verses 34–39)

Jesus announced the misery Jerusalem's inhabitants would bring upon themselves.

He did not dwell here upon **his own approaching sufferings**.

The picture of **a hen gathering her chicks beneath her wings** illustrates Christ's tender love and faithful care for all who trust in him.

He calls sinners into that shelter, protects them and nourishes them toward eternal life.

Henry understands Jesus' words as foretelling both **the dispersion and unbelief of Jewish people** and **their future conversion to Christ**.

That is the commentator's prophetic interpretation and must not be represented as an unqualified factual claim about all Jews.

Jerusalem's guilt and its subsequent suffering were serious.

Yet merely nominal Christian churches should also fear judgment when their members refuse genuine faith.

Even now, **the Savior remains willing to receive all who come to him**.

It is the proud and unbelieving unwillingness of sinners, not lack of mercy in Christ, that keeps them from eternal happiness.
