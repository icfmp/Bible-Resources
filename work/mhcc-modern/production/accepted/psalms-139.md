# Psalm 139 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God knows all things. | 1–6 |
| He is every where present. | 7–16 |
| The psalmist's hatred to sin, and desire to be led aright. | 17–24 |

## The Lord knows every thought (verses 1–6)

God possesses **perfect knowledge** of all we think and do.

It is better to meditate upon this truth and apply it to our own lives in prayer than merely to dispute about it out of curiosity.

Everyone readily acknowledges God's **omniscience**—his knowledge of all things—and **omnipresence**—his presence everywhere.

Yet few live as though they really believe them.

The Lord notices every step, **whether right or wrong**.

He knows what rule guides us, where we are heading and whose company we seek.

When we withdraw from everyone else, he knows our inward life.

Before we speak a good or empty word, he knows the thoughts and intentions from which it arises.

Wherever we are, we live beneath his eye and within reach of his hand.

We cannot explain fully how he knows us so thoroughly.

This truth should restrain us from sin.

## No darkness hides us or separates us from his care (verses 7–16)

We cannot see God, but **he sees us**.

The psalmist did not really want to escape the Lord.

He asked where anyone could go to leave God's presence.

Neither the furthest parts of the world nor heaven nor hell lie beyond his reach.

No veil of darkness hides an action or person from him.

Even secret wrongdoing is as plain before him as evil committed in daylight.

But this is also a great encouragement.

**No suffering or location can remove the believer from the supporting and comforting presence of the almighty Friend**.

If a persecutor kills a believer's body, the soul will all the sooner enter heaven.

Even the grave cannot separate the body from the Savior's love.

**He will raise the body in glory**.

No outward circumstance can ultimately divide the believer from the Lord.

Those who continue doing their duty may find joy in faith, hope and prayer wherever they are.

## God's precious thoughts and a prayer for a cleansed heart (verses 17–24)

God's plans for his people are too deep for us to search out.

We cannot count his many mercies.

What a help it would be in keeping us reverent toward the Lord throughout the day if our first thoughts each morning were of him!

And how much more will we praise him when **we awaken in the world of glory**!

Our bodies were wonderfully formed.

We should never use their members or senses as instruments of sin.

Our **immortal, rational souls** are even greater gifts.

Yet if God had not loved sinners, our reason and immortality would make our guilt and eternal loss all the more dreadful.

Let us therefore meditate with delight upon **God's love to sinners in Jesus Christ**, which surpasses calculation.

People who fear God hate sin, but **grieve for sinners**.

Even when they avoid their wrongdoing, **they should pray for their conversion and salvation**, because God can accomplish both.

The Lord knows us fully, while we know ourselves only imperfectly.

Ask him through **his word and Spirit** to search and test our hearts.

If any wicked way is within us, may he show it and remove it.

The old and good way of godliness is pleasing to God, beneficial to us and leads to everlasting life.

All saints want to remain on that path without wandering away or growing weary.
