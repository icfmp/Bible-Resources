# Hosea 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The abuse of God's favour leads to punishment. | 1–8 |
| A promise of God's mercy. | 9–16 |

## Ephraim turns from God to idols (verses 1–8)

As long as **Ephraim reverenced and worshiped God**, the tribe held an honored place.

But when Ephraim abandoned God for idols, it fell from that position.

The people who sacrificed to idols were told to **kiss the calves**, expressing worship, affection and submission.

But the Lord will **not give his glory to another**.

Those who worship images will therefore be brought to shame.

No lasting and genuine comfort is found **anywhere except in God**.

The Lord not only cared for Israel during the wilderness years; he also brought the people into **Canaan, a fertile and pleasant land**.

Yet worldly success, when it feeds pride, makes people **forget God**.

Therefore the Lord would confront them in judgment like **the fiercest beasts of their forests**.

**Abusing divine goodness leads to more severe judgment**.

## Destruction through sin and mercy through redemption (verses 9–16)

Israel had **destroyed itself through rebellion**.

It could not rescue itself.

Its only help came **from the Lord**.

The same truth applies to **spiritual redemption** from the lost condition into which all human beings have fallen through deliberate sins.

God sometimes gives sinners **the very things they wrongfully demand**, but in displeasure.

For faithful believers, whether God **gives or takes away**, he acts in love.

For the unrepentant, both receiving and losing earthly things can become expressions of wrath and bring no true comfort.

Without **repentance and faith in the gospel**, sinners will soon face severe distress.

Israel's predicted national ruin also contained the promise that God would intervene mercifully and powerfully to **preserve a remnant**.

Yet that deliverance only foreshadowed **the redemption of the true Israel through Christ's death, burial and resurrection**.

**Christ will destroy death and the grave**.

The Lord will not abandon his promise or change his purpose.

In the meantime, Israel would be devastated for its sins.

Without good works bearing fruit **through the Holy Spirit**, other kinds of prosperity are as empty as uncertain worldly wealth.

God's judgment would **dry up the branches and wither the shoots**.

Those who rebel against him would face suffering worse than even the cruelty of warfare.

May the Lord save us **from such misery and from sin, its cause**.
