# Psalm 31 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Confidence in God. | 1–8 |
| Prayer in trouble. | 9–18 |
| Praise for God's goodness. | 19–24 |

## Confidence in God (verses 1–8)

Faith and prayer belong together. It is **the prayer of faith** that prevails.

David entrusted his soul to God in a particular way. Our Lord Jesus used the words of **verse 5** when he gave up his last breath on the cross, offering himself willingly as a sacrifice for sin and laying down his life as a ransom.

David, however, spoke as a man in distress. His greatest concern was for his soul—his spirit and better part.

Some people think worldly business or multiplying worries excuse them from caring for their souls. In fact, troubles make such care even more urgent, so that the inner person need not suffer harm when the outward person wastes away.

The redemption of the soul is so precious that without **Christ's undertaking**, it would be lost forever.

Trusting divine mercy gives David reason to rejoice.

God observes whether suffering humbles us for sin and makes our souls better. His people will face dangers and rescues throughout life, until he finally delivers them from **death, their last enemy**.

## Prayer amid distress (verses 9–18)

David's troubles made him a man of sorrows, picturing **Christ, who was acquainted with grief**.

There is an important difference: David recognized that his own sins deserved correction, while **Christ suffered for our sins**.

David's friends were too afraid to help him. We need not be surprised when human support fails, but we should make sure we have **a Friend in heaven who never fails**.

Those who place their spirits in God's hands can trust him to arrange everything for their ultimate good.

**Our times are in God's hands**: he may lengthen or shorten life and make its circumstances bitter or sweet according to his wise purposes.

Neither our own hands nor those of friends or enemies finally determine our course.

David therefore prayed to be saved **for God's mercy, not because of personal merit**.

He looked ahead to the day when those who slander and attack God's people will be silenced by divine judgment.

Meanwhile, let us seek to silence foolish accusations, if possible, by **doing good**.

## Praise for God's goodness (verses 19–24)

Instead of giving way to despair or impatience, we should reflect upon how good God is to those who fear and trust him.

All mercy reaches sinners through the extraordinary gift of **God's only Son as the atoning sacrifice**.

Do not surrender to unbelief or assume that hardship means you have been cut off from God's sight and handed over to arrogant human enemies.

Lord, pardon our complaints and fears. Increase our **faith, patience, love and gratitude**. Teach us to rejoice in trouble and in hope.

Christ's deliverance and his victory over enemies should strengthen believers in every earthly affliction. Having suffered courageously with their Master, they will enter **his joy and glory**.
