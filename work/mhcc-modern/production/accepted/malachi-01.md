# Malachi 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The ingratitude of Israel. | 1–5 |
| They are careless in God's institutions. | 6–14 |

## Israel fails to recognize God's love (verses 1–5)

All our advantages, both the good circumstances we experience and the spiritual privileges we enjoy, arise from **God's free love**.

He is the One who makes our lives differ from other people's lives.

The suffering sinners experience or fear is the rightful consequence of wrongdoing.

But every true hope and comfort we receive is a gift of **the Lord's undeserved mercy**.

God chose his people so that they would be **holy**.

If we love him, it is because **he first loved us**.

Yet we are all tempted to disregard the value of his mercies while trying to excuse our own sins.

## The priests dishonor God's worship (verses 6–14)

Each of us can apply to ourselves the faults exposed in the priests.

Since God is both our **Father and Master**, we owe him reverence and honor.

But these priests mocked the warnings given to them.

When sinners attempt to silence the convictions of their own consciences, they bring ruin upon themselves.

Those who neglect God's appointed ordinances, attend them carelessly and leave them unchanged are in effect saying, **“The Lord's table is contemptible.”**

The priests dishonored his name through their offerings.

They did not understand that their sacrifices pointed toward **the spotless Lamb of God**.

They resented their cost, treating everything that brought no worldly profit as waste.

This also warns us about our worship.

When we worship **without understanding**, we bring a **blind offering**.

When we act carelessly and without heartfelt devotion, we offer something **sick**.

When we rely only on outward bodily actions instead of engaging our hearts, we bring something **lame**.

When we allow distracted and vain thoughts to dominate worship, we offer something **torn**.

These are not small offenses.

They dishonor God and harm our own souls.

For an action to be acceptable to God, merely doing something outwardly good is insufficient.

It must come from **a right motive**, be performed **in the right way** and be directed toward **the right purpose**.

The countless mercies we continually receive make our laziness and stinginess in serving him more blameworthy.

God promised a time when **spiritual worship** would flourish.

The incense offered to his name pictures **prayer and praise**, and the pure offering is worship arising from sincerity.

That became possible when true worshipers began to worship the Father **in Spirit and truth**.

We may trust God's mercy to forgive our earlier sins, but not to give us permission to keep committing them.

**A willing heart is acceptable, even when its service remains imperfect.**

But a deceitful person who gives the best to Satan and sinful desires while keeping the worst for God remains under judgment.

People still dishonor God's table and worship today, even if their outward religious practices differ from those of the ancient priests.
