# Zechariah 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's defence of his church. | 1–8 |
| Christ's coming and his kingdom. | 9–11 |
| Promises to the church. | 12–17 |

## God defends his church (verses 1–8)

The prophet foretold judgments against several surrounding nations.

Henry relates these warnings to wars involving **the Macedonians and Alexander's successors**.

Amid those conflicts, God promised to protect his own people.

His church lay among enemies like **a lily surrounded by thorns**.

The Lord's power and goodness are visible in his special preservation of that church.

He stations himself around it like an encamped defender.

Proud armies may pass by, leave and return, but his eyes are always upon his people.

Their opponents cannot finally prevail.

The time will come when **no oppressor will pass through his people's land again**.

## The Messiah comes as a peaceful king (verses 9–11)

Zechariah rejoiced at the prospect of the Messiah's coming.

Henry notes that ancient Jewish interpreters also understood the passage as messianic.

Jesus openly took the role of **Israel's King when he entered Jerusalem amid the people's hosannas**.

But his kingdom is **spiritual**, not a kingdom advanced by physical force or worldly weapons.

His gospel would be proclaimed throughout the world and received among the nations.

A state of sin is **a state of captivity**.

It resembles a pit or dungeon without water or comfort.

By nature we are prisoners in that pit.

Through **the precious blood of Christ**, countless people once enslaved by Satan have been freed from the place where they would otherwise have perished without hope.

As we admire Christ's grace, let us seek to display his holiness and truth in our own hearts and actions.

## Prisoners of hope are called to Christ (verses 12–17)

Henry sees the promised deliverance of the Jews as a picture of **redemption through Christ** and of gospel blessings enjoyed by believers.

Sinners are prisoners, yet they may be **prisoners of hope**.

Their condition is sad, but not beyond remedy.

There is still hope for them through God's promise.

Christ is a **Stronghold and strong Tower**.

Believers are safe in him from God's wrath, the curse of the law and attacks by their spiritual enemies.

We must flee to him with living faith and trust him through every trial.

The passage also refers, in Henry's interpretation, to **the apostles and early preachers of the gospel**.

God was manifestly with them.

His words, proclaimed through their mouths, pierced their listeners' hearts and consciences.

The Lord protected them amid persecution and filled them with his Spirit.

The **Good Shepherd** saved them as his flock and honored them like precious jewels in his crown.

The blessings, gifts and comforts of the Spirit poured out at **Pentecost (Acts 2)** and afterward are pictured here.

God's people have faced severe spiritual conflicts and will face more.

But the Lord will give them victory.

The more we serve him and experience his goodness, the more we appreciate the beauty of our Redeemer.

Whatever he gives us, let us use it gladly in his service.

After enjoying his blessings, let us exclaim, **“How great is his goodness!”**
