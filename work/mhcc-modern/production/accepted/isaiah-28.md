# Isaiah 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The desolations of Samaria. | 1–4 |
| The prosperity of Judah; with reproofs for sinfulness and unbelief. | 5–15 |
| Christ is pointed out as the sure Foundation for all believers. | 16–22 |
| God's dealings with his people. | 23–29 |

## Samaria's pride and drunkenness (verses 1–4)

Whatever people admire about themselves, however insignificant it may be, becomes like **a crown** to them.

But pride prepares the way for destruction.

How foolish people behave when overcome by drink!

Henry says those enslaved to drunkenness become subject to Satan. Excessive drinking is an exhausting form of bondage.

It destroys health, damages work and possessions, harms families and puts souls in danger, all to satisfy a degrading appetite.

Such behavior is particularly shameful among people who claim to worship God.

It is right for him to remove abundance when his people consistently misuse it.

What they boast about is only a **fading flower** or **early fruit**, picked and eaten as soon as it ripens.

## Judah rebuked for misleading teaching (verses 5–15)

Isaiah turned toward **Judah**, the remaining portion of God's people.

Only those who glory in **the Lord of hosts himself** possess lasting happiness.

From him come the wisdom and strength required for service and spiritual conflict.

Only through **Jesus Christ** does the holy God graciously communicate with sinful human beings.

Teachers may be intoxicated not only by wine but also by **false doctrine and imaginary ideas about the Messiah's kingdom and salvation**.

Such teachers mislead themselves and many others, filling the places they influence with error.

God's people need the same lessons and principles repeated to them often, so that they understand them more clearly.

Through his word, God calls us to what truly benefits us.

Serving him is the only rest from the weary bondage of sin; lasting refreshment comes only under **the easy yoke of the Lord Jesus**.

The people despised these plain teachings as childish and insignificant.

Those who mock what they ought to learn may justly be judged through the very words they rejected.

A believer at peace with God can, in a figurative sense, be at peace even with death: it cannot do ultimate harm to someone belonging to Christ.

But it is absurd to imagine death can be a friend while **sin makes God an enemy**.

People who rely on **their own righteousness** or on an intended deathbed repentance are trusting lies.

To promise to stop sinning only when one has lost the physical ability to continue in it is no genuine change of heart.

## Christ, the tested cornerstone (verses 16–22)

God has laid in Zion **the only reliable foundation** for escaping coming judgment: **Jesus Christ**.

This foundation was established in God's eternal purposes.

Christ is **a stone**: strong enough to support his entire church.

He is **a tried and chosen stone**, approved by God and never failing anyone who truly trusts him.

He is **the cornerstone**, joining the building together and bearing its weight.

He is **precious** to the Lord and every genuine believer.

Whoever, in any age or nation, rests the hope of an undying soul upon him will not ultimately be put to shame.

Faith in Christ quiets the soul and teaches us to wait for God's timing.

Any alternative foundation will fail.

The person who seeks **justification outside Christ's righteousness**, or seeks **wisdom, strength and holiness without the Holy Spirit**, or seeks lasting happiness outside **God's favor**, cannot obtain what is needed.

Self-righteousness provides **a bed too short to lie on and a covering too narrow to shelter under**.

The Lord will accomplish his plans and display his glory.

Those who profess to belong to his church but live like unbelieving nations should expect judgment.

Never mock the rebukes of God's word or the warnings of approaching judgment.

## The Lord knows how to thresh his people (verses 23–29)

A wise farmer works carefully and treats each crop according to its nature and needs.

The Lord, who gave farmers this skill, is himself **wonderful in counsel and excellent in action**.

According to the situation, he warns, corrects, spares, shows mercy or brings judgment.

Afflictions are like **threshing instruments** in his hands.

They loosen believers' attachment to the world, remove the chaff that clings to them and prepare them for useful service.

God adjusts the trial to his people's strength.

He does **not make their burden heavier or longer than is necessary**.

When his purpose has been achieved, their suffering ceases.

He gathers **the wheat into his storehouse**, while **the chaff is burned with unquenchable fire**.
