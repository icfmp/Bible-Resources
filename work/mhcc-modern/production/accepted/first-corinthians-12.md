# 1 Corinthians 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The variety of use of spiritual gifts are shown. | 1–11 |
| In the human body every member has its place and use. | 12–26 |
| This is applied to the church of Christ. | 27–30 |
| And there is something more excellent than spiritual gifts. | 31 |

## Spiritual gifts serve the church, not personal pride (verses 1–11)

The extraordinary gifts of the Holy Spirit were powers given **during Christianity's first generations**, helping convince unbelievers and spread the gospel.

**Spiritual gifts and saving grace are very different**.

Both are freely given by God.

But grace is given for **the salvation of the person receiving it**.

Gifts are given for **the good and salvation of others**.

Someone may therefore possess remarkable gifts **without possessing saving grace**.

The Corinthians exercised many extraordinary gifts in public gatherings, sometimes making a public display of them while lacking **true godliness and Christian love**.

Before conversion they had been pagans, not governed by Christ's Spirit.

No one can truly confess Christ as Lord **with believing dependence upon him** unless the Holy Spirit produces that faith.

Nor could anyone truly believe in their heart, or confirm Christ's message through a miracle, without the Spirit's power.

There are different gifts and offices, but all come from **one God, one Lord and one Spirit: Father, Son and Holy Spirit**, the source of spiritual blessings.

No gift exists merely for the recipient's benefit.

Serving others with a gift makes its use truly fruitful.

Paul's examples include understanding the truths of Christianity clearly, expressing doctrine accurately, knowing mysteries and giving wise guidance.

They also include healing, miracles, a special ability to explain Scripture, and speaking or interpreting languages.

All knowledge of God's truth and all ability to share it must result in **glory to God alone**.

Indeed, **greater gifts create greater temptations to pride**.

Those possessing them need more grace to remain humble and spiritually minded, and may undergo more difficult experiences through which God humbles them.

No one has reason to boast about gifts received or despise others who lack them.

## Every member contributes to Christ's one body (verses 12–26)

Christ and his church form **one body**, with Christ as Head and believers as members.

Baptism is a divinely appointed outward ordinance identifying people with this body.

It signifies **new birth** and is associated with the washing of regeneration (**Titus 3:5**).

But **only the Spirit's renewing work**, not outward baptism by itself, makes a person a living member of Christ.

Likewise, believers receive strength through fellowship with Christ at the Supper **not simply by drinking wine, but by receiving the one Spirit**.

Every member of the body has a particular form, position and purpose.

Even a member regarded as insignificant belongs to the body.

Differences among members are necessary.

Christians likewise have different abilities and responsibilities.

We should fulfill **the duties of our own positions** without resentment, rivalry or complaining.

All the members need one another.

Every member of Christ's body **can and ought to be useful to fellow members**.

The entire body should be united by **the strongest bonds of love**.

All its members should seek the good of the whole.

Every Christian depends upon others and ought to give as well as receive help.

How much more **the spirit of unity** should govern the church!

## God appoints different offices and gifts (verses 27–31)

Hatred, jealousy, pride, contempt and quarrels are unnatural among Christians.

They resemble members of the same body **fighting or ignoring one another**.

Paul condemns the pride and contentiousness with which the Corinthians compared spiritual gifts.

The Holy Spirit appoints different offices and distributes distinct abilities.

Henry describes **leading ministers**, teachers able to interpret Scripture, others laboring in word and doctrine, workers able to heal sickness, those helping people in weakness, people managing the church's charitable funds and practical administration, and those able to speak different languages.

Notice that **speaking languages comes last** in this list.

How empty it is to possess this gift merely to entertain people or exalt oneself!

God does **not give every gift to every believer**.

If he did, the church would cease to have differentiated members, as though a natural body were made entirely of eyes or ears.

**The Spirit distributes gifts according to his own will**.

Christians must be content when given a less visible responsibility, without despising those with greater abilities.

Likewise, those given more prominent gifts must never despise those with less.

How blessed the church would be if every member faithfully performed the duty assigned!

Instead of seeking the highest status or most impressive gifts, **leave the appointment of God's instruments to him**.

Final approval will not belong to those who sought the most prominent positions, but to those who **faithfully used the trust given them** and worked diligently for their Master.
