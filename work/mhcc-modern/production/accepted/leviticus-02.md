## The grain offering (verses 1–11)

The offerings of flour, called *meat offerings* in Henry's English, may picture Christ as presented to God for us and as the Bread of Life for our souls.

But Henry thinks they more directly represent our debt of gratitude for God's providential gifts and the good works that please him.

In older English, *meat* could mean food of any kind.

Most of this offering was meant to be eaten, not burned.

These grain offerings appear after the burnt offerings.

That order matters.

Without a share in Christ's sacrifice and a heart devoted to God, even our good religious works cannot be accepted.

Leaven represents pride, malice and hypocrisy.

These are the opposites of the humility, love and sincerity that God delights in.

Honey represents sensual pleasure.

Attachment to such pleasure can draw people away from prayer and good works.

Christ's character and sacrifice were entirely free from everything pictured by leaven.

His life of suffering and his agonizing death were the very opposite of worldly self-indulgence.

His people are called to follow him and become like him.

## The offering of firstfruits (verses 12–16)

Salt was required with every offering.

By this God taught Israel that even the sacrifices they brought were, in themselves, lacking what made them pleasing.

Every act of religious service must be seasoned with grace.

Christianity is the salt of the earth.

Instructions were also given for presenting firstfruits at harvest.

Someone grateful for God's goodness in providing an abundant crop could bring the first ripe, full heads of grain to him.

Whatever we offer God should be the best of its kind, even if all we have to give is a handful of green ears of grain.

Oil and frankincense were added to the firstfruits.

Wisdom and humility make young believers' hearts and service gentler and sweeter.

Then the first, still-green fruits of their lives become acceptable to the Lord.

God delights in early expressions of faith and devotion, the first ripe fruit of the Spirit.

Holy love toward God is the fire by which all our offerings are made.

The frankincense pictures Christ's mediation and intercession, through which our service becomes acceptable.

Blessed be God that we possess the reality of which all these observances were only shadows.

There is an excellence in Christ himself and in his work as Mediator that no picture or ceremony can fully show.

We must depend on him so completely that we never lose sight of him in anything we do, if we want our service to be accepted by God.
