# Job 11 — Modern English

## Zophar rebukes Job (verses 1–6)

Zophar attacked Job forcefully.

He portrayed him as someone who enjoyed hearing himself speak despite having nothing useful to say, and who defended lies.

Zophar wished God would show Job that his punishment was actually **less than he deserved**.

In our disputes, we are often too eager to call upon God to intervene.

We assume that if he spoke, he would certainly agree with **our side**.

We should submit every disagreement to the Lord's judgment, because his judgment accords with truth.

But those most eager to invoke divine judgment are not necessarily the ones who are right.

## Zophar speaks about God's greatness (verses 7–12)

Zophar spoke well about God's greatness and glory and human folly and vanity.

We should recognize our own weakness and be humbled.

God sees the foolishness of human beings who want to become wise and be recognized as wise, though their hearts are like an **untamed wild donkey's colt**.

Human beings are empty and foolish, yet proud of themselves.

They desire the appearance of wisdom without submitting to wisdom's laws.

They reach for knowledge God has forbidden.

Like the first parents, they try to become wise beyond what has been written and lose **the tree of life through seeking the tree of knowledge**.

How can a creature in this condition claim to argue on equal terms with God?

## Zophar promises good if Job repents (verses 13–20)

Zophar urged Job to repent and offered encouragement.

Yet even this counsel was mixed with harsh assumptions about Job.

Zophar believed prosperity in this life always belonged to the righteous.

He thought Job should be considered a hypocrite unless worldly prosperity returned.

Zophar said Job could lift his face without stain.

In Henry's spiritual application, people may approach the throne of grace confidently rather than with the fear Job described earlier (**Job 9:34**).

When God looks upon us through **his Anointed One**, our downcast faces can rise.

Though once polluted, we can be washed by **Christ's blood** and presented without stain.

We can draw near in full assurance of faith when our hearts have been cleansed from guilty consciences (**Hebrews 10:22**).
