# Ezekiel 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Divine judgments against the wicked at Jerusalem. | 1–13 |
| Divine favour towards those in captivity. | 14–21 |
| The Divine presence forsakes the city. | 22–25 |

## Judgment on Jerusalem's confident rulers (verses 1–13)

When Satan cannot persuade people that God's judgment is uncertain, he tries to make them believe it is **far away**.

The rulers of Jerusalem imagined themselves safe inside their city.

They compared its walls to a **cooking pot** and themselves to the **meat protected inside**.

They expected the walls to withstand the siege as confidently as a pot withstands the heat beneath it.

But people who **flatter themselves toward destruction** must be warned that they will have no peace while continuing in sin.

Those who would remain in the city were not the secure living inhabitants but **the people buried there**.

Those who feel most secure may be the least safe.

God may single out a particular sinner's judgment to warn others.

Yet Henry does **not** claim certainty about exactly when **Pelatiah** died—whether during the vision's events or near their future fulfillment.

Like Ezekiel, we should be deeply moved by the sudden death of others and continue **pleading for God's mercy toward those who remain**.

## God promises to give his people new hearts (verses 14–21)

The godly Israelites in Babylon were scorned by those still living in Jerusalem.

But God gave the exiles **gracious promises**.

He would give them **one heart**: a heart firmly devoted to him rather than divided and wavering.

Those who are made holy receive **a new spirit**—a renewed character and new inclinations.

They act from **new principles**, follow **new rules** and seek **new goals**.

A new name or a new appearance is not enough without **a new spirit**.

Anyone who is in **Christ** is a new creation.

A sinful heart is like stone: it does not feel what it should.

People may live amid death and suffering without being concerned or humbled.

The Lord promises to make their hearts **tender and responsive**.

This is **God's own work**, a gift he promised.

The change from death to life is wonderful and blessed.

People whose inward principles are renewed will also show **changed conduct**.

The two belong together.

When sinners recognize how much they need these blessings, let them turn God's **promises into prayers offered in Christ's name**.

He will fulfill what he has promised.

## God's glory withdraws from Jerusalem (verses 22–25)

The vision shows **God's presence leaving the city and temple**.

It rises from **the Mount of Olives**, which Henry takes as a picture of **Christ's ascension to heaven from that same mountain**.

The Lord will never finally abandon his people.

Yet a particular part of the **visible church** may lose the experience of his presence because of its sins.

Woe to those from whom he withdraws **his presence, glory and protection**.
