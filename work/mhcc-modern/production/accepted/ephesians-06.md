# Ephesians 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The duties of children and parents. | 1–4 |
| Of servants and masters. | 5–9 |
| All Christians are to put on spiritual armour against the enemies of their souls. | 10–18 |
| The apostle desires their prayers, and ends with his apostolic blessing. | 19–24 |

## Children and parents honor God within the family (verses 1–4)

Children have a duty **to obey their parents**.

True obedience involves **inward respect** as well as outward compliance.

Throughout history, people who have honored their parents have often experienced the benefits of that discipline.

Parents also have serious duties.

They must avoid **impatience and unreasonable harshness**.

They should act prudently, explain what is right and seek to **convince their children's judgment** instead of relying solely on force.

Children should be taught through **appropriate, compassionate correction** and instruction in the duties God commands.

These responsibilities are often neglected, even in families that profess Christianity.

Some parents' behavior turns their children against religion.

That does not excuse the children's disobedience, but it may have contributed dreadfully to it.

Only God can change a heart.

Yet he often blesses parents' **faithful teaching, godly example and prayers**.

Parents who care mainly that their children will become wealthy or socially accomplished, while showing little concern for their souls, should not expect God's blessing upon that priority.

## Servants and masters are accountable to Christ (verses 5–9)

Paul's teaching addresses the obligations of **servants and masters**.

Henry notes explicitly that in the ancient setting these servants were **generally enslaved people**.

The apostles instructed both enslaved workers and their masters in their duties so that wrongs might be reduced, **until slavery itself was rooted out through Christianity's influence**.

Workers are instructed to act with respect, honesty and faithfulness.

They should not merely pretend to obey while planning to disregard their responsibilities.

Nor should they work well only when their supervisor is watching.

The Lord Jesus sees their service, and devotion to him should make them sincere and diligent in every lawful task.

Work done in love rather than resentment can become easier to perform and pleasing to Christ.

Even humble and exhausting service carried out from duty and for God's glory receives his reward.

Those exercising authority also have obligations.

**Act toward those under your authority as you would wish to be treated**.

Be fair and show sincere concern for their wellbeing.

Do not be **tyrannical, harsh or domineering**.

You have a **Master in heaven**, and before Jesus Christ you and the people working under you are **fellow servants**.

If everyone remembered their duties toward God and the account they must soon give him, relationships within households would become more just and peaceful.

## Put on the whole armor of God (verses 10–18)

Our spiritual struggle requires **courage and strength from God**.

Those wishing to show genuine grace must seek **every necessary grace** and put on the **whole armor of God**, which he supplies.

The armor is meant to be worn throughout the battle.

We cannot lay it aside until our struggle is ended and our course completed.

Our battle is not against human enemies alone, nor only against our remaining sinful nature.

We face an enemy who possesses countless ways to deceive unstable souls.

Evil spirits assault us in the things that concern our souls, seeking to destroy **God's image within us**.

By grace we must resolve **not to yield to Satan**.

Resist him and he will flee.

But every concession to evil gives him further ground.

If we doubt the justice of our cause, the faithfulness of our Leader or the strength of our armor, we give him an advantage.

Paul drew his picture from a heavily armored soldier facing fierce attack.

There is **no armor for the back**—nothing for someone who retreats from Christian duty.

**Truth and sincerity** are the belt that holds the armor in place.

There can be no true religion without genuine sincerity.

**Christ's righteousness credited to us** is the breastplate protecting us against God's judgment.

**Christ's righteousness implanted within us** also strengthens the heart against Satan's attacks.

Resolution protects our legs.

The gospel of peace prepares our feet to stand firmly or move forward over difficult ground.

We need a clear knowledge of the gospel to find proper motives for obedience during trials.

**Faith is essential in the hour of temptation**.

Like a shield, faith receives Christ and the benefits of redemption, relying on the unseen realities of his promise.

Satan's fiery darts include violent temptations and **harsh thoughts about God or ourselves**.

Faith applies **God's word and Christ's grace** to extinguish them.

**Salvation** is our helmet.

The solid hope of victory and eternal life helps keep our thoughts from being corrupted.

For attack, the apostle names **one weapon only**: **the sword of the Spirit, God's word**.

It opposes evil desires and blasphemous thoughts rising from within.

It also answers unbelief and false teaching arriving from outside.

Even **one biblical passage understood accurately and used rightly** can destroy a powerful temptation or objection.

**Prayer holds every piece of our armor together**.

We have many responsibilities, but we must keep regular times for prayer.

Formal prayer is not always possible while performing other duties.

Nevertheless, **brief prayers lifted to God throughout the day** are always appropriate.

Our ordinary thoughts should be directed toward holy things.

Prayer should include **public, private, secret, shared, solitary, solemn and spontaneous petitions**.

It should contain **confession, requests for mercy and thanksgiving**.

Every prayer must depend on the Holy Spirit's gracious help and instruction.

Continue praying even when answers seem delayed.

And pray not only for yourself, but for **all God's people**.

Our enemies are powerful and our strength is small.

But **our Redeemer is almighty**.

Through his strength we can overcome.

## Pray for gospel ministers and receive the blessing (verses 19–24)

The gospel was a mystery until God made it known.

Ministers of Christ are entrusted with **proclaiming that revelation**.

Even the most gifted and respected ministers need the prayers of ordinary believers.

We should pray especially for servants of Christ exposed to **severe danger or hardship** through their work.

Paul wished his readers **peace, love and faith**.

Peace includes reconciliation with God, a clear conscience and harmony among believers.

The Spirit gives the graces that produce **faith, love and every Christian virtue**.

Paul asked for these gifts to increase even in people who had already begun receiving them.

Every blessing comes from God **through Jesus Christ our Lord**.

Divine grace includes God's favor and every spiritual and earthly good that flows from it.

Such grace belongs to those who **sincerely love the Lord Jesus Christ**.
