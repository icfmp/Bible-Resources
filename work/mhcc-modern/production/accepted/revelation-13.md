# Revelation 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A wild beast rises out of the sea, to whom the dragon gives his power. | 1–10 |
| Another beast, which has two horns like a lamb, but speaks as a dragon. | 11–15 |
| It obliges all to worship its image, and receive its mark, as persons devoted to it. | 16–18 |

## The first beast and its persecuting authority (verses 1–10)

John saw a terrifying beast rising from the sea.

Henry understands it as a **tyrannical, idolatrous and persecuting power** emerging from great public disturbances.

He connects it with worldly empires that had oppressed God's people for centuries, beginning as far back as the Babylonian captivity.

He particularly relates the fourth beast of **Daniel's prophecy** to **the Roman Empire**, under which many believers suffered cruel persecution.

The dragon—Satan—supplied the beast's authority and supported its power.

The **wounded head** may, in Henry's interpretation, signify the overthrow of Roman pagan idolatry.

Its **healing** may symbolize what Henry polemically called **“popish idolatry”**, a revived form of the same spiritual evil under a different outward appearance.

This reflects **Henry's historical Protestant interpretation**, not a fact or identity demonstrated beyond dispute by Revelation 13.

The world admired the beast's power, strategy and success.

People gave honor and obedience to the devil's instruments, granting creatures reverence that belongs **to God alone**.

Yet Satan's authority and his apparent success remain strictly limited.

Christ has a **chosen remnant**, redeemed by his blood, recorded in his book and sealed by his Spirit.

The beast and its allies may conquer the **body** and take away physical life, but cannot conquer the souls of genuine believers or make them finally abandon their Savior and join his enemies.

Those whose names are written in the book of life are characterized by **endurance in gospel faith** and faithful worship of God during severe temptation.

Encouraging this steadfastness is, Henry says, **the principal design of the whole book of Revelation**.

## The second beast, enforced worship and the number 666 (verses 11–18)

Those who see the first beast as a form of worldly power interpret the second as another oppressive authority—**a spiritual power disguising itself as true religion and concern for people's souls**.

It presents itself as derived from Christ, and initially appears gentle.

But it soon speaks like the dragon.

Its false teaching and harsh commands reveal, in Henry's understanding, that it serves Satan rather than **the Lamb**.

It exercises the first beast's authority for the same end: drawing people away from the worship of the true God and placing human consciences under **human control**.

Henry describes **deceptive signs and supposed miracles** as instruments used to persuade people to worship the image of the earlier persecuting power.

Harsh censures are another means.

Those refusing its worship may be deprived of **ordinary civil rights**, opportunities to buy or sell, and positions of profit or trust.

Receiving its mark signifies binding oneself to advance its cause with all one's influence, strength and efforts.

To make the beast's image is to establish a form of its worship and authority.

To worship that image is to submit to the defining practices of the power it represents.

The beast's number is **666**.

It invites the exercise of human wisdom while displaying the far greater wisdom of God.

But Henry says explicitly that **the intended person or institution remains a mystery**.

The number has been applied to nearly every religious controversy.

He even considers it reasonable to doubt whether **its true meaning had yet been discovered**.

What the believer can understand is that every enemy of God is known and numbered.

Their power has a limit, and their appointed end will come.

All nations will finally submit to **the King of righteousness and peace**.
