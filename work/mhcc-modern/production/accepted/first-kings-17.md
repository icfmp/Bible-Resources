# 1 Kings 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elijah fed by ravens. | 1–7 |
| Elijah sent to Zarephath. | 8–16 |
| Elijah raises the widow's son to life. | 17–24 |

## Ravens feed Elijah (verses 1–7)

God wonderfully equips people for the work he has prepared for them.

The times called for an Elijah, and Elijah was suited to those times.

The Holy Spirit knows how to equip servants for their particular circumstances.

Elijah announced to Ahab that God was displeased with Israel's idolatry.

The Lord would discipline the people by withholding rain, something the gods they worshiped could never provide.

God told Elijah to hide himself.

When providence calls us to quietness and solitude, we should accept it.

If we cannot perform useful work, we should be patient.

When we cannot work actively for God, we should sit quietly and wait for him.

The Lord appointed **ravens** to bring Elijah meat, and they did so.

Anyone who lives from one day's provision to the next can learn to trust God's care for **each day's bread on the day it is needed**.

God could have sent angels instead.

He chose ravens to show that even the humblest creatures can accomplish his will as effectively as the mightiest.

Elijah seems to have lived this way for **more than a year**.

The brook's natural supply of water, which came through ordinary providence, failed.

But the miraculous food that God had expressly promised never failed.

When the heavens fail to give rain, the earth's resources fail as well.

Created comforts may disappear precisely when we most need them, like streams drying up in summer.

But the river that makes glad the city of God never runs dry.

There is a spring of water that wells up to eternal life.

Lord, give us that **living water**!

## Elijah is sent to Zarephath (verses 8–16)

Many widows lived in Israel during Elijah's lifetime.

Some might gladly have received him into their homes.

Yet the Lord sent him instead to a city in **Sidon**, among the Gentiles.

In that sense, he became the first prophet sent to the Gentiles.

**Jezebel** was Elijah's greatest enemy.

To show how powerless her hatred was, God found a place of refuge for Elijah **in her own country**.

God chose no rich or powerful host.

He chose a poor, lonely widow who herself lacked food.

He made her both willing and able to sustain his servant.

It is his way and his glory to use and honor those considered weak and foolish.

The widow demonstrated extraordinary faith.

She trusted the prophet's promise that giving to him first would not leave her without food.

People who rely on God's promises are prepared to give themselves and what they possess in his service.

The growth of this woman's faith was as remarkable a miracle **in the kingdom of grace** as the continuing increase of her flour and oil was **in the kingdom of providence**.

Blessed are those who believe and obey hopefully even when circumstances offer no hope.

The widow shared the food she had for a single meal.

Through God's provision, she and her son then ate for **more than two years** during famine.

Food supplied by God's special favor, and enjoyed in Elijah's company, was doubly sweet.

Those who trust the Lord have his promise that in evil times they need not be ashamed and that in famine he can satisfy them.

## Elijah raises the widow's son (verses 17–24)

Neither genuine faith nor faithful obedience keeps suffering and death out of our lives.

When the widow's child died, she spoke to Elijah mainly to express her grief, not because she expected him to revive the boy.

When God takes away our comforts, we may remember sins committed long before, perhaps in our youth.

His purpose may be to teach us to remember those sins against ourselves and repent.

Elijah's prayer was undoubtedly directed by the Holy Spirit.

The child came back to life.

See the **power of prayer**, and the power of **the One who hears prayer**.
