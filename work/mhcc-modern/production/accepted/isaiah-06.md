# Isaiah 6 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The vision which Isaiah beheld in the temple. | 1–8 |
| The Lord declares the blindness to come upon the Jewish nation, and the destruction which would follow. | 9–13 |

## Isaiah's vision of divine holiness (verses 1–8)

In this symbolic vision, the prophet sees the temple opened, even into its innermost sanctuary.

He stands outside while the divine presence appears upon the **mercy seat**, above the ark of the covenant, among the cherubim and seraphim. God's glory fills the temple.

**John 12:41** explains the vision as Isaiah's sight of **Christ's glory** and his testimony about him.

Henry regards this as clear evidence that **Jesus our Savior is God**.

Through Jesus Christ, God is seated upon a **throne of grace**, and the way into his presence is opened.

The temple also pictures God's earthly church, filled with his glory.

The flowing hem of his robe fills the sanctuary, and all creation belongs to him. Yet he also dwells in every contrite heart.

The seraphim are holy angels. Henry explains their name as **burning ones**, full of love for God and zeal for his glory against sin.

They cover their faces in humility, ready to obey every command of God even without knowing all his secret purposes.

One sight of Christ in glory would put an end to human vanity, arrogance and proud ignorance.

Isaiah became overwhelmed by the awareness of his own impurity.

Without a Mediator between us and the holy God, **we are undone**.

Even a glimpse of divine glory exposes our supposed righteousness as **filthy rags**.

No human being could stand to speak before God's holiness, justice and majesty without finding mercy and grace through Christ.

The burning coal may picture the prophet's assurance that his sin was forgiven and his ministry accepted **through Christ's atonement**.

Only what comes through the Savior's satisfaction and intercession can cleanse and comfort the soul.

We need our sins removed to speak confidently **to God in prayer** or **from God in preaching**.

Those who recognize sin's burden and danger may seek this cleansing.

God's messengers may take courage: he has sent them to speak in his name and will sustain them in their calling.

## The warning of spiritual blindness and the remnant (verses 9–13)

The Lord sent Isaiah to announce the coming ruin of his people.

Many hear the sound of God's word yet never receive its power within the heart.

God may, in righteous judgment, leave those who **persistently reject truth** to blindness of mind.

But no humble person genuinely seeking Christ should imagine that this punishment is meant to discourage sincere inquiry.

It falls upon those **determined to retain their sins**.

Let everyone ask for **the enlightening Holy Spirit**, so that God's mercies may become precious and the heart may avoid such dreadful hardness.

Even under judgment the Lord would preserve **a holy remnant**, compared with a tenth remaining.

God continues preserving his church.

Visible religious communities and professing believers may be cut back like barren branches. Yet the **holy seed** will put forth new growth, from which many branches of righteousness will arise.
