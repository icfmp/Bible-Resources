## The people inquire about their fasts (verses 1–7)

When we sincerely want to learn God's will in a difficult matter, we must consult **his word and his ministers** and seek his direction through earnest prayer.

In doubtful situations, it is wise to seek counsel from people whose special task is to search and explain the Scriptures.

The Jews wondered whether they needed to continue their fasting days now that the city and temple appeared likely to be completed.

God first answered by **rebuking their hypocrisy**.

These fasts were not pleasing to him unless undertaken with a better attitude and purpose.

The outward form of worship remained, but its life, soul and power were missing.

Every holy exercise should be done **for God**, with his word as our rule and his glory as our purpose.

We should seek to please him and receive his favor.

Instead, **themselves and their own interests** had occupied the center of their religious acts.

Weeping during appointed fasts was not enough.

They should have studied the earlier prophets and learned why God had confronted their ancestors.

Whether we live in prosperity or adversity, we must leave sin behind and fulfill our duties toward God.

## Sin brought the captivity (verses 8–14)

The judgments God sent upon ancient Israel were recorded as warnings to Christians.

The Lord called his people not merely to keep fasts and offer sacrifices but to **act justly and love mercy**, bringing peace and well-being to their neighbors.

God's law governs and restrains **the heart**, not only the outward act.

Yet the people filled their minds with prejudices against his word.

They refused instruction and hardened themselves.

Few things are harder than the heart of a presumptuous sinner.

Consider the terrible consequences this brought upon their fathers.

Great sins committed against **the Lord of hosts** meet his great anger, whose power none can resist.

When sin is cherished in the heart, it will ruin our prayers.

Yet **the Lord always hears a brokenhearted, repentant sinner's cry**.

Those who die impenitent and unbelieving will find neither remedy nor refuge from the misery they refused to take seriously during life.
