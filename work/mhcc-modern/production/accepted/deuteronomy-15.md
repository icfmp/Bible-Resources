# Deuteronomy 15 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

## The year of release and generosity to the poor (verses 1–11)

The year of release pictured the grace of the gospel.

Through it, the Lord proclaims the acceptable year of salvation, in which our debts—our sins—are forgiven.

God's law is spiritual.

It restrains even the thoughts of our hearts.

We are mistaken if we think our thoughts are outside his knowledge or authority.

The law's command to forgive debts exposed the evil in some hearts.

Instead of being generous, certain people refused to **lend or give** because the year of release was approaching.

They used a law of generosity as an excuse for selfishness.

To avoid committing sin, we must reject sinful thoughts before they produce actions.

It is dreadful to make poor people cry out against us with good reason.

Do not resent helping a neighbor, and do not distrust God's provision.

Give freely and cheerfully, because God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:7).

## The release of servants (verses 12–18)

The law concerning Hebrew servants was repeated.

It included a requirement that masters provide some resources when servants were released, so that people who had worked without wages could begin to support themselves.

When members of a household take their duties toward one another seriously, they can expect God to bless their family life.

We should remember that we owe divine justice a debt we cannot pay.

We are like poor, dying slaves.

But the Lord Jesus Christ became poor and shed his blood so that our debt could be paid, our souls ransomed and all our needs supplied.

When the gospel is preached clearly, the Lord's year of release is announced.

It proclaims forgiveness of sin, freedom for the soul and rest in Christ.

As faith in Christ and love for him grow, they overcome selfishness and the world's unkindness.

They remove the excuses that unbelief, mistrust and greed continually invent.

## The firstborn animals (verses 19–23)

This law prescribed what Israelites were to do with the firstborn animals of their flocks and herds.

Christians are not bound by that distinction.

We no longer make a difference between the first calf or lamb and the animals that follow.

Instead, we should apply the gospel lesson.

Dedicate yourself—and **the first and best of your time and strength**—to God.

Use every comfort and possession for his praise and according to his law, remembering that everything we enjoy came from his hand.
