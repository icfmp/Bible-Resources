# Lamentations 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The miserable state of Jerusalem, the just consequences of its sins. | 1–11 |
| Jerusalem represented as a captive female, lamenting, and seeking the mercy of God. | 12–22 |

## Jerusalem's desolation follows her sins (verses 1–11)

At times the prophet speaks **in his own person**.

At other times **Jerusalem is personified as a distressed woman**, or the words are spoken by some of the Jews.

The lament portrays the Jewish nation's misery.

Because of the greatness of her sins, Jerusalem had become **a captive and a slave**.

She found no rest from suffering.

If we permit **sin, our greatest enemy**, to govern us, we should not be astonished when other enemies are also allowed to rule over us.

The people experienced the extremes of **famine and distress**.

In these terrible circumstances Jerusalem acknowledged her sins and asked the Lord to consider her suffering.

This is the only way to find rest under such burdens.

God's just anger against human transgression has filled the world with **sorrow, lamentation, sickness and death**.

## Jerusalem mourns and seeks mercy (verses 12–22)

Jerusalem is pictured sitting in grief upon the ground.

She asks everyone passing by to consider whether her example should concern them.

Her outward suffering was great, but **the guilt she felt made her inward suffering even harder to bear**.

True sorrow over sin must reach the depths of the soul.

This scene shows sin's dreadful character and calls us to flee from coming judgment.

Yet however much we may learn from Jerusalem's suffering, **we can learn far more from the suffering of Christ**.

Does he not address every one of us from the cross?

**“Is it nothing to you, all you who pass by?”**

May all our sorrows lead us to **Christ's cross**, teach us to attend to his example and enable us to follow him willingly and joyfully.
