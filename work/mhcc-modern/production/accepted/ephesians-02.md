# Ephesians 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The riches of God's grace towards men, shown from their deplorable state by nature, and the happy change Divine grace makes in them. | 1–10 |

## Saved by grace from spiritual death (verses 1–10)

**Sin is death to the soul**.

Someone dead in trespasses and sins has no appetite for genuine spiritual happiness.

Seeing a dead body fills us with dread. The undying human spirit has departed, leaving behind the ruined remains of its earthly life.

But if we understood the matter correctly, we would be **far more deeply disturbed by a soul spiritually dead in sin**.

Living in sin means adopting the patterns of this world.

Wicked people become slaves of Satan, who works through proud and sinful desires and rules in hearts that resist God.

Scripture teaches that people are naturally **children of disobedience and exposed to wrath**, whether their sins take the form of bodily indulgence or the equally serious pride of the spirit.

What reason we all have to seek the grace that can turn **children of wrath into God's children and heirs of glory**!

The source of God's mercies is his **great love and rich compassion**.

Every genuinely converted sinner is a sinner saved from **both sin and wrath**.

Saving grace is God's **free, undeserved favor**.

He saves us **not by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ**.

Grace also gives the soul **new life**.

A regenerated person now lives in holiness, having been born of God.

Through God's pardoning and justifying grace, that person is freed from **sin's guilt**.

While sinners roll in the dust, believers sanctified by Christ's grace are raised spiritually to sit **in heavenly places**, no longer ruled by this world's values.

God's past acts of conversion and salvation encourage future generations to hope in **his mercy and grace**.

Our faith, conversion and eternal salvation are **not works we have accomplished**.

No one can boast.

Everything is **the gift of God**, accomplished as he gives life through his power.

God also prepared us for good works.

He gives us knowledge of his will, while the Holy Spirit transforms us so that we increasingly glorify him through holy conduct and perseverance.

This teaching cannot honestly be used to justify wrongdoing.

Anyone claiming that salvation by grace encourages sin has misunderstood or rejected Scripture's teaching.

## Remember what it meant to be far from Christ (verses 11–13)

**Christ and his covenant** are the foundation of all Christian hope.

Paul described the Gentiles' former condition in terrible terms.

But who could rescue themselves from that condition?

How sad that the same description may apply even to some people who have been **baptized in Christ's name** while still lacking genuine faith.

Consider the horror of being permanently separated from God's people, cut off from the body of Christ, excluded from the covenant of promise and left with **no hope and no Savior**.

A person in that condition has no fellowship with God as a loving Father and must face **his righteous judgment**.

What genuine believer can think about that danger without trembling?

Salvation is far from those who persist in wickedness.

Yet God comes close to help his own people **through Christ's suffering and death**.

## Christ makes peace and opens access to the Father (verses 14–18)

Jesus Christ made peace **through the sacrifice of himself**.

In every sense **Christ himself is our peace**.

He is the author, center and substance of our reconciliation with God.

He also unites believing Gentiles and Jews into **one church**.

Through **Christ's person, sacrifice and mediation**, sinners are allowed to approach God as Father and are welcomed into his presence.

Their worship and service are received under the Holy Spirit's instruction, because they are united to the Father and the Son.

Christ purchased **the right of access to God**.

The Spirit gives us **the desire to come, the strength to come and grace to serve acceptably**.

## One household and one holy temple (verses 19–22)

Paul described the church through several pictures.

It is **a city**: everyone truly converted receives the rights of citizenship.

It is **a family household**: believers become members of God's own family—his servants and children.

It is **a building** founded upon the teaching of Jesus Christ, proclaimed through **Old Testament prophets and New Testament apostles**.

God now dwells within every believer.

Through the power and presence of **the Holy Spirit**, God's people become **his holy temple**.
