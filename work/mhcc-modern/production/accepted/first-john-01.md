# 1 John 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle prefaces his epistle to believers in general, with evident testimonies to Christ, for promoting their happiness and joy. | 1–4 |
| The necessity of a life of holiness, in order to communion with God, is shown. | 5–10 |

## The apostles testify to the incarnate Word of life (verses 1–4)

The apostle writes about the **eternal and uncreated Excellence**, the One who existed from the beginning **equal with the Father**, and eventually appeared in human nature to save sinners.

For years the apostles witnessed **Christ's wisdom, holiness, miracles, love and mercy**.

They saw him crucified for sinners and then raised from the dead.

They also **touched him**, giving them direct evidence of his bodily resurrection.

This divine person—the **Word of life and Word of God**—took human nature to become the **Author and Giver of eternal life** through the redeeming power of his blood and the renewing work of his Spirit.

The apostles proclaimed what they had seen and heard so that others could share their comfort and eternal blessings.

They enjoyed access to God the Father, experiencing his truth inwardly and displaying it through their conduct.

**Fellowship with the Father and the Son begins and continues through the work of the Holy Spirit**.

Christ's gifts do not resemble scarce worldly possessions, which provoke jealousy when shared.

God's joy and fellowship are **sufficient for all**, so any number of people may receive them without diminishing anyone else's share.

Those who truly know this fellowship naturally wish for other people to share the same blessedness.

## Walking in God's light requires confession and cleansing (verses 5–10)

The message from Jesus, the eternal Word, deserves joyful reception.

**God is perfect light** in a world of spiritual darkness.

His doctrines and commands share the purity of his nature.

Just as God's perfect happiness cannot be separated from his holiness, our happiness grows as we become holy.

Walking in darkness means **living contrary to God's revealed will**.

God does not enjoy heavenly fellowship with people who choose to remain unholy.

A professed faith contradicted by a persistently sinful life is false.

Yet the eternal Son took flesh and blood, died to **wash away our sins in his own blood**, and gives us the sacred influences through which sin is progressively overcome.

The command to walk in holiness is an **effect and evidence of knowing God through Christ**, not a basis for claiming our own righteousness.

Even people walking closest to God recognize that their **best days and deeds remain mixed with sin**.

The atoning sacrifice God has provided demonstrates the sinfulness of humanity and the continuing need of believers for pardon.

Christians must therefore **keep confessing their sins and applying by faith to Christ's blood**.

Let us plead guilty before God, humbly ready to learn the worst about our own condition.

Confess sin honestly in its full seriousness, relying solely on God's mercy and faithfulness through **Christ's righteousness** for complete forgiveness and deliverance from sin's power and practice.
