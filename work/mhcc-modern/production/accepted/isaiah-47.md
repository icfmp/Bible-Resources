# Isaiah 47 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's judgments on Babylon. | 1–6 |
| Carelessness and confidence shall not prevent the evil. | 7–15 |

## Babylon is brought low (verses 1–6)

Babylon is pictured as a woman brought into extreme distress.

She is stripped of her former dignity and made to suffer.

Instead of sitting in splendor, she sits on the ground and grinds grain at a **handmill**, one of the lowest and hardest forms of labor.

God was righteous in bringing this judgment, and no one could prevent it.

The prophet celebrates **the Lord of Hosts, the Redeemer and Holy One of Israel**.

God sometimes permits wicked people to prevail against his people.

But those who cruelly oppress the people of God will themselves face punishment.

## Pride and false confidence cannot prevent judgment (verses 7–15)

We should beware of copying Babylon's attitudes and speech.

Do not trust in **tyranny and oppression**.

Do not boast of your abilities, rely on yourself, or claim all your successes as proof of personal wisdom and careful planning.

Otherwise you risk sharing Babylon's judgments.

People enjoying great prosperity often imagine that hardship can never reach them.

It is also common for sinners to suppose they are safe because their wicked actions are **hidden from others**.

That false feeling of security will itself contribute to their destruction.

From these prophecies, learn **humility and trust in God**.

His word tells us what will finally become of the righteous and the wicked.

It teaches us how to escape the judgment to come, glorify God, experience peace during life, receive hope at death and enter everlasting happiness.

Let us therefore keep away from every deception that would draw us from his truth.
