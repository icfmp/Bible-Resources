# Jeremiah 38 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jeremiah is cast into a dungeon, from whence he is delivered by an Ethiopian. | 1–13 |
| He advises the king to surrender to the Chaldeans. | 14–28 |

## Jeremiah is imprisoned and rescued (verses 1–13)

Jeremiah continued to preach God's word plainly, just as the officials continued in their malice.

Wicked people commonly regard faithful ministers as enemies because those ministers show them **how much harm they are doing to themselves by refusing repentance**.

Jeremiah was thrown into a dungeon.

Many faithful witnesses for God have died quietly and unnoticed in prisons.

But **Ebed-melech**, an Ethiopian, spoke courageously and truthfully to the king: **the men who had dealt with Jeremiah had acted wickedly**.

Notice how God can raise up friends to help his people in the deepest distress.

The king ordered Jeremiah's release, and Ebed-melech saw that the prophet was pulled out of the dungeon.

His particular **tenderness toward Jeremiah** deserves attention.

This should encourage us to stand openly for God.

In these opposing characters, we recognize an enduring difference: **God's children become like their Father**, while **Satan's children imitate their master**.

## Jeremiah advises Zedekiah to surrender (verses 14–28)

Jeremiah was reluctant to repeat warnings that appeared only to **endanger his own life** and **increase the king's guilt**.

Yet he pressed Zedekiah with the truth and challenged the fear that held him back from obedience to God.

**The less people fear God, the more they tend to fear other people.**

They often lack the courage to act according to their own judgments and consciences, even when they know what is right.
