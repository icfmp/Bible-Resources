# Ephesians 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle sets forth his office, and his qualifications for it, and his call to it. | 1–7 |
| Also the noble purposes answered by it. | 8–12 |
| He prays for the Ephesians. | 13–19 |
| And adds a thanksgiving. | 20, 21 |

## Paul's calling to make the gospel mystery known (verses 1–7)

Paul was imprisoned because he had faithfully preached God's truth.

Yet he was **a prisoner of Jesus Christ**, protected and cared for by the very Lord for whose sake he suffered.

The gospel's promises and joyful announcement all come from **God's rich grace**.

Through that message, the Holy Spirit ordinarily produces grace in human souls.

The mystery Paul described was **God's once-hidden purpose of salvation through Jesus Christ**.

Earlier generations had not received such a clear and full account of it as was given to the New Testament apostles and prophets.

A central part of the revelation entrusted to Paul was that **Gentiles would be called to salvation through faith in Christ**.

The gifts of divine grace are accompanied by **God's effective power**.

The Lord not only appointed Paul to his apostolic office.

He also **equipped him** to carry out its responsibilities.

## The unsearchable riches of Christ are announced (verses 8–12)

When God gives people an honorable calling, he often makes them **humble in their own eyes**.

And where he gives the grace of humility, he gives the other graces needed for duty.

Notice how magnificently Paul describes **the unsearchable riches of Jesus Christ**!

Many have not received these riches.

Yet what a privilege even **to hear them proclaimed and freely offered**!

Those who refuse such a gift have no reason to blame its Giver.

Both the first creation—when God brought the world into existence from nothing—and the new creation, when sinners are transformed by converting grace, come **from God through Jesus Christ**.

Christ's spiritual riches remain as vast and certain as ever.

Angels marvel at God's wisdom in redeeming his church.

Yet self-satisfied and worldly people often dismiss that same wisdom as foolishness.

## Paul prays for strength and a deeper knowledge of Christ's love (verses 13–19)

Paul seemed more troubled by the possibility that the Ephesians would become discouraged because of his suffering than by the suffering he had to endure himself.

He therefore asked for **spiritual blessings**, the greatest gifts of all.

He prayed for **strength through God's Spirit in the inner person**.

This includes strength of soul and faith so that believers can serve God and perform their duties.

When Christ's law is written upon the heart and his love is poured into it, **Christ lives there**.

Where his Spirit dwells, Christ himself dwells.

We should desire that good affections become **deeply rooted and stable** within us.

How wonderful to enjoy a settled awareness of God's love for us in Christ!

Consider the dimensions Paul uses to describe that love.

Its **breadth** reaches people of every nation and social rank.

Its **length** continues from eternity past to eternity future.

Its **depth** reaches people sunk in the deepest sin and misery.

Its **height** raises them into heavenly happiness and glory.

Those who receive grace upon grace from Christ's fullness can be described as being **filled with God's fullness**.

Should that not satisfy the human heart?

Why do we try to complete our happiness by filling ourselves with a thousand worthless distractions?

## End prayer with praise (verses 20 and 21)

Prayer should naturally lead to **thanksgiving and praise**.

Because of what Christ has already accomplished in our souls, we may **ask and expect even more** from his grace.

The conversion of sinners and the comfort of believers will bring **glory to Christ forever**.
