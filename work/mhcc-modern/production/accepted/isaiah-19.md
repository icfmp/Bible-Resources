# Isaiah 19 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Judgments upon Egypt. | 1–17 |
| Its deliverance, and the conversion of the people. | 18–25 |

## Egypt humbled through its own troubles (verses 1–17)

God would enter Egypt in judgment.

He would raise up **the causes of Egypt's downfall from among its own people**.

When ungodly people escape an immediate danger, they often imagine themselves safe.

But unless they repent, sin's consequences will pursue them and eventually overtake them.

The Egyptians would fall under a harsh ruler, a prediction Henry says was fulfilled soon afterward.

They were famous for **learning and scientific skill**.

Yet God would allow their wise men to follow corrupt plans and quarrel with one another, until the nation's own divisions made it an object of contempt and pity.

God can make sinners afraid of the very people they once looked down upon and oppressed.

He can also make evildoers **terrify one another**, while ordinary circumstances appear threatening on every side.

## Egypt's future turning to the Lord (verses 18–25)

The prophetic words **“In that day”** do not always refer to the event described immediately before them.

Here Isaiah looks toward a later period.

Egyptians would learn to speak **the holy language of Scripture**, not merely recognizing it but using it.

Converting grace changes people's **hearts**, and therefore also changes their speech, because the mouth speaks from what fills the heart.

So many Jews would settle in Egypt, Henry says, that **five cities** would soon be filled.

A place formerly notorious for **worshiping the sun** would experience a remarkable spiritual reform.

**Jesus Christ is the great Altar** who makes the gifts offered through him acceptable.

There people would offer the gospel's spiritual sacrifices: **prayer and praise**.

Those wounded and humbled by God's correction should take courage.

When suffering leads them to return and call upon the Lord, **he heals their souls** and changes sorrowful petitions into joyful thanksgiving.

Gentile nations would be joined together within **Christ's flock**, under **the great Shepherd**.

They would also be united with **the Jews**.

All would be received by the same Lord and share the same blessing.

When believers meet at the same **throne of grace** and serve in the same work of worship, they should allow quarrels to end and be joined together through holy love.
