# Numbers 8 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The lamps of the sanctuary. | 1–4 |
| Consecration of the Levites, and their service. | 5–26 |

## Lighting the sanctuary's lamps (verses 1–4)

Aaron himself lit the sanctuary lamps.

In this he pictured his divine Master, Jesus Christ.

Scripture shines like a light in a dark place (2 Peter 1:19).

Without that light, even the church would remain in darkness—just as the tabernacle, which had no windows, would have been dark without its lamps.

Ministers serve by lighting these lamps spiritually, explaining and applying God's word.

Jesus Christ is the only Light of our dark and sinful world.

Through his atoning work, his word and the Holy Spirit, he spreads his light.

## The Levites are cleansed and appointed (verses 5–26)

These verses provide directions for solemnly setting apart the Levites.

All Israel needed to know that the Levites had not claimed this honor for themselves.

They were called to it by God.

But merely being set apart from others was not enough.

Those appointed for God's work must devote themselves to him and to the responsibilities he has assigned them.

Christians are to be baptized, and ministers are to be ordained.

Before offering God our work, we must offer him ourselves.

The Levites were to be cleansed.

Those who carry the Lord's vessels must be clean.

Moses sprinkled the appointed purifying water over them.

This pictured Christ's blood being applied to our souls through faith, making us fit to serve the living God.

God declared his acceptance of the Levites.

Anyone who expects to enjoy the privileges of God's sanctuary should also be willing to perform its service.

On one hand, God does not need any of his creatures to serve him.

On the other, he never appoints anyone merely to carry a title while doing nothing.

Everyone he acknowledges as a servant receives work to do.

Even angels serve him.
