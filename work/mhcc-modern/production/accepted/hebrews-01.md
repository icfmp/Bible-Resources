# Hebrews 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The surpassing dignity of the Son of God in his Divine person, and in his creating and mediatorial work. | 1–3 |
| And in his superiority to all the holy angels. | 4–14 |

## God's final revelation through his Son (verses 1–3)

In earlier generations, God spoke to his people at various times and in various ways: sometimes directly, sometimes through **dreams, visions and divine influence upon prophets' minds**.

The gospel revelation is greater because **God has now spoken through his Son**.

When we contemplate the power, wisdom and goodness of Jesus Christ, we behold those of his Father too (**John 14:7**). The entire fullness of deity dwells **really, not merely symbolically**, in Christ.

When the world fell under the curse and judgment of sin, **God's Son sustained it by almighty power and goodness** as he undertook its redemption.

The glory of his divine person and mediatorial office leads us to the glory of his grace.

Because of who Jesus is, **his suffering possessed sufficient worth to satisfy the honor of God** offended by human sin.

We cannot thank God enough for revealing salvation to sinners with increasing clarity.

And that Christ himself **purified us from our sins** is a wonder of love exceeding all our power to praise or admire.

## Christ is greater than every angel (verses 4–14)

Some Jews treated angels with excessive, even idolatrous reverence because the law and other divine messages had been delivered through them. Some regarded them as intermediaries and offered them forms of religious homage.

The writer therefore shows not only that **Jesus created all things, including angels**, but also that **the risen Messiah, exalted in human nature**, rules over all angels and powers.

He establishes this from comparisons with the Old Testament.

Angels are servants who carry out God's will. But **Christ is God**: without deity he could not have accomplished the Mediator's work or worn his crown.

As the **Messiah, the Anointed One**, he was equipped and confirmed for this mediatorial office.

Christ has equals only **as a human being**, while as Son of God he surpasses every prophet, priest and king who ever served God.

**Psalm 102:25–27** speaks of his almighty work in creating the world and transforming it.

He will fold up the earth and heavens like a garment when their present use is finished, yet he remains **forever the same**.

Like a sovereign who remains sovereign when ceremonial clothing is put away, Christ never loses his dignity.

Sin changed the world for the worse; Christ will transform it for the better. Let this make us watchful and eager for the world to come, rather than attached to fading things.

Though Jesus has done so much to make people his friends, some remain his enemies. Eventually they will become his footstool, whether through **humble submission or final destruction**.

Angels, even the most exalted, remain servants under Christ.

Believers now possess the status of **heirs**, not yet the full inheritance.

Angels minister to them under Christ and the Spirit: resisting evil spirits, protecting their bodies, and giving instruction or comfort.

At the last day they will gather the saints, while people who set their hopes on perishing treasure will be separated from Christ.
