# Ruth 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elimelech and his sons die in the land of Moab. | 1–5 |
| Naomi returns home. | 6–14 |
| Orpah stays behind, but Ruth goes with Naomi. | 15–18 |
| They come to Bethlehem. | 19–22 |

## Elimelech's family in Moab (verses 1–5)

Elimelech was not wrong to care about providing for his family.

But Henry says his decision to move to **Moab** cannot be justified.

The move ended with the loss of his family.

It is foolish to imagine we can escape a hardship that God has placed in our path and calls us to bear.

Moving somewhere else seldom improves our spiritual situation.

We do not know what may happen when we bring young people into harmful company and remove them from public worship.

We may think their religious foundations are strong and that they are well prepared to resist temptation.

Yet we cannot see the outcome.

The text does not say that the Moabite women whom Elimelech's sons married had converted to Israel's faith.

All earthly trials and pleasures last only a short time.

Death continually removes people of every age and condition, undoing our worldly comforts.

We should therefore value most highly the good things that endure forever.

## Naomi decides to return (verses 6–14)

After the deaths of her sons, Naomi began planning to return home.

When death enters a family, the survivors should consider what needs changing in their lives.

God sometimes allows earthly life to become bitter so that heaven becomes more precious to us.

Naomi appears to have been a woman of faith and devotion.

She said goodbye to her daughters-in-law **with prayer**.

Friends should part in love and prayer.

Was Naomi right to discourage them from accompanying her, when going with her might help them escape Moab's idolatry and worship Israel's God?

Henry says Naomi certainly wanted that good outcome, but she did not want them to adopt Israel's religion **only to please her**.

People who profess faith merely to satisfy friends or enjoy their company make unreliable converts.

If they accompanied Naomi, they should make a considered, personal decision, understanding its cost.

Anyone professing faith should **count the cost** first.

Many people prefer the earthly rest of marriage, financial security or comfortable circumstances to the rest Christ offers their souls.

When their loyalty is tested, they may leave Christ, perhaps with real sorrow.

## Orpah stays; Ruth follows Naomi (verses 15–18)

Consider Ruth's determination and genuine love for Naomi.

Orpah was distressed at parting, but she did not love Naomi enough to leave Moab for her sake.

Similarly, many people value Christ and feel affection toward him, yet fail to receive his salvation because they will not give up other things for him.

They love him in a sense, but leave him because they love something else **more**.

Ruth is an example of God's grace moving a soul to choose the better portion.

Naomi could ask for nothing more than Ruth's solemn declaration.

A firm resolution is powerful against temptation.

When someone follows a religious path without a settled decision, that person is like a half-open door inviting a thief.

A determined heart **closes and bolts the door**, resists the devil and causes him to flee.

## Naomi and Ruth arrive in Bethlehem (verses 19–22)

They came to **Bethlehem**.

Suffering can change a person's situation very quickly and dramatically.

May God's grace prepare us for every such change, especially the greatest change of all.

**Naomi** means *pleasant* or *lovable*.

**Mara** means *bitter* or *bitterness*.

Naomi felt that sorrow described her life.

She returned home empty: poor, widowed and without children.

But believers possess a fullness no hardship can empty, a good portion that cannot be taken away.

Suffering is a bitter cup.

Naomi acknowledged that hers had come under God's providence.

When the Lord humbles us through our circumstances, we should humble our hearts before him.

Suffering is not beneficial merely because it hurts; it does us good **when we respond to it rightly**.
