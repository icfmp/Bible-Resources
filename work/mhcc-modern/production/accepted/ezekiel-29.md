# Ezekiel 29 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The desolation of Egypt. | 1–16 |
| Also a promise of mercy to Israel. | 17–21 |

## Egypt's boasting and its ruin (verses 1–16)

Worldly people boast about their possessions while forgetting that **everything they have came from God** and ought to be used for him.

What reason do we have to boast?

**Self is the great idol** that people everywhere worship in disregard of God and his authority.

The Lord can drive people away from the very circumstances in which they feel most secure and comfortable.

Those who cling to such a person or source of security may perish along with it.

That is how pride, presumption and false confidence end.

God opposes those who harm his people, and even more those who **lead them into sin**.

Egypt would become a kingdom again, but a **very lowly kingdom**, with little wealth or political power.

Henry says its later history shows the fulfillment of the prophecy.

It is not only in justice, but also **in wisdom and goodness toward us**, that the Lord sometimes breaks the created supports on which we lean, so they will no longer be our confidence.

## God recompenses Babylon and preserves mercy for Israel (verses 17–21)

Those who besieged **Tyre** gained little plunder.

But when God uses ambitious or greedy people for his purposes, he may reward them **according to the desires of their own hearts**; everyone receives an appropriate recompense.

Mercy would soon be shown to the house of Israel.

The history of nations helps explain ancient prophecies, and events fulfill God's word.

Even in the deepest trouble, the Lord can be **sowing the seeds of future well-being**.

Happy are those who seek **his favor, grace and likeness**.

They delight in serving him and do not covet an earthly reward.

The blessings they seek will remain theirs forever.
