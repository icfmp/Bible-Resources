## Christ is the eternal and divine Word (verses 1–5)

Jesus is called **the Word** because, just as our speech makes our thoughts known to other people, the Son of God came to reveal **his Father's mind** to the world.

John plainly affirms that Christ is **God**.

He existed **in the beginning** and was **with God**.

Everything that has been created was made **by him**—not merely through him as though he were an instrument subordinate to the true Creator.

Without him nothing came into existence, from the greatest angel to the smallest worm.

This shows his complete fitness for the work of our redemption and salvation.

Both the light of reason and the life of every living creature come from Christ and continue to depend on him.

The eternal Word shines as the true Light, yet the darkness does not understand or receive him.

Let us pray continually for eyes opened to this Light, that we may walk in it and become **wise for salvation through faith in Jesus Christ**.

## The Word enters the world as a human being (verses 6–14)

John the Baptist came as a witness to Jesus.

It shows how dark the human mind has become that even when the Light appeared, someone had to call people's attention to it.

Christ is **the true Light** in the fullest sense.

Every person brought into saving light receives it through **his Spirit and grace**.

Those who remain unenlightened by him perish in spiritual darkness.

When Jesus took on our human nature and lived among us, the Son of the Most High entered this lower world.

He was **in the world but not of it**.

He came to save a lost world that he himself had created.

Yet the world did not recognize its Maker.

When he returns as Judge, the world will be forced to recognize him.

Many claim to belong to Christ while refusing to receive him, because they will not part with their sins or allow him to rule them.

**All God's children have been born again.**

The new birth comes through **God's word as the means** (**1 Peter 1:23**) and **God's Spirit as the Author**.

Christ was always present in the world through his divine nature.

But in the fullness of time he came in a new way: **God manifested in the flesh**.

His divine glory shone through the veil of humanity.

Ordinarily, the people who know someone best discover their weaknesses.

It was the opposite with Jesus: **those closest to him saw the most of his glory**.

Though he outwardly took the form of a servant, his character was full of grace and truth.

His holy teaching and miracles revealed the glory of the Son of God.

He was fully pleasing to his Father and therefore fitted to plead for sinners; he also perfectly knew the truth he had come to reveal.

## John bears witness to Christ's fullness (verses 15–18)

Christ entered public ministry after John, but in every deeper sense **he existed before John**.

Jesus was alive before appearing on earth as a man.

In him dwells **all fullness**.

Through faith, fallen sinners receive from him everything that makes them wise, strong, holy, useful and happy.

All the blessings we receive through Christ can be described with one word: **grace**.

Grace includes both **God's goodwill toward us and God's good work within us**.

God's law is holy, just and good, and we must use it rightly.

But it cannot provide **pardon, righteousness or power to obey**.

It teaches us how to live in a way that honors God's saving truth, but it cannot replace the gospel.

Every mercy coming to sinners comes through Jesus Christ.

No one can come to the Father except through him.

No one can truly know God except as the Father is revealed in **his only-begotten and beloved Son**.

## John explains his own role (verses 19–28)

John refused to accept the title **Messiah**.

He came in the **spirit and power of Elijah**, but he was not Elijah himself.

He was also not the prophet they expected Moses to have promised: someone who, according to their mistaken hopes, would free them from Roman rule.

Instead, John described his mission in a way that urged people to listen and prepare.

He baptized with **water as a profession of repentance** and as an outward sign of the spiritual blessings the coming Messiah would give.

The Messiah was already among them, though they did not recognize him.

John said he was unworthy to perform even the lowest service for Jesus.

## John calls Jesus the Lamb of God (verses 29–36)

When John saw Jesus, he pointed him out as **the Lamb of God**.

The Passover lamb—with its shed and sprinkled blood, its roasted flesh and the meal in which it was eaten—pictured the salvation of sinners **through faith in Christ**.

Likewise, the lambs offered **morning and evening** pointed toward Christ, who was sacrificed to redeem us to God by his blood.

John preached repentance.

Yet he taught his listeners to seek **forgiveness of sins only through Jesus and his death**.

It accords with God's glory to pardon everyone who depends upon Christ's atoning sacrifice.

Jesus **takes away the sin of the world** and has purchased pardon for those who repent and believe the gospel.

That should encourage us personally: if he takes away the world's sin, **why not my sin?**

He bore our sin **for us**, and so takes it **from us**.

God could have removed sin simply by destroying sinners, as he did in the old world.

Instead, he found a way to remove the guilt of sin while sparing the sinner, by making his Son **sin for us—that is, a sin offering**.

Seeing Jesus take away sin should teach us to hate it and resolve to abandon it.

We must not cling to what the Lamb of God came to remove.

John also testified to the sign at Jesus' baptism, when God bore witness to his Son.

He declared Jesus to be **the Son of God and the promised Messiah**.

John used every opportunity to lead people toward Christ.

## Andrew and another disciple follow Jesus (verses 37–42)

What most strongly moves an awakened soul to follow Jesus is recognizing that **he alone takes away sin**.

Whatever fellowship takes place between Christ and our souls, **he begins the conversation**.

He asked the disciples, **“What are you seeking?”**

We should ask ourselves the same question when we begin following him.

What do we truly desire?

Are we seeking God's favor and eternal life?

Jesus invited them to come without delay.

**Now is the accepted time** (**2 Corinthians 6:2**).

It is good to be where Christ is, whatever that place may be.

We should care for the spiritual welfare of our relatives and seek to bring them to him.

Those who come to Jesus should come with a settled resolution to remain faithful and steadfast, like **a firm stone**.

Only his grace makes such perseverance possible.

## Philip and Nathanael follow Jesus (verses 43–51)

True Christianity is **following Jesus**, devoting ourselves to him and walking in his footsteps.

Nathanael initially objected because of Jesus' home town.

Those who want to benefit from God's word must beware of prejudices against **places and groups of people**.

We should examine things for ourselves.

Often we discover good where we expected none.

Many people remain away from the path of faith because of unreasonable assumptions.

The best response to such prejudice is often simply **“Come and see.”**

Nathanael was **an Israelite without deceit**.

He was sincere rather than hypocritical, upright and godly.

Jesus knows what we really are.

Do we desire him to know us as genuine Christians?

Even true believers have weaknesses, imperfections and sins, but deliberate hypocrisy does not characterize the believer's heart.

Jesus knew what had happened when Nathanael was under the fig tree.

Henry says he was **probably praying earnestly** for the hope and consolation of Israel, unseen by human eyes.

This was evidence that Jesus knew the secrets of his heart.

Through Christ we enjoy fellowship with, and receive benefit from, the holy angels.

He unites and reconciles the things of heaven and earth.
