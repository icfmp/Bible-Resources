# Lamentations 2 — Modern English

## Original heading

**Lamentation for the misery of Jerusalem.**

## God withdraws protection from Jerusalem (verses 1–9)

The passage gives a sorrowful picture of God's church, Jacob and Israel.

It particularly directs attention to **the Lord's hand in the people's suffering**.

Yet God does not become an enemy of his people simply because he is angry with them and corrects them.

When he withdraws protection, **city gates and iron bars can provide no security**.

It is right for God to humble people through judgment when they have degraded themselves through sin.

Those who fail to value or observe his Sabbaths and appointed worship may justly lose the comfort and benefit of them.

What use do people make of **Bibles** if they refuse to learn from them?

Those who misuse God's prophets may rightly lose them.

It can be painful to direct suffering people's attention to their own wrongdoing and God's judgment.

Nevertheless, that recognition may be necessary because their sins are the source of their miseries.

## The terrible suffering and a call to prayer (verses 10–22)

There were many reasons for mourning.

Countless people died through famine.

Even children were killed by their mothers and eaten, in fulfillment of the dreadful warning of **Deuteronomy 28:53**.

Many more died by the sword.

False prophets deceived the people.

Their neighbors mocked them.

**Mocking another person's suffering is a serious sin and adds to that suffering.**

Their enemies celebrated their defeat.

Yet those hostile to God's church often mistake its severe setbacks for its final destruction.

They will discover their error.

The suffering people are called to lament, and to seek comfort for their grief.

**Prayer is a remedy for every wound, even the deepest and most painful.**

In prayer we bring our circumstances to the Lord and entrust them to him.

**May his will be done.**

Let us fear God, walk humbly before him and take care that we do not fall.
