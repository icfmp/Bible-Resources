# Acts 11 — Modern English

## Peter explains the welcome of Gentiles (verses 1–18)

The weakness of fallen human nature appears even in godly people when they are displeased to hear that **others have received God's word**, merely because the Lord did not work according to their own religious expectations.

We too often assume that certain people cannot be helped, even though they may prove eager to learn when given an opportunity.

It seriously harms the church to exclude people from fellowship and God's appointed means of grace **because they are not like us in every respect**.

Peter carefully explained what had happened.

We should make allowance for the weaknesses of fellow believers.

Rather than becoming offended or responding angrily, explain our motives and show what we have done and why.

Preaching through which God grants the **Holy Spirit** plainly has divine approval.

Those deeply devoted to their own religious regulations should take care **not to oppose God**.

People who love the Lord will glorify him when they learn that he has granted repentance leading to life to fellow sinners.

Repentance itself is **God's gift**.

His mercy does not merely accept repentance when offered. **His mighty grace produces it within us**.

He removes a heart of stone and gives a tender heart of flesh.

A broken and contrite spirit is the sacrifice God receives.

## The gospel spreads in Antioch (verses 19–24)

The first witnesses who brought the gospel to Antioch had been scattered from Jerusalem through persecution.

What was intended to damage the church became the means of blessing it.

**Human hostility was overruled to bring praise to God**.

What should Christ's ministers preach but **Christ crucified and glorified**?

Their preaching was accompanied by divine power.

Ministers could only speak to the outward ears of listeners; **the Lord himself reached hearts and consciences**.

People believed the gospel's truth and turned from a careless, worldly way of life toward holiness and spiritual-mindedness.

They turned from empty ceremonies to worship **in spirit and truth**.

Above all, they turned to **the Lord Jesus**, who became everything to them.

This is the work of conversion that must take place in every one of us.

True faith produces this turning to the Lord.

When Jesus is preached simply and according to Scripture, he grants success.

Sincere believers like **Barnabas**, filled with faith and the Spirit, rejoice in grace working in others.

Barnabas possessed both **the grace of faith and the fruits of faith working through love**.

## Believers are called Christians and send relief (verses 25–30)

Until then Jesus' followers had commonly been called **disciples**, meaning learners.

They now began to be called **Christians**.

The name properly describes someone who, after serious consideration, follows Christ, believes his promises and makes obedience to his teaching and example the principal concern of life.

Many people bear the title **Christian** although it does not truly describe them.

The name without the reality increases guilt rather than granting benefit.

But genuine faith carries the promises of **this life and the life to come**.

Lord, help Christians set aside needless labels and divisions and love one another as Christ's followers should.

Genuine believers care for brothers and sisters experiencing hardship.

Such compassion bears fruit to God's praise.

If everyone in the world lived as a true Christian, how joyfully would people help one another!

The earth would resemble one great family whose members sought to be kind and dutiful toward one another.
