# Psalm 103 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An exhortation to bless God for his mercy. | 1–5 |
| And to the church and to all men. | 6–14 |
| For the constancy of his mercy. | 15–18 |
| For the government of the world. | 19–22 |

## Bless the Lord for forgiveness and healing (verses 1–5)

**When God forgives sin**, he removes the obstacle that kept good things from us and restores us to his favor.

Think how much our sins have provoked him—yet they are pardoned.

So many provocations, yet all forgiven!

As we continue to sin and repent, God continues to forgive.

Because of Adam's fall, our bodies experience weakness and disease, and our souls suffer their own infirmities.

**Christ alone forgives all our sins and heals all our infirmities**.

The person who has experienced healing from the disease of sin has good reason to be assured of forgiveness.

When God's Spirit renews his people's weakened faith, granting grace, comfort, fresh life and joy as a pledge of eternal life, their strength seems restored **like the vigor of youth** (**Job 33:25**).

## The compassion of our heavenly Father (verses 6–14)

God is good to all, but he has shown particular favor to Israel through revealing himself and his grace.

His **ways** include the commandments he calls us to obey, along with his promises and purposes.

He has always been full of compassion.

How unlike God are those who seize every excuse to rebuke someone and never know when to stop!

What would become of us if God treated us that way?

Scripture speaks constantly of his mercy, and we have all experienced it.

A father has compassion on his children in many situations.

When they lack understanding, **he teaches**.

When they are stubborn, **he bears patiently**.

When they are sick, **he comforts**.

When they fall, **he helps them rise**.

When they do wrong and submit, **he forgives**.

When they are wronged, **he sets things right**.

This is how the Lord takes pity on those who fear him.

He remembers how frail our bodies are and how foolish our souls can be.

He knows **how little we can accomplish and how little we can endure**.

His compassion takes account of all these limitations.

## God's faithful mercy outlasts life (verses 15–18)

Human life is brief and uncertain.

A garden flower may last longer because its wall and gardener shelter it.

But a **flower growing in an open field** is exposed not only to its own decay, but also to cold weather and animals that may cut or trample it.

That is our condition.

God knows our frailty and has compassion on us.

We should remember it ourselves.

His mercy is **better than life**, because it will last longer than life.

His righteousness and his faithful promises remain blessings to **children and grandchildren who follow their faithful ancestors in godliness**.

## The Lord reigns over everything (verses 19–22)

The One who made everything also rules everything, accomplishing both through his powerful word.

He directs people and events toward his glory.

Holy angels continually praise him.

Let all his works praise him.

Unceasingly delighting in God would have been natural to us if humanity had not fallen.

Through **new birth**, such delight begins to return.

In heaven it will be perfect forever.

We cannot be completely happy until we find **unwearied joy in perfectly obeying God's will**.

Let every redeemed heart say, **Bless the Lord, O my soul!**
