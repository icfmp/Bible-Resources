# 1 Samuel 21 — Modern English

## David visits Ahimelech (verses 1–9)

In distress, David fled to God's tabernacle.

It is a great comfort in trouble to have a God to whom we can go, explain our circumstances and ask confidently for guidance.

But David told Ahimelech a serious lie.

How should we regard this?

Scripture does not conceal it, and **we must not defend it**.

David did wrong, and the consequences were dreadful: his lie contributed to the killing of the Lord's priests.

Later, he remembered the deed with regret.

David had shown strong faith and courage before, but both failed him here.

Fear and weak trust led him into disgraceful wrongdoing.

Had he relied properly on God, he would not have resorted to such a sinful method to preserve himself.

The record is given as a warning, **not permission to imitate him—even under the greatest pressure**.

David asked Ahimelech for food and a sword.

The priest considered it lawful for David and his companions to eat the bread of the Presence.

Christ, the Son of David, later used this incident to teach that **mercy takes precedence over sacrifice**, and ceremonial restrictions must yield to moral duty.

**Doeg** entered the sanctuary just as David did.

We cannot tell merely by someone's attendance at public worship what is in that person's heart.

Some come sincerely to serve God.

Others pretend to worship while observing teachers and gathering accusations against them.

Only God, and later events, can distinguish David from Doeg when both are present in the tabernacle.

## David pretends to be insane at Gath (verses 10–15)

Persecuted believers have sometimes been treated more kindly by Philistines than by Israelites.

David had reason to trust **Achish**.

Yet fear overcame him.

His behavior was humiliating and revealed that his courage and faith were wavering.

The more simply we trust and obey God, the more safely and peacefully we can make our way through this troubled world.
