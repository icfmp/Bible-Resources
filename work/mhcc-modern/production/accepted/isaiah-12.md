# Isaiah 12 — Modern English

## Original heading

**This is a hymn of praise suited to the times of the Messiah.**

## A song of salvation and thanksgiving

This song would be fitting when Israel's exiles finally returned from their long captivity.

It is especially fitting when **a sinner first discovers peace and joy through faith**.

It also belongs to believers whose peace is renewed after God's correction for past wandering, and to **all the redeemed together before God's throne in heaven**.

The promise is certain and its blessings abundant.

Everything received through Jesus Christ calls us to wholehearted thanksgiving.

Through **Jesus Christ, the Root of Jesse**, divine anger was turned away, because **he is our Peace**.

Those whom God has reconciled to himself receive his comfort.

They learn to rejoice in him and in the assurance that they belong to him.

Let the believer say: I will trust God to prepare me for his salvation and keep me until its full realization.

I will entrust every concern to him, confident that he can make all things work together for good.

**Faith in God is a powerful remedy against tormenting fears.**

Many Christians know God as their **strength** but fail to make him their **song**.

They remain in spiritual darkness and miss the joy that should accompany their confidence.

Those who rely upon his strength ought to praise him for it, giving him the glory and accepting the comfort.

Salvation arises from **God the Father's love**, reaches us **through God the Son**, and is applied by **the renewing power of God the Spirit**.

When faith sees this, the frightened sinner learns to hope in God and loses the fear that formerly enslaved the soul.

The purifying and sanctifying work of the Holy Spirit is often pictured as **water springing from a fountain**.

This grace comes through **Christ's mediation** and is conveyed to our souls through **God's appointed means of worship and instruction**.

Praise God that **wells of salvation** are open on every side, from which we may draw living water and consolation.

In the latter part of the song (**verses 4–6**), believers urge one another to praise the Lord and invite still more people to join them.

Disagreements about **dates, seasons and other secondary matters** must not divide Christian hearts.

Our deepest concern should be to belong among those to whom Christ will finally say:

**“Come, you who are blessed by my Father; receive the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world.”**
