# Leviticus 10 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Nadab and Abihu offer unauthorized fire (verses 1–2)

After Moses and Aaron, few men in Israel seemed more likely to receive honor than Nadab and Abihu.

Henry considers it probable that they had become proud and had drunk wine.

While the people bowed down before the Lord in worship, the two men rushed into the tabernacle to burn incense.

They did so at the wrong time, both together rather than one at a time, and used fire that had not come from the altar.

If the offense had been committed unintentionally, they could have brought a sin offering.

But they acted presumptuously, showing contempt for God's majesty and justice.

Such deliberate defiance was to be punished by death.

They died while committing their sin.

This judgment exposed the incompleteness of the Aaronic priesthood from its very beginning.

It could not protect anyone from the fire of God's wrath, except insofar as it foreshadowed the priesthood of Christ.

## Aaron responds to the death of his sons (verses 3–7)

The most comforting thoughts in suffering come from God's word.

Aaron had good reason to feel overwhelmed with grief and alarm.

Yet by remaining silent in submission, he showed reverence for God's righteous judgment.

When God corrects us or our families for sin, we should receive his correction and confess that he is the Lord and may do what seems good to him.

Whenever believers worship, they come near to God as spiritual priests.

We should therefore approach every act of worship seriously, remembering the holiness of the One before whom we stand.

God judges people who treat his sacred name carelessly or make light of his worship.

## Priests must not drink while ministering (verses 8–11)

Aaron and his sons were forbidden to drink wine or strong drink while they served in the tabernacle.

Ministers of the gospel likewise must not be given to wine (1 Timothy 3:3).

A person may die suddenly, even while drunk.

Since death can overtake us at any time, we should practice sobriety.

## Eating the holy offerings (verses 12–20)

Suffering should normally make us more diligent in carrying out our duties, not draw us away from them.

Nevertheless, God makes allowance when a person is genuinely unable to carry out a duty because of a natural, non-sinful weakness.

He desires mercy and not sacrifice.

Let this sad history warn us.

Some people approach worship with great zeal but little understanding, governed by fleshly feelings, superficial thoughts and human inventions.

Their worship may be outwardly impressive, but it is not the inward devotion of soul and spirit.

Their incense is then lit by a fire that did not descend from heaven—a fire the Holy Spirit never kindled in their hearts.
