# Psalm 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Concerning the kingdom of Christ, and the subjects of that kingdom. | 1–6 |
| Concerning the King of that kingdom. | 7–10 |

## The kingdom and its people (verses 1–6)

We do not belong to ourselves. **Our bodies and souls belong to God**.

Even people who neither know him nor acknowledge their relationship to him still belong to him.

Anyone who understands that the soul will exist forever, after surveying all the earth and its possessions, will recognize that worldly things cannot satisfy it.

Such a person will begin to desire to approach God and ask: **What must I do to live in the happy and holy place where God makes his people holy and happy?**

Religion is worthless if it never becomes **a matter of the heart**.

Only **Christ's blood** can cleanse our sins, and only the **Holy Spirit's washing** can renew us in holiness.

Through this work we become God's people and receive blessing from the Lord and righteousness from the God of our salvation.

God will make those who truly belong to him happy, completely and forever.

When God grants righteousness, his intention is also **salvation**.

Those whom he prepares for heaven will reach heaven safely and find what their souls have been seeking.

## The King of glory (verses 7–10)

The magnificent entrance described here first refers to the solemn arrival of the ark in the tent David prepared for it, or in Solomon's later temple.

Henry also applies it to **Christ's ascension into heaven** and the welcome he received there.

Our Redeemer found heaven's gates closed because of sin, but having made atonement by his own blood, he could demand entry **with full authority**.

The angels were commanded to worship him (**Hebrews 1:6**).

They ask in wonder who this King is, and the answer declares that he is mighty in battle, powerful to save his people and subdue his and their enemies.

The psalm may also be applied to Christ entering human hearts through **his word and Spirit**, making them his temples.

He stands at the door and knocks (**Revelation 3:20**).

The heart's gates should open to him as a property is delivered to its rightful owner.

The psalm can also point us toward **his glorious second coming**.

Lord, open the everlasting door of our souls by your grace. May we receive you now and belong wholly to you, and finally be numbered with your saints in glory.
