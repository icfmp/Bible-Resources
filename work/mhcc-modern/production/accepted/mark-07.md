# Mark 7 — Modern English

## God's commands and human traditions (verses 1–13)

One great purpose of Jesus' coming was to set aside **the ceremonial law**.

To prepare for that change, he exposed the **human ceremonies added to God's law**.

The clean hands and pure heart Christ gives his disciples are very different from the outward, superstitious practices of Pharisees in every age.

Jesus reproved religious leaders for rejecting God's command in the name of their customs.

Children whose parents are poor must help them **as far as they are able**.

If it was a capital offense under the law to curse parents, how much more terrible is leaving them to starve!

Yet the Pharisees developed a religious excuse allowing people to escape this duty while appearing devout.

## What truly makes a person unclean (verses 14–23)

It is our **evil thoughts, sinful desires, words and actions** that defile us.

A polluted spring sends out polluted streams.

So a corrupt heart produces corrupt reasoning, appetites, passions, speech and conduct.

Understanding the law spiritually and recognizing the evil of sin should move us to seek **the Holy Spirit's grace**.

Only he can restrain and overcome the corrupt thoughts and affections working within us.

## Jesus heals the Canaanite woman's daughter (verses 24–30)

Christ never turned away someone who sincerely fell at his feet seeking mercy.

Even a trembling and poor sinner may approach him.

This woman was not only personally faithful but also **a caring mother**.

Her love for her daughter led her to Jesus.

Christ's reply about children being fed first indicated that mercy for the **Gentiles was approaching**, not that it would be forever refused.

The woman did not speak lightly of God's mercy.

Instead, she recognized how abundant the miracles among Israel had been and asked for one cure as **a crumb beside that abundance**.

The Savior left proud Pharisees resisting him but showed compassion to a humble sinner who looked to him for the children's bread.

He continues seeking and saving the lost.

## Jesus restores hearing and speech (verses 31–37)

People brought a man unable to hear or speak to Jesus and asked him to consider his suffering and use his healing power.

Christ used **more visible actions than usual** when healing this man.

They served as signs of **his power** and encouraged the man's faith and that of his friends.

The people who came to Jesus had different needs and received help in different ways.

Yet everyone who truly approached him for mercy received what was needed.

The same is true in **the central concerns of the soul**.
