# Hebrews 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A comparison between the priesthood of Melchizedec and that of Christ. | 1–3 |
| The excellence of Christ's priesthood above the Levitical priesthood is shown. | 4–10 |
| This is applied to Christ. | 11–25 |
| The faith and hope of the church encouraged from this. | 26–28 |

## Melchizedek foreshadows Christ (verses 1–3)

Melchizedek met Abraham as he returned from rescuing Lot.

His name means **King of Righteousness**, expressing a character that made him a figure of the Messiah and his kingdom.

The name of his city means **Peace**. As king of peace, he prefigured **Christ the Prince of Peace**, who reconciles humanity to God.

Scripture does **not record the beginning or end of Melchizedek's life**. Through this silence, he represents the Son of God, whose existence is eternal and whose priesthood has **no predecessor or successor**.

Scripture everywhere honors the great King of Righteousness and Peace, our glorious High Priest and Savior.

The more carefully we study it, the more we discover that **the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy**.

## A priesthood greater than Levi's (verses 4–10)

Melchizedek's greatness shows that the promised High Priest whom he represented must be **greater than the Levitical priests**.

Abraham's highest privilege was possessing **God's promises**.

Anyone who receives these promises for present and eternal life is truly rich and blessed.

Everyone who receives Jesus Christ enjoys that honor.

Let us engage in spiritual struggles trusting his word and strength, credit our victories to his grace and seek his blessing in all our ways.

## Jesus, priest of an everlasting covenant (verses 11–25)

The Levitical priesthood and its law **could not bring perfection**; they give way to a new priesthood through which believers may be made complete.

The law itself revealed that Levitical priests were **frail and mortal**. They could not preserve even their own earthly lives, much less save others' souls.

But Jesus holds his office through **the power of endless life within himself**.

He can preserve his own life and grant spiritual and eternal life to everyone trusting his sacrifice and intercession.

The **better covenant** for which Jesus is Surety is contrasted here **not with the covenant of works**, which condemns transgressors, but with **the covenant at Sinai and its legal dispensation**.

The new covenant gives Christ's church clearer knowledge, fuller liberty and richer privileges.

Aaron's order required many priests and successive high priests, but **Christ is the one everlasting High Priest**.

He is therefore able to **save completely, at every time and in every circumstance**.

Since our privileges exceed those of believers under the old dispensation, we should desire deeper spirituality and holiness.

## The pure and sufficient High Priest (verses 26–28)

Christ's holiness is complete.

His very nature contains **no habit, principle, tendency or desire toward sin**. Even the best Christians still experience sinful inclinations, but none existed in him.

He is harmless and free from every actual wrong: **he committed no violence, and no deceit was found in his mouth**.

He is entirely undefiled.

We may struggle even to avoid being implicated in others' sins, but Jesus is free from every such stain.

Therefore no one who comes to God **in the name of his beloved Son** need despair.

Jesus will deliver his people in temptation, suffering, prosperity, death and final judgment.
