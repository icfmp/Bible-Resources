# 1 Corinthians 14 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Prophecy preferred to the gift of tongues. | 1–5 |
| The unprofitableness of speaking in unknown languages. | 6–14 |
| Exhortations to worship that can be understood. | 15–25 |
| Disorders from vain display of gifts; | 26–33 |
| and from women speaking in the church. | 34–40 |

## Explaining Scripture is more useful than uninterpreted languages (verses 1–5)

Henry understands **prophesying** in this passage as **explaining Scripture**.

Paul compared it with speaking in other languages.

Uninterpreted tongues attracted more attention and more readily gratified spiritual pride.

But they did **less to accomplish Christian love's purpose**, because they did less to benefit hearers' souls.

**No one can be spiritually built up by words they do not understand**.

Even the finest teaching accomplishes no good among hearers who cannot speak or understand the language being used.

The value of every ability or possession depends upon **how useful it is**.

Even sincere religious feeling must be guided by **understanding**, or it may dishonor the very truths people claim to promote.

## Worship without intelligible speech cannot edify (verses 6–14)

Even an apostle could not build up the church if his words were incomprehensible to his hearers.

Speech that communicates no meaning to its audience is **like speaking into the air**.

The speaker and hearers become foreigners to one another.

The purpose of speech has not been fulfilled.

Christian gatherings should conduct **every act of worship** in a way that allows everyone to join in and receive spiritual benefit.

**Plain, readily understood language is most appropriate for public worship and religious teaching**.

A true follower of Christ should care far more about **helping others** than about earning a reputation for brilliant scholarship or eloquent speaking.

## Speak and pray so that hearers understand (verses 15–25)

People cannot sincerely agree with prayers **whose meaning they cannot understand**.

A faithful gospel minister is far more concerned to benefit other people's souls than to earn public admiration.

That concern shows the minister is **Christ's servant**.

Children enjoy novelty and unusual displays.

Believers should not imitate that weakness.

We should be like children **without malice or deceit**, yet mature in our understanding of God's righteousness and ignorant only of **the techniques of wrongdoing**.

Henry regards it as a fearful judgment when people are led by teachers who force them to worship **in an unfamiliar language**.

No benefit can come from instruction that is not understood.

If unbelieving visitors enter a church and hear unintelligible preaching or prayer, they may conclude Christianity is absurd.

But when Scripture is clearly explained and the great truths and duties of the gospel are proclaimed, even an unbeliever or someone without formal education may be **converted**.

God's word may awaken conscience, expose secret sins and move that person to acknowledge their guilt and confess that **God truly is present among his people**.

Scripture, when clearly and faithfully taught, has extraordinary power to touch the heart.

## Orderly worship should build up the whole congregation (verses 26–33)

Public worship should always be governed by one purpose: **“Let everything be done for building up.”**

If someone speaks in a language unknown to the congregation, another person must be able to interpret it.

Then two miraculous gifts can work together to build up the church and confirm the hearers' faith.

Those explaining Scripture should also speak **in an orderly sequence**.

Two or three may address a meeting, but **not simultaneously**.

Someone genuinely inspired by the Spirit of God will show **decency and order** in delivering what God has revealed.

God never teaches us to neglect our duties or behave in ways unworthy of our age and responsibilities.

## Conduct in worship and apostolic instruction (verses 34–40)

Paul urged believing women to seek answers about religious matters from their husbands at home.

Henry draws the lesson that **Christian families should gather for spiritual conversation and growth in knowledge**.

The Spirit of Christ **cannot contradict himself**.

Claims of spiritual revelation that contradict the apostle's instruction cannot come from that same Spirit.

Churches preserve **peace, truth and order** when they seek what benefits the congregation, are patient with what does not harm its welfare, and maintain **good conduct, decency and order**.
