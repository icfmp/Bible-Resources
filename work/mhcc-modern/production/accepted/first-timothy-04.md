# 1 Timothy 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Of departures from the faith that began already to appear. | 1–5 |
| Several directions, with motives for due discharge of duties. | 6–16 |

## Reject false restrictions and receive God's gifts with thanks (verses 1–5)

Through the Holy Spirit, Scripture foretold **a widespread departure from true faith and from the pure worship of God**.

This would occur during **the Christian era**, described as the last days.

Some false teachers declare evil **what God has permitted**.

Others command as necessary duties **what he has left to human freedom**.

God has given us more than enough opportunity for watchfulness, self-denial and obedience through **his actual commandments**.

We do not need invented duties that forbid the things he has allowed.

Nevertheless, permission never justifies **immoderate or improper use of God's gifts**.

Nothing we receive will truly benefit us unless we seek **his blessing upon it through prayer**.

## Godliness is more valuable than outward self-denial (verses 6–10)

External forms of restraint have only limited value.

What good is it to discipline the body while failing to **put sin itself to death**?

The hardest work performed merely in outward practices cannot make a person holy.

The great value of godliness rests partly upon **God's promises**.

Those promises include blessings in this present life, but **especially in the life to come**.

When we lose something for Christ's sake, **we will not ultimately lose through belonging to Christ**.

Christ is described as **Savior of all people**.

How much more will he reward those who sincerely **seek him and serve him**!

He will provide what is truly good for those whom he has made **new creations**.

## Teach by doctrine and example, even when young (verses 11–16)

Young people's age will not make them contemptible if they avoid **vanity, foolish behavior and corrupt habits**.

Those who teach others through doctrine must also **teach through their lives**.

Their words should build up believers, and their conduct should be holy.

They should be examples of **love for God and for faithful people**, and of **heavenly-mindedness**.

Ministers ought to regard these responsibilities as **their principal calling and work**.

Through such diligence, their progress will become visible, benefiting both themselves and other people.

This is the path toward growing **in knowledge and grace** while helping others grow as well.

A minister's doctrine must be **biblical, clear, centered on the gospel and practical**.

It should be carefully **stated, explained, defended and applied**.

Such responsibilities leave little time for pursuing worldly pleasures, pointless social visits, frivolous conversation or purely decorative amusements.

May every believer also grow in a way that **others can recognize**.

May each seek to experience **the gospel's power within the soul** and produce its fruit in daily conduct.
