# Jeremiah 35 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The obedience of the Rechabites. | 1–11 |
| The Jews' disobedience to the Lord. | 12–19 |

## The Rechabites obey their ancestor (verses 1–11)

**Jonadab** was known for wisdom and godliness. He had lived almost **three hundred years earlier**; his life is also mentioned in **2 Kings 10:15**.

Jonadab had instructed his descendants **not to drink wine**.

He also directed them to live in **tents or movable dwellings**, which would teach them not to regard any earthly place as their permanent home.

Remaining humble and content was the safest way for them to continue living in the land as strangers.

**Humility and contentment** are always wise and are often the best protection people can have.

Jonadab wanted his descendants to avoid unlawful pleasures. He therefore taught them to deny themselves **even certain pleasures that were lawful in themselves**.

Since we are strangers and pilgrims in the world, we should abstain from sinful bodily desires.

Those with little to lose will fear times of loss less. People who do not cling tightly to possessions will find it easier to surrender them if they are taken away.

Those best prepared to endure hardship practice **self-denial** and regard worldly vanities as unimportant.

The descendants of Jonadab faithfully followed the rules he had given them.

They made exceptions only where ordinary precautions were necessary for their safety during a time of widespread danger.

## Their obedience exposes Judah's disobedience (verses 12–19)

The Rechabites' faithfulness was tested as a **sign**.

Their obedience highlighted the much greater disobedience of the Jews toward God.

The Rechabites had obeyed a man who was only a human being like themselves.

Jonadab had never done for his descendants what **God had done for his people**.

The Lord promised blessing to the Rechabites.

Scripture does not report **how that promise was fulfilled**. Henry expressed confidence that it was fulfilled and noted reports by travelers that a distinct Rechabite people could still be found **in his own era**. That report is a historical claim of the commentator, not an independently established identification.

Let us heed the godly advice of faithful predecessors. It is good to follow their wise counsel.
