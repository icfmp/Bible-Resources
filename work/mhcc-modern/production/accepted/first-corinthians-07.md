# 1 Corinthians 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle answers several questions about marriage. | 1–9 |
| Married Christians should not seek to part from their unbelieving consorts. | 10–16 |
| Persons, in any fixed station, should usually abide in that. | 17–24 |
| It was most desirable, on account of the then perilous days, for people to sit loose to this world. | 25–35 |
| Great prudence be used in marriage; it should be only in the Lord. | 36–40 |

## Marriage and remaining unmarried (verses 1–9)

Paul answered questions the Corinthians had raised about marriage.

He considered it good for some Christians to remain unmarried **under the particular circumstances of that time**.

Yet marriage and its blessings have been established by **God's wisdom**.

No one may break his law. Within its perfect requirements, however, God grants freedom to serve him in ways suited to people's different abilities and circumstances.

**Other people are often poor judges of these circumstances.**

Each Christian should consider personal responsibilities and seek God's counsel before deciding.

## Marriage to an unbelieving spouse (verses 10–16)

A husband and wife should not separate except on grounds **Christ permits**.

Divorce was common among Jews and Gentiles in Paul's time, sometimes for the smallest reasons.

But marriage is **God's institution**, appointed as a lasting commitment.

We are to pursue peace with all people, insofar as it depends upon us (**Romans 12:18**).

How much more should we seek the comfort and peace of **those closest to us**, even if a spouse does not share Christian faith!

Married people should make it a serious aim to promote each other's happiness.

Why should someone professing Christ abandon a husband or wife when that relationship offers a particularly important opportunity to show genuine love?

**Stay and work earnestly for the conversion of your spouse**, where remaining in the marriage is the duty God has given.

In every calling and relationship, God has called believers **to peace**.

Do everything possible to maintain harmony, as far as **truth and holiness permit**.

## Serve Christ faithfully in your present condition (verses 17–24)

Christianity reaches people in **every social position and circumstance**.

Someone can honor the gospel through faithful conduct in any lawful occupation or place.

Christians should be content with the position assigned to them and behave in a way that reflects their faith.

**Our happiness depends on what we are in relation to Christ, not what we are in the world's eyes**.

Faith must not be used as an excuse to abandon **natural or civil responsibilities**.

Normally believers should carry out their existing duties quietly and contentedly, trusting divine Providence.

## Live with eternal priorities in troubled times (verses 25–35)

Considering the troubles facing believers at that time, remaining unmarried could be **preferable**.

Paul did not thereby condemn marriage.

How contrary to his instruction are those who **forbid marriage** to large groups of people or bind them with vows of celibacy regardless of whether this calling suits them!

Paul encouraged Christians to hold the things of this world loosely.

**In personal relationships**, do not set your heart entirely on their comforts.

**In suffering**, do not let worldly grief dominate, because joy in God remains possible even amid sorrow.

**In earthly pleasures**, remember that they are not your final rest.

**In business**, even when trade prospers and wealth increases, hold possessions as though they were not your ultimate property or hope.

**In every worldly concern**, keep the world from ruling your heart, so you will not misuse its gifts when they are placed in your hands.

All earthly things are temporary appearances.

Nothing here lasts.

Prudent attention to practical responsibilities is a duty.

But **anxious and overwhelming preoccupation** with them is wrong.

The best calling for a person is the one that **most benefits that person's soul** and best guards against worldly traps and distractions.

Examine the advantages and dangers of your circumstances. Use the former wisely and avoid the latter as much as possible.

Whatever legitimate responsibilities occupy you, always preserve **time for the Lord's concerns**.

## Wise decisions about marriage and widowhood (verses 36–40)

Henry explains that Paul is **understood by some** to be discussing the responsibility of parents when arranging marriages for their children.

On this reading, children should seek and normally follow parental guidance in choosing a spouse.

But parents must also consult their children's wishes.

Parents do **not** have unlimited power to dictate such a lifelong decision without good reasons or regard for their children.

Paul concludes with advice to widows.

**Remarriage is not unlawful**, provided it is undertaken **in the Lord**.

In choosing close relationships and considering a change in circumstances, we should be governed by **reverence for God, his commands and dependence upon his Providence**.

A change of life should follow careful thought and a reasonable expectation that it will **help rather than harm spiritual concerns**.
