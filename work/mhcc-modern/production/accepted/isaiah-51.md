# Isaiah 51 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Exhortations to trust the Messiah. | 1–3 |
| The power of God, and the weakness of man. | 4–8 |
| Christ defends his people. | 9–16 |
| Their afflictions and deliverances. | 17–23 |

## Remember God's grace and trust the Messiah (verses 1–3)

People blessed with **new birth** should remember that they were originally **formed in sin**.

This remembrance humbles our opinion of ourselves and deepens our appreciation of God's grace.

There is no greater comfort than becoming **useful for God's glory**.

The more people grow in holiness and do good, the greater their genuine happiness becomes.

Let us reflect seriously upon our guilt.

Such reflection keeps our hearts humble and our consciences **awake and sensitive**.

It also makes **Jesus Christ more precious** to us.

Remembering grace strengthens our efforts and prayers for other people's good.

## God's salvation outlasts everything earthly (verses 4–8)

Christ's gospel will be proclaimed and made known.

How will we escape if we neglect it?

There is **no salvation without righteousness**.

Our earthly existence will pass away like smoke.

Our bodies will be laid aside like worn-out clothing.

But people whose happiness rests upon **Christ's righteousness and salvation** will still enjoy its comfort when time and days themselves have ended.

Clouds may hide the sun for a while, but they cannot stop its course.

Likewise, the believer will continue to enjoy the portion God has promised, while those who mock Christ remain in darkness.

## The Redeemer defends and comforts his people (verses 9–16)

Those whom Christ redeemed **with his blood and by his power** will ultimately be delivered joyfully from every enemy.

If God intends so great a final happiness for us, will he not also give the particular rescues we need on the way?

In this changing world, joy may turn into sorrow almost instantly.

But **in the world to come, sorrow will never return**.

God's people prayed for a new display of his power.

He answered with the **comfort of his grace**.

If we feared **sinning against God** as we ought, we would have much less fear of **human disapproval**.

Happy is the person who lives in reverent fear of God.

The church of Christ remains secure through the power and Providence of the Almighty.

## Jerusalem's suffering and promised release (verses 17–23)

God calls his people to attend to the things that bring **everlasting peace**.

Jerusalem had provoked him and was made to experience the bitter results.

People who should have comforted her became **tormentors themselves**.

They lacked the patience needed to keep control of their own souls, and the confidence in God's promises needed to preserve spiritual consolation.

Jerusalem was drunk, not now with the wine of Babylon's idolatry, but with **the cup of affliction**.

Yet God's cause may appear lost for a time without being lost forever.

He can protect it by awakening the **consciences** of those opposing it or by **defeating their plans**.

Oppressors wanted people to submit their souls to human authority—to believe and worship whatever their rulers prescribed.

But violence can obtain only **outward and hypocritical conformity**.

**Consciences cannot be compelled by force.**
