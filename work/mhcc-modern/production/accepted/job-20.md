# Job 20 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Zophar speaks of the short joy of the wicked. | 1–9 |
| The ruin of the wicked. | 10–22 |
| The portion of the wicked. | 23–29 |

## Zophar says sinful joy is brief (verses 1–9)

Zophar spoke about the certain misery awaiting the wicked.

The apparent triumph of evil people and the happiness of hypocrites do not last.

Sinful pleasures and gains bring suffering, regret and pain.

They ultimately end in remorse, distress and destruction.

Pretended religion adds hypocrisy to other wrongdoing, so its guilt and final judgment are greater.

## Zophar describes the bitterness of sinful gain (verses 10–22)

Zophar described the dreadful condition of wicked people.

He spoke of sinful bodily desires as **the sins of youth**.

A sinner may hide cherished sinful pleasures, savoring them secretly as though keeping something sweet beneath the tongue.

But God knows what is in the heart.

He knows what is hidden beneath the tongue and will expose it.

Love of the world and its wealth is sinful when it governs the heart.

Violence and injustice bring divine judgment on individuals, families and nations.

The pleasure of sin becomes **bitter poison** to the person who embraced it.

The same is true of unlawful wealth.

Even in the midst of abundance, wicked people may experience distress because their minds are filled with anxiety.

It is a great mercy when God's sanctifying grace leads someone to return what was unjustly gained, **as Zacchaeus did**.

But when the horror of a despairing conscience forces restitution, **as with Judas**, the act brings no true comfort or spiritual benefit.

## Zophar announces the wicked person's judgment (verses 23–29)

After describing the suffering produced by wicked conduct, Zophar described the ruin brought by God's judgment.

**Only Christ protects against that storm**, as the refuge promised in **Isaiah 32:2**.

Zophar concluded that this was the divinely appointed portion of a wicked person.

Henry's verdict is striking: **never was a doctrine better explained or worse applied**.

Zophar intended to prove Job a hypocrite, though he had no grounds for doing so.

We should receive the sound warning and apply it **to ourselves**, learning reverence and turning from sin.

One true sight of Jesus, opened and impressed upon us by the Holy Spirit, can quiet a thousand sinful questions about why believers suffer.
