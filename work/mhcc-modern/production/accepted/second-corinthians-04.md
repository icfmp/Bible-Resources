# 2 Corinthians 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostles laboured with much diligence, sincerity, and faithfulness. | 1–7 |
| Their sufferings for the gospel were great, yet with rich supports. | 8–12 |
| Prospects of eternal glory keep believers from fainting under troubles. | 13–18 |

## Faithful ministry and treasure in fragile vessels (verses 1–7)

Even the best people would lose heart if they did not receive God's mercy.

But the mercy that has helped us through earlier difficulties and sustained us until now can be trusted to sustain us to the end.

The apostles did not hide dishonorable purposes beneath a convincing appearance of goodness. They did not make the gospel ministry a tool for personal advantage.

People who live sincerely and uprightly will generally retain the respect of wise and good observers.

**Through the gospel, Christ reveals his glory to human minds**.

But the devil's purpose is to keep people in ignorance. Since he cannot prevent the gospel's light from entering the world, he strives to keep individuals away from it or set their hearts against it.

Henry traces rejection of the gospel to **the deliberate blindness and wickedness of the human heart**.

The apostles did not preach to exalt themselves or make themselves the center of the message.

They proclaimed **Jesus Christ as Savior and Deliverer**, the One who saves completely everyone who comes to God through him.

Ministers are **servants for people's spiritual good**, but that does not mean becoming servants of their passing moods or sinful desires.

Seeing sunlight fill the sky is wonderful. But the light of the gospel shining into a human heart is even more wonderful and beneficial.

Light marked the beginning of the original creation. Likewise, the Holy Spirit's first work in a person's **new creation** is to shine spiritual light into the soul.

Yet this treasure of gospel truth and grace has been placed in **earthen vessels**.

Christian ministers share the weaknesses, temptations and emotions of other people.

God could have commissioned angels to announce the gospel, or chosen the most highly admired people in the world.

Instead, he chose comparatively weak and unimpressive instruments.

This made the Lord's power more evident, both in sustaining his ministers and in transforming the people who received their message.

## Severe suffering sustained by grace (verses 8–12)

The apostles experienced extraordinary suffering, yet they also received extraordinary support.

Friends may abandon believers, and enemies may persecute them.

But **God will not leave or forsake his people**.

There may be inner fears as well as outward conflicts. Nevertheless, God's servants are not destroyed.

Paul described their sufferings as reflecting **the sufferings of Jesus Christ**.

Through them, the power of Christ's resurrection and the grace flowing from the living Savior could also become visible.

Compared with the apostles' hardships, many other Christians at that time enjoyed relatively comfortable circumstances.

The contrast should make those of us living in ease thankful rather than indifferent to the sufferings of faithful servants.

## Eternal glory outweighs present trouble (verses 13–18)

Faith is an effective remedy against faintheartedness in affliction.

The apostles knew that **Christ had been raised** and that his resurrection guaranteed their own.

This hope gives courage in suffering and lifts believers above the fear of death.

Their trials also served the **church's good and God's glory**.

A minister can serve the church not only through preaching and conduct but even through suffering endured faithfully.

The hope of everlasting life was the apostles' comfort and support.

What physical sight might judge to be **heavy, long and unbearable**, faith recognized as **light and momentary** when measured against what lay ahead.

All temporal affliction becomes light in comparison with the **immeasurable weight of eternal glory**.

If Paul could describe his many severe and prolonged trials as light and brief in this sense, how should we view our comparatively small difficulties?

Faith teaches us to judge both present suffering and eternal hope correctly.

Some things are visible; others are unseen.

The unseen realities of God's promised future are **eternal**, whereas the visible things of this world are **temporary**.

Let us stop making earthly advantages our highest ambition or present suffering our greatest fear.

Instead, let us diligently seek assurance of **the everlasting happiness God promises his people**.
