# Psalm 32 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The happiness of a pardoned sinner. | 1, 2 |
| The misery that went before, and the comfort that followed the confession of sins. | 3–7 |
| Sinners instructed, believers encouraged. | 8–11 |

## Happiness through forgiveness (verses 1 and 2)

Sin is the source of human misery. But the true believer's transgressions of God's law have been forgiven because they are **covered by the atoning sacrifice**.

**Christ bore our sins**; therefore God does not count them against us. Christ's righteousness is credited to us. God laid our sins upon him and made him a **sin offering** for us.

The decision not to count sin against someone belongs to **God the Judge**. It is God who justifies.

Yet the forgiven person is also sincere and seeks holiness through the **Holy Spirit's power**.

Such a person does not pretend to repent while intending to keep sinning because God is gracious. True believers do not misuse the doctrine of free grace as a license for sin.

God promises every kind of genuine blessing to those whose sins he forgives.

## From the burden of sin to comfort (verses 3–7)

It is difficult to persuade sinners to receive free mercy humbly, confess their wrongdoing fully and condemn their own sin.

Yet **confessing our sins before God** is the only true path to peace of conscience.

Confession and repentance **do not earn forgiveness**, but are necessary to experience God's pardoning mercy.

How happy is the moment when a burdened soul is enabled to pour out its sorrow before God and embrace his **covenant mercy in Christ Jesus**!

We should seek the Lord when Providence calls us and his Spirit stirs us to turn toward him.

When guilt has softened the heart, every human refuge has failed and the troubled mind can find no rest, God applies the healing balm **by his Spirit**.

## Instruction and encouragement (verses 8–11)

God teaches by his word and guides his people through his will.

The psalm warns sinners because **the path of sin ends in sorrow**.

It also comforts the saints: life in fellowship with God is truly the happiest and most satisfying life.

Lord Jesus, teach us to rejoice **in you and in your salvation**, so that our joy may be real.
