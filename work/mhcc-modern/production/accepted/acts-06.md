# Acts 6 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

| The appointment of deacons. | 1–7 |
| Stephen falsely accused of blasphemy. | 8–15 |

## Servants are appointed for the church (verses 1–7)

Until this point the disciples had been described as **united in one mind**.

But as their number increased, complaints and murmuring began to arise.

The apostles were already fully occupied by **God's word**. Their teaching, prayer and responsibility for it demanded their attention and energy.

Those chosen to administer the distribution of material support had to possess the **appropriate qualifications**.

They needed the gifts and graces of the Holy Spirit to handle that trust faithfully.

They were to be **truthful, trustworthy and opposed to greed**.

Everyone appointed to serve the church should be commended to God's grace through the prayers of the congregation.

The church blessed these servants in the Lord's name.

God's word and grace receive special glory when they transform **people who seemed least likely to respond**.

## Stephen is falsely accused (verses 8–15)

Stephen's opponents could not answer his reasoning, so they prosecuted him as a criminal and arranged **false witnesses**.

It is an extraordinary protection of Providence that even more godly people have not been murdered through perjury and abuse of legal procedures.

Many who hate Christ's followers have shown no scruples about false oaths and injustice.

Wisdom and holiness can make someone's face shine, yet they do not prevent mistreatment.

What can be said of human beings who claim to be rational but attempt to support religion through **lies and murder**?

History contains countless examples of this contradiction.

The deepest cause lies less in the intellect than in **the fallen heart, deceitful and deeply wicked**.

Nevertheless, someone serving the Lord with a clear conscience, cheerful hope and the consolation of God can remain calm and even joyful **in the face of danger and death**.
