# Isaiah 42 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The character and coming of Christ. | 1–4 |
| The blessings of his kingdom. | 5–12 |
| The prevalence of true religion. | 13–17 |
| Unbelief and blindness reproved. | 18–25 |

## The gentle servant and his coming (verses 1–4)

This prophecy was fulfilled in **Jesus Christ** (**Matthew 12:17**).

Let us rest our souls upon him and rejoice in him. For his sake, the Father will be pleased with us.

The Holy Spirit not only came upon Christ but **remained upon him without measure**.

He bore patiently the contradiction and opposition of sinners.

His kingdom is **spiritual**. He did not arrive surrounded by earthly honors.

He treats people oppressed by doubts and fears with great tenderness.

Some are like a **bruised reed**. Others resemble a smoking wick newly lit, already in danger of going out.

He does not despise them or burden them with more service or suffering than they can bear.

Through many miracles and his resurrection, he fully demonstrated the truth of his holy teaching.

Through his gospel and grace he establishes principles in human minds that make people wise and just.

Distant nations will wait for and welcome his law and gospel.

If we want to make our calling and election sure and enjoy the Father's delight, we must **look to Christ, listen to him, trust him and obey him**.

## The blessing of Christ's kingdom (verses 5–12)

Redemption restores human beings to the obedience owed to their Creator.

Christ is **the light of the world**.

By his grace he opens minds Satan has blinded and frees people from the bondage of sin.

The Lord has upheld his church in former times.

He now gives fresh promises, which will be fulfilled as certainly as the earlier promises were.

When Gentiles join the church, God receives glory both **in them and through them**.

Let us give the Creator what belongs to him, taking care never to serve created things more than the One who made them.

## God's truth overcomes opposition (verses 13–17)

The Lord will reveal his power and glory.

His voice will cry out **through the preaching of his word**.

The gospel contains warnings as well as promises. Its **warnings of judgment must be preached alongside its blessings**, to awaken a sleeping world.

The Lord triumphs through **the power of his Spirit**.

Those who oppose and blaspheme the gospel will be silenced and brought to shame.

Obstacles to its advance will be removed.

For people blind by nature, God will show the path to life and happiness **through Jesus Christ**.

Where knowledge is weak, he will turn darkness into light. Where obedience is weak, he will make the path plain.

God guides the people whom he brings onto the right path, and will not abandon them.

This is prophetic language but also a promise that can encourage **every believer**.

## The blindness and unfaithfulness of God's people (verses 18–25)

The people are called to pay attention and consider their true condition.

Many perish because they fail to notice what is clearly before them.

Their ruin comes not from a total lack of information but from **carelessness**.

God is pleased to make his own righteousness known.

Yet because of their sins, his people were stripped of their possessions. Henry connects this warning with **the later destruction of the Jewish nation**.

No one can resist or escape God's righteous anger.

Sin brings terrible harm because it provokes divine judgment.

People who refuse to be humbled by lesser judgments should expect greater ones.

How many professing Christians remain as spiritually blind as those who have never known the truth!

God delights to save sinners through **Christ's righteousness**, but also glorifies his justice in judging proud people who despise his mercy.

Because God once poured out judgment on a people he had specially favored, we should fear lest we, while a promise of entering his rest remains, **fail to receive it**.
