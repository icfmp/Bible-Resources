# Numbers 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Offerings, The daily sacrifice. | 1–8 |
| The offering on the sabbath and new moons. | 9–15 |
| Offerings at the passover, and on the day of first-fruits. | 16–31 |

## The daily offerings (verses 1–8)

God renewed the instructions concerning sacrifices.

Israel was entering a new generation, and the people would need to remain at peace with him while fighting their enemies.

The daily offering was called a **continual burnt offering**.

Christians are told to pray continually.

At the very least, we should offer solemn prayer and praise every morning and evening.

A particular instruction here is that the drink offering should use **strong wine**.

Henry understands this as a reminder to give God the best we possess.

The wine was also a figure of Christ's blood, remembered in the church through the wine of the Lord's Supper.

It also pictured the blood of martyrs, whose lives were poured out like a drink offering in the service of others' faith (Philippians 2:17).

## The Sabbath and new moon offerings (verses 9–15)

Two additional lambs were to be offered each Sabbath, beyond the two offered daily.

Henry applies this to Christians by urging greater devotion on the Sabbath.

The purpose of Sabbath rest is to leave us freer for the holy work that should fill that day.

The monthly offerings showed gratitude for the renewal of God's earthly blessings.

Whenever Providence gives us a reason for joy, we should remember Christ's great sacrifice as the true foundation of all our spiritual happiness.

The New Moon celebrations also foreshadowed gospel worship (Isaiah 66:23).

Just as the moon receives light from the sun and is renewed through its influence, so the church receives light from Jesus Christ, the Sun of Righteousness.

Through him the church is continually renewed, especially under the gospel.

## Passover and firstfruits offerings (verses 16–31)

These repeated offerings remind us of the lasting effectiveness of Christ's sacrifice and of our continuing need to rely upon it.

Busy responsibilities, threatening circumstances or times of prosperity should never make us neglect prayer and worship.

They should give us even greater reason to seek the Lord's help and return thanks.

Our religious duties must be accompanied by repentance, faith in Jesus Christ and love toward him.

They should result in genuine holiness in our conduct toward others.

Otherwise God rejects even impressive public worship and lengthy devotion.

Christ is able to meet every need of every day, week, month and year, in every act of worship and every circumstance.
