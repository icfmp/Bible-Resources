# Nehemiah 1 — Modern English

## Original heading

**Nehemiah's distress for the misery of Jerusalem, His prayer.**

## Nehemiah's concern and prayer

Nehemiah served as cupbearer to the Persian king. When God has work to accomplish, he will always have suitable people to do it.

Nehemiah lived comfortably and held an honored position. Nevertheless, he remembered that he was an Israelite and that his fellow Israelites were suffering.

He was ready to help them in every way he could. To find out how best to help, he asked about their condition. We should take a special interest in the condition of God's church and the state of religion.

Every earthly Jerusalem has shortcomings that need the support and service of its friends. Only the heavenly Jerusalem is without defect.

Nehemiah went first to God in prayer so that he could approach the king with greater confidence. Our strongest arguments in prayer come from God's own promises—the word on which he has taught us to hope.

We must also use the practical means available to us, but the earnest and effective prayer of a righteous person accomplishes much. Fellowship with God best prepares us for dealing with other people.

After we have entrusted a concern to God, our minds are set free. We gain contentment and composure, and our difficulties no longer seem insurmountable.

We know that if what we seek would harm us, God can readily prevent it; and if it would be good for us, he can just as readily bring it about.
