# Isaiah 43 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's unchangeable love for his people. | 1–7 |
| Apostates and idolaters addressed. | 8–13 |
| The deliverance from Babylon, and the conversion of the Gentiles. | 14–21 |
| Admonition to repent of sin. | 22–28 |

## God's enduring love and protection (verses 1–7)

God's favor and good will toward his people provide abundant comfort to every believer.

Wherever there is **a new creation**, God himself has formed it.

All whom he has redeemed through **his Son's blood** he has set apart as his own.

When God is for us, what enemy should make us afraid?

What are all the lives and treasures of Egypt and Ethiopia when compared with **Christ's blood**?

True believers are precious in God's sight, and he delights in them.

Even when they pass through **fire and water**, they need not fear destruction while God remains with them.

He will uphold them and bring them through.

God will gather the faithful from every direction.

With such promises before them, they should put away anxious fear.

## God's people testify against idols (verses 8–13)

The worshipers of idols are challenged to defend the objects in which they trust.

People who make idols and rely on them become like the idols themselves.

They retain human bodies and faculties, but abandon ordinary good sense.

By contrast, God's people know **the power of his grace, the sweetness of his comfort, his kind Providence and the truth of his promises**.

Every servant of God has a story to tell about what he has done **within and for them**.

Such testimony may encourage other people to recognize, believe and rejoice in his **power, truth and love**.

## Deliverance from Babylon and the greater redemption (verses 14–21)

The prophet announces the coming rescue from Babylon.

Yet the words also point beyond that nearer deliverance.

Henry sees in them **redemption through Christ**, **the conversion of the Gentiles** and **the recall of the Jews**.

Everything God does to rescue sinners and bring believers to final glory is astonishing.

Yet all of it rests upon the greater wonder of love revealed in **the redemption of humanity**.

## The Lord calls sinners to repentance (verses 22–28)

People who neglect prayer have grown weary of God.

The Master did not exhaust his servants through harsh demands; they wearied him through their repeated disobedience.

Still his mercy remains extraordinary:

**He himself blots out transgressions.**

This gives sinners courage to repent, because **there is forgiveness with God**.

Forgiveness is a completely free act of divine mercy.

When God forgives, he **no longer holds the pardoned sins against us**.

He does not do so because we deserve forgiveness.

He pardons for **his own mercy**, according to his **promise**, and above all for **his Son's sake**.

To forgive in this way is his glory.

Can human beings justify themselves before him?

That attempt is hopeless. Our first father violated God's covenant, and all of us have followed his example.

We can expect pardon only by **faith in Christ**, accompanied by genuine repentance and followed by **newness of life**, hatred of sin and love for God.

Let us remind the Lord of the promises he has made to repentant sinners and of the **satisfaction his Son has made for them**.

Bring those promises before him in earnest prayer for forgiveness.

This is the sure way to be **justified freely by his grace** and to find peace.
