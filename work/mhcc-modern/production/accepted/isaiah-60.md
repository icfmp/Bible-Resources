# Isaiah 60 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The glories of the church of God, when the fulness of the Gentiles shall come in. | 1–8 |
| and the Jews shall be converted and gathered from their dispersions. | 9–14 |
| and the kingdoms of this world shall become the kingdom of our Lord, and of his Christ. | 15–22 |

## The nations come to Christ's light (verses 1–8)

As far as we know God personally and enjoy his favor, **our light has come**.

If God's glory rests upon us and honors us, we should return praise to him not only **with our lips but also through our lives**.

Henry finds nothing in the earlier history of the Jews that fully answers the prophecy of this chapter.

He therefore understands it as looking chiefly toward **events still to come**, including the purity and enlargement of God's church.

The prophet describes the conversion of human souls.

They hurry toward **Jesus Christ**, his church, his word and appointed worship like **doves flying home**.

They fly there for refuge and shelter, and there they find rest.

What a wonderful sight it is to see people hastening toward Christ!

## The church receives peoples from far away (verses 9–14)

God promises to be gracious.

We must begin with **his promise**, because every mercy flows from it.

Many people from distant countries will enter the church.

Christ always welcomes those who come to him.

**The gate of mercy remains open day and night.**

Everything surrounding the church will be put to use for her welfare.

But those who refuse to submit to **Christ's golden scepter**, his word and Spirit, and the rules of his household, will face judgment under **his iron rod**.

The special advantages of different nations and kinds of people will contribute to the church's beauty.

This beauty is not merely outward greatness.

It consists chiefly in **holiness**, and in the gifts and comforts of the Holy Spirit that adorn and enrich gospel worship.

Blessed be God: the **gates of Zion** remain open to sinners returning to him.

## God completes his church's glory (verses 15–22)

The final fulfillment of these promises must surpass anything the Old Testament church experienced.

Nations and kings will devote their resources to the church's good.

The rescue accomplished for her will reveal unmistakably **the Lord's own work**.

Everything will be transformed for the better.

No longer will the land hear threats from violent people or the cries of those who suffer violence.

Her walls will represent safety.

Her gates will proclaim praise to God.

The closing images also appear in the description of **the New Jerusalem** (**Revelation 21:23; 22:5**).

Henry recognizes two possibilities for their fulfillment: **a future glorious state of the church on earth**, or **the triumphant church in heaven**.

Only a fulfillment of that magnitude matches the promise.

People who make God their only light will find him **their sufficient and everlasting light**.

That happiness will know neither change nor imperfection.

No earthly people are completely righteous.

But heaven contains no mixture of sin.

Those who enter it will be **wholly righteous**, their spirits made perfect.

The church's glory will bring honor to God.

When his work is complete, everyone will recognize its wonder.

It may now seem impossible.

But **the God of unlimited power** has undertaken it.

Its fulfillment may appear delayed, but the Lord will hasten it **at the time appointed by his wisdom**, not the time demanded by our foolishness.

Let this hope encourage us during hardship and stir us to diligence.

May we be granted a rich welcome into **the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ**.
