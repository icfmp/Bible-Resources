# Job 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Satan obtains leave to try Job. | 1–6 |
| Job's sufferings. | 7–10 |
| His friends come to comfort him. | 11–13 |

## Satan is permitted another test (verses 1–6)

It is a great comfort that neither human beings nor evil spirits will be our final judges.

Our judgment comes from **the Lord, who never makes a mistake**.

Job continued holding firmly to his integrity as his defense.

God spoke with pleasure about the power of his own grace at work in Job.

People naturally desire to preserve their lives and protect their interests.

But Satan presented Job as though he were **entirely selfish**, concerned only for personal comfort and safety.

The devil and his agents frequently misrepresent God's servants in this way.

Satan was granted another opportunity to test Job, but only **within a limit set by God**.

If the Lord did not restrain this roaring lion, how quickly he would devour us!

In being falsely accused by Satan, Job was a figure of **Christ**, about whom the first promise declared that the serpent would wound his heel but be defeated.

## Job's bodily suffering and his wife's words (verses 7–10)

The devil tempts his own followers, draws them into sin and torments them after their ruin.

With this child of God, he first inflicted suffering and then tempted Job to **respond sinfully to the suffering**.

He urged Job to curse God.

Job's disease was extremely painful.

When we suffer severe illness, we should not imagine God has treated us in a way wholly unknown to his most faithful servants.

Job humbled himself under God's mighty hand and sought to bring his thoughts into agreement with his circumstances.

His wife remained alive but became another source of pain and temptation to him.

Satan still attempts to draw people away from God, as he tempted the first parents, by persuading them to think harshly and falsely about the Lord.

Job resisted and overcame this temptation.

We receive countless blessings we have not earned from a just and holy God.

How could we refuse to accept suffering when our sins deserve more than we endure?

Let us reject murmuring as firmly as boasting.

So far Job had withstood the trial and **shone most brightly in the furnace**.

Some sinful impulses may have stirred in his heart, but **grace prevailed over them**.

## Job's friends come to comfort him (verses 11–13)

Job's friends appear to have been people of high standing as well as wisdom and godliness.

Much of life's comfort comes from friendship with thoughtful and virtuous people.

They came to mourn with Job and showed genuine sorrow.

They came to comfort him and sat beside him.

Yet they apparently began to suspect that his unprecedented troubles were punishment for some hidden wrongdoing concealed under his profession of godliness.

Some people regard visiting a suffering friend as no more than a polite duty.

We should regard it as a genuine obligation of compassion.

If the example of Job's friends does not move us to pity the afflicted, let us seek **the mind of Christ**.
