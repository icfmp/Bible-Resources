# 2 Kings 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Reign of Jehoahaz. | 1–9 |
| Jehoash, king of Israel, Elisha dying. | 10–19 |
| Elisha's death, The victories of Jehoash. | 20–25 |

## Jehoahaz rules Israel (verses 1–9)

In earlier times Israel had been honored as a people devoted to prayer.

In his distress, Jehoahaz appealed to the Lord for help.

He did not seek the help of the golden calves—what help could they offer?

He sought the living God.

Notice how quickly God shows mercy, how ready he is to hear prayer, and how willing to find a reason to act graciously.

Otherwise, he would not have appealed to the ancient covenant that Israel had broken and forfeited so many times.

This should draw us to him and encourage even those who have turned away to repent and return.

With the Lord there is forgiveness, so that he may be feared.

If he answers even a distressed person's request for temporary relief, how much more will he regard a prayer of faith for spiritual blessings!

## Jehoash visits the dying Elisha (verses 10–19)

King Jehoash came to receive Elisha's last counsel and blessing.

There can be great spiritual benefit in visiting faithful believers during illness or near death.

The comfort they find in God as they die can strengthen our own faith.

Elisha promised the king success.

Yet Jehoash still had to seek God's direction and strength rather than rely entirely on his own hands.

The dying prophet's weak hands, signifying God's power, gave the arrow greater meaning and strength than the king's vigorous hands alone.

By treating the sign lightly, the king lost part of what it represented.

This grieved the dying prophet.

Good people are distressed when others reject blessings they could have received and lose opportunities for victory over spiritual enemies.

## Elisha dies; Jehoash defeats Syria (verses 20–25)

God has many ways of correcting a people who have offended him.

Trouble may arrive from the direction least expected.

The invasion mentioned after Elisha's death suggests that losing faithful prophets can be a warning of coming judgment.

Elisha's dead body became the means by which another dead man was restored to life.

This miracle confirmed the truth of his prophecies.

It may also point toward Christ, through whose death and burial the grave has become a safe passage to life for every believer.

Jehoash defeated the Syrians **as many times as he had struck the ground with the arrows**.

Then his victories stopped.

Many people later regret the smallness of their faith and desires when it is too late to regain the blessings they neglected.
