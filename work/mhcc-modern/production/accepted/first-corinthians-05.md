# 1 Corinthians 5 — Modern English

## The church must not tolerate scandalous sin (verses 1–8)

Paul exposed a **shameful abuse** that the Corinthian church had overlooked.

It seems that party loyalty and a mistaken view of Christian liberty had protected the offender from correction.

It is grievous when professing Christians commit crimes **that even unbelievers would be ashamed to commit**.

Spiritual pride and false teaching encourage these scandals and allow them to spread.

How terrible sin's effects can be!

**Satan rules wherever Christ does not rule**. Anyone who remains outside Christ's kingdom remains under Satan's power.

The example of an influential person can do exceptional harm, spreading widely through the community.

Corrupt principles and sinful practices, if left uncorrected, can **harm the whole church**.

Believers need new hearts and new lives.

Both their everyday conduct and their religious worship must be **holy**.

The sacrifice of **Christ our Passover** does not make personal or communal holiness unnecessary.

On the contrary, his sacrifice supplies the strongest reasons and motives for pursuing it.

Without holiness, we can neither live by genuine faith in Christ nor benefit comfortably and fruitfully from the ordinances he has appointed.

## Avoid close fellowship with those who dishonor Christ's name (verses 9–13)

Christians should avoid **close and familiar fellowship with those who openly disgrace the Christian name while claiming to belong to him**.

People persisting in such conduct should, as far as possible, be left to the companionship of others who follow the same sins.

It is a sorrowful fact that **some people called Christians are more dangerous companions than those who openly remain outside the faith**.

The church must not let outward profession conceal openly scandalous behavior.
