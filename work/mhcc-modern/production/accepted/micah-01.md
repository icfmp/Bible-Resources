# Micah 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The wrath of God against Israel. | 1–7 |
| Also against Jerusalem and other cities, Their precautions vain. | 8–16 |

## God's wrath against Israel (verses 1–7)

The whole earth and everything within it is summoned to hear the prophet.

God's holy temple cannot protect people who profess to worship him while living hypocritically.

Neither **the great and powerful, pictured by mountains**, nor **ordinary people, pictured by valleys**, can protect themselves or their land from divine judgment.

God does not overlook sin among those who claim to belong to him.

Indeed, their sins especially offend him because they bring **disgrace upon his name**.

When we suffer the consequences of wrongdoing, it is wise to examine **what particular sin has caused us to suffer**.

People and places held in the greatest honor can be especially exposed to **spiritual diseases**.

The sins of leaders and rulers will be punished severely.

The judgment will correspond to the sin.

What the people sacrificed to their idols will never make them prosperous or truly help them.

**Wealth gained through one sinful desire is often consumed by another.**

## Jerusalem and the other cities cannot escape (verses 8–16)

Micah grieved over Israel's desperate condition.

But the people should not announce it in **Gath**, giving enjoyment to those who delight in God's people's sins or suffering.

The prophet spoke of lying in the **dust** as a mourner.

Let every house in Jerusalem become a house of **Aphrah**, meaning **“house of dust”** in Henry's explanation.

When God humbles us to the dust, it is right for us to humble ourselves beneath his mighty hand.

Many towns would share this grief.

Their names contained meanings that pointed to the calamities approaching them.

The intention was to awaken **a holy fear of God's wrath**.

All refuges other than **Jesus Christ** become **false shelters** for people who place their ultimate trust in them.

Every earthly inheritance passes to later heirs, except **the heavenly inheritance**.

Every human glory will eventually become shame, except the honor **that comes from God alone**.

Sinners may now ignore the suffering of their neighbors.

But the time for their own judgment will also come.
