# Isaiah 4 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## The consequences of war (verse 1)

Henry observes that **this opening verse continues the subject of chapter 3**.

As disaster swept through the land, unmarried women—who faced social shame under the customs of the time—would depart from customary behavior and seek husbands for themselves.

This was one consequence of the war and devastation Isaiah foretold.

## The Branch of the Lord and Zion's cleansing (verses 2–6)

Isaiah's promise concerns not only **Christ's kingdom established in the apostolic age**, but also its future enlargement as scattered Jews are gathered into the church.

Christ is called **the Branch of the Lord** because God's power plants him and causes him to flourish for divine praise.

The gospel is the Branch's fruit. All its blessings, spiritual graces and consolations spring from Christ.

It is called **the fruit of the earth** because its work appeared in this world and meets our needs in our present condition.

We show that we belong to true Israel when we see our greatest beauty in **Christ and holiness**, rather than merely possessing Israel's name.

Jerusalem's restoration and renewed fruitfulness would be a sign of this greater blessed age.

God keeps for himself **a holy seed** even when many who hold a visible place in Zion fall through unbelief.

When the Son of Man cleanses his kingdom, **only those made holy** will remain.

God's Providence may expose and remove sinners through judgment. But **the Spirit of grace renews and converts**.

He is the **Spirit of judgment**, enlightening the mind and convicting the conscience.

He is also **the Spirit of burning**, awakening holy affection and strengthening believers to pursue good with zeal.

Love for Christ and for souls, together with hatred of sin, moves believers to turn ungodliness away from Jacob.

Trials become a furnace that removes the **dross** from God's people. Through the Spirit's illuminating and powerful work, their sinful desires gradually lose their hold, and believers become more like the holy God.

The Lord protects **his church and all who truly belong to it**.

Gospel truth and appointed worship are the church's glory. **Grace within a soul** is that soul's glory, and God guards those who possess it.

Only the weary seek rest. Only those who recognize an approaching storm seek shelter.

Realizing that sin exposes us to divine displeasure, let us immediately **take refuge in Jesus Christ** and gratefully receive the protection he gives.
