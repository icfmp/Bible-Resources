# Exodus 26 — Modern English

## The richly woven curtains (verses 1–6)

God made his presence known to Israel through a tabernacle, or tent, because that suited the people's life in the wilderness.

He adapts the signs of his favor and the gifts of his grace to the circumstances and needs of his people.

The inner curtains were to be richly made.

They were embroidered with cherubim.

Henry understands these figures as a reminder that God's angels camp around his church (Psalm 34:7).

## The goats' hair curtains and coverings (verses 7–14)

The outer curtains were made of less valuable materials.

They were longer and wider than the inner ones, so that they covered them completely.

Other coverings of animal skins protected the whole structure.

Henry sees in these layers a picture of Christ's person and teaching, the church of true Christians, and heavenly realities.

Outwardly, these things may appear humble and unimpressive.

Inwardly, however, and in God's sight, they are glorious and precious.

## The boards, bases and crossbars (verses 15–30)

Each silver base weighed about 115 pounds, according to Henry's reckoning.

The bases stood in rows upon the ground.

Strong boards of acacia wood, covered in gold, were fitted into pairs of bases by their joints.

Together they formed the walls along the two sides and the western end.

Bars passing through golden rings bound the walls together.

Curtains were then spread over the structure.

The tabernacle could be moved, yet it was solid and stable.

Its materials were also extremely costly.

In all this, Henry sees a picture of God's church.

It is built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Jesus Christ himself as the chief cornerstone (Ephesians 2:20–21).

## The veil of the Most Holy Place (verses 31–37)

A veil hung from pillars and divided the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place.

It prevented people from looking into the innermost sanctuary.

The apostle explains its meaning in Hebrews 9:8.

The ceremonial law could not make worshipers perfect.

Simply observing its ordinances could not bring anyone into heaven.

The way into the Most Holy Place had not yet been fully revealed while the first tabernacle still stood.

Life and immortality remained hidden until the gospel brought them to light.

The tearing of the veil when Christ died pointed to that new revelation (Matthew 27:51).

Through Jesus' blood we now have freedom to enter the Most Holy Place in our worship.

But that access never removes our duty to approach God with holy reverence.

Another curtain hung at the outer entrance of the tabernacle.

That curtain was the only outward protection the sanctuary had.

God himself takes care of his church on earth.

If he chooses, a curtain can defend his house as effectively as bronze gates and iron bars.

With these pictures of Christ and his church before us, what judgment do we make of them?

Do we see glory in Christ's person?

Do we recognize excellence in his character, something precious in his salvation, and wisdom in the teaching of the cross?

Can our religion stand examination?

Are we more concerned to have hearts approved by God than reputations admired by other people?
