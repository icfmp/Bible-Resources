# Acts 27 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Paul's voyage towards Rome. | 1–11 |
| Paul and his companions endangered by a tempest. | 12–20 |
| He receives a Divine assurance of safety. | 21–29 |
| Paul encourages those with him. | 30–38 |
| They are shipwrecked. | 39–44 |

## Paul sails toward Rome (verses 1–11)

God's counsel had determined that Paul should reach Rome **before Festus decided to send him there**.

The Lord had work for Paul to do in that city.

The account records the route they followed and the places where they landed.

God encourages those who suffer for him to trust his care, because he can move **unexpected people** to befriend them.

Sailors must make the best use of whatever wind they receive.

So must we all while traveling across the ocean of this world.

Even when circumstances are against us, we should keep making progress as well as we can.

Some people avoid losing ground in adversity but do not make progress during favorable conditions.

Others, including genuine Christians, spend so much effort merely maintaining their spiritual condition that they hardly advance.

**A harbor that looks pleasant is not necessarily safe**.

Many people show respect to faithful ministers while refusing their advice.

Eventually events expose the emptiness of such confidence and the foolishness of that conduct.

## The ship is overtaken by a storm (verses 12–20)

People who set out on life's voyage with favorable conditions cannot know what storms are waiting ahead.

Therefore we should never assume that earthly security has been permanently achieved.

We are not completely safe until **we enter heaven**.

For many days the sailors saw neither sun nor stars.

Believers sometimes experience an equivalent spiritual darkness, walking without any felt light or comfort.

Look how easily worldly wealth changes from something greatly desired into a heavy burden.

In a crisis it may become too heavy to carry safely—even heavy enough to sink the person holding onto it.

Worldly people readily surrender wealth to preserve their lives, yet are reluctant to use it for godliness, charity or suffering for Christ.

Anyone would choose to lose possessions rather than life.

Yet many choose to **wreck faith and a good conscience rather than part with property**.

The sailors tried every available method to save themselves, without success.

When sinners finally abandon confidence in their own ability to save themselves, they may be ready to receive **God's word and trust his mercy through Jesus Christ**.

## Paul receives assurance of deliverance (verses 21–29)

Paul had warned the sailors, but they refused to listen.

Nevertheless, he offered them renewed comfort when they faced danger.

Those who admit their folly and turn from it can still receive help.

People often bring trouble upon themselves by refusing to be content and trying to improve their circumstances against wise advice.

Notice Paul's solemn profession that he belonged to God.

No storm can prevent the Lord's favor from reaching his people; he is **always available to help**.

Faithful servants can be encouraged during difficulties: as long as God has more work for them, he will preserve their lives.

Had Paul **needlessly placed himself in dangerous company**, he might have shared the consequences without such a promise.

But because God had called him into these circumstances, **the others were preserved for Paul's sake**.

God told him the people on board had been given to him.

Nothing gives a good person greater satisfaction than knowing they can be **a blessing to others**.

Paul comforted his companions with the comfort he had received from God.

Since God always keeps his promises, people who trust those promises should remain joyful.

With God, **speaking and acting are not separate things**.

Likewise, for believers, faith and enjoying the certainty of God's promise should belong together.

**Hope is the soul's sure and steadfast anchor, reaching within the veil**.

People in spiritual darkness should cling to Christ and not set out upon another dangerous voyage.

Remain with him and wait until the day dawns.

## Paul encourages everyone to persevere (verses 30–38)

God had appointed the final outcome: the passengers would be saved.

He also appointed **the means** of their rescue, including the experienced sailors.

Our duty is to use the means available to us; the outcome belongs to God.

We do not trust God but **test him** when we claim his protection while refusing reasonable steps within our power.

How selfish people often are! Some are prepared to preserve themselves even by exposing others to destruction.

How blessed the people on this ship were to have Paul among them.

He was in fellowship with heaven and brought courage and encouragement to those around him.

Worldly sorrow leads to death, while joy in God brings **life and peace even in danger**.

The promises of God comfort us only as we **believe and depend on him to fulfill them**.

We must wait for promised salvation through the means he appoints.

If God has chosen us for salvation, he has also appointed that we receive it through **repentance, faith, prayer and persevering obedience**.

It is deadly presumption to expect his saving promise to be fulfilled while refusing those appointed means.

People are encouraged to trust Christ as their Savior when those inviting them clearly demonstrate that they trust him themselves.

## The passengers are shipwrecked (verses 39–44)

The ship had survived the storm in open water, where it had room to move.

Yet when it became stuck, it broke apart.

The same can happen to the heart that fastens its affections upon worldly possessions.

When Satan's temptations break against that heart, it may perish.

But while the heart remains **above the world**, it can endure the storms of daily cares and troubles.

The sailors could already see the shore, yet they still suffered shipwreck at the harbor.

This teaches us never to grow careless merely because safety appears near.

However severe the difficulties along the path to God's promised salvation, **the promise will certainly be fulfilled**.

In due time all believers will arrive safely in heaven.

Lord Jesus, you have assured your people that **none who belong to you will perish**.

You will bring them all safely to the heavenly shore.

What a joyful arrival that will be!

You will present them to your Father and grant your Holy Spirit full and eternal possession of them.
