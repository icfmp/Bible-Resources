# 2 Corinthians 9 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Titus is sent to help complete the collection (verses 1–5)

When we want others to do good, we should treat them with **care, gentleness and good judgment**, allowing appropriate time for the work.

Christians should consider what brings honor to their public profession of faith.

They should seek to make the doctrine of God their Savior attractive in everything they do.

Supporting needy believers is such an obvious duty that one might think Christians would need no reminder.

Yet **self-love** competes so powerfully against the love of Christ that we often need to be reminded and encouraged to fulfill even familiar obligations.

## Give thoughtfully, freely and cheerfully (verses 6–15)

A mind ruled by earthly interests may regard charitable gifts as money **thrown away**.

But when gifts arise from proper motives, they are like **seed sown in a field**, with the promise of a valuable harvest.

That does not mean we should give thoughtlessly.

Works of mercy, like all other good works, should be carried out with **careful thought and a real purpose**.

We should consider both **our own circumstances** and **the needs of the people we hope to help**.

Whether the gift is large or small, it should be given **freely, without resentment and with joy**.

Some people give generously and yet find their resources increased.

Others hold back more than they ought and become poorer.

With stronger faith and greater love, we would spend less wastefully on ourselves and sow more generously in hope of a good result.

Can someone ultimately lose by doing what pleases God?

God can make **every kind of grace abound** toward us and within us.

He can grant an increase of **spiritual and temporal blessings**, provide enough for our needs and enable us to live contentedly with what we possess.

His provision is not intended only for personal comfort.

He also gives us resources **with which to meet other people's needs**, treating them as seed that should be sown.

Acts of charity show whether our professed submission to the gospel is real.

They honor the Christian faith and bring praise and glory to God.

Let us follow **Christ's example**, continuing to do good without growing weary and recognizing that it is more blessed to give than to receive.

Above all, **thank God for his indescribable gift**.

His grace inclines some believers to give and moves others to receive with gratitude.

And his greatest gift is **Jesus Christ**, the priceless expression of his love.

Through Christ, every other blessing necessary for life and godliness comes freely to us, beyond anything we can adequately measure or express.

May his glorious name be praised forever!
