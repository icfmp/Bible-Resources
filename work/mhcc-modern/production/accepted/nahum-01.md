# Nahum 1 — Modern English

## God's justice, power and goodness (verses 1–8)

About a hundred years before Nahum, the people of Nineveh had repented when Jonah preached to them.

Their city was spared, but afterward they became even more wicked than before.

Nineveh did not truly know the God with whom it was contending.

Nahum therefore announced what kind of God he is.

Everyone should receive these words with faith.

They are terrifying to wicked people but comforting to believers.

Let sinners read them and tremble; let faithful people read them and rejoice.

The Lord's anger against sin is set beside his goodness toward his people.

They may be little known or respected in the world, **but the Lord knows those who belong to him**.

The Bible's portrait of Jehovah does not fit the notions of proud people who imagine they can decide what God must be like.

The God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ is **slow to anger and ready to forgive**.

Yet he will not declare the unrepentant wicked innocent.

Trouble and anguish await everyone who continues doing evil.

Who pays proper attention to the power of his anger?

## Assyria's destruction and the good news of peace (verses 9–15)

The powers of evil devise many plans against the Lord and his kingdom on earth.

All such plots will finally fail.

Some sinners are brought to judgment quickly.

One way or another, God will put an end to all his enemies.

People may feel calm and secure, without the least fear of disaster.

Yet they can be cut down like grass or grain when the destroying messenger passes through.

Through the downfall of Assyria, the Lord intended to bring great deliverance to his own people.

Those who disgrace themselves through conspicuous wrongdoing may be brought to public disgrace through judgment.

The news of deliverance would be welcomed with great joy.

Scripture also applies these words to the **great redemption accomplished by Jesus Christ** and to the lasting good news of the gospel (**Romans 10:15**).

Christ's ministers are messengers announcing the peace made available through him.

How welcome this message is to those who recognize the misery and danger brought upon them by sin!

Promises made to God during times of trouble should be fulfilled afterward.

Let us be thankful for his appointed ordinances and gladly take part in them.

And let us look forward with cheerful hope to a world where the wicked cannot enter and where sin and temptation will no longer be known.
