# Joel 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's judgments. | 1–14 |
| Exhortations to fasting and prayer; blessings promised. | 15–27 |
| A promise of the Holy Spirit, and of future mercies. | 28–32 |

## The coming judgment and call to repentance (verses 1–14)

The priests were commanded to **sound the alarm** about God's approaching judgments.

God's ministers must warn people about **the terrible consequences of sin** and reveal his judgment against human ungodliness and unrighteousness.

The vivid description portrays **the devastation caused by locusts**.

Henry says it may also describe **the destruction wrought by Chaldean invaders**.

If sinful nations need warnings about earthly disasters, how much more urgently must sinners seek rescue from **the wrath to come**!

Our chief concern in this life should be **belonging to our Lord Jesus Christ**.

We should loosen our attachment to things that will soon be taken from everyone who treats them as idols.

Repentance includes outward signs of sorrow and shame—**fasting, weeping and mourning**.

But tears over hardship should become **tears over the sin that caused it**.

Tearing our clothes is worthless if our **hearts remain unbroken**.

True repentance requires humbling ourselves, mourning our wrongdoing and **turning away from it**.

Henry says we may be confident that God will **forgive genuine repentance**.

But he distinguishes this from the removal of every outward affliction: **that is not promised**, though the possibility of relief encourages us to turn back.

## A public fast and the promise of mercy (verses 15–27)

The priests and rulers were called to proclaim **a solemn fast**.

The sinner's plea is simple: **“Spare us, good Lord.”**

God is ready to help his people.

He waits **to show grace**.

The people asked for mercy, and he answered their prayer.

His promises are genuine answers to prayers offered in faith.

With God, **speaking and acting are not separate matters**.

Henry notes that some interpreters understand these promises **figuratively**.

On that reading, they point toward **the grace of the gospel** and are fulfilled through the abundant comforts kept for believers in **the covenant of grace**.

## The Holy Spirit is promised (verses 28–32)

The promise **began to be fulfilled at Pentecost**, when the Holy Spirit was poured out.

It continued through **converting grace and miraculous gifts**, given to **both Jews and Gentiles**.

God's judgments upon a sinful world precede **the final judgment of the world**.

To **call on the Lord** truly involves knowing him, having faith in him, desiring him and depending upon him.

It must also be accompanied by **conscientious obedience**, which demonstrates that this faith is sincere.

Those who will be delivered on the great and final day are the people **effectively called from sin to God, from self to Christ, and from earthly things to heavenly things**.
