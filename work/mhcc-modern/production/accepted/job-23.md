# Job 23 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job complains that God has withdrawn. | 1–7 |
| He asserts his own integrity. | 8–12 |
| The Divine terrors. | 13–17 |

## Job longs to bring his case before God (verses 1–7)

Job appealed from his friends to God's righteous judgment.

He wanted his case to be heard immediately.

Blessed be God: **we know where we can find him**.

He is in Christ, reconciling the world to himself, and sits upon a **throne of mercy**, ready to show grace.

There the sinner may come.

There the believer may present a case and plead on the basis of **God's promises, his covenant and his glory**.

Patiently waiting for death and judgment is wise and right, accompanied by holy reverence.

Passionately demanding that they come immediately is sinful and foolish.

Such impatience was unworthy of Job.

## Job speaks of his upright conduct (verses 8–12)

Job knew the Lord was present everywhere.

But his distress confused his thinking so badly that he could not grasp God's merciful presence or find comfort in laying his troubles before him.

Everything seemed dark.

He felt that God stood far away and looked on him with displeasure.

Nevertheless, Job believed that God would test him and finally approve him, because he had sought to keep God's commands and delighted in his word.

Yet here he was **justifying himself more than God, even against God** (**Job 32:2**).

Job was right to reject the baseless charges of his friends.

But he was wrong to assert so confidently that God's affliction could not involve discipline for sin.

Henry also finds a second mistake: Job appeared to deny that Providence sometimes brings justice to injured people or punishes wickedness in **this present world**.

## Job trembles before God's sovereignty (verses 13–17)

Job never doubted that his trials came under God's hand.

He did not believe in blind chance.

How then did he understand them?

He emphasized that the complete reward of God's servants lies **in another life**, since wicked people often escape their deserved consequences on earth.

Yet God's grace already gives believers a first taste of the Spirit and a promise that God will complete the good work he has begun.

A suffering believer should not conclude that prayer is useless or that despair is justified.

God may intend the affliction to awaken **repentance and prayer**.

May we obey and trust him amid suffering and remain willing to live or die according to his will.

We do not know what good purposes may be served by extending or shortening our earthly lives.
