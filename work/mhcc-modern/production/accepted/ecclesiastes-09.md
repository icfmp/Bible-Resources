# Ecclesiastes 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Good and bad men fare alike as to this world. | 1–3 |
| All men must die, Their portion as to this life. | 4–10 |
| Disappointments common. | 11, 12 |
| Benefits of wisdom. | 13–18 |

## Righteous and wicked people experience similar earthly events (verses 1–3)

We should not give up studying God's word and works merely because we cannot answer every question they raise.

Such study still teaches us many things useful to ourselves and others.

But human beings cannot always tell from outward circumstances whether someone is especially loved by God or lies under his wrath.

**God will certainly distinguish the righteous from the wicked in the coming world**.

In the present life, the difference between them lies especially in the inward help and comfort the righteous receive and in the spiritual benefit they gain from trials as well as mercies.

Human hearts, when left to themselves, are full of wrongdoing.

Prosperity can make sinners increasingly bold, even encouraging defiance of God.

On this side of death the righteous and the wicked often seem to receive the same treatment.

**On the other side, their conditions will be utterly different**.

## Mortality and duties in the present life (verses 4–10)

Even the poorest and least respected living person's condition is better than that of an honored person who has **died without repentance**.

Solomon urges the wise and godly to enjoy cheerful confidence in the Lord, whatever their place in life.

The simplest food received in answer to prayer, as a gift from their Father's love, can be especially pleasant.

This does not mean setting our hearts on sensual enjoyment.

It means wisely and gratefully using the things God has given us.

The gladness described here springs from **knowing God's favor**.

This world is the place where we serve.

The next is the place where we receive our reward.

Whatever our circumstances, we have work to do.

Above all, sinners must seek **the salvation of their souls**.

Believers should demonstrate the reality of their faith, honor the gospel through their conduct, glorify God and serve the people of their generation.

## Human expectations often disappoint (verses 11 and 12)

Success rarely comes exactly as we expect.

We must make proper use of available means without placing our final confidence in them.

If we succeed, give God praise.

If our efforts are frustrated, submit to his will.

People who postpone concern for their souls risk being caught in **Satan's net**.

He offers some attractive worldly object, for which they turn away from or neglect the gospel, and they continue in sin until destruction overtakes them suddenly.

## Wisdom achieves what power cannot (verses 13–18)

A person may accomplish through wisdom what cannot be achieved through physical strength.

**If God is for us, who can stand against us?**

Solomon recognizes the remarkable power of wisdom even when the wise person suffers social disadvantages.

How powerful words spoken appropriately can be!

Good and wise people may nevertheless have to find satisfaction simply in **having done good or tried to do it**.

They may be unable to accomplish everything they hoped or to receive the honor their work deserved.

One sinner can destroy enormous amounts of good.

People who ruin their own souls destroy much that is valuable.

They may also draw many others along the same destructive path.

This shows who truly benefits or harms a nation or household: **one faithful servant may do much good, and one sinner may destroy much good**.
