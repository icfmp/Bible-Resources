# Isaiah 59 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Reproofs of sin and wickedness. | 1–8 |
| Confession of sin, and lamentation for the consequences. | 9–15 |
| Promises of deliverance. | 16–21 |

## Sin separates us from God's help (verses 1–8)

When prayer seems unanswered and the rescue we long for does not come, the reason is not that God has grown weary of **hearing prayer**.

It is often that we have grown weary of **praying**.

See sin as it truly is: **extremely sinful**.

See also its consequences: **extremely harmful**.

Sin separates us from God and therefore deprives us of every true good while exposing us to evil.

Yet multitudes feed on false and wicked systems of belief, bringing destruction upon themselves.

Their cleverness and carefully designed schemes cannot rescue them, any more than the **spider's web** can protect the spider from danger.

No plan of **self-made salvation** can help someone who rejects the Redeemer's **robe of righteousness**.

Everyone who lacks **the Spirit of Christ** rushes toward one form of evil or another.

People who disregard divine truth and justice remain strangers to **peace**.

## The people acknowledge their wrongdoing (verses 9–15)

If we shut our eyes against the light of God's truth, he may justly hide from us the things that would bring peace.

The sins of those who **profess to belong to God** are even more blameworthy than the sins of others.

A nation's wrongdoing brings public judgments when justice is not upheld by its authorities.

People may complain bitterly under calamities.

Yet no complaint can truly help them while they continue rejecting **Jesus Christ and his gospel**.

## The Redeemer comes with the Spirit (verses 16–21)

This passage connects with the prophecies that follow.

Henry says it is **generally understood** as announcing the coming of the Messiah, who will be **both Avenger and Deliverer of his church**.

No one was able to intercede with God and turn aside his judgment.

No one stood forward to uphold justice and truth.

Therefore **the Lord himself acted with his own strength and righteousness** on behalf of his people.

He will make his justice against the enemies of his church clearly visible.

When the enemy threatens to sweep everything away without restraint, **the Spirit of the Lord will stop him and put him to flight**.

The God who delivered his people before will deliver them again.

Yet a far greater salvation was promised for **the fullness of time**—the redemption through the Messiah toward which all the prophets looked.

The **Son of God** comes as **our Redeemer**.

The **Spirit of God** comes as **our Sanctifier**.

Thus the **Comforter remains with the church forever** (**John 14:16**).

The word of Christ will continue in the mouths of his faithful people.

Whenever anyone claims to speak the mind of the Spirit, **that claim must be tested against Scripture**.

We have reason to grieve the spread of unbelief and irreverence.

But the Redeemer's cause will achieve complete victory, even on earth.

At the end, each believer will be **more than a conqueror** when received by the Lord into heaven's glory.
