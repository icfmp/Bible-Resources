# Nehemiah 13 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

## Nehemiah removes unlawful influences (verses 1–9)

Israel was a people set apart and was not to mix indiscriminately with the surrounding nations.

Notice the benefit of the **public reading of God's word**.

When we listen properly, Scripture shows us sin and duty, right and wrong, and where we have wandered from the Lord.

We profit when it moves us to separate ourselves from evil.

Anyone seeking to drive sin out of the heart—the living temple of God—must also remove what supplies it with pleasure and strength.

Take away the **food and fuel of sinful desire**. This is what it means to put sin to death.

Once repentance has driven sin from the heart, **Christ's blood must be applied by faith**, and the heart should be furnished with the graces of the Holy Spirit for every good work.

## Nehemiah corrects the support of worship (verses 10–14)

Sacred office does not excuse someone from correction or deserved discipline when that person sets an evil example.

The Levites had been wronged because their allotted support had not been paid.

They had gone elsewhere to earn a living for themselves and their families. Their service in the sanctuary could no longer sustain them.

**Inadequate support leads to an impoverished ministry.**

The work was neglected because the workers had been neglected.

Nehemiah held the rulers accountable.

Ministers and people who abandon religious duties, as well as rulers who fail to uphold them according to their responsibility, must answer for their actions.

Nehemiah acted promptly to restore the Levites to their places and ensure that they were paid fairly.

He continually looked to God and entrusted all his concerns to him.

It pleased him to have been useful in restoring and supporting religion among his people.

He asked God to remember the work, not from pride, but as a humble appeal regarding the honesty of his intentions.

He prayed, **“Remember me”**, not “Give me what I have earned.”

He asked that his work not be forgotten, not that it be advertised for praise.

Yet God both rewarded him and preserved the record of what he had done.

God does more than we can ask.

## Nehemiah stops Sabbath-breaking (verses 15–22)

People seeking to promote godliness must pay attention to keeping the Lord's day holy.

True religion cannot flourish while Sabbaths are treated with contempt.

No wonder Judah's faith and conduct had deteriorated when the people neglected worship and profaned the Sabbath.

Those who profane it often fail to recognize the harm they do.

We must answer for sins encouraged in others by our own example.

Nehemiah condemned their conduct as evil because it sprang from contempt for God and disregard for their souls.

He reminded them that violating the Sabbath was among the sins that had brought earlier judgments.

Repeating the same sins would expose them to further judgment.

His courage, zeal and prudent action provide an example. Henry considers it likely the reform endured.

Nehemiah nevertheless confessed himself a sinner who could **demand nothing from God on the basis of justice**.

He asked the Lord for mercy.

## Nehemiah confronts unlawful marriages (verses 23–31)

If one parent is ungodly, human sinful nature may incline children to follow that parent's example.

This is a serious reason why Christians should not enter marriages that bind them unequally to unbelievers.

Parents must also give careful attention to how their children speak, so that they do not learn the corrupt and irreverent language associated here with Ashdod.

Nehemiah exposed the evil of these marriages.

Those who remained stubborn were punished. Henry explains the physical punishment as **ordered through the lawful officers under the civil law then in effect** (**Deuteronomy 25:2–3**), rather than describing a private beating.

He prayed that God would **remember them**—that the Lord would convince and convert them and remind them of their duty.

Even the best public service may be forgotten by its beneficiaries.

Nehemiah therefore entrusted his reward to God.

His final request also expresses what we most need:

**“Remember me, my God, for good.”**

We may hope humbly that God will remember us and the service we have rendered, while still acknowledging our sins and seeking mercy after a whole life of useful work.

Like Nehemiah, we have reason to repent deeply and pray to be spared according to the abundance of God's mercy.
