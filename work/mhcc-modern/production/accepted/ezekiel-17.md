# Ezekiel 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A parable relative to the Jewish nation. | 1–10 |
| to which an explanation is added. | 11–21 |
| A direct promise of the Messiah. | 22–24 |

## The parable of the great eagles (verses 1–10)

Powerful conquerors are aptly compared with **birds or beasts of prey**.

Yet their destructive passions are directed and overruled to accomplish **God's purposes**.

Those who depart from the Lord and then seek security by changing from **one earthly support to another** merely change the form of their sin.

They will not truly prosper.

## The parable is explained and Zedekiah rebuked (verses 11–21)

The prophecy now explains the parable through the history of the Jewish nation.

**Zedekiah had been ungrateful to his benefactor**, which was itself a sin against God.

In every solemn oath, the one who swears calls God as a witness to the truthfulness of the promise.

**We owe truth to everyone**, not only to members of our own religion.

When people who profess true religion betray those who practice false religion, their profession makes their wrongdoing **more serious**, not less.

God will punish their treachery even more severely.

He will not leave unpunished those who misuse his name.

No one who dies still bearing **unrepented guilt** will escape God's righteous judgment.

## God promises the Messiah (verses 22–24)

Human unbelief cannot make **God's promise fail**.

The tree imagery used earlier as a threat now becomes part of a promise.

Henry understands this promise as referring especially to **Jesus Christ, the Son of David, God's Messiah**.

Satan's kingdom has long exercised great power, but it will be broken.

Christ's kingdom, once despised and disregarded, will be firmly established.

Blessed be God: our Redeemer will be known **to the farthest ends of the earth**.

Under his shadow we may find refuge from **the coming wrath** and from every spiritual enemy and danger.

Believers bear fruit **in him**.
