# Isaiah 29 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Judgements on Jerusalem and on its enemies. | 1–8 |
| The senselessness and hypocrisy of the Jews. | 9–16 |
| The conversion of the Gentiles, and future blessings for the Jews. | 17–24 |

## Jerusalem humbled and its enemies defeated (verses 1–8)

The name **Ariel** may mean **the altar of burnt offerings**, according to Henry.

Jerusalem should recognize that outward religious services cannot free a nation from righteous judgment.

People who practice religion hypocritically cannot please God or make peace with him through ceremonies alone.

In former days the Lord's protection had surrounded the city, as though a mighty host of angels camped around it.

Now he would oppose Jerusalem itself.

Proud looks and speech would be brought low through humiliating events.

But Isaiah also foretold the defeat of Jerusalem's enemies.

**Sennacherib's army vanished like a dream**.

So the crowds that fight against God's worship through the centuries will ultimately fall.

Sinful people who lull themselves with comforting fantasies will awaken to a dreadful reality after death.

## Scripture sealed to the proud, not the teachable (verses 9–16)

It is both sorrowful and astonishing to see sinners feel secure while continuing in rebellion.

Educated people dismissed the prophecies as too obscure to understand, while uneducated people excused themselves by saying they lacked learning.

But **the Bible remains a sealed book to learned and unlearned alike unless they approach it with humble, teachable hearts**.

We must read to learn God's truth and will.

Worship means coming near to God.

When the heart is full of reverence and love, its speech will express those affections.

Yet many offer only **the labor of their lips**.

They pretend to address God while their thoughts wander among countless foolish things.

They substitute their own religious inventions for the worship he has commanded.

Many participate only to follow custom or pursue their personal advantage.

There is a crucial difference between **the wandering thoughts and defects that distress a genuine believer** and **a heart deliberately withdrawn from God**.

The former needs help and repentance; the latter is the hypocrisy God condemns here.

People who hide behind religious language to serve selfish purposes deceive themselves.

Those who quarrel with God or imagine they can conceal their intentions from him effectively accuse their Creator of ignorance.

God will expose and abolish such perverse conduct.

## Gentiles brought to life; Israel promised blessing (verses 17–24)

The extraordinary change promised here may concern Judah's historical circumstances, but it also **looks further ahead**.

Through the gospel, a great harvest of souls would be gathered **from the Gentiles into Christ's church**.

Then the wilderness would become a fruitful field, while the former fruitful field—the unbelieving Jewish community—would resemble a deserted forest.

People who genuinely rejoice in God even while troubled will eventually find greater cause for joy.

**Meekness strengthens holy happiness**.

Powerful enemies will be brought low, and mockers within the community will be judged.

We all sometimes speak carelessly or misunderstand what we hear.

But it is particularly unjust to **make someone an offender for a single word**.

The proud did everything possible to bring trouble upon those who corrected them.

Yet the God who redeemed **Abraham from his troubles and snares** will deliver Abraham's genuine children through faith.

For godly parents, one of the greatest comforts is seeing their children **renewed by God's grace**.

May those who have misunderstood the truth and murmured against it receive understanding and sound teaching.

The **Spirit of truth** can correct their errors and lead them into truth.

Let us therefore pray for people who have been misled.

True religion changes human hearts, giving a humble, devout spirit both **peace and joy**.
