## Laws against falsehood and injustice (verses 1–9)

The law of Moses bears clear marks of sound moral judgment and wise government.

All its provisions serve its stated purposes: Israel was to worship the one true God and remain distinct from the pagan nations around it.

We must never allow partisan loyalties, friends, witnesses or popular opinions to distort our judgments.

They must not lead us to minimize serious wrongs or exaggerate minor ones.

They must not cause us to excuse guilty people, accuse innocent people or misrepresent the facts in any way.

## The seventh-year rest, Sabbath, and festivals (verses 10–19)

Every seventh year, Israel's land was to rest.

The people were not to plow or sow it.

They could eat what grew on its own, but were not to collect it for storage.

Henry understands this law as teaching dependence on God's providence.

It also taught Israel to trust God's faithfulness in providing greater harvests while they followed his appointed seasons.

The rest of the land pictured the heavenly rest.

In that rest, the labors, worries and concerns of this world will end forever.

Israel was strictly forbidden to honor the gods of the surrounding nations.

The people were particularly inclined toward idolatry, so they were to erase even the remembrance of those false gods.

God also required his people to gather solemnly in worship at the place he would choose.

They were to come together before the Lord.

What a good Master we serve!

He has made rejoicing before him one of our duties.

We should gladly devote to his service the portion of time he requires.

His Sabbaths and appointed acts of worship should be feasts to our souls.

The Israelites were not to come before God empty-handed.

Likewise, we should never approach his worship **empty-hearted**.

Our hearts should be filled with holy longing for him and renewed dedication to his service.

God takes pleasure in spiritual sacrifices of this kind.

## God promises to guide Israel to Canaan (verses 20–33)

God promised to guide and protect Israel throughout the wilderness journey and bring the people into the promised land.

He said that he would send **his angel** before them.

With this promise came a command: they must obey the angel God would send.

Christ is the Angel of Jehovah, Henry says, as the apostle teaches in 1 Corinthians 10:9.

God also promised Israel a secure and pleasant settlement in Canaan.

The condition was entirely reasonable.

They were to serve only the true God, not the gods of the nations—gods that were no gods at all.

Consider how generous God's promises were.

He promised good food, continuing health, growing possessions and long life.

In this way, godliness has a promise for the present life as well.

He also promised victory over their enemies.

God sent swarms of hornets ahead of Israel's armies.

He can use even such small creatures to judge the enemies of his people.

In kindness toward the church, God often defeats its enemies **little by little**.

That keeps us watchful and continually dependent on him.

In the same way, sinful habits and desires are driven from believers' hearts gradually, not all at once.

Alongside the promises came another command: Israel must not make alliances of friendship with idolaters.

Anyone who wants to avoid evil practices must also avoid company that draws them into evil.

Living among people devoted to wrongdoing can be dangerous.

Other people's sins may become traps for us.

Our greatest danger comes from people who lead us to sin against God.
