# Psalm 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David's integrity. | 1–7 |
| The character of his enemies. His hope of happiness. | 8–15 |

## David prays with an upright heart (verses 1–7)

This Psalm is a prayer.

Prayers that only pretend to be sincere are useless.

But when our hearts lead our prayers, God receives them graciously.

David was accustomed to prayer.

He had not waited until suffering and danger to begin seeking God.

His faith therefore encouraged him to expect a hearing.

Steadfast determination and vigilance against **sins of speech** provide important evidence of integrity.

Knowing the natural human inclination toward evil and his own particular temptations, David had used **God's word as protection** against Satan's destructive paths.

It brings comfort in trouble to remember that grace has enabled us to avoid sinful ways.

Those following God's paths should pray continually that he will **keep their feet steady on those paths**.

David asked God to hold him up.

Everyone who wants to continue in divine service must obtain **fresh daily strength and grace through faith and prayer**.

He requested the Lord's **wonderful loving kindness**—not merely ordinary gifts but the special favor God shows to those who love his name.

## Protection amid hostile enemies and eternal hope (verses 8–15)

David was surrounded by enemies and asked the Lord to keep him safe.

Henry understands the prayer as pointing prophetically toward **Jesus Christ**, preserved through every trial of humiliation until the glory and joy of exaltation.

It also teaches Christians to entrust their souls to God, believing he will preserve them for his heavenly kingdom.

Our worst enemies are **those who threaten our souls**.

Those who threaten our bodies act under divine Providence.

They are like **God's sword**, which cannot move without his permission and which he will put away when its work is finished.

They are like **his hand**, by which he disciplines his people.

**We cannot escape God's hand except by fleeing into it.**

It brings comfort when human power seems terrifying to remember that every such power depends upon God's sovereignty.

Most people consider worldly possessions their highest good and make no provision for the coming life.

What this world calls treasures becomes worthless **rubbish** when compared with eternal blessings.

The poorest and most afflicted Christian need not envy someone who possesses the world but has no better portion.

Clothed in **Christ's righteousness**, with a heart and life renewed by grace, may we look upon God's face by faith and continually set him before us.

Each morning may his likeness, shown in Scripture and impressed upon us by renewing grace, satisfy our hearts.

True eternal happiness is prepared for those who are **justified and sanctified**.

They receive it when the soul awakens at death from its bodily life, and when the body rises at the resurrection from the grave.

No soul is fully satisfied except in God's favor and gracious work.

Even that satisfaction will not become **complete until heaven**.
