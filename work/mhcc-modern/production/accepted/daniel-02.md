# Daniel 2 — Modern English

## Nebuchadnezzar is troubled by his dream (verses 1–13)

The greatest rulers are often especially troubled by **worries and anxieties**.

These may keep them awake at night, while ordinary laborers sleep deeply and peacefully.

We seldom know the uneasiness experienced by people who live amid great splendor and what others imagine to be pleasure.

The king demanded that his learned men reveal **the dream itself**.

If they failed, he would have them executed as deceivers.

People are often more eager to know **future events** than to discover **the way of salvation or the path of duty**.

Yet knowledge of the future often increases **anxiety and distress**.

The king condemned people who had deceived him by claiming to possess extraordinary powers, but now demanded that they do something **beyond what they had professed to be able to do**.

## Daniel and his companions pray for an answer (verses 14–23)

Daniel humbly prayed that God would reveal the king's dream and its meaning.

**Friends who pray for us are valuable friends**.

Even the greatest and best people should welcome the prayers of others.

We ought to show that we appreciate such friends and their prayers.

Daniel and his companions prayed **specifically**.

Whatever we request, we can expect to receive it only as a gift of **God's mercy**.

He invites us to bring our needs, burdens and dangers before him.

They appealed to God because of the grave danger they faced.

The mercy they sought was granted.

**The earnest prayers of righteous people accomplish much**.

Daniel thanked God for revealing what would save his and his companions' lives.

How much more grateful should we be that God reveals **the salvation of our souls**, even to people who are not counted among the world's wise and educated!

## Daniel speaks before the king (verses 24–30)

Daniel corrected the king's opinion of **magicians and diviners**.

The inability of created beings should direct us toward **the all-sufficient Creator**.

God can do for us and disclose to us what no one on earth can, especially **the work of redemption** and the secret purposes of his love within it.

Daniel confirmed that the dream was important because it concerned **the kingdoms and changing affairs of this world**.

Those whom God has greatly honored should put away every thought of **their own wisdom or merit**, so that **the Lord alone** receives praise for the good they possess and accomplish.

## The image and the kingdoms (verses 31–45)

The image represented successive earthly empires that would dominate nations and affect the affairs of **the Jewish church**.

Henry interprets its parts as follows.

**First, the head of gold:** the **Chaldean Empire**, then in power.

**Second, the chest and arms of silver:** the empire of **the Medes and Persians**.

**Third, the belly and thighs of bronze:** the **Greek Empire**, founded by Alexander.

**Fourth, the legs and feet of iron:** the **Roman Empire**.

Henry further understands the **ten toes** as ten kingdoms into which Rome would divide.

Some would be weak like clay, others strong like iron.

Attempts to reunite them into a stronger empire would prove unsuccessful.

These are **Henry's interpretations of the vision**, not independently established details supplied in its symbols.

The **stone cut without human hands** represents **the kingdom of our Lord Jesus Christ**.

Christ's kingdom would rise amid earthly kingdoms and overthrow **Satan's kingdom** within them.

He is **the stone the builders rejected**, precisely because human hands had not shaped him.

He has nevertheless become **the chief cornerstone**.

Christ's government and peace will increase without end.

The Lord will reign not only until time ends, but **after time itself is no more**.

Henry believes the events fulfilled so far correspond closely to the vision, while **future ages** will see the stone break the whole image and **fill the earth**.

## Daniel and his companions are honored (verses 46–49)

Our duty is to direct everyone's attention toward **the Lord**, the Author and Giver of all good gifts.

Some people recognize God's power and majesty without choosing to serve him themselves.

But everyone should seek **God's glory** and **the greatest good of humankind**.
