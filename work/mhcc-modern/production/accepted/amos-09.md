# Amos 9 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The ruin of Israel. | 1–10 |
| The restoration of the Jews and the gospel blessing. | 11–15 |

## Israel's ruin and a remnant preserved (verses 1–10)

The prophet saw the Lord standing **upon the idolatrous altar at Bethel**.

No matter where sinners try to escape God's justice, **his judgment will overtake them**.

Those whom God brings to heaven through grace can never be thrown down.

But those who try to climb there **through proud confidence in themselves** will be brought low and covered with shame.

What makes escape impossible and judgment certain is that **the Lord sets his eyes upon the wicked for harm rather than for good**.

He would scatter the Israelites and bring suffering upon them, **as grain is shaken in a sieve**.

Yet some would be saved.

Henry considers the remarkable preservation of **the Jews as a distinct people** to be foretold here.

People who claim to belong to God while making themselves like the world should expect to be treated as the world is treated.

Their outward religious profession will not protect them from judgment.

## God's church restored through Christ (verses 11–15)

**Christ died to gather God's scattered children together**.

Here they are described as people **called by the Lord's name**.

God himself speaks the promise.

He is able to fulfill it, has decided to do so, and has committed **the power of his grace** to accomplishing it.

Henry suggests that **verses 13–15** may refer partly to **the earliest Christian period**.

Yet he expects a fuller and more glorious fulfillment when **the fullness of both Jews and Gentiles enters the church**.

We should continue praying earnestly for the promised peace, purity and beauty of God's church.

The Lord wonderfully preserves **his chosen people amid even the most terrible disorders and suffering**.

When everything looks hopeless, he revives his church and fills it with **every spiritual blessing in Jesus Christ**.

Glorious will be the age when **not one of God's good promises remains unfulfilled**.
