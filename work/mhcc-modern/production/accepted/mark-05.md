# Mark 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The demoniac healed. | 1–20 |
| A woman healed. | 21–34 |
| The daughter of Jairus raised. | 35–43 |

## Jesus releases a man held by demons (verses 1–20)

Some people deliberately persist in sin and resemble the man in this account.

God's commands act like chains restraining sinners from evil, yet people break those restraints when determined to continue wicked conduct.

This reveals Satan's destructive power in them.

A Roman **legion** could number **six thousand soldiers or more**.

The use of the name **Legion** in this account indicates a dreadful number of fallen spirits opposed to both God and humanity.

We are no match for our spiritual enemies through our own strength.

But through **the Lord and his mighty power**, even numerous enemies need not overcome us.

When Christ delivers a person enslaved by grievous sin, that person willingly sits at the Savior's feet to hear his word.

Christ changes miserable captives of Satan into **saints and servants of God**.

The local people disliked Jesus when they discovered their pigs had been lost.

God's patience and mercy can still be seen when a loss of property spares human life and warns sinners to seek the salvation of their souls.

The healed man eagerly told others what Christ had done for him.

Many marveled, but **few followed**.

It is possible to admire Christ's works while never seriously longing for Christ himself.

## A woman is healed (verses 21–34)

A place that rejects the gospel may lose it to another place where the good news will be better received.

A synagogue ruler came urgently to Jesus about his **twelve-year-old daughter**, who was dying.

Another healing occurred during the journey.

We should do good **on the road as well as at home**, as **Deuteronomy 6:7** teaches concerning instruction.

People often turn to Christ only after every other source of help fails.

All other helpers eventually prove powerless when asked to bring real peace to a troubled soul.

Some try distractions and worldly company.

Others immerse themselves in business or give way to excessive pleasures.

Some attempt to establish **their own righteousness**.

Others torment themselves through vain superstitions.

None of these methods can give the soul rest.

But the person whom Christ heals from sin experiences a **real inward change for the better**.

Jesus knows secret faith, just as he knows secret sins.

The woman told him everything truthfully.

Christ wants his people to be comforted and possesses the power to give peace to distressed hearts.

The more simply we depend on him and expect his gracious help, the more we recognize that he has become **our salvation**.

Those spiritually healed through faith have reason to **go in peace**.

## Jairus's daughter is raised from the dead (verses 35–43)

When Jairus heard that his daughter had died, he may have wondered whether he should still ask Jesus to come.

That uncertainty is **Henry's supposition** rather than a stated thought of Jairus.

But do we need less of God's grace, the Spirit's comfort and the prayers of ministers and friends when **death enters a household** than when a family member is ill?

Faith is our best remedy against grief and fear at such a time.

Believe the resurrection and do not be overcome by fear.

Christ raised the child with a word of power.

His command pictures the gospel's call to people naturally dead in trespasses and sins.

**Christ's word gives spiritual life**.

All who saw the miracle or heard about it wondered at his power.

We cannot now assume that deceased children and relatives will be restored to earthly life.

But we may still find genuine **consolation under our trials**.
