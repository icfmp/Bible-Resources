# Numbers 7 — Modern English

## The leaders' offerings for the tabernacle (verses 1–9)

The tribal leaders brought their offerings for the tabernacle's service after the sanctuary had been fully erected.

Required acts of worship must take priority over voluntary gifts.

The more authority and resources God grants someone, the greater the opportunity to serve him and the surrounding community.

No sooner had the tabernacle been established than the people made provision for moving it.

Even when life seems newly settled, we should prepare for future changes and especially for death—the greatest change.

## The leaders' offerings for the altar (verses 10–89)

The leaders and prominent men were among the first to take part in honoring God.

People in authority should use their position, influence and wealth to promote faith and God's service wherever they live.

This was a joyful festival of dedication.

Yet a **sin offering** appeared among the sacrifices.

Even when we serve God at our best, we remain aware of sin and need repentance.

In every act of worship, faith must look toward Christ as our Sin Offering.

Each leader brought an offering on a different day.

God's work should not be carried out in a confused rush.

Taking time may not make us finish faster, but it will help us do it better.

A task extending across twelve days should not be treated as a tedious burden.

The offerings were identical.

This showed that every tribe had an equal share in the altar and an equal right to the blessings signified by its sacrifices.

The One who spoke to Moses from between the cherubim—the **Shekinah**, or visible divine majesty—was, Henry says, the **eternal Word**, the second Person of the Trinity.

All God's fellowship with human beings comes through his Son.

Through that Son he created the world and rules the church.

He is the same yesterday, today and forever.
