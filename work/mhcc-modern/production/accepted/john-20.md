# John 20 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The sepulchre found to be empty. | 1–10 |
| Christ appears to Mary. | 11–18 |
| He appears to the disciples. | 19–25 |
| The unbelief of Thomas. | 26–29 |
| Conclusion. | 30, 31 |

## The tomb is found empty (verses 1–10)

If Christ had given his life as a ransom but never taken it up again, there would be no clear evidence that God had accepted his sacrifice as satisfaction for sin.

Mary was distressed to find his body missing.

Believers who are weak in faith often complain about circumstances that are actually **grounds for hope and joy**.

It is fitting for disciples who enjoy special privileges to be especially active in fulfilling their duties, enduring effort and risk in good work.

We should do our best without envying those who do better or despising people who are slower yet do everything they can.

The disciple whom Jesus especially loved reached the tomb first. **Christ's love motivates devoted service** more effectively than anything else.

Peter followed behind. He had denied Jesus, and a guilty conscience can slow our service to God.

The disciples had not yet understood Scripture's teaching that Christ **must rise from the dead**.

## Jesus appears to Mary Magdalene (verses 11–18)

Those who seek Christ with affection—even through tears—have reason to hope they will find him.

Sometimes believers complain about clouds and darkness even when God uses them to humble their hearts, put sin to death and make Christ more precious.

Even an angel's presence and smile cannot satisfy a soul without Jesus and the Father's favor in him.

Only those who have experienced spiritual desertion fully know the pain of losing the felt assurance of Christ's love and hope of heaven.

But Christ often **exceeds the expectations** of those seeking him.

Mary's longing to find Jesus was unmistakable.

He made himself known by speaking to her. Christ commonly reveals himself personally to his people **through his word applied to the soul**.

Those who love Jesus delight in acknowledging him as **their Master**.

He warned Mary not to expect the same form of his bodily presence as before.

Through union with Christ, his Father is **our Father**, and his God is **our God**.

Christ shares our human nature, while believers are made partakers of the divine nature through grace.

His ascension to heaven, where he intercedes for us, brings great consolation.

Earth is not our final home or resting place; since Jesus ascends, our desires should seek **the things above**.

Those who know Christ's word should help others benefit from it.

## Jesus appears to the gathered disciples (verses 19–25)

This happened on **the first day of the week**, a day the New Testament repeatedly mentions and which Henry understands as established as **the Christian Sabbath in remembrance of the resurrection**.

The disciples had locked the doors for fear of persecution.

Jesus nevertheless stood among them, arriving miraculously and silently.

When believers must gather privately, locked doors cannot keep Christ's presence away.

When he assures them of his love through the Spirit, he gives comfort in his promise that because he lives, they will live also.

Seeing more of Christ always brings greater joy to disciples.

Jesus breathed upon them and said, **“Receive the Holy Spirit.”**

Their spiritual life and fitness for ministry depend entirely on him.

When Christ's word is received by faith in the heart, its true effect comes through **the Spirit's life-giving breath**; without it there is neither light nor life.

Jesus commissioned the apostles to declare **the way sins are forgiven**.

Henry explicitly rejects the view that the apostles possessed an independent power to forgive or condemn as judges.

They were authorized to **declare the marks of those God accepts or rejects** at the final judgment.

Through their teaching we can discern genuine believers from false professors.

When we meet in Christ's name, especially on his appointed day, he will meet with us and speak peace.

Disciples should strengthen one another by telling absent friends what they heard and experienced.

Thomas required conviction **on his own terms**. In doing so he limited the Holy One of Israel.

He might justly have been left to his unbelief after so much evidence.

Sometimes disciples prolong their own sorrows through neglect or refusal to believe.

## Thomas sees and believes (verses 26–29)

God appointed that one day in seven should be kept holy from the beginning.

Henry understands Christ's repeated appearances at the disciples' assemblies on **the first day of the week** as confirming that day for worship under the Messiah.

The church has continued observing it through the generations.

Jesus knows every unbelieving word and thought.

Yet he graciously accommodated Thomas's weakness instead of leaving him in unbelief.

We likewise should **bear with the weak** (**Romans 15:1–2**).

The warning remains: unbelief deprives people of grace, hope, joy and fellowship with Christ.

Thomas was ashamed of his former unbelief and responded with affectionate confession: **“My Lord and my God.”**

He laid hold of Christ wholeheartedly.

Jesus graciously receives genuine believers even when their faith is slow or weak.

All who hear or read the gospel are called to receive the testimony God has given concerning his Son (**1 John 5:11**).

## Why these signs were written (verses 30 and 31)

Jesus performed many other signs, but these were written down so that readers might believe he is **the promised Messiah, Savior of sinners and Son of God**.

Through that faith they may receive eternal life by his mercy, truth and power.

May we believe Jesus is the Christ and, by believing, **have life through his name**.

## Editorial review — John 20

**Entire secondary original-English text directly read:** [CCEL, John 20](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xxxv.xx.html). Released `MHCC 0.1.1` remains authoritative for exact native SWORD entries/annotations and independent QA.

- **1–10:** resurrection confirming sacrifice, Mary mistaking good news for loss, **John first and Peter slowed by guilt**.
- **11–18:** spiritual desertion, Christ revealing himself through applied word, **Father/God of Christ shared through union**, ascension toward heavenly home.
- **19–25:** first-day assemblies as **Henry's Christian Sabbath claim**, doors no barrier, **Spirit as source of life**, apostles **declare** forgiveness rather than **grant judicial pardon**, Thomas demanding personal method.
- **26–29:** first-day recurrence and weak faith, **Romans 15:1–2**, Thomas's explicit **deity confession**, **1 John 5:11**.
- **30, 31:** **two individual source reference anchors**, grouped for readability only, written signs pointing to Christ and life.
