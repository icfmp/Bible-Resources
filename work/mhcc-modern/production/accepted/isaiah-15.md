# Isaiah 15 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The Divine judgments about to come upon the Moabites.**

## The destruction approaching Moab

Henry says this prophecy would be fulfilled **within three years**. Its fulfillment would confirm Isaiah's prophetic calling and support belief in the other messages he delivered.

The judgment announced against Moab unfolds in several ways.

**Verse 1:** The enemy would surprise the nation's principal cities. Terrible changes can come in a very short time.

**Verse 2:** The Moabites would seek help from their idols.

People without faith may find no genuine comforter when they suffer. Yet fear and disaster seldom lead them, by themselves, to come before the forgiving God with **sincere sorrow and believing prayer**.

**Verse 3:** Cries of grief would spread throughout the country.

It offers little relief to know that many other people share the same suffering.

**Verse 4:** Even Moab's soldiers would lose their courage.

God can quickly remove whatever a nation regards as its strongest protection.

**Verse 5:** The suffering would grieve people living nearby.

Although the Moabites were enemies of Israel, they were also **fellow human beings**. Their misery should still awaken compassion.

**Verses 6–9:** Isaiah describes widespread lamentation as Moab becomes prey to Assyria.

The country would be plundered, and **famine** would follow the devastation of war.

Those who dedicate themselves to gaining and storing up worldly possessions rarely consider how suddenly everything they possess may be taken away.

Let us warn even our enemies about the danger before them.

And as we warn them, **pray that they may find forgiveness for their sins**.
