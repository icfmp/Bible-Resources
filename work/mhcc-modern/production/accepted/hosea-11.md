# Hosea 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's regard for Israel; their ingratitude. | 1–7 |
| The Divine mercy yet in store. | 8–12 |

## God's parental love and Israel's rebellion (verses 1–7)

God loved the Israelites while they were weak, helpless, foolish and stubborn **like little children**.

He carried and fed them as **a nurse cares for an infant**, enduring their difficult behavior.

Everyone who has grown up should often remember **God's kindness during childhood**.

The Lord cared for us and worked patiently with us, not only like **a father or teacher**, but like **a mother or nurse**.

In the wilderness, he showed Israel the way forward and **held them by the arms**.

Through the ceremonial law given to Moses, he instructed them in his commandments.

He guided them to keep them from wandering and supported them to keep them from falling.

In the same way **God's spiritual Israel** depends upon his sustaining power.

It is God who draws souls to himself.

No one can come to him unless **he draws that person**.

He bound the people with **cords of love**, described by a word Henry understands as stronger than the earlier cords.

He relieved the heavy burdens they had endured.

Yet Israel responded with terrible **ingratitude**.

God's counsel would have saved them, but **their own plans brought ruin**.

They repeatedly turned away.

They could not be relied upon to remain faithful.

They turned from **God, the highest good**, and eagerly embraced temptation.

Their hearts had become **set on doing evil**.

True happiness belongs only to people whom God **teaches by his Spirit**, **sustains by his power**, and **leads in his ways**.

Through grace he removes the desire and dominion of sin, creates a longing for **the gospel's blessed feast**, and enables people to feed upon it and live forever.

## God's mercy remains available (verses 8–12)

God is **slow to anger** and unwilling to abandon completely the people called by his name.

When a sacrifice was needed for sin and a Savior for sinners, **he did not spare his own Son**, so that he might spare us.

Such mercy is offered during **the day of God's patience**.

People who persist in sin until they exhaust that opportunity will face **the great day of his wrath**.

Human compassion is small compared with **God's tender mercy**.

His ways of welcoming returning sinners are as far above ours as **heaven is above earth**.

God knows how to forgive miserable sinners.

He is faithful and just to forgive because **Christ has purchased pardon**, and God himself has promised it.

Our trembling at Christ's word should draw us **toward him**, not drive us away.

The children who tremble should **run to their Father**.

Everyone responding to the gospel invitation will receive **a place and a name in the gospel church**.

Israel's religious service had become hypocritical.

In Judah, however, the people still showed regard for God's law and followed their faithful ancestors.

Let us remain faithful.

God honors those who honor him, while those who despise him will be lightly regarded.
