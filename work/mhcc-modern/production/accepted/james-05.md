# James 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The judgments of God denounced against rich unbelievers. | 1–6 |
| Exhortation to patience and meekness under tribulations. | 7–11 |
| Cautions against rash swearing Prayer recommended in afflictive and prosperous circumstances, Christians to confess their faults to each other. | 12–18 |
| The happiness of being the means of the conversion of a sinner. | 19, 20 |

## Oppression and self-indulgence face judgment (verses 1–6)

Public disasters fall especially heavily upon people who live securely in sensual pleasure, although every social class suffers in such times.

The treasures people idolize will soon perish.

Those hoarded possessions will remain only as **evidence against their owners** at judgment.

Never defraud or oppress other people. Avoid even the appearance of doing so.

God does not forbid reasonable and lawful enjoyment, but **living for pleasure**, especially sinful pleasure, provokes his anger.

Indulgence in bodily appetites makes people unfit to attend to the concerns of their souls.

Upright people may be condemned and killed by oppressors. **God notices their suffering**.

Above all, **Henry writes in the collective language of his period that “the Jews” had condemned and crucified Jesus Christ, the righteous One**.

## Wait patiently for the Lord's coming (verses 7–11)

A farmer patiently waits for a crop. Should believers be less willing to wait for **a crown of glory**?

If our waiting is longer, the prize is far greater.

The Lord's coming is near in the sense that his promises will be fulfilled and his people's losses and sufferings repaid.

Humans measure time by their short lifespans. But **thousands of years are like a few days to God**.

A handful of years can seem endless to us, yet all time is a moment to him.

Consider **Job**. God's pity and tender mercy were not evident to everyone during his trials, but the outcome made them clear.

Believers, too, will discover that their endurance ends in blessing.

Serve God and bear difficulties in faith, assured that **the end will crown everything**.

Only eternal happiness in him is secure. Every other object of hope is temporary.

## Pray in suffering, confess faults and seek grace (verses 12–18)

Careless and profane swearing shows contempt for **God's name and authority**.

It gives no real profit, pleasure or honor. It is an unnecessary expression of hostility to God, even among people who profess his name and participate in worship.

The Lord will not treat his name as meaningless.

When suffering, **prayer is especially fitting**. The heart is humbled, and prayer becomes the means through which faith and hope grow.

The healing of sick people is attributed **not to anointing with oil but to prayer**.

During illness, cold, formal prayers are inadequate. We should pray **in faith**.

The chief blessing to ask for ourselves and others in sickness is **the forgiveness of sins**.

Do not encourage people to postpone repentance by suggesting that a final confession, minister's absolution, words of exhortation or religious ceremony will remedy an entire life deliberately lived without godliness.

**Confessing faults to one another** helps restore peace and brotherly love.

An effective prayer comes from a righteous believer **justified in Christ** and walking by grace in obedient holiness.

Its earnestness is formed in the heart through **the Holy Spirit**, awakening holy affections, believing expectations and dependence on God's promises at his mercy seat.

**Elijah's example** shows prayer's power.

We must never regard the result as a person's **merit**, but as God's **grace**.

It is not enough merely to repeat a prayer. We must truly **pray while praying**, concentrating thought and directing sincere desires toward God.

Though answers today may not involve as many visible miracles, **just as much divine grace** may accompany them.

## Restore sinners instead of merely changing their opinions (verses 19 and 20)

No wise or holy person should claim never to be mistaken or refuse to acknowledge an error.

Practical wrongdoing often rests on mistaken principles or beliefs.

But genuine **conversion** means turning a sinner away from the error of a sinful life.

It is **not merely changing religious party, preferred opinion or way of thinking**.

The only way to have sin truly covered is to **forsake it**.

When one person is converted, many sins are prevented in that person and potentially in others whom they influence.

**The salvation of one soul is worth infinitely more than preserving multitudes of human lives or benefiting an entire nation**.

Whatever our position, let us work without sparing effort in God's service, knowing labor done for the Lord is never wasted.

Henry marvels that for **six thousand years** God had continued multiplying pardons without exhausting his grace.

Divine mercy remains an **ocean always full and always flowing**.

May we receive this mercy through **Christ's blood and the sanctifying work of the Holy Spirit**.
