# Job 40 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job humbles himself to God. | 1–5 |
| The Lord reasons with Job to show his righteousness, power, and wisdom. | 6–14 |
| God's power shown in Behemoth. | 15–24 |

## Job humbles himself before God (verses 1–5)

Fellowship with the Lord truly convinces and humbles the believer, making that person willing to abandon even deeply cherished sins.

Before the Lord grants remarkable deliverances, his people often need to be thoroughly convinced of their faults and brought low before him.

God had shown Job how little he understood about the works of nature.

Job therefore could not claim the wisdom needed to judge all God's purposes or methods of Providence.

The Lord asked whether someone who contended with the Almighty had the knowledge to instruct him.

Now Job began to soften into genuine repentance.

His friends had not persuaded him.

But **the Lord's voice possesses power**.

When the Spirit of truth comes, he convinces the heart.

Job yielded to divine grace.

He acknowledged that he had done wrong and could say nothing to justify himself.

He understood his own sinfulness and called himself **vile**.

True repentance changes the way a person thinks about self.

Job now recognized himself as a weak, foolish and sinful creature who should never have spoken against God's wise conduct.

One glimpse of God's holiness would terrify the strongest rebel.

How then will the wicked endure his glory at judgment?

But when divine glory is revealed to us **in Jesus Christ**, we are humbled **without being destroyed by terror**.

We can lower ourselves before him while still loving him as children love their Father.

## God shows Job who can humble the proud (verses 6–14)

People who learn from what God has said become ready to receive more instruction.

Even those genuinely convicted of sin often need to be convinced more thoroughly and humbled more deeply.

Only God has the power to bring the proud down.

He also has the wisdom to know **when and how** to do it.

We have no right to instruct him how to govern the world.

Our own hands cannot save us.

We cannot make ourselves acceptable through our achievements, much less protect ourselves from God's justice.

We must therefore commit ourselves into **his hand**.

A believer's continuing renewal follows the same path as the first conversion: **conviction of sin, humility and watchfulness against what remains**.

When we recognize several sins, we may still need God to expose many more.

## Behemoth demonstrates God's power (verses 15–24)

God described a vast animal with astonishing strength.

**Behemoth** means **beasts**.

Henry says most readers of his time understood the creature as the **river-horse, or hippopotamus**, familiar from Egypt.

That identification is reported as his interpretation, not treated as a zoological certainty established by the original text.

God made this enormous creature, fearfully and wonderfully formed.

Its strength is itself a gift from the Creator.

If even this animal overwhelms human strength, how much more ought we to humble ourselves before the Lord!

The God who made the human soul knows every way to reach it.

He can bring the sword of divine justice against it.

But godly people have spiritual weapons: **the whole armor of God**, by which they resist and overcome the tempter.

Thus their immortal souls may remain safe whatever happens to their fragile, mortal bodies.
