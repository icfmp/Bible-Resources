## Hezekiah begins reform (verses 1–19)

As soon as Hezekiah became king, he began to reform Judah's worship.

Those who begin their work with God begin at the right place, and he can bless their efforts.

Turning away from God's appointed worship is genuinely a way of turning away from **God himself**.

People still neglect true worship if **Scripture is not read and explained**, pictured by lighting the temple lamps, or if **prayer and praise are not offered**, pictured by burning incense.

Neglect of worship had caused the troubles Judah had endured.

Only the Lord can prepare a human heart for living faith.

When much good is accomplished quickly, **all the glory belongs to him**.

Those who love God and care about people's souls will rejoice.

Everyone who does good work should seek to do it well.

## The offerings of atonement (verses 20–36)

When Hezekiah learned the temple was ready, he acted at once.

The sins of the preceding reign required atonement.

Mourning over sin and abandoning it were not enough; the people also brought **a sin offering**.

Likewise, our repentance and reform do not themselves earn pardon.

Forgiveness is available only **through Jesus Christ**, who became sin—that is, **a sin offering**—for us.

While the offerings burned, the Levites sang.

Grief over sin must never prevent us from praising God.

The king and congregation consented to the proceedings.

Merely being present where others worship is not enough.

We must worship God **with our own hearts**.

Our spiritual sacrifices of praise and thanksgiving should be accompanied by giving ourselves and everything we possess to God.

These sacrifices become acceptable to the Father **only through the Redeemer**.
