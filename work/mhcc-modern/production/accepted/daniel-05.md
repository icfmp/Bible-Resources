# Daniel 5 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Belshazzar's feast and the writing on the wall (verses 1–9)

**Belshazzar** openly defied God's judgments.

Henry reports that **most historians** understood **Cyrus** to have been besieging Babylon at the time.

Complacency and indulgence are sad signs of approaching ruin.

Merriment that dishonors sacred things is profoundly sinful.

Some songs sung at modern banquets are hardly better than the songs pagans offered to their gods.

See how God suddenly filled Belshazzar and his officials with terror.

**God's written word** is enough to frighten even the most proud and defiant sinner.

What we perceive of God—like the portion of the hand writing in **the book of creation** and **the book of Scripture**—should fill us with reverence for what we cannot see.

If this was his **finger**, what would we see if he revealed his **arm**, or his whole majesty?

The king's guilty conscience warned him not to expect good news from heaven.

God can make the strongest sinner tremble in an instant.

He need only allow that sinner's own thoughts to overwhelm the mind.

No bodily pain equals the inward agony that can suddenly seize a sinner in the midst of pleasure, luxury and celebration.

For some, that terror leads them to **Christ for pardon and peace**.

Many others fear punishment without being humbled by their sins, seeking relief through false comforts instead.

The ignorance and uncertainty displayed by people who claim wisdom, particularly concerning **Holy Scripture**, can only increase sinners' despair.

## Daniel is called to interpret the writing (verses 10–17)

Daniel had been forgotten at court and was living quietly.

Henry estimates that he was then **ninety years old**.

Many consult God's servants about strange questions or obscure matters but never ask about **salvation or the path of duty**.

Daniel treated the offered reward as insignificant.

He spoke to Belshazzar as to someone **already condemned**.

If faith enabled us to see how rapidly this world is passing away, we would treat its honors and gifts as of little value.

Yet we should still perform our duties in the world and do all the useful service we can.

## Daniel announces Belshazzar's judgment (verses 18–31)

Belshazzar had learned nothing from **the judgments against Nebuchadnezzar**.

He had instead deliberately dishonored God.

Sinners are content to worship gods that neither see, hear nor know.

But they will stand before **the God to whom everything is exposed**.

Daniel read the sentence written on the wall.

Henry applies its pattern as a warning to every unrepentant sinner.

At death, one's **days are numbered and completed**.

After death comes judgment: the sinner is **weighed and found wanting**.

After judgment comes **eternal punishment**.

While these events were taking place in the palace, Henry reports the opinion that **Cyrus's army entered the city**, after which Belshazzar was killed and submission followed.

Every unrepentant sinner will ultimately see God's warning fulfilled, whether that person relies upon **self-righteous obedience like a Pharisee** or maintains **an outwardly impressive but hypocritical profession of the gospel**.
