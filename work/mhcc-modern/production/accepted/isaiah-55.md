# Isaiah 55 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An invitation to receive freely the blessings of the Saviour. | 1–5 |
| Gracious offers of pardon and peace. | 6–13 |

## Come and receive the Savior's gifts without payment (verses 1–5)

**Everyone who desires salvation's blessings is welcome to receive them.**

In Christ there is **enough for everyone and enough for each person**.

Those satisfied with worldly things, who feel no need of Christ, experience no spiritual thirst.

They are not troubled about their souls.

But where God gives grace, he also gives **a desire for grace**.

Where he gives that desire, he will satisfy it.

**Come to Jesus Christ!**

He is the **open fountain** and the **rock struck to release water**.

Come to the holy ordinances, to the streams that make God's city rejoice.

Come to healing water and living water (**Revelation 22:17**).

Jesus himself used this invitation (**John 7:37**).

**Come and buy**: make the gospel's grace your own by personally accepting it.

**Come and eat**: receive and enjoy the gift.

The world disappoints our expectations.

We hope to find even water there and go thirsty.

But Christ **exceeds our expectations**.

When we come to him, we find **wine and milk**.

The gifts offered cannot be assigned an earthly price.

They have already been purchased, at the full cost of **Christ's own precious blood** (**1 Peter 1:19**).

Our needs are beyond counting, and we have nothing with which to satisfy them.

If Christ and heaven belong to us, we will remain forever indebted to **free grace**.

Listen carefully.

Let proud hearts bow.

It is not enough to hear an invitation; **accept God's offer**.

All the world's wealth and pleasure cannot provide lasting consolation and satisfaction for the soul.

They do not even satisfy our bodily appetites.

Everything is vanity and frustration.

Let the disappointments of earthly life drive us to seek **satisfaction in Christ alone**.

Only then will our souls find rest.

**Listen, and your soul will live.**

See how freely happiness is offered!

By **the sure mercies of David**, Henry understands **the Messiah**.

All the Savior's mercies are **covenant mercies**.

He purchased them, God promised them in him, and they are distributed through his hands.

We could not discover the way to living water by ourselves.

Christ is given as **Leader and Commander**, to show us what to do and to empower us to do it.

Our calling is to **obey and follow him**.

No one comes to the Father except through him.

He is the **Holy One of Israel**, faithful to his promises.

God has promised to glorify Christ by giving him the nations as his inheritance.

## Seek the Lord while he may be found (verses 6–13)

God now freely offers **pardon, peace and lasting happiness**.

Seeking him will not be useless while his word calls us and his Spirit works upon our hearts.

But a day is coming when the opportunity will end.

That may happen during a person's earthly life.

It will certainly be over **at death and judgment**, when the door is closed.

Repentance requires not only **a change in conduct**, but also **a change in the mind**.

We must revise our judgments about people and things.

It is not enough to stop outward wrongdoing.

We must struggle against **evil thoughts and desires**.

To repent is to **return to the Lord against whom we rebelled**.

If we return, God will **multiply pardon**, just as we multiplied our offenses.

No one should trifle with that abundant mercy or use it as an excuse to keep sinning.

Human thinking differs sharply from God's thoughts concerning sin, Jesus Christ, holiness, this world and the world to come.

But nowhere does the difference appear more strikingly than in **forgiveness**.

We may forgive another person yet remain unable to forget the offense.

But when God forgives sin, **he no longer remembers it against the sinner**.

God's word is effective in **Providence and grace** just as surely as it governs the natural world.

His holy truth produces changes in human hearts that rain and snow, though essential for the earth, cannot produce.

It never returns to God without accomplishing the purposes for which he sent it.

If we examine the church especially, we will see how much the Lord has accomplished for it and will yet accomplish.

The Jews' return to their homeland pictured greater promised blessings.

**Gospel grace transforms the person who receives it**.

Rescued from coming wrath, the converted sinner experiences **peace of conscience**.

Christ's love moves that person to devote life to the Redeemer's service.

Someone once irreverent, quarrelsome, selfish or ruled by sensual pleasures becomes **patient, humble, kind and peaceful**.

The hope of helping bring about such change should move us to **spread the good news of salvation**.

And may **the Spirit of all truth** give us such a view of **the fullness, freedom and greatness of God's mercy in Christ** that every narrow view of sovereign grace is removed from us.
