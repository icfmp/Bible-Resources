# Psalm 27 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The psalmist's faith. | 1–6 |
| His desire toward God, and expectation from him. | 7–14 |

## The Lord as light and strength (verses 1–6)

The Lord is the believer's light and the strength of that person's life.

We live and move **not merely through him but in him**.

His gracious presence, power, promises, readiness to hear prayer and the Spirit's witness within believers form the **secret shelter of his tabernacle**.

There his people find holy confidence and inward peace.

David longed for continual fellowship with God through his appointed ordinances.

All God's children desire to live in **their Father's house**.

They do not want merely to visit it like travelers spending one night, nor remain temporarily like servants who must eventually depart.

They want to dwell there **all their days**, as children belonging to their father.

If we expect praising God to fill our eternity, should it not also occupy our present lives?

However a Christian's earthly circumstances may differ, the favor and service of God remain **the one necessary thing**.

The believer seeks it, prays for it and rejoices in it.

## Seeking God's face (verses 7–14)

Wherever a believer may be, there is a path through prayer to **the throne of grace**.

God calls us through his Spirit, his word and worship, and through particular events—both merciful and difficult.

When we foolishly pursue false and worthless things, he graciously calls us to seek our true happiness in him.

The invitation **“Seek my face”** goes out generally, but each of us must answer personally: **“I will seek your face.”**

The word is not useful to us until we receive its instruction.

Grace makes hearts willing to answer God's gracious call.

David asked for the Lord's favor, continuing presence, guidance and protection.

God's help often comes when **every human helper has failed**.

He is a more reliable Friend than even the most devoted earthly parents could ever be.

David believed he would **see the goodness of the Lord**.

The hope of everlasting life, together with foretastes of its glory and pleasures, is a powerful defense against fainting in trouble.

Meanwhile the Lord gives strength to bear present burdens.

Let us look to **the suffering Savior**, pray in faith and encourage one another to wait for the Lord with patience and earnest prayer.
