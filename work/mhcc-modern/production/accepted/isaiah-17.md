# Isaiah 17 — Modern English

## Damascus and Israel brought low (verses 1–11)

**Sin makes cities desolate**.

It is strange that conquerors should glory in harming other human beings. Better for such ruined places to become grazing grounds for flocks than to shelter people openly rebelling against God and holiness.

Israel's fortresses and the northern kingdom would be brought down.

People who **share in wrongdoing** may rightly come to **share in its consequences**.

Israel's sins had made the people ripe for destruction.

Their former glory would be removed as quickly as grain disappears when a farmer harvests a field.

Nevertheless, God would preserve **a small remnant**.

Only a few would remain—one here and another there, as when fruit is left after harvest.

Those preserved would be brought toward **holiness**.

They would return to God, recognize his hand in events and give him his rightful praise.

This is the purpose of Providence under **our Maker**, and the work of grace under **the Holy One of Israel**.

The people would turn from idols, things they had invented through their own imaginations.

We have reason to regard as beneficial those trials that **separate us from our sins**.

God is **the Rock of our salvation and our strength**.

Forgetfulness of him lies at the root of every sin.

The pleasant plants and shoots imported from distant soil represent **foreign and idolatrous forms of worship** with their associated sinful practices.

People might cultivate those plants with great energy, but the effort would ultimately be wasted.

Let this picture teach the danger and unavoidable consequences of sin.

## The Assyrian threat vanishes (verses 12–14)

The strength and rage of Assyria resembled enormous waves of the sea.

Yet once the God of Israel rebuked the invaders, they would flee like **chaff before the wind**.

In the evening, Jerusalem would be terrified by the presence of a powerful enemy.

By morning, the invading army would be almost entirely destroyed.

Happy are those who remember God as **their salvation** and depend on his power and grace.

The troubles believers endure, and the success their enemies enjoy, are both temporary.

But **the lasting joy of God's people** and the final defeat of those determined to destroy them will endure forever.
