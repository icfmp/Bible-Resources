# 2 Samuel 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David subdues the Philistines, the Moabites, and the Syrians. | 1–8 |
| The spoil dedicated. | 9–14 |

## David defeats surrounding nations (verses 1–8)

David subdued the Philistines, who had troubled Israel for many years.

In the same way, after the saints have fought long and often against the powers of darkness, **the Son of David will place those enemies beneath their feet** and make them more than conquerors.

David also defeated the Moabites and made them pay tribute.

Two portions were destroyed, and a third was spared.

Henry urges that **the line of mercy should be stretched as far as possible**.

David also defeated the Syrians.

The Lord preserved him through these wars, and in his psalms David repeatedly gave God the glory for his victories.

## The captured treasures are dedicated to God (verses 9–14)

David set apart the precious things he obtained for the future construction of the temple.

He had destroyed the **golden idols** of the Philistines (**2 Samuel 5:21**), but dedicated the **golden vessels** to God's service.

The grace of the Son of David acts in a similar way when it wins a soul.

Everything that stands opposed to God must be destroyed.

Every sinful desire must be put to death and crucified.

But whatever can honor God should be dedicated to him.

In this way its purpose and rightful ownership are changed.

God employs his servants differently.

Some, like David, fight battles.

Others, like Solomon, build.

One servant prepares work that another completes, and God receives the glory for both.

## David governs with justice (verses 15–18)

David neither committed injustice nor withheld or delayed justice from anyone.

He worked diligently and was ready to receive requests and appeals.

He judged without favoritism.

In these qualities he pictured Jesus Christ.

Let us submit to Christ, seek his friendship and regard his service as our joy.

Let us diligently perform the duties he has given each of us.

David appointed his sons to important positions of authority.

But believers, who are Christ's **spiritual descendants**, receive still greater honor.

Through him they are made **kings and priests to God** (**Revelation 1:6**).
