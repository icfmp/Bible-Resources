# Esther 7 — Modern English

| Original outline | Verses |

## Esther accuses Haman (verses 1–6)

If our love of life causes us to plead earnestly before people who can only kill the body, how much more fervently should we pray to **God, who can judge both body and soul**!

How earnestly should we pray for the salvation of family, friends and everyone around us!

When petitioning powerful people, we must sometimes hold back even just complaints for fear of offending them.

But when we approach the King of kings with reverence, we cannot ask or expect too much.

Although we deserve only God's judgment, he is both willing and able to do far more than we can ask or imagine.

## Haman dies on the gallows he built (verses 7–10)

The king became angry.

People who act according to their own will sometimes later condemn themselves for their actions.

When anger rises, we should delay making decisions and thus show control over ourselves and obedience to sound reason.

People who are proudest and most overbearing when powerful often become the most servile and timid when their position collapses.

The day will come when those who hate and persecute God's chosen people will gladly seek help from them.

The king returned even angrier with Haman.

His attendants were ready to carry out his judgment.

How little confidence proud people can have in the influence they imagine they possess!

The enemies of God's church have often been trapped by their own schemes.

Such judgments display the Lord's righteousness.

The king's anger was finally satisfied.

Few would pity Haman for dying on the gallows he had constructed for another; Henry calls readers to recognize God's justice in the outcome.

Let those who commit evil tremble, turn to the Lord and seek **forgiveness through Jesus' blood**.
