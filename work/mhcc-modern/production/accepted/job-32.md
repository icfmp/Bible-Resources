# Job 32 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elihu is displeased at the dispute between Job and his friends. | 1–5 |
| He reproves them. | 6–14 |
| He speaks without partiality. | 15–22 |

## Elihu is distressed by the argument (verses 1–5)

Job's friends had stopped speaking, but they were not persuaded.

Other people had been listening.

**Elihu** was rightly displeased with Job for being more eager to clear his own reputation than to defend God's justice and goodness.

He was also displeased with Job's friends because they had not treated Job fairly.

Few quarrels begin—and even fewer continue—without some wrongdoing **on both sides**.

People who seek truth must not reject what is right in either party's arguments or defend what is wrong in either one.

## Elihu corrects both parties (verses 6–14)

Elihu professed to speak under the Holy Spirit's inspiration and sought to correct both Job and his friends.

He recognized that older people with more experience should normally speak first.

Yet God distributes wisdom as he chooses.

This gave Elihu courage to express his view.

Through careful attention to **God's word** and dependence upon **the Holy Spirit**, young people may become wiser than older people.

But real wisdom makes them **quick to listen, slow to speak** and willing to hear others patiently.

## Elihu speaks without flattery (verses 15–22)

Even if we are convinced the Holy Spirit has given us something valuable to say, we should wait for the proper opportunity.

God brings **order, not confusion**.

A faithful person finds refreshment in speaking for God's glory and helping others grow in faith.

The more we contemplate the majesty of **God our Maker**, and the more we fear **his wrath and justice**, the less we will fear or flatter people.

If we keep **the Lord's mercy and the terrors of his wrath** before us, we can remain steady in whatever duty he assigns us.
