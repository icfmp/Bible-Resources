# Romans 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The duty of subjection to governors. | 1–7 |
| Exhortations to mutual love. | 8–10 |
| To temperance and sobriety. | 11–14 |

## Submit to lawful civil authority (verses 1–7)

The gospel's grace teaches peaceful submission where pride and fallen thinking see only reasons to grumble.

Whatever the character of the people who govern us, **their rightful authority must be respected and obeyed**.

In the ordinary course of public affairs, rulers are a terror not to peaceful and honest people but to evildoers.

Because sin corrupts human beings, some are restrained from crime only by **fear of punishment**.

If you benefit from government, do what you can to preserve public order rather than disturb it.

Private people should behave quietly and peacefully in the place God has assigned them (**1 Timothy 2:1–2**).

Christians must not engage in **fraud or dishonest tricks**.

Smuggling, trafficking in contraband, withholding lawful duties or deliberately evading taxes violates God's express command.

Such wrongdoing also harms honest neighbors, who may be left carrying a greater burden.

Anyone who participates in or assists these schemes shares responsibility for them.

It is painful to find professing Christians defending dishonest practices of this kind.

The godly should be known as **peaceable and honest members of their communities**, regardless of how others behave.

## Fulfill your duties through love (verses 8–10)

Avoid unnecessary expenditure.

Do not enter debts you lack the ability to repay.

Avoid reckless speculation, rash promises or other decisions that may prevent you from giving everyone what is owed.

**Do not remain in anyone's debt**.

Give others what rightly belongs to them.

Never spend on yourself what you owe to someone else.

Many understand how troublesome debt is, but think too little about its **moral seriousness**.

Love toward other people includes all the duties of the second table of the law.

The last five of the Ten Commandments are summed up in the command, **“Love your neighbor as yourself.”**

We should love others **with the same sincerity**, even when not necessarily with exactly the same degree or intensity as self-love.

Loving someone means sincerely seeking that person's welfare.

From this flows the golden rule: treat others as you would wish them to treat you.

**Love is a living, active principle of obedience to the law**.

We should avoid every degree of harm to other people's bodies, relationships, possessions and reputations.

And beyond avoiding harm, we should seek to be **useful in every situation**.

## Wake from spiritual sleep and put on Christ (verses 11–14)

Paul teaches **four things** for the Christian's daily direction.

**First, when to awaken:** **now**.

Wake from careless spiritual security, laziness, negligence, spiritual death and spiritual dullness.

We live in a busy and dangerous time.

Our final salvation is nearer than when we first believed.

We are closer to the end of the journey, so we should watch our path and increase our diligence.

**Second, how to prepare:** the night is nearly gone and day is approaching.

Take off **the works of darkness**, like clothing worn through the night.

Put on **the armor of light**.

A Christian should regard themselves as **undressed if unarmed**.

The Holy Spirit's graces form our armor against Satan's temptation and the assaults of this evil world.

Above all, **put on Christ**.

Take his **righteousness for justification**.

Receive his **Spirit and grace for sanctification**.

Receive him as **Lord to govern** and **Jesus to save**; in both, he is **the Christ anointed by the Father**.

**Third, how to walk:** now that we are awake and ready, we must not sit still.

Live in a way that pleases the God who always sees us.

Walk honorably as in daylight, avoiding the deeds of darkness.

Excessive drinking and wild behavior often accompany sexual immorality, quarrels and envy (**Proverbs 23:29–35**).

**Fourth, what to provide for:** first, provide for your soul.

But bodily needs also matter.

Two kinds of bodily concern are forbidden: **anxious, burdensome preoccupation** and **indulgence of disordered desires**.

Natural needs should be met, but sinful appetites restrained.

Asking God for food we truly need is right; Jesus taught us to pray for daily bread.

But demanding food merely to gratify craving provokes God (**Psalm 78:18**).
