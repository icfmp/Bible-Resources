## Ezra travels to Jerusalem (verses 1–10)

Ezra went from Babylon to Jerusalem seeking the welfare of his people.

The king favored him and granted all his requests, supplying what he needed to serve his country.

Many people accompanied him.

He enjoyed the king's favor **because of God's favor**.

Every person and created thing can become to us only what God makes them.

We should recognize his hand in everything that happens and thank him.

## The king commissions Ezra (verses 11–26)

The generosity of pagan kings in supporting God's worship put to shame the actions of many kings of Judah.

It also rebukes wealthy professing Christians who refuse to give toward God's cause.

But **the weapons of Christian ministers are not physical or military weapons**.

Their means of bringing people into obedience to Christ are **faithful preaching, holy living, earnest prayer and patient suffering when called to it**.

## Ezra thanks God (verses 27 and 28)

Ezra blessed God for **two things**.

**First, his commission.** Whenever we find good in our hearts or in others', we must acknowledge that God put it there and praise him.

He is the One who works in us both to **will and to do what is good**.

**Second, his encouragement.** God had extended mercy toward Ezra.

Ezra acted bravely, yet attributed his courage to **God's hand**, not to his own strength.

When he supports us, we are bold and joyful.

When he withdraws his help, we become weak as water.

Whatever good we can accomplish for God or our neighbors, **all the glory belongs to him**.
