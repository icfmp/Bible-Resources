# 1 Timothy 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle salutes Timothy. | 1–4 |
| The design of the law as given by Moses. | 5–11 |
| Of his own conversion and call to the apostleship. | 12–17 |
| The obligation to maintain faith and a good conscience. | 18–20 |

## Christ our hope; teaching that builds up faith (verses 1–4)

**Jesus Christ is the Christian's hope**.

Every hope we possess of everlasting life rests upon him.

When Christ lives within us, he is **the hope of glory**.

Paul appears to have been the one God used to bring **Timothy to faith**.

Timothy then served beside Paul in gospel ministry, like an obedient son working with a loving father.

Teaching that provokes endless uncertainty and argument does not **build up the church**.

Speculative disputes often tear down Christian fellowship rather than strengthen it.

The only way to preserve and grow in **godliness of heart and conduct** is to exercise faith continually in **God's truth and promises through Jesus Christ**.

## The purpose of the law is genuine love (verses 5–11)

Anything that weakens our love **for God or for fellow believers** undermines the purpose of God's commandments.

The gospel fulfills its purpose when sinners, through **repentance toward God and faith in Jesus Christ**, are moved to practice **Christian love**.

People who are declared righteous in **God's appointed way** do not find the moral law an enemy.

But unless we are **justified through faith in Christ** and genuinely turn from sin, the law's curse still stands against us.

Even according to Christ's glorious gospel, an unrepentant person remains **unfit for heaven's holy happiness**.

## Paul's conversion displays Christ's mercy to sinners (verses 12–17)

Paul knew he would justly have perished if the Lord had counted his sins strictly and judged him for every wrongdoing.

He survived spiritually only because of the extraordinary **mercy and grace** shown to him when he was **dead in sin**.

God worked faith and love for Jesus within his heart.

The statement is **true and wholly reliable**: **the Son of God willingly and deliberately came into the world to save sinners**.

No one who considers Paul's experience can reasonably doubt Christ's **love or power to save** someone who truly desires to trust him.

Jesus Christ **died on the cross**.

He now reigns on **the throne of glory** and saves all who come to God through him.

Let us praise the grace of **God our Savior** and give glory to **Father, Son and Holy Spirit—three Persons in one Godhead**—for everything accomplished in us, through us and for us.

## Fight the good fight with faith and conscience (verses 18–20)

Christian ministry is **warfare against sin and Satan** under Jesus Christ, **the Captain of salvation**.

The good hopes other believers place in us ought to encourage us to perform our duty.

In every responsibility, we should seek **an upright conscience**.

Even the severest judgments of the early church were intended **to stop further wrongdoing and bring the offender to repentance**.

Anyone tempted to throw away a good conscience or use the gospel as an excuse for sin should remember that this is also the way to **make shipwreck of faith**.
