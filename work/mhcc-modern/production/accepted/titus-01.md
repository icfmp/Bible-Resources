# Titus 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The apostle salutes Titus. | 1–4 |
| The qualifications of a faithful pastor. | 5–9 |
| The evil temper and practices of false teachers. | 10–16 |

## Gospel truth leads to godliness and hope (verses 1–4)

Everyone freed from slavery to **sin and Satan** is a servant of God.

True gospel teaching accords with **godliness** and teaches us to reverence the Lord.

The gospel gives not only faith but **hope**, turning heart and mind away from the world and lifting them toward heaven.

God promised its message long ago. We should be thankful for the privilege of hearing it.

Since **faith comes from hearing God's word**, everyone appointed and called to preach must proclaim that word.

**Grace** is God's free favor and acceptance. **Mercy** brings the results of that favor, including pardon for sin and freedom from misery now and forever. **Peace** flows from mercy: reconciliation with God through **Christ our Peace**, and right relationships with ourselves and God's creatures.

Grace is the **source** from which these blessings flow.

## Qualifications for elders and overseers (verses 5–9)

Paul's requirements for **elders, also called bishops or overseers**, agree with those given to Timothy.

Pastors oversee God's flock, set an example and serve as **stewards of God's household**. For that reason their conduct must be **above reproach**.

Paul explains both the qualities they must possess and the conduct they must avoid.

They must be faithful servants of Christ, capable ministers of the **teaching and practice of the gospel**, and examples of good works.

## Confront false teachers without becoming harsh (verses 10–16)

Faithful ministers must resist false teachers promptly, so that their folly is exposed before it spreads further.

These teachers used religion to pursue selfish worldly gain. **Love of money** lay beneath their actions.

They should be refuted by **sound teaching from Scripture**.

Christians should reject conduct condemned even by ordinary human conscience: lying, envy, crafty cruelty, sensual brutality, sloth and idleness.

Yet **Christian gentleness** is not cowardly tolerance of sin and error; nor is it angry impatience.

The character of people and nations may differ, but **the fallen human heart** is deceitful in every place and age.

Even the sharpest correction must seek **the offender's good** and restoration to sound faith.

For those defiled by unbelief, even lawful and good things are misused and turned into sin.

Many claim to know God while denying him through their actions. Such hypocrisy is miserable: **the appearance of godliness without its power**.

But we should be slower to level that accusation at others than to examine whether **it applies to ourselves**.
