# 2 Chronicles 16 — Modern English

## Original heading

**Asa seeks the aid of the Syrians, His death.**

## Asa's misplaced trust and death

A prophet of the Lord faithfully rebuked Asa for making an alliance with Syria.

God is displeased when people distrust him and rely more heavily upon human strength than on his power and goodness.

It is foolish to lean on a broken reed when we have **the Rock of Ages** to support us.

To show Asa his folly, the prophet reminded him that he, of all people, had good reason to trust God, who had already proved such a powerful Helper.

Our many experiences of God's goodness make later distrust more blameworthy.

Yet see how deceitful our hearts can be!

When we have no other source of help and necessity drives us to God, we trust him.

But when other supports appear, we readily depend on them too much.

Asa became angry at the prophet's correction.

What can human beings become when God leaves them to themselves?

Asa abused his authority to persecute God's prophet and was then left to misuse it further by oppressing his own subjects.

**Two years before his death**, Asa developed a disease in his feet.

Using physicians was **his duty**.

His sin and folly lay instead in **trusting those physicians as though they could give what only God could give**.

During every conflict and suffering, we must especially examine our hearts, seeking wholeness toward God through **faith, patience and obedience**.
