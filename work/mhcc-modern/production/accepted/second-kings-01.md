# 2 Kings 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The revolt of Moab—Sickness of Ahaziah, king of Israel. | 1–8 |
| Fire called from heaven by Elijah—Death of Ahaziah. | 9–18 |

## Moab rebels; Ahaziah becomes ill (verses 1–8)

When Ahaziah rebelled against the Lord, **Moab rebelled against him**.

Sin weakens us and brings us into poverty and distress.

A person's rebellion against God is often punished by the rebellion of those who owe that person obedience.

Ahaziah fell through a lattice or railing.

Wherever we are, there is only a step between us and death.

People call their homes their castles, yet no house can protect its occupants from divine judgment.

The whole creation groans under the weight of human sin.

Ultimately it, too, will give way under that burden, like the lattice through which Ahaziah fell.

No one is safe while making God an enemy.

Those who refuse to consult God's word to find comfort will eventually hear it as a warning of judgment, whether they wish to hear it or not.

## Elijah calls down fire; Ahaziah dies (verses 9–18)

Elijah prayed for fire from heaven to consume the arrogant and defiant men sent against him.

He did not do so merely to preserve his own safety.

His purpose was to confirm his prophetic mission and reveal God's anger against ungodliness and injustice.

Henry explains that Elijah acted by **an exceptional divine prompting**.

But when Jesus' disciples wanted to do something similar, **the Savior refused to permit it** (Luke 9:54).

The gospel's dispensation of **the Spirit and grace does not allow his disciples to act in that way**.

Elijah was moved by concern for God's glory, whereas the disciples were defending their own reputation.

The Lord judges deeds according to the motives behind them, and his judgment is true.

The third captain humbled himself and appealed to the mercy of God and Elijah.

Nothing good can be gained by fighting against God.

Those who learn submission from the dreadful consequences of others' stubbornness act wisely.

The courage of faith has often frightened even the proudest sinners.

Ahaziah was so terrified by Elijah's message that neither he nor anyone around him attempted to harm the prophet.

Who can hurt someone under God's protection?

Like Ahaziah, many people expecting to prosper through sin are suddenly taken from this life.

Everything in this chapter warns us to seek the Lord while he may be found.
