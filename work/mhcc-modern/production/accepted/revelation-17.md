# Revelation 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| One of the angels who had the vials, explains the meaning of the former vision of the antichristian beast that was to reign 1260 years, and then to be destroyed. | 1–6 |
| And interprets the mystery of the woman, and the beast that had seven heads and ten horns. | 7–18 |

## The woman on the scarlet beast (verses 1–6)

**Henry identifies the woman with Rome**, and more particularly interprets the symbols as pointing from pagan Rome toward what he calls **papal Rome**.

In his account, pagan Rome conquered and ruled mainly through **military power**, while allowing subject nations much of their previous religion and custom.

He accuses the later papal system of gaining and keeping power over kings and nations through political manipulation, deception, and promises of spiritual advantage.

This is **Henry's historical Protestant polemical interpretation**, not an identity conclusively settled by the text of Revelation or a statement about every Roman Catholic believer.

Worldly honor, riches, ceremony and pride appeal to minds governed by bodily desires and worldly ambitions.

Yet outward prosperity and splendor provide **no security against God's judgment**.

Henry understands the **golden cup** as a symbol of enticing appearances, deception and influence that lead others into idolatry and sin.

The woman is called **the mother of prostitutes**, picturing a power that trains others in false worship and wickedness.

She is also described as drunk with the **blood of the saints and martyrs of Jesus**, persecuting them without being satisfied.

Henry is horrified by the extraordinary quantities of Christian blood shed by people who called themselves Christians.

He believes such persecution gives solemn confirmation to the warnings found in biblical prophecy.

Do not be deceived by **fashionable, profitable or outwardly magnificent religion**.

Turn instead from the **mystery of iniquity** and study the great **mystery of godliness**.

Look to Christ's example and learn **humility and thankfulness**.

The more we resemble him, the less easily false religion deceives us.

## The beast's heads, horns and final defeat (verses 7–14)

The beast **“was, and is not, and yet is.”**

In Henry's reading, the ancient form of pagan Roman idolatry and persecution had disappeared, yet another form of religious domination later revived similar patterns.

Those living within its influence would submit blindly to deception, except for the faithful remnant God had chosen.

The beast's **seven heads** were, in his interpretation, the **seven hills of Rome** and **seven forms of government**.

He explained the sequence as **five already past, one existing when John wrote, and one still future**.

In his polemical scheme, the papacy became an **eighth ruling authority**, restoring idolatry in a new form.

The **ten horns** represented ten kings whose kingdoms were yet to rise when John received the vision.

Henry associated them with kingdoms arising after the Roman Empire's breakup, temporarily supporting Rome's interests.

These are specific interpretive claims made by Henry, not a historical reconstruction independently established by the biblical text.

Whatever the details of those symbols, **Christ must reign until every enemy is under his feet**.

He wins because he is **King of kings and Lord of lords**, holding ultimate authority over every earthly and spiritual power.

His followers are called and equipped for faithful service and will remain loyal to him.

## The rulers turn against the woman (verses 15–18)

God governs even the hearts and purposes of rulers.

Through his Providence they accomplish what he has foretold **without intending to fulfill his plan**.

The kings eventually discover their foolishness and how the woman had deceived and enslaved them.

They become instruments in her destruction.

Henry connects the great city that ruled kings in John's day with **Rome**.

In the final conflict, people who had united to commit wrongdoing turn upon one another with hatred and fury.

The wicked may become active agents of each other's downfall.

Believers, however, will be welcomed into the Lord's glory.

**The Lord's people are his own portion**.

His purposes stand firm, and he will accomplish all that pleases him, for his glory and the lasting happiness of those who serve him.
