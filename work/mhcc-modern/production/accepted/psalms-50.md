# Psalm 50 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The glory of God. | 1–6 |
| Sacrifices to be changed for prayers. | 7–15 |
| Sincere obedience required. | 16–23 |

## God's glory and judgment (verses 1–6)

This psalm instructs us about **Christ's coming** and the final judgment, when God will call humanity to account.

The Holy Spirit is the Spirit of judgment.

Everyone needs to know how to worship the Lord **in spirit and truth**.

On the great day, those who refused to hear God's law will be made to hear his judgment.

Happy are people who enter the **covenant of grace** through faith in the Redeemer's atoning sacrifice and show genuine love through righteous conduct.

God rejects people who rest upon outward religious performances.

He graciously receives those who seek him sincerely.

Only through **Christ, the great Sacrifice**, can anyone be accepted by God. The sacrifices of the Mosaic law had value only through their relation to him.

God's judgments are righteous and true.

Even sinners' consciences will finally acknowledge his justice.

## Prayer and thanksgiving beyond empty ritual (verses 7–15)

Obedience is better than sacrifice, and loving God and neighbor is worth more than every burnt offering.

We must never rest in the performance of ceremonies—or in any external form of religion.

**God demands the heart**.

How could invented religious forms please him when repentance, faith and holiness are neglected?

In distress, we should call upon the Lord through earnest prayer.

Even when suffering comes under his providential correction, it should **drive us toward him, not away from him**.

We should acknowledge him in all our ways, depend upon his wisdom, power and goodness, and entrust ourselves wholly to him.

Thus we maintain fellowship with God, meeting difficulty with **prayer** and deliverance with **praise**.

A believing worshiper receives not only a gracious answer but also **grace to thank God** for that answer.

## God requires sincere obedience (verses 16–23)

**Hypocrisy is wickedness**, and God will judge it.

People who teach God's laws to others too often live in disobedience to those same commands.

They deceive themselves by abusing God's patience and misunderstanding his character and gospel.

At final judgment, sinners' wrongdoing will be exposed completely.

God will set their sins in order—from childhood and youth through adulthood and old age—to their lasting shame and terror.

Those who have forgotten him, persisted in wickedness or neglected salvation should recognize the urgency of their condition.

The Lord's patience is extraordinary, especially considering how badly people misuse it.

If they refuse to turn, they will recognize their mistake when it is too late.

**People who forget God also forget who they themselves are**.

Our highest purpose is to glorify him.

Whoever offers true praise gives him glory, and such spiritual sacrifices are accepted through **our Lord Jesus Christ**, the Priest and the altar to whom we entrust them.

Let us praise God fervently in spirit, receive his mercy with gratitude and seek to glorify him **in both words and deeds**.
