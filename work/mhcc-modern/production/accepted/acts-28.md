# Acts 28 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Paul kindly received at Melita. | 1–10 |
| He arrives at Rome. | 11–16 |
| His conference with the Jews. | 17–22 |
| Paul preaches to the Jews, and abides at Rome a prisoner. | 23–31 |

## Paul receives kindness on Malta (verses 1–10)

God can turn strangers into friends, especially during suffering.

People whose manners are regarded as unsophisticated may show more kindness than those thought to be highly civilized.

The islanders' compassion condemns many inhabitants of supposedly civilized nations who profess Christianity but fail to help people in distress.

When the viper attached itself to Paul, the islanders assumed he was a murderer whom **divine justice was punishing**.

They understood that God rules the world and that events do not happen through mere chance, not even the smallest events.

They also understood that wicked deeds deserve punishment and good actions deserve reward, and that murder is particularly terrible.

But they made **two mistakes**.

**First**, they assumed God punishes every wicked person **during this life**.

In fact, some receive judgment now as evidence of his Providence, while others remain unpunished for a time as a warning of **judgment to come**.

**Second**, they assumed everyone who suffers an extraordinary calamity must be extraordinarily wicked.

Revelation corrects this error. Godly people often experience great afflictions that test and strengthen their faith and patience.

Paul escaped the bite unharmed.

Likewise, by Christ's grace, believers may shake off Satan's temptations with holy determination.

When other people insult and accuse us, we may cast away their unjust reproaches as Paul cast the snake into the fire, provided our consciences testify to our integrity.

Such attacks can do no lasting harm unless they draw us away from our duty.

God made Paul remarkable among the islanders, preparing the way for them to receive the gospel.

Wherever the Lord leads his people, he can raise up friends and make them **a blessing to others who are suffering**.

## Paul reaches Rome (verses 11–16)

Ordinary travel details are seldom worth remembering.

But fellowship among believers and kindness shown by friends deserve to be recorded.

The Christians in Rome were not ashamed of Paul because he was a prisoner.

They were even more careful to show him affection and respect, greatly comforting him.

When friends treat us kindly, we should recognize **God moving their hearts** and give him thanks.

When we meet people who honor Christ in unfamiliar places, we have reason to praise God.

How many powerful conquerors have entered Rome in magnificent triumph, crowned with honor, yet brought harm to the world!

Paul entered chained as a poor prisoner, but **he was a greater blessing to humanity than any of them**.

This should correct our admiration for worldly status.

God can give prisoners favor in the eyes of their captors.

When he does not immediately release his people from captivity, he may still **make their bondage easier or give them peace while enduring it**.

For this, they can be thankful.

## Paul meets the Jewish leaders (verses 17–22)

Paul was honored by the fact that those examining him had found no criminal wrongdoing.

He had appealed to Caesar to clear his name, **not to accuse his nation**.

True Christianity concerns what matters to **all human beings**, rather than narrow opinions or private interests.

It seeks no earthly reward as its ultimate goal; its benefits are **spiritual and eternal**.

Yet Christ's holy religion has always been spoken against.

In towns and villages where Jesus is proclaimed as the only Savior and people are called to a changed life, those who follow him are still dismissed as a sect, party or dangerous group.

Such reproaches will continue as long as people live in opposition to God.

## Paul preaches Christ while imprisoned (verses 23–31)

Paul tried to persuade Jewish hearers concerning Jesus.

Some were affected by God's word; others became harder.

Some received light, while others **closed their eyes**.

The gospel has always produced these different responses.

Paul warned them that the Holy Spirit had described such resistance beforehand.

Those who hear the gospel but pay no attention should tremble.

Who will heal them if they refuse God's remedy?

The listeners went away debating among themselves.

But **people can argue extensively without reasoning rightly**.

They may find fault with one another while refusing the truth.

Discussion alone cannot convince sinners unless **God's grace opens their understanding**.

Although we grieve for those rejecting salvation, we should rejoice that God sends his salvation to others who will receive it.

If we are among them, we should thank him for the grace that made us different.

Paul remained resolved to know and preach **Jesus Christ and him crucified**.

When Christians are distracted from their chief purpose, let them ask: **What does this have to do with Jesus? Does it bring us nearer to him and help us walk in him?**

Paul preached **Christ, not himself**, without being ashamed of the gospel.

He had only limited opportunities for service while imprisoned, but he did not complain.

The door opened to him was narrow, yet no one was permitted to close it.

God made that opportunity fruitful for many, so that believers appeared even in **Nero's household (Philippians 4:22)**.

Paul's chains themselves promoted the gospel (**Philippians 1:13**).

Believers in Rome and Christians throughout the world have reason to thank God that Paul spent a mature period of his Christian life in captivity.

Henry considers it **probable** that, while imprisoned and perhaps chained to a soldier, Paul wrote **Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians and Hebrews**.

This is Henry's historical interpretation, **not a verified assertion about the disputed authorship of Hebrews**.

These writings display great Christian love and spiritual experience.

Today's believers may possess less of Paul's sense of triumph and heavenly joy.

Yet everyone who follows the same Savior has **the same certainty of final safety and peace**.

May we live increasingly in the Savior's love and seek his glory in every deed.

Through his strength, may we overcome our enemies now.

And through his undeserved grace and mercy, may we share the company of those who sit with him on his throne, as he overcame and sat at the Father's right hand forever.
