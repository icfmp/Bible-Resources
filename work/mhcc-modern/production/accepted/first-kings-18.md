# 1 Kings 18 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Elijah sends Ahab notice of his coming. | 1–16 |
| Elijah meets Ahab. | 17–20 |
| Elijah's trial of the false prophets. | 21–40 |
| Elijah, by prayer, obtains rain. | 41–46 |

## Elijah prepares to meet Ahab (verses 1–16)

Even the severest judgments cannot, by themselves, humble or change a sinner's heart.

Only **the blood of Jesus Christ** can atone for sin's guilt, and only **the sanctifying Spirit of God** can cleanse its corruption.

The priests and Levites had gone to Judah and Jerusalem (2 Chronicles 11:13–14).

In their place God raised up teachers who read and explained his word.

These prophets probably came from the schools established earlier by Samuel.

They did not possess Elijah's special prophetic gift, but they taught the people to remain faithful to the God of Israel.

Jezebel tried to destroy these teachers.

The few who escaped death were forced to hide.

God preserves faithful people among every social rank, both high and low.

He accepts the faith, reverence and love produced by his Spirit through the Redeemer.

Observe how wonderfully he raises up friends for his servants, giving shelter in difficult times.

Bread and water were scarce.

Yet **Obadiah** found enough to keep the hidden prophets alive.

Ahab worked hard to save his **livestock**, but did nothing to save his own **soul**.

He searched for grass instead of seeking God's favor.

He tried to protect himself from the consequences of judgment without asking how to remove its cause.

It is a hopeful sign for a people when God calls his ministers to appear openly again.

Then even the bread of affliction is easier to bear because they can see and hear their teachers.

## Elijah confronts Ahab (verses 17–20)

We can often tell how people feel about God from how they treat his people and ministers.

Even the best and most useful servants, including Elijah, have been accused of disturbing a nation.

But **those who bring God's judgment upon themselves through wrongdoing cause the trouble**, not those who announce the warning and call people to repentance.

## Elijah and the prophets of Baal (verses 21–40)

Many Israelites wavered in their convictions and changed their conduct.

Elijah called them to decide whether **Jehovah or Baal** was the true, self-existent, supreme God, the Creator, Governor and Judge of the world—and then to follow him alone.

It is dangerous to waver between serving God and serving sin, between Christ's rule and the control of our sinful desires.

If Jesus is the only Savior, we should trust him alone for everything.

If the Bible is God's word, we should reverence and receive **all of it**, submitting our understanding to the divine teaching it contains.

Elijah proposed a test.

Baal had every outward advantage, but God's servants learned they need never fear people's opposition when defending the truth.

Let the God who answers by fire be acknowledged as God.

**Sacrifice and atonement came before the removal of judgment in mercy**.

The God who can pardon sin and signify his acceptance by consuming the sin offering is the One able to bring relief from the nation's calamity.

God has never required his worshipers to honor him through the practices of Baal's followers.

Serving the devil may sometimes flatter bodily pleasure, but it is ultimately cruel, as envy, drunkenness and false religion show.

The Lord calls us to put sinful desires to death, but he takes no pleasure in **bodily penances and needless harsh treatment**.

Who commanded those practices?

A few words prayed with confident faith, earnest love for God's glory and concern for souls can accomplish far more than bodily torment.

Such prayer may arise from longing for God's likeness and his favor.

The earnest prayer of a righteous person is powerful.

Elijah was seeking **God's glory and Israel's good**, not personal recognition.

When fire fell, the people agreed: Jehovah is God.

Some, we hope, were genuinely changed in heart.

**Most may have been convinced without being converted.**

Blessed are those who have not seen such a miracle and yet believe and respond to God more deeply than some who witnessed it.

## Elijah prays until rain comes (verses 41–46)

Israel had gone some distance toward reform by acknowledging the Lord as God and agreeing to the judgment of Baal's prophets.

God therefore allowed blessing to return to the land.

Elijah continued praying.

Even when an answer to earnest prayer is delayed, we should keep praying with faith rather than becoming weary or giving up.

At last a little cloud appeared.

It soon spread across the sky and watered the land.

Great blessings often begin almost invisibly.

Heavy rain can come from a cloud the size of a hand.

We should not despise the day of small beginnings.

The same is true of God's gracious work within the soul.

The Spirit's first movements may be barely perceptible, but eventually grow to the astonishment of people and the praise of angels.

Elijah sent Ahab home and accompanied him.

God will strengthen his people for every service to which his commands and providence call them.

God's justice and holiness can frighten sinners, produce confessions and secure outward obedience for a time.

But they must also learn his **mercy, love and truth in Jesus Christ** before they are drawn to genuine humility, trust and love.

The Holy Spirit uses both in converting sinners.

When people are impressed by divine truth, we should urge them to practice the duties Christ commands.

## Editorial review — 1 Kings 18

**Complete secondary original-English text read:** [CCEL, 1 Kings 18](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xii.xviii.html). Pinned original **MHCC 0.1.1** remains authoritative for native SWORD entry and annotation alignment and later independent acceptance.

- **1–16:** judgment cannot regenerate; **Christ's blood/Spirit's cleansing**, priests/Levites departing with **2 Chronicles 11:13–14**, ordinary teachers rather than Elijah-style prophets, Jezebel persecuting them, **Obadiah feeding hidden prophets**, Ahab caring for **animals rather than his soul** — retained.
- **17–20:** reputation of ministers judged through hostility and **true trouble caused by sinners, not the messengers** — retained.
- **21–40:** indecision between **Jehovah/Baal**, full Bible accepted, God answering through fire and atonement **before rain**, **bodily self-injury contrasted with sincere short prayer**, Elijah seeking divine glory, **conviction not automatically conversion** — retained.
- **41–46:** continuing prayer, little cloud to great rain, **imperceptible beginnings of grace**, divine strength, judgment producing merely outward fear versus Christ's mercy drawing genuine trust and love — retained.

**Secondary textual defect:** the CCEL witness says “If the Bible be the **world** of God,” while the intended sense is “word of God.” The modern text gives the intelligible sense, **but the published MHCC 0.1.1 must be checked before identifying this as an original error or approving any restoration**. No source changes are made.
