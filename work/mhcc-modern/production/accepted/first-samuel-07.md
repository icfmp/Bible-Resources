# 1 Samuel 7 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The ark removed to Kirjath-jearim. | 1–4 |
| The Israelites solemnly repent. | 5, 6 |
| The Lord discomfits the Philistines. | 7–12 |
| They are subdued, Samuel judges Israel. | 13–17 |

## The ark is brought to Kiriath-jearim (verses 1–4)

God will provide a resting place for his ark.

If some people push it away, he will incline others to receive it.

There is nothing unusual about the ark being kept in a private home.

Christ and his apostles taught **from house to house** when public places were unavailable.

**Twenty years** passed before Israel deeply felt the lack of God's ark.

During that time Samuel worked to revive true religion.

The Bible uses very few words to describe it, but they speak powerfully.

It was one of Israel's most effective seasons of spiritual renewal.

## Israel confesses its sin (verses 5 and 6)

The people drew water and poured it out before the Lord.

This expressed humility and sorrow for wrongdoing.

They poured out their hearts before him in repentance.

Their confession was frank and complete.

They firmly resolved to abandon their sinful practices.

Publicly they acknowledged, **“We have sinned against the Lord.”**

In doing so, they gave God the glory and accepted the shame of their own guilt.

If we confess our sins in this way, we may trust that God is faithful and righteous to forgive us.

## God defeats the Philistines (verses 7–12)

The Philistines invaded Israel.

When sinners begin to repent and change their conduct, they should expect Satan to bring his whole force against them and work through every available instrument to discourage them.

The Israelites urgently asked Samuel to pray for them.

What comfort it is that our great Intercessor in heaven **never stops praying for us**!

Jesus Christ continually appears in God's presence on our behalf.

Samuel's sacrifice without prayer would have been an empty picture without the reality to which it pointed.

The Lord answered graciously.

Samuel set up a memorial to honor God and encourage Israel.

Across the generations, the church has had many reasons to erect its own **Ebenezers**, monuments to fresh deliverances.

Neither outward persecution nor inward sin has prevailed against the Lord's church, for **“Thus far the Lord has helped us.”**

He will continue helping his people until the end of the world.

## Samuel continues to lead Israel (verses 13–17)

During this great revival of true religion, the ark was not returned to Shiloh.

Nor was it placed with the tabernacle anywhere else.

Henry sees this lack of emphasis on the old ceremonial arrangement as evidence that its principal purpose lay in what it **pictured**.

When the spiritual meaning was neglected, the ceremonies became empty activity.

They could not compare with **repentance, faith and love toward God and other people**.
