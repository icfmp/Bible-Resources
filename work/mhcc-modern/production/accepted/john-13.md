# John 13 — Modern English

## Jesus washes his disciples' feet (verses 1–17)

The Lord Jesus has people who belong to him. He purchased them at great cost, set them apart and called them to devote themselves to him.

**Those he loves, he loves to the end.** Nothing can separate a true believer from Christ's love.

Because we do not know when our last hour will arrive, we must never neglect the constant preparation it requires.

We cannot fully understand how the devil enters human hearts. Yet some sins are so dreadful, with so little ordinary enticement from the world or flesh, that Henry sees Satan's direct influence in them.

Jesus washed his disciples' feet to teach us that no task should be considered beneath us if it glorifies God and helps our fellow believers. We must undertake our duties and remove whatever hinders us.

The washing pictured **the cleansing of souls from sin's pollution**.

The disciples did not yet know why Jesus did everything he did. Events that initially seem painful may later reveal his kindness.

**Rejecting the gospel because its gifts seem too generous to be true is unbelief, not humility.**

Only those spiritually washed by Christ have a share in him. He both **justifies and sanctifies** everyone whom he saves.

Peter went beyond submission and asked to be washed completely. Genuine desire for holiness seeks purity in **every faculty and part of the person**.

Believers receive their fundamental cleansing when they receive Christ as Savior. Yet those already justified must attend to **daily cleansing**, confessing fresh guilt and resisting whatever might defile them.

Yesterday's pardon should equip us to withstand today's temptation.

Nor should we be surprised if a hypocrite among Christ's professed followers is exposed.

The Lord also taught **mutual service**. We must accept help from one another as well as give it.

How unworthy it is to lord over fellow believers when our Master himself served them! His love continues after reconciling people who had once been his enemies.

## Jesus foretells the betrayal (verses 18–30)

Jesus had often spoken about his approaching suffering and death, but the betrayal by Judas caused him particular grief.

The sins of those professing Christ's name grieve him. We should not restrict this warning to Judas.

Think of the **unbeliever** who studies Scripture only to undermine its authority, the **hypocrite** who professes Scripture while refusing to obey it, and the **apostate** who abandons Christ for a worthless advantage.

Human beings eat the bread supplied by divine Providence and then raise their heel against their generous Provider.

Judas departed as though tired of Jesus and his apostles.

Those who practice evil love darkness rather than light.

## Jesus commands the disciples to love one another (verses 31–38)

### The glory of the cross and the new command (verses 31–35)

Jesus had displayed glory through many miracles, yet spoke of his coming suffering as bringing **greater glory than any miracle of his humbled ministry**.

Through the cross, satisfaction was made for the wrong done to God's honor by human sin.

The disciples could not yet follow him to heavenly happiness. Believers will follow later, but must now wait and fulfill his work.

Jesus gave them a **new commandment**: they were to love one another **for his sake and by his example**, seeking each other's good and advancing the gospel together, like one body animated by one spirit.

Strangely, this command remains new to many professing believers. People attend to almost any other teaching of Christ before this one.

If Christians fail to love one another, they give others reason to doubt the sincerity of their discipleship.

### Peter's confidence and Christ's warning (verses 36–38)

Peter passed over Jesus' command to love and questioned him about matters Jesus had deliberately kept hidden.

We often want to learn the **secret things that belong to God** rather than the **revealed duties that belong to us and our children**.

We prefer satisfying curiosity to directing our consciences, or debating heavenly mysteries to learning how to reach heaven.

Plain and helpful truth is often abandoned while doubtful disputes become endless quarrels.

We dislike being told we cannot do something, forgetting that **without Christ we can do nothing**.

Jesus knows us better than we know ourselves. He has many ways to reveal the condition of those he loves and to **hide pride from them**.

May we preserve the unity of the Spirit through peace, love one another sincerely and walk humbly with our God.
