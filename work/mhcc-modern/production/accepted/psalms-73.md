# Psalm 73 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |

| The psalmist's temptation. | 1–14 |
| How he gained a victory over it. | 15–20 |
| How he profited by it. | 21–28 |

## The temptation to envy the wicked (verses 1–14)

The psalmist was strongly tempted to envy prosperous wicked people.

Many of God's faithful servants have faced the same testing.

He began, however, with an unshakable truth: **God is good**.

Remembering that goodness guards the mind against Satan's temptations.

Even strong believers can find their faith badly shaken. Some storms test the firmest anchors.

Foolish and wicked people sometimes enjoy great outward success and seem to escape many troubles.

They may live without fearing God and still become wealthy and influential.

Some remain relatively healthy until death and die without much pain, while godly people may spend their lives in illness and die with severe suffering.

Others experience no apparent terror when facing death.

But **we cannot judge a person's state after death from the circumstances of that death**.

These observations made God's people uncertain what to say, especially when their own suffering was bitter.

The psalmist spoke from real pain.

He felt tempted to believe that serving God had been pointless.

Yet genuine sanctification calls us to cleanse **both soul and body** from sin.

The heart is purified by **Christ's blood received through faith** and by the Holy Spirit beginning his work within.

That inward change appears outwardly in a heartfelt determination to pursue holiness and a blameless way of life.

**Serving God and keeping his ordinances are never in vain**.

## The sanctuary reveals the end of the wicked (verses 15–20)

The psalmist restrained himself from speaking his harmful doubts to others.

He still respected God's people and did not want to injure their faith.

When we stop ourselves from giving voice to evil thoughts, that restraint may show genuine repentance for those thoughts.

Few things wound God's people more than hearing that serving the Lord is useless, because this contradicts their whole experience.

He prayed for understanding and came before God in **the sanctuary**.

There he considered **the end toward which the wicked were moving**.

They seemed secure at the height of prosperity, yet were preparing for ruin.

A suffering righteous person's troubles end in peace.

The wicked person's pleasures end in destruction.

Their prosperity is fleeting and precarious, like walking on **slippery ground**.

It is a show without substance, **a shadow or a dream** that appears pleasant during sleep but may itself disturb the sleeper.

## Learning to draw nearer to God (verses 21–28)

God would not allow such a temptation if his grace were insufficient not merely to preserve his people, but also to make them **spiritually richer through the trial**.

Envy and discontent hurt the soul.

Looking back, the psalmist recognized his own folly and ignorance.

When good people fall into wrong thoughts, words or actions during intense temptation, they should afterward acknowledge them with sorrow and shame.

The credit for deliverance belongs **not to our wisdom but to God's gracious presence and Christ's intercession**.

Those committed to the Lord receive counsel from **his word and Spirit**, their best guides here.

Then he receives them into glory.

The hope of heaven can reconcile believers to dark and mysterious earthly Providence.

This experience drew the psalmist **closer to God**.

Even heaven would not make us happy without **God's presence and love**.

The world's beauty and splendor vanish.

Our bodies lose their strength through illness, age and death. When bodily strength fails, confidence and comfort often fail too.

But **Jesus Christ** offers to be everything needed by any poor sinner willing to renounce other final hopes.

Our sins have separated us from God.

A mere profession of Christ while we continue in deliberate sin only increases condemnation.

Let us therefore draw near and remain near through **faith and prayer**.

Those who sincerely trust the Lord always have reason to thank him.

Blessed Lord, since you have promised to be our portion in the coming world, keep us from choosing anything else as our portion here.
