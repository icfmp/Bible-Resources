# Exodus 31 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

## Bezalel and Oholiab receive gifts for the work (verses 1–11)

The Israelites had worked as builders and brickmakers in Egypt.

They had not learned the skill needed to produce the intricate workmanship required for the tabernacle.

But the same Holy Spirit who later enabled the apostles to speak in many languages miraculously gave Bezalel and Oholiab the abilities they lacked.

Honor received from God always comes with work to do.

To be employed in his service is itself a great honor.

When God calls people to a task, he either finds them fitted for it or makes them fit for it.

The Lord gives different gifts to different people.

We should each give diligent attention to the particular work entrusted to us.

And whatever skill or wisdom we possess, we must remember that God has put it into our hearts so that we may obey his commands.

## The Sabbath must still be observed (verses 12–17)

God had ordered a tabernacle to be built for his worship.

But the people were not to imagine that the sacredness of the work, or their need to finish it quickly, gave them permission to build on the Sabbath.

The Hebrew word *shabbath* means **rest** or **ceasing from work**.

The Sabbath points toward the rest in glory that remains for God's people.

For this reason, Henry argues, the Sabbath's moral obligation continues until time itself gives way to eternity.

## Moses receives the stone tablets (verse 18)

God wrote his law on tablets of stone.

Their material showed how enduring the law is.

But it also illustrated the hardness of the human heart.

It would be easier to carve words into stone than to write anything truly good upon our naturally corrupt hearts.

The tablets were written by the finger of God—by his will and power.

Only God can write his law on the human heart.

He gives us hearts of flesh.

Then, through his Spirit, who is called the finger of God, he writes his will within us (2 Corinthians 3:3).
