# Proverbs 20 — Modern English

## Verse 1 — The folly of drunkenness

Even highly capable people, as well as those lacking education, may make themselves foolish through the taste and excitement of **strong drink**.

## Verse 2 — Fear the greater King

Earthly rulers may terrify those who provoke them. How much more foolish it is to provoke **the King of kings**!

## Verse 3 — Choose peace over quarrels

Seeking disputes is great folly. For peace's sake, be willing to yield **even a claim that is technically just**.

## Verse 4 — Work now for a future harvest

Those who labor patiently in preparing for **eternity** are also likely to be diligent in honest earthly responsibilities.

## Verse 5 — Draw out wise counsel

People capable of giving valuable advice do not always volunteer it. Those who patiently draw it out will be well rewarded.

## Verse 6 — Do good rather than boast

It is rare to find people who do **more good than they talk about**, or than they even care to hear praised.

## Verse 7 — Integrity gives peace to the family

Honest people need not constantly devise schemes or fear their past deceit. Their households also benefit from their integrity.

## Verse 8 — The responsibility of good rulers

People in high authority who are themselves good can accomplish much good and prevent much evil.

## Verse 9 — Cleansing is the Holy Spirit's work

By grace, some can truthfully say they are purer than before. But this improvement is **the work of the Holy Spirit**.

## Verse 10 — God rejects dishonest commerce

Love of money produces many kinds of deceit. The Lord will not bless wealth acquired through fraudulent practices.

## Verse 11 — Watch children's behavior

Parents should carefully observe their children's conduct so they can guide and correct them appropriately.

## Verse 12 — Use God's gifts for God

All our powers, senses and abilities come from God and should be used for his service.

## Verse 13 — Work rather than indulge idleness

Those who surrender themselves to sleep and pleasure may lack even life's necessities, which honest labor could have supplied.

## Verse 14 — Do not cheat in bargaining

People sometimes manipulate sellers or buyers in order to get a favorable price. They should be **ashamed of fraud and lies**, not proud of a bargain.

## Verse 15 — Knowledge exceeds riches

Those who prefer true knowledge to wealth follow the path of godliness and happiness. If we believed this fully, **God's word** would be cherished and worldly wealth would lose its seductive attraction.

## Verse 16 — Avoid reckless guarantees

Those who guarantee obligations recklessly or entangle themselves in immoral relationships put themselves in danger. Such arrangements do not deserve our confidence.

## Verse 17 — Fraud's sweetness turns bitter

Dishonest gain may taste sweet to a sinful mind pleased by its own cleverness, but the memory of it will become bitter.

## Verse 18 — Counsel for spiritual conflict

We especially need wise counsel in **spiritual warfare**. God's **word and Spirit** are the best advisers on every question.

## Verse 19 — Do not purchase flattering praise

People who trust someone simply because that person speaks pleasantly may pay dearly for such admiration.

## Verse 20 — The misery of disrespecting parents

Children who persist in dishonoring their parents will find neither genuine peace nor comfort.

## Verse 21 — Sudden riches can vanish

A fortune gained too quickly may disappear just as quickly.

## Verse 22 — Wait for the Lord

Trust the Lord's timing, obey his will, and rely upon him for protection rather than seeking revenge.

## Verse 23 — Dishonesty makes a bad bargain

A deal achieved through fraud eventually proves to be a loss.

## Verse 24 — Depend on God for every undertaking

We cannot wisely plan our lives or manage our affairs **independently of the Lord**.

## Verse 25 — Watch conscience's evasions

The excuses people offer to quiet their own consciences show how deceitful the fallen heart can be.

## Verse 26 — Justice distinguishes right from wrong

Just authority should restrain and punish evil, distinguishing wicked conduct from virtuous conduct.

## Verse 27 — Conscience as a lamp

The rational soul and conscience are like a lamp within us. We should use that light to examine **our motives and attitudes by God's revealed will**.

## Verse 28 — The glories of God's throne

**Mercy and truth** are the glories of God's kingship.

## Verse 29 — Different ages, different strengths

Both young and old possess distinctive advantages. Neither should envy or despise the other.

## Verse 30 — Correction may purify

Severe correction can sometimes accomplish real good. Fallen human nature makes us dislike rebuke, but if God uses painful trials to purify our hearts and prepare us for his work, we have reason to thank him.
