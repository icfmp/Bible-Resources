# Psalm 102 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A sorrowful complaint of great afflictions. | 1–11 |
| Encouragement by expecting the performances of God's promises to his church. | 12–22 |
| The unchangeableness of God. | 23–28 |

## Giving sorrow a voice in prayer (verses 1–11)

All of God's word helps guide our prayers.

But in passages like this, the **Holy Spirit places words directly in our mouths**.

This prayer has been provided for afflicted people; let them bring it before God.

Even faithful believers can be nearly overwhelmed by trouble.

It is both our duty and our good to pray.

A suffering soul finds relief in humbly telling the Lord about its pain.

Still, we must say, **Blessed be the Lord's name, whether he gives or takes away**.

The psalmist viewed himself as nearing death, his days passing **like a shadow**.

## Trusting the Lord's promises for his church (verses 12–22)

We are dying creatures, but **God lives forever** and continues to protect his church.

We can be confident that he will not neglect it.

When we consider our sinfulness, spiritual darkness, lifelessness and the many failings of our prayers, we may fear they will not be received in heaven.

But the gospel assures us otherwise.

**We have an Advocate with the Father**, and we live under **grace rather than law**.

Redemption is the subject of the Christian church's praise.

Israel's earthly rescue and restoration pictured that far greater work.

Lord Jesus, look upon us and bring us into **the glorious freedom of your children**, so that we may bless and praise your name.

## The unchanging God and our passing lives (verses 23–28)

Illness can quickly weaken our strength and cut short the years we expected to have.

We should prepare for that possibility.

We must acknowledge God's hand in this and recognize that even an early death can be consistent with his love.

People who have used their strength faithfully sometimes grow weak.

Others whom we believe the world can hardly spare have their lives shortened.

Amid every change and danger facing the church, it is comforting to remember that **Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today and forever**.

When our bodies die or friends are taken from us, we may remember that **God is everlasting**.

Do not miss the assurance of a happy end to the believer's trials.

Everything else changes, wears out and perishes, like clothing being folded away.

**Jesus lives forever**.

Because he lives, the future of his people is secure.

He has promised, **Because I live, you also will live**.
