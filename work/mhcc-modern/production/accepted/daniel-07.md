# Daniel 7 — Modern English

## The vision of four beasts (verses 1–8)

This vision represents the same future events as **Nebuchadnezzar's dream**.

The great sea disturbed by winds represents **the earth and its people**, shaken by ambitious rulers and conquerors.

The four beasts correspond to **the four empires represented by the four parts of Nebuchadnezzar's image**.

Mighty conquerors are instruments through which God brings judgment upon a sinful world.

The **fierce beasts** portray their hateful character.

Nevertheless, each empire's authority has a **limit**.

God makes even human wrath serve his praise, and he restrains the anger that remains.

## The Son of Man receives his kingdom (verses 9–14)

These verses comfort and strengthen God's people in view of persecutions they would experience.

Many New Testament descriptions of **the coming judgment** clearly refer back to this vision, particularly **Revelation 20:11–12**.

The Messiah is called **the Son of Man**.

He came in the likeness of sinful flesh and lived as a human being, yet **he is also the Son of God**.

Henry understands this passage as foretelling **Christ's glorious coming**, when he will destroy every power opposing him and make **his kingdom universal upon earth**.

Before the final solemn revelation of God's glory and judgment to all creation, each person's destiny will be determined **at death**.

Before the end, the Father will publicly give **his incarnate Son**, our **Mediator and Judge**, the nations as his willing subjects.

## Daniel receives an interpretation (verses 15–28)

We should desire to understand fully and accurately what God has revealed.

Those who seek that understanding should **pray earnestly and faithfully**.

The angel explained the vision to Daniel.

Daniel especially wanted to know about **the little horn** that made war against the saints and overcame them.

Henry interprets that horn as predicting **the persecution of true Christians by papal Rome**.

He also interprets John's later visions as referring first to Rome and as drawing upon Daniel's prophecy.

These are **Henry's historical-prophetic identifications**, not details the biblical symbols themselves explicitly name.

Daniel was given the happy prospect of **God's kingdom prevailing among human beings**.

Henry connects this to **the second coming of Christ**, when God's people will triumph through **the complete overthrow of Satan's kingdom**.

The saints of the Most High will possess the kingdom forever.

Yet we must never conclude from that promise that **earthly political dominion rests upon possession of grace**.

The promised gospel kingdom is a kingdom of **light, holiness, love and grace**.

Its present privileges and comforts are the first taste of the kingdom of glory.

Its complete fulfillment is the **eternal happiness of the saints** in a kingdom that cannot be shaken.

The gathering of **God's entire family** will be among the blessings of Christ's return.
