# 1 Kings 13 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Jeroboam's sin reproved. | 1–10 |
| The prophet deceived. | 11–22 |
| The disobedient prophet is slain, Jeroboam's obstinacy. | 23–34 |

## Jeroboam's altar is condemned (verses 1–10)

By threatening the altar, God's prophet also pronounced judgment upon its founder and worshipers.

False worship would not continue, but the Lord's word would remain forever.

The prophecy plainly announced that David's family would continue and support true religion, while the ten tribes would be powerless to stop what God had decreed.

When the Lord judicially hardens a sinner's heart, the person may become unable to draw back through repentance the hand that was stretched out in wrongdoing.

Such spiritual judgment, represented by Jeroboam's injured hand, is far more dreadful than a physical disability.

When Jeroboam needed help, he turned **not to the golden calves, but to God's power and favor**.

A time may come when people who hate faithful preaching will gladly ask faithful ministers to pray for them.

Yet Jeroboam asked only for his **hand to be healed**.

He did not seek pardon for his sin or a changed heart.

He was briefly impressed by both judgment and mercy, but the impression faded.

God forbade his messenger to eat or drink at Bethel to express his abhorrence of the people's idolatry and apostasy and to teach us not to share in works of darkness.

We have not learned genuine self-denial if we cannot refuse even one forbidden meal.

## The prophet is deceived (verses 11–22)

The old prophet's behavior showed that he was not truly godly.

When Jeroboam changed the nation's worship, this man had preferred his comfort and interests to faithfulness.

He employed a wicked way of persuading God's messenger to return.

His story was entirely a lie.

Believers may be particularly vulnerable to temptation when disobedience comes disguised under plausible claims of holiness.

Why was the lying prophet apparently left unpunished while the faithful messenger, who was deceived, received immediate and severe judgment?

God's judgments are deeper than we can understand.

There will also be a judgment to come.

No excuse can justify deliberate disobedience.

The story illustrates the danger of listening to the great deceiver.

Those who give in to the devil when he tempts them will find him terrifying when he becomes their tormentor.

He flatters people to draw them into sin and afterward works to drive them into despair.

## The disobedient prophet dies; Jeroboam does not repent (verses 23–34)

God is displeased by the sins of his own people.

Neither religious office, closeness to God nor past faithful service protects someone who willfully disobeys him.

God calls those he employs to observe his instructions strictly.

We cannot determine a person's spiritual state merely from suffering, nor measure a sin's guilt by the punishment immediately received.

For some, bodily suffering may lead to the salvation of the spirit.

For others, the body may enjoy prosperity while the soul becomes ready for judgment.

Jeroboam refused to turn from his evil way.

He believed the golden calves would secure his family's crown.

In reality, they contributed to the destruction of that crown and his house.

People who try to preserve their position through sin betray their own interests.

Let us fear **success in sinful ways** rather than treat it as a blessing.

Pray to be guarded against falsehood and temptation, and strengthened to follow God's commands with self-denying perseverance.
