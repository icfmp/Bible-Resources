# Psalm 90 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |

| Submission to Divine chastisements. | 7–11 |

## The eternal God and our brief lives (verses 1–6)

Henry supposes this psalm concerns the judgment pronounced upon Israel in the wilderness (**Numbers 14**).

God's favor and protection are **the only lasting refuge and comfort** for our souls in this troubled world.

**Christ Jesus is the dwelling place and refuge** to whom we may always turn.

Our lives are passing away, and every earthly comfort is temporary.

But **God lives forever**, and believers discover his faithfulness.

When sickness or suffering brings us close to death, God calls us to turn back to him through **repentance and a renewed life**.

A thousand years count for nothing beside his eternity.

There is some relationship between one minute and a million years, but **no comparable measure between time and eternity**.

The events of a thousand years, whether past or future, are more fully present to the eternal mind of God than yesterday's events are to ours.

At the resurrection, **body and soul will be reunited**.

Time slips past almost unnoticed, as when people sleep.

Life resembles **floodwater** that passes quickly.

We flourish briefly like grass.

Old age may cause us to wither gradually, or illness and disaster may cut us down suddenly.

## Receiving God's discipline with reverence (verses 7–11)

The trials of God's people often arise from his fatherly love.

But the judgments of sinners—and believers' chastisements for particular sins—must also be recognized as signs of his displeasure toward wrongdoing.

**God knows every secret sin**. No one succeeds in hiding transgression from him.

Our years cannot be recalled any more than words already spoken.

Life is marked by toil and trouble, and it may be cut short in the years we expected to enjoy.

We should therefore stand in awe before God.

Fallen angels know his anger.

Sinners undergoing final judgment know it.

Yet who among us can fully describe its power?

Few consider it seriously enough.

Those who mock sin or take Christ lightly do not understand divine judgment.

Who can endure that consuming fire?

## Asking for wisdom and enduring mercy (verses 12–17)

To gain true wisdom, we must ask God to teach us through **the Holy Spirit**.

We need his instruction and the comfort and joy that accompany renewed assurance of his favor.

We appeal to **mercy**, not to any merit of our own.

God's favor is a fountain of lasting joy and can more than compensate for past suffering.

May grace within us produce **good works**, and may divine comfort bring gladness to our hearts and faces.

We ask God to establish the work of our hands—and, in order for that work to stand, **to establish us in it**.

Do not waste these brief precious days pursuing fantasies that leave us spiritually poor.

Seek instead **forgiveness of sins and the heavenly inheritance**.

Pray that the Holy Spirit will convert our hearts and that **the beauty of holiness** will be visible in our behavior.
