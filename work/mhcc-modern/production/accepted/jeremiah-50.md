# Jeremiah 50 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The ruin of Babylon. | 1–3, 8–16, 21–32, 35–46 |
| The redemption of God's people. | 4–7, 17–20, 33–34 |

## Babylon's downfall and God's people's return (verses 1–7)

The Babylonian king had treated Jeremiah with kindness.

Yet the prophet was still obliged to announce the destruction of **that king's empire**.

When those friendly toward us are enemies of God, we must not proclaim a false peace to them.

Babylon's destruction is described as thorough and final.

But within these warnings comes comfort for the Jews.

They would return **first to their God**, and then to their country.

The promise of their **conversion and reform** prepares the way for the other blessings promised to them.

Their tears would no longer be merely the grief of worldly losses, as when they went into captivity, but **godly sorrow over sin**.

They would seek the Lord as their God and abandon idols.

Then they would think of returning to their homeland.

This illustrates the return of lost souls to God.

Genuine converts sincerely desire to reach the right end and continually take care to remain on **the right path**.

The condition of the people before their restoration was truly sorrowful.

Even when professing Christians commit sins, those sins never justify others' pleasure in persecuting or destroying them.

## Babylon is devastated and Israel regathered (verses 8–20)

Babylon's devastation is described through many images.

**The Lord's anger against sin** is its underlying cause.

The nation would be laid waste because it had rebelled against God.

Sin makes people targets for the arrows of divine judgment.

The mercy promised to **the Israel of God** would not merely come after Babylon's destruction: its deliverance would **arise through that destruction**.

The scattered sheep would be gathered from the wilderness and returned to good pasture.

Everyone who returns to God and to duty discovers true satisfaction of soul.

Deliverance from suffering becomes real comfort when it comes as a fruit of **forgiven sin**.

## God's forces bring Babylon low (verses 21–32)

Forces would be gathered and given the power to destroy Babylon.

By carrying out the task God appointed, they would bring to pass what he had announced.

**Pride** causes God to oppose a person and quickly brings ruin nearer.

Babylon's own arrogance would be its downfall.

She had proudly opposed **the Holy One of Israel**.

Who can preserve in power those whom God has determined to bring down?

## The weak have a mighty Redeemer (verses 33–46)

Israel could take comfort in knowing that **although God's people were weak, their Redeemer was strong**.

This applies to believers who struggle under the power of sin and corruption and feel the weight of their weakness and many shortcomings.

Their Redeemer is able to guard what they entrust to him.

Sin will not have dominion over them.

He will give them **the rest that remains for God's people**.

Babylon's wrongdoing and punishment are also described.

Her central sins included **idolatry and persecution**.

The Lord does not save his people **so they can remain in their sins**, and he will certainly not approve the wickedness of their declared enemies.

Judgment for Babylon's sins would leave the country desolate.

When we read about prosperous Babylon's destruction and afflicted Israel's rescue, we learn to choose **suffering with God's people** rather than brief enjoyment of the pleasures of sin.

## Editorial review — Jeremiah 50

**Entire English secondary original read:** [CCEL, Jeremiah 50](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhcc.xxv.l.html). The released `MHCC 0.1.1` remains authoritative for native-SWORD keys, notes, cross-references and independent acceptance.

**Outline versus exposition:** CCEL's **original outline is thematic, with non-contiguous ranges**: ruin of Babylon **1–3, 8–16, 21–32, 35–46**; redemption of God's people **4–7, 17–20, 33–34**. Its **four actual prose expositions** are **1–7; 8–20; 21–32; 33–46**. Both forms have been independently preserved; neither is silently replaced by the other.

- **1–7:** Jeremiah's gratitude to king **not suppressing warning**, God restored **before land**, **godly grief** instead of worldly sorrow, converts' **goal and path**, professing Christians' sin never justifying their oppressors — retained.
- **8–20:** Babylon's sin/judgment, **Israel's mercy arising from Babylon's destruction**, scattered sheep returning to pasture, deliverance as **forgiveness's fruit** — retained.
- **21–32:** divine forces and **arrogance toward Holy One** bringing downfall — retained.
- **33–46:** **weak Israel, mighty Redeemer**, sin's defeat and eternal rest, Babylon's **idolatry/persecution**, no divine approval of either his own people's sins or their enemies' wickedness, **affliction with faithful above sin's passing enjoyment** — retained.
