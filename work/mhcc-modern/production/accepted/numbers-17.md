# Numbers 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Twelve rods laid up before the Lord. | 1–7 |
| Aaron's rod buds, and is kept for a memorial. | 8–13 |

## The twelve staffs are placed before the Lord (verses 1–7)

God had already performed several miracles to punish Israel's sin.

Now, in his grace, he performed another miracle to **prevent** further sin.

Twelve staffs were brought before the Lord.

Henry considers it likely that these were old, dry staffs carried by the tribal leaders as signs of their authority.

The wood had no living sap left.

The people were told that the staff belonging to the leader whom God had chosen for the priesthood would sprout and blossom.

Moses did not say that the matter had been settled sufficiently already.

Nor did he try to decide the outcome himself.

He left the decision before the Lord.

## Aaron's staff blossoms (verses 8–13)

The other staffs remained unchanged.

Aaron's dry staff became like a living branch.

It showed buds, blossoms and fruit at the same time.

This was unmistakably miraculous.

God thus confirmed that Aaron had received his special blessing and appointment.

Fruitfulness is among the best signs of a divine calling.

What God plants will flourish.

The staff was then preserved to stop the people's complaining before they brought death upon themselves.

God's work in providence and the reminders of what he has done are meant to turn us away from sin.

Christ himself came to take away sin.

Scripture describes him as a shoot growing from the root of Jesse.

By ordinary human expectations, there seemed little reason to think he would flourish.

But like the dead staff that came to life, he rose to confound his enemies.

The Israelites cried out in fear that they were all about to die.

Yet they were still resisting God's judgments rather than accepting responsibility for their own pride and stubbornness.

It is wicked to complain against God during suffering in a way that brings still more sin.

If we perish through our rebellion, the guilt is ours.

God's judgment will prevail.

Even the most stubborn opponents will eventually be forced to recognize their folly.

How great are the mercies offered to us through a clearer and better covenant, founded on better promises!
