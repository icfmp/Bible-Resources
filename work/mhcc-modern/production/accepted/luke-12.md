# Luke 12 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ reproves the interpreters of the law. | 1–12 |
| A caution against covetousness, The parable of the rich man. | 13–21 |
| Worldly care reproved. | 22–40 |
| Watchfulness enforced. | 41–53 |
| A warning to be reconciled to God. | 54–59 |

## Jesus warns against denial and hypocrisy (verses 1–12)

Faith in God's **universal providence** should support us in danger and help us continue trusting him as we perform our duty.

God notices even sparrows and their smallest concerns. He therefore watches over the least interests of his disciples.

Those who acknowledge Jesus now will be acknowledged by him before God's angels at the final judgment.

Those who deny him to protect their life or gain worldly power will ultimately suffer a far greater loss: Christ will not acknowledge them.

But **a trembling believer who has fallen and genuinely repents need not doubt the possibility of forgiveness**.

That differs entirely from the hardened, determined hostility toward the Holy Spirit which, as Henry explains, is not forgiven **because it is not repented of**.

## Greed and the rich fool (verses 13–21)

Christ's kingdom is **spiritual, not an earthly empire**.

Henry says Christianity does not seek political rule as its reward. It requires everyone to act justly, but does not promise worldly power or wealth as the advantage of receiving grace.

The benefits promised to Christ's disciples are of a higher kind.

We constantly need warning against **greed**.

Wealth cannot provide true happiness, comfort or satisfaction to a soul.

The rich fool was a clever manager of worldly affairs but neglected **God's providence, life's uncertainty, the value of his soul and eternity**.

Even professing believers sometimes hold up such worldly-minded people as models to imitate.

Our thoughts are not hidden from God.

When the man's land produced abundantly, instead of thanking the Lord and rejoicing that he could do more good, he was troubled: **What shall I do now?**

The poorest beggar could scarcely have spoken with greater anxiety.

Possessing more often creates more cares.

The man intended to use his abundance only for bodily pleasure, not to help others.

God would require his soul from him and call him to account.

What folly to pursue benefits for the body and for a few years while neglecting the soul and eternity!

## Jesus calls his little flock away from anxiety (verses 22–40)

Jesus repeatedly warned against anxious, tormenting care for earthly things (**Matthew 6:25–34**).

Cast your cares on God, for this is the way to find peace.

Just as worry cannot change our height, it cannot give us sovereign control over our circumstances.

We should accept our situation under God's care instead of endlessly chasing worldly comforts.

Even necessary material things become harmful when pursued in panic and excessive worry.

Do not imagine every future disaster and invent needless cares in trying to prevent it.

If we value **holiness**, we will not be ruled by a desire for luxury.

Examine whether we belong to Christ's **little flock**.

He is our Master, and we are both **working servants and waiting servants**.

Remain ready to meet him, as household servants waiting late into the night for their master's return.

Henry connects this with **Jesus' ascension, his summons to each believer through death, and his future return to judge the world**.

Because the hour is unknown, remain prepared like someone guarding a house when a thief may arrive.

## Watchfulness despite division (verses 41–53)

Everyone must apply Jesus' warnings personally.

Each person knows many wrong things they should avoid and many right duties they neglect. Ignorance therefore leaves no one without responsibility.

The gospel's spread would cause division and suffering.

That does not mean Christianity itself is violent or hostile; it is **pure, peaceful and loving**.

Conflict arises because its truth contradicts people's **pride and sinful desires**.

Before the gospel could be proclaimed widely, Jesus had a **baptism of suffering and death** to undergo—very different from baptism with water or the Spirit.

We should eagerly make the truth known even if it causes disagreement or opposition within families.

Through that witness people are converted and God receives glory.

## Seek reconciliation while there is time (verses 54–59)

People should show the same practical wisdom about their souls that they show in ordinary affairs.

Hasten to make peace with God before it is too late.

When conscience tells us that our sin has set us against the Lord, come to him as **God reconciling the world to himself through Christ**.

While we are alive, we are still on the way, and **this is the time** to receive his mercy.
