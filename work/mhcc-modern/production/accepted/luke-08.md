# Luke 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The ministry of Christ. | 1–3 |
| The parable of the sower. | 4–21 |
| Christ stilleth the tempest and casteth out devils. | 22–40 |
| The daughter of Jairus restored to life. | 41–56 |

## Jesus' ministry and the women who supported it (verses 1–3)

Jesus' regular work was **preaching and teaching the gospel**.

The message of God's kingdom was the good news he had come to bring.

Some women accompanied him and supported his work with their own resources.

That Jesus had need of their practical kindness reveals the **humble, poor condition** he willingly accepted.

That he received their help shows his deep humility.

Though rich, **he became poor for our sake**.

## The sower, the lamp and Jesus' true family (verses 4–21)

The parable of the sower contains important warnings and instructions about **hearing God's word**.

How blessed we are—and how completely indebted to free grace—if teaching that entertains others without transforming them becomes clear truth to us, teaching and directing our conduct.

We must notice what hinders us from benefiting from what we hear.

Do not listen **carelessly or superficially**.

Do not allow **prejudice** to block the word.

After hearing, guard your heart so that you do not lose what you have gained.

God continues or withdraws the gifts entrusted to us according to how we use them for **his glory and others' good**.

It is not enough merely to avoid suppressing the truth through unrighteousness.

We should also **hold out the word of life**, shining for people around us.

Faithful listeners demonstrate their faith by **doing what Christ says**.

Jesus recognizes those who hear and obey him as **members of his own family**.

## Jesus calms the storm and drives out demons (verses 22–40)

Those who embark upon a journey **at Christ's own command** should nevertheless be ready for storms and even serious danger.

When guilt and fear of judgment overwhelm the soul, only Jesus can give relief.

We must turn to him and cry, **Master, I am lost unless you help me!**

When he brings us safely through danger, we should be ashamed of our distrustful fears and give him all the glory for our rescue.

The account of the possessed man teaches us about evil spirits.

Henry says that such spirits **do not now act in precisely the same way as in that episode**, but we must always be alert to their malice.

They are numerous and hostile toward humanity and its good.

Christ leads those under his rule **through loving guidance**.

The devil drives those under his rule **with violent cruelty**.

What comfort for believers that every power of darkness remains under the authority of **the Lord Jesus**!

It is an act of mercy when someone under Satan's influence is rescued rather than destroyed.

Christ will not force his company upon those who despise him.

People who reject him risk never receiving another opportunity, even while others would gladly welcome him.

## The woman is healed and Jairus's daughter restored (verses 41–56)

While doing our duty and helping people, we need not complain simply because crowds and hurried circumstances surround us.

Yet when there is no useful purpose in such activity, wisdom often calls us to avoid it.

Some suffering people are helped, healed and saved by Jesus **without attracting attention** in a crowd.

The woman approached trembling, but she possessed saving faith.

**Fear and trembling can coexist with true faith**.

Jesus encouraged Jairus: **Do not fear; only believe, and your daughter will be restored**.

The loss of an only child is a terrible grief.

Trusting God through that loss and through fear that it might continue forever is exceedingly difficult.

Perfect faith overcomes fear; as fear increases, faith is often weakened.

Christ's gracious power accompanies his commands and makes them effective.

After raising the child, he told those present to **give her food**.

People newly raised from spiritual death likewise need **spiritual nourishment**, so they may grow as newborn children do.
