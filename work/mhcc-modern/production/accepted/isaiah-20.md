# Isaiah 20 — Modern English

## Original heading

**The invasion and conquest of Egypt and Ethiopia.**

## Isaiah's sign of the coming captivity

Isaiah became a sign to the people through his unusual clothing when he appeared in public.

As a prophet, he normally wore **sackcloth**, expressing his separation from worldly vanity.

God told him to remove it from his waist, to wear **no outer garment** and to walk **barefoot**.

This sign foretold that the king of Assyria would lead away captives from **Egypt and Ethiopia** in the same humiliated, stripped-down condition.

The world often judges believers foolish when their obedience to God makes them noticeably different from others.

But the Lord sustains his servants even when obedience involves particularly difficult trials.

What he asks them to endure for his sake is generally light when compared with the suffering that multitudes endure year after year through sin.

Whoever makes a created person or thing the foundation of hope, trust or pride—giving it the place belonging to God—will eventually be disappointed and ashamed.

Yet disappointment in human supports should not drive us to despair.

Instead it should **drive us toward God**, whose promises will not fail.

This same lesson remains important for us.

When the hour of urgent need arrives, where should we turn for help except to **the Lord our Righteousness**?
