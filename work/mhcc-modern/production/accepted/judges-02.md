# Judges 2 — Modern English

## The angel of the Lord rebukes Israel (verses 1–5)

The One speaking to Israel was the great **Angel of the covenant**—the Word, the Son of God.

He spoke with divine authority as Jehovah and called the people to account for their disobedience.

The Lord reminded them both of what he had done and what he had promised.

People who turn away from fellowship with God to share in fruitless works of darkness do not understand what they are doing.

Soon they will have no excuse when called to account.

Their folly has consequences.

Those who expect to benefit from friendship with God's enemies deceive themselves.

God often allows people to experience their sin as its own punishment.

Thorns and snares lie on the path of those who stubbornly oppose him.

The people wept, recognizing their folly and ingratitude.

They trembled at God's word, and with good reason.

It is remarkable that sinners can read Scripture without tears.

If Israel had remained close to the Lord and kept its duty, the sound of its assemblies would have been joyful singing.

But through sin the people had brought themselves to tears.

Worship is naturally a matter for joy, praise and gratitude.

Our sins make weeping necessary.

It is good to weep over sin, but **tears, prayers and even corrected behavior cannot atone for wrongdoing**.

## A new generation forgets the Lord (verses 6–23)

This passage summarizes the pattern of Israel's history during the judges.

Abandoning God made the nation weak and miserable.

Faithfulness would have made it strong and happy.

The punishment corresponded to the sin.

The Israelites worshiped the gods of surrounding nations, even the lowest of them, and God allowed the rulers of those nations to dominate them.

Anyone who has seen God keep his promises can be equally certain that he will keep his warnings.

He could have abandoned the Israelites justly.

Yet his compassion led him to raise up judges.

The Lord was with these judges, and so they became deliverers.

Even when the church experiences terrible distress, God will find or equip people to help it.

Israel, however, was not thoroughly reformed.

The people remained devoted to idols and obstinately returned to sin.

Those who abandon a way of life they once knew and professed can become increasingly bold in wrongdoing and hardened in heart.

God allowed Canaanites to remain, and Israel suffered from the very people it had chosen to tolerate.

Likewise, people cherish their corrupt desires.

God may justly leave them to the power of those desires until they become the cause of their ruin.

Scripture tells us that our hearts are deceptive and deeply wicked.

Yet we seldom believe that warning until we have recklessly approached temptation and discovered it through bitter experience.

Let us examine ourselves, pray without ceasing, be rooted in love and have Christ dwelling in our hearts through faith.

Let us declare war against every sin and pursue holiness throughout our lives.
