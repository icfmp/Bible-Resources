# Leviticus 17 — Modern English

## Sacrifices at the tabernacle (verses 1–9)

While the Israelites lived in the wilderness, every animal they killed from their herds was to be brought to the entrance of the tabernacle.

After being presented as an offering, its meat was returned to the owner to be eaten according to the regulations for fellowship offerings.

Once Israel entered Canaan, this requirement would apply only to animals offered in sacrifice.

Christians' spiritual sacrifices are no longer restricted to a particular location.

We have no earthly temple or altar that makes an offering acceptable.

The unity of the gospel is not founded on a single place, but on one heart and unity in the Spirit.

Christ is our Altar and the true Tabernacle.

God dwells among humanity in him.

Only through Christ can any offering be accepted by God.

To establish other mediators, altars or sacrifices supposed to make atonement would, in effect, be to establish other gods.

Yet God's willingness to accept the worship we offer in our homes is no excuse for neglecting the worship of his gathered people.

## The prohibition concerning blood (verses 10–16)

The law forbidding Israel to eat blood is confirmed here.

But Henry explicitly says it was a **ceremonial law** and therefore no longer binds believers after Christ's coming.

The reality replaces its shadow.

Animal blood is no longer appointed as a ransom.

Only Christ's blood makes true and effective atonement.

The former reason for abstaining from blood has therefore ended, and it may now be used as ordinary nourishment.

But we must honor Christ's blood all the more.

It truly makes reconciliation with God.

We must never treat it as a common thing or regard it indifferently.
