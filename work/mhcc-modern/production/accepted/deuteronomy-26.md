# Deuteronomy 26 — Modern English

## Offering the firstfruits with grateful remembrance (verses 1–11)

When God fulfills his promises to us, he expects us to acknowledge the fulfillment and give him the honor for his faithfulness.

The comforts we receive through created things become doubly pleasant when we recognize that they flow from his promises.

A person bringing firstfruits was to remember the humble beginnings of the nation.

“My father was a Syrian who was about to perish.”

Moses describes Jacob as a Syrian.

Israel's ancestors had gone to live in Egypt as foreigners.

There they became enslaved.

They were once poor, despised and oppressed.

Even after becoming wealthy and powerful, the people had no reason for pride, self-confidence or forgetfulness of God.

Their offering should express heartfelt gratitude for the Lord's goodness to Israel.

The comfort we enjoy privately should make us grateful for public peace and abundant provision too.

Present blessings should call to mind mercies received long ago and those still promised for the future.

The worshiper brought the basket of firstfruits before God.

Whatever good gift we receive, he wants us to enjoy it gratefully, recognizing him as the source of every comfort.

## Prayer after the third-year tithe (verses 12–15)

Even if the land produces an abundant harvest, it will give little true satisfaction unless God also adds his blessing.

This ceremony pictured the relationship between God, reconciled to his people, and each genuine believer.

It displayed the privileges and duties that belong to this relationship.

Under the covenant of grace in Jesus Christ, we should watch carefully over our lives.

We ought to show by our conduct that the Lord is our God and we are his people, while waiting in the way he appointed for the complete fulfillment of his generous promises.

## The covenant and wholehearted obedience (verses 16–19)

Moses urged Israel to obey God's commands for the very purpose for which they were given.

They are the Lord's laws: **do them**.

Do not merely argue about them or turn away after hearing them.

Do not carry them out carelessly or hypocritically.

Obey with your whole heart and soul.

If we have professed to take the Lord as our God but refuse to follow his commandments, we have broken a sacred commitment.

We were **chosen for obedience** (1 Peter 1:2).

We were **chosen to be holy** (Ephesians 1:4).

We have been purified to become God's special people, eager not only to do good works but to devote ourselves zealously to them (Titus 2:14).

Holiness is true honor.

It is also the only path to everlasting honor.
