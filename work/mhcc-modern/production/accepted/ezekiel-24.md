# Ezekiel 24 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The fate of Jerusalem. | 1–14 |
| The extent of the sufferings of the Jews. | 15–27 |

## Jerusalem is pictured as a pot on the fire (verses 1–14)

The **pot heated over the fire** represented Jerusalem being besieged by **the Chaldeans**.

People of every position and class were inside the city walls and were about to become their enemies' prey.

They should have removed their sins as **scum is skimmed from boiling food**.

Instead, they became more corrupt, and their suffering grew worse.

Jerusalem would be **leveled to the ground**.

The appointed hour of judgment may appear to approach slowly, but it will certainly arrive.

How sorrowful to consider how many people gain nothing from **God's appointed worship or the events of his Providence**!

## Ezekiel's bereavement is a sign (verses 15–27)

Mourning those who die is normally **a duty**.

But religion and sound judgment must restrain our grief.

We should **not grieve as people without hope**.

Believers need not imitate the words or gestures of those who do not know God.

The people asked Ezekiel what his strange actions meant.

The Lord was about to remove from them **everything they held most precious**.

Just as Ezekiel did not publicly mourn his own loss in the customary manner, the people's coming sorrow would exceed ordinary expressions of lament.

Blessed be God: we need not sink under suffering forever.

Even if every earthly comfort fails and all sorrows arrive together, **a broken heart and a mourner's sincere prayer remain acceptable to him**.
