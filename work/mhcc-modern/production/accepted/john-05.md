# John 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The cure at the pool of Bethesda. | 1–9 |
| The Jews' displeasure. | 10–16 |
| Christ reproves the Jews. | 17–23 |
| Christ's discourse. | 24–47 |

## Jesus heals a man at Bethesda (verses 1–9)

By nature, **we are all helpless in spiritual matters**—blind, lame and weakened by sin.

Yet God has made full provision for our healing, if we receive it.

Henry describes an angel entering the pool and stirring its water, so that the first person to step in would be healed of whatever disease afflicted them.

He draws a lesson from the lost opportunity: **do not allow an occasion for mercy to pass by**, because it may never return.

The man had been unable to use his limbs for **thirty-eight years**.

How can we complain about one uncomfortable night when many others, better than ourselves, have scarcely known a single day of health?

Jesus chose this particular sufferer out of the whole group.

Those enduring long affliction should take comfort in knowing that **God has not lost count of the years**.

The man spoke of others' failure to help him, yet did not become bitter or abusive.

We should practice both thankfulness and patience.

Christ healed the man although he had neither asked Jesus for help nor expected him to come.

Jesus commanded, **“Rise and walk.”**

A command from God to repent and live does not mean fallen sinners can make themselves spiritually alive without divine grace.

No more did Jesus' command imply that the paralyzed man possessed the physical strength to rise without miraculous help.

**The power belonged to Christ**, and he must receive the glory.

What a joyful surprise when the man suddenly found himself strong and able to walk!

Likewise, spiritual healing is shown through a changed life.

If Christ has cured our souls, let us go where he sends us, take up whatever he lays upon us and walk obediently before him.

## The authorities object to the healing (verses 10–16)

Someone delivered from punishment for sin may be tempted to return to sin once fear has faded, unless **God's grace removes sin at its source**.

The miseries from which believers have been rescued should warn us against returning to the deeds that caused them.

Every providential mercy carries the message **“Go and sin no more.”**

Jesus knew the warning was needed.

During illness people often make many resolutions.

Shortly after recovery, they keep only some.

After more time passes, they may forget them altogether.

Jesus spoke of **the wrath to come**.

Even serious pain lasting months or years is incomparable with the everlasting consequences of unrepentant sin.

## Jesus declares his equality with the Father (verses 17–23)

The divine power displayed in the healing proved Jesus' identity as **the Son of God**.

He declared that he works together with and like his Father, doing whatever is right.

His opponents understood that he claimed a relationship to God beyond that of an ordinary human being.

They became more hostile, accusing him not only of breaking their Sabbath rules but also of **blasphemy by making himself equal with God**.

The Father entrusted judgment, now and at the last day, to his Son.

The purpose is that everyone should **honor the Son just as they honor the Father**.

Anyone who refuses that honor to Christ does not truly honor the Father who sent him, whatever else they may claim.

## Jesus teaches about life and judgment (verses 24–47)

### New spiritual life and the resurrection (verses 24–29)

Jesus declared his authority as the Messiah.

The time had arrived when those spiritually dead could **hear the Son of God's voice and receive new life**.

He also foretold a future day when those physically dead in their graves would hear his voice and rise.

The Judge of all humanity must possess **perfect knowledge and unlimited power**.

Let us believe his testimony so that our faith and hope rest upon God, and we may not come under condemnation.

May Christ's voice reach people dead in sin, producing works consistent with repentance and preparing them for the solemn judgment.

### Jesus acts with the Father and is witnessed by his works (verses 30–38)

Jesus spoke again of the complete agreement between **Father and Son**, declaring himself the Son of God.

He had a witness greater than John's: **the works his Father gave him to accomplish**.

Those works confirmed everything he said.

Yet God's word had no abiding place in the hearts of those who refused to believe the One sent according to the ancient promises.

The Father's testimony, made effective through **the Holy Spirit's power**, still brings sinners to conversion and announces Jesus as the beloved Son in whom God is pleased.

When hearts are filled with pride, ambition and love of the world, there is **no room for God's word to dwell within them**.

### Study Scripture to find Christ (verses 39–44)

The Jews believed eternal life was revealed in their Scriptures.

Some assumed that merely possessing and studying the written word meant they already had life.

Jesus challenged them to search the Scriptures more carefully.

Henry observes that Jesus' statement can be understood either as describing their practice—**“You search the Scriptures”**—or as commanding them—**“Search the Scriptures.”**

Both readings teach an important duty.

We should not only read or hear Scripture but **study it diligently and carefully**.

We must search for **eternal life as our great goal**, and for **Christ as the new and living way** to that life.

It is possible to be deeply learned in Scripture's wording yet unfamiliar with its spiritual power.

A person may approach biblical study seeking **personal recognition** instead of God's honor.

Jesus rebuked his hearers for refusing himself and his teaching, revealing their lack of **genuine love for God**.

Many profess great religion while showing by their neglect of Christ that they do not possess the love of God.

It is living love for the Lord within the heart that he receives.

His opponents undervalued Christ because they overvalued **themselves**.

How can people truly believe if they make **human praise and approval an idol**?

### Moses' writings also point toward Jesus (verses 45–47)

Many people trust a system of doctrine, or a party associated with respected names, without understanding those doctrines or those teachers' real purposes.

The Jews in this way misunderstood Moses.

Let us read Scripture with prayer, desiring eternal life.

Notice how **Christ is its great subject**, and turn daily to him for the life he gives.
