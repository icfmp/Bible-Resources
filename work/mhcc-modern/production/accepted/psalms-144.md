# Psalm 144 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David acknowledges the great goodness of God, and prays for help. | 1–8 |
| He prays for the prosperity of his kingdom. | 9–15 |

## Remembering mercy and asking for more (verses 1–8)

When people become skilled and successful despite having few natural advantages, they should be especially aware that **God has been their Teacher**.

The greatest victory he gives is **victory over one's own spirit**, through self-control.

A prayer for further help should begin with thanksgiving for the mercy already received.

God exercised special power by moving Israel's people to submit to David.

That event pictured the way **souls are brought into obedience to the Lord Jesus Christ**.

Our earthly lives have little lasting substance.

It is strange that a soul destined to live forever devotes so many of its concerns to a body that will soon die.

Life passes like **a moving shadow**.

Even when believers enjoy great honor, they should remember **their own unworthiness and sin**, so that they are protected from pride and self-importance.

The Lord often comes to help his people when **they are sinking and every other source of help has failed**.

## Praying for families, peace and the Lord's rule (verses 9–15)

New mercies call for new thanksgiving.

Praise God not only for what he has already granted, but also for the good **he has promised and we still hope to receive**.

Physical deliverance, whether from a sword or from wasting disease, would be of little ultimate value if a person remained under **sin's dominion and the judgment to come**.

David therefore prayed for his kingdom's prosperity.

Parents find joy in seeing their children likely to grow and do well.

May they be like healthy plants, not **weeds or thorns**.

May they grow rather than wither, bear fruit for God in their generation and become **strong in the Spirit while young**.

It is right to desire prosperity and full storehouses if these gifts make us **more thankful toward God, generous to friends and charitable to poor people**.

If our abundance produces none of these things, what profit is there in it?

We should also desire **peace**.

War causes countless harms, whether a people are attacking others or defending themselves.

A nation's real happiness declines as its people neglect God's worship and service.

Those who belong to **the Savior, the Son of David**, share the blessings of his kingdom and victories.

They are happy because **the Lord is their God**.
