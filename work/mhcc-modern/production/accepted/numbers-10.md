## The silver trumpets (verses 1–10)

These verses establish the trumpet signals used to communicate publicly with the camp.

Israel was to receive its laws from God in every detail, so even these signals were given to Moses by divine command.

The trumpets pictured the preaching of the gospel.

It sounds an alarm to sinners, calling them to repent.

It announces freedom to people enslaved to Satan and gathers God's worshipers.

It directs and strengthens them on the journey to heaven.

It calls them to fight against the world and sin, and gives them confidence of victory.

It points them to Christ's sacrifice and reminds them of the Lord's presence protecting his people.

The gospel trumpet must make a clear and distinct sound.

Its message must suit both the people addressed and the purpose for speaking: conviction, humbling, comfort, encouragement, correction or instruction.

God has appointed the public proclamation of the gospel, and everyone to whom it comes is called to listen.

## Israel travels from Sinai to Paran (verses 11–28)

The Israelites had spent nearly a year at Mount Sinai.

Now that their worship had been organized, they set out toward Canaan.

True religion begins with knowing God's holy law and humbling ourselves over sin.

But we must keep growing in the knowledge of Christ and his gospel, and in the powerful reasons, encouragements and help he gives us for holiness.

The people traveled when God commanded them (Deuteronomy 1:6–8) and when the cloud led them.

Those who submit to God's word and Spirit can follow a steady course even when the route seems confusing.

If we are sure that we have not lost our God and Guide, we need not fear becoming lost ourselves.

They left the wilderness of Sinai and camped in the wilderness of Paran.

So many of our changes in this world simply move us from one wilderness to another.

Moves that we expect to improve our circumstances do not always do so.

We will not find perfect rest or a permanent home until we reach heaven.

But everything will be well there.

## Moses invites Hobab to accompany Israel (verses 29–32)

Moses invited his relative Hobab to come with the people to Canaan.

Those traveling toward the heavenly Canaan should invite and encourage friends to come too.

Other people sharing the joys of heaven will not reduce our own happiness in the least.

It is good to enjoy fellowship with people who enjoy fellowship with God.

Yet the visible attractions of this world can powerfully distract people from the unseen blessings of eternity.

Moses explained that Hobab could be useful to Israel.

He did not need Hobab to decide where to travel or camp—the cloud provided that guidance.

But Hobab could help identify practical features of the places they passed through or stayed in.

Depending on God's providence is entirely consistent with accepting wise help from our friends.

## Moses prays when Israel moves and rests (verses 33–36)

Israel's departures and arrivals teach us to begin and end our daily journeys and work with prayer.

When the ark moved, Moses prayed for God to rise and scatter his enemies.

There are people who openly or secretly oppose God, his truth, laws, worship and people.

But God's rising to act is sufficient to defeat them.

When the ark rested, Moses prayed for the Lord to return and dwell with his people.

Israel's well-being and happiness depended on God's continuing presence.

Its safety did not come from its numbers but from his favor and his willingness to remain among the people.

God would go ahead and provide suitable places to rest during their travels.

He promised never to abandon or forsake them.
