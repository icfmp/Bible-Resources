## Manasseh repents (verses 1–20)

We have already seen Manasseh's terrible wickedness.

Here Scripture records his repentance, a remarkable demonstration of **God's rich forgiving mercy and renewing grace**.

Manasseh lost his freedom, was separated from the advisers and companions who had encouraged evil, and faced the prospect of dying in a miserable prison.

He began to look back on his life and cry out for deliverance and mercy.

He confessed his sins, condemned himself, humbled himself before God and despised the dreadful wickedness he had committed.

Yet he still hoped to receive pardon through the Lord's abundant mercy.

Now Manasseh knew that **Jehovah is God**, able to save and deliver.

He learned to fear, trust, love and obey him.

From then on, his character changed and he began to walk in **newness of life**.

Who can tell how much pain of conscience he suffered while remembering his long years of apostasy, the many people he led into sin and destruction, and the blood of faithful people shed through his persecution?

Who can claim that the way to heaven has been closed against them when someone so wicked was admitted through repentance?

However much evil you recognize in your own heart, here is someone equally guilty who discovered the way to repent.

**Do not deny yourself what God has not denied you.**

It is not sin alone, but **unrepentance**, that keeps a sinner outside heaven.

## Amon's wicked reign (verses 21–25)

Amon's father did evil, but Amon did worse.

Whatever warnings or convictions he experienced, he never humbled himself before God.

His life was soon cut short while he remained in his sins.

He stands as a warning to everyone tempted to treat God's patience and mercy toward Manasseh as permission to continue sinning.

May God help us be truthful with ourselves and judge our own character correctly before death fixes our condition forever.
