## Hezekiah invites all Israel to the Passover (verses 1–12)

Hezekiah made the Israelites from the northern kingdom as welcome to the Passover as his own subjects.

Let us give ourselves to the Lord.

Instead of insisting on doing what pleases us, resolve to do what pleases **him**.

Our fallen minds are naturally stubborn and unwilling to comply with God's ways.

We have inherited that disposition from our ancestors, and it must be overcome.

People whom grace has turned to God should do everything they can to bring others to him.

Many will mock the invitation.

Yet some will be humbled and blessed, perhaps where we least expect it.

**God's rich mercy** is the strongest reason for calling sinners to repentance.

Even the worst sinners who humble themselves before the Lord, seek his grace and devote themselves to his service can be saved.

How wonderful it would be if messengers went through every city, village and country with this good news!

## The Passover is celebrated (verses 13–20)

The essential thing in attending God's solemn worship is **a heart sincerely directed toward him**.

Without this, everything else is worthless.

Even with a sincere, settled heart, worshipers may fall short of some sanctuary purification requirements.

Those defects need God's pardoning and healing grace.

**Failing within a duty** can be sinful, just as **failing to perform the duty at all** can be sinful.

If God judged even our best acts by the strict standard of his justice, we would be condemned.

Forgiveness must be sought through prayer, **through the blood of Christ**.

Every defect is cause for humility and repentance, not for despair.

But nothing can replace a heart prepared to seek the Lord.

## The Feast of Unleavened Bread (verses 21–27)

Many prayers accompanied the fellowship offerings.

In these prayers the people recognized the Lord as the God of their fathers, their covenant God.

There was also much faithful preaching.

The Levites **read and explained the Scriptures**.

Faith comes through hearing, and true religion has often flourished when preaching was abundant.

The people sang psalms every day.

Praising God should occupy a large place in our gatherings.

After observing the feast for **seven days**, they found such joy in worship that they continued for **another seven days**.

They did so gladly.

Holy duties should be performed with holy joy.

When sinners humble themselves before the Lord, they can expect to rejoice in his appointed worship.

Those who taste that joy will not soon grow tired of it.

They will gladly prolong their time with God.
