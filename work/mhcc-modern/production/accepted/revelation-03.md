# Revelation 3 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Epistles to the church at Sardis; | 1–6 |
| at Philadelphia; | 7–13 |
| and Laodicea. | 14–22 |

## Sardis: wake up and strengthen what remains (verses 1–6)

Jesus Christ possesses the Holy Spirit, with **all the Spirit's powers, graces and operations**.

He knows his churches and all their works. He exposed the hypocrisy and terrible spiritual decline of Sardis.

Outwardly, the church seemed healthy. Inwardly, much of its religion was only **a form of godliness without its power**: it had a reputation for life but lacked the principle of life itself.

Many members were hypocrites altogether; others retained spiritual life but had fallen into weakness and disorder.

Christ therefore called them to wakefulness, watchfulness and energetic obedience. They were to depend on **the Holy Spirit's grace** to revive faith and holy affections and to strengthen those who had not yet died spiritually.

Whenever we stop watching, we lose ground.

Their works were unfinished and hollow. **Prayers** lacked sincere holy desires, **acts of giving** lacked genuine love, and **Sabbath observance** lacked fitting devotion of heart.

Their outward forms did not match their inward condition; once the spirit of worship is missing, its forms cannot continue for long.

To seek revival in ourselves and others, we must compare **what we profess with how we actually live**. This should humble us, awaken us and move us to hold firmly to what remains.

Christ warns of severe consequences for ignoring his counsel, yet he does not leave the church without hope.

He remembers the **faithful remnant** at Sardis and promises them honor.

Those who overcome will receive white clothing: the purity of grace perfected in **the purity of glory**.

Christ holds the book of life, remembering all who will inherit eternal life and those who maintain genuine devotion amid evil times.

On the great day, he will acknowledge the names of his faithful people before God and all the angels.

## Philadelphia: the open door and enduring faithfulness (verses 7–13)

Jesus Christ holds **the key of authority and government over the church**.

He opens opportunities for his congregations, opens doors for his ministers to speak, and opens hearts to receive his word.

He also shuts heaven's door to those who waste their time of grace in spiritual sleep and to those who continue in evil, however confident they may seem.

Philadelphia received praise, though also a gentle correction.

Christ accepts **a little strength**, but believers must never be content to remain weak. They should grow in grace, become strong in faith and bring glory to God.

Christ can display his favor so clearly that even enemies of his people must acknowledge it.

Through grace their opposition may soften, and they may desire fellowship with those they once opposed.

He promises to preserve faithful believers in their hardest trials.

Those who have kept his gospel in times of peace will find that he sustains them during temptation and persecution.

The same grace that made them fruitful in peaceful days will enable them to remain faithful in suffering.

Christ promises an enduring reward to those who overcome.

He will make them **pillars in God's temple**, monuments to his free and powerful grace that will never be destroyed or removed.

His new name written upon them will identify the Lord under whose leadership they fought and won their spiritual battle.

## Laodicea: repent of complacency and welcome Christ (verses 14–22)

Laodicea was **the last and worst** of the seven churches of Asia.

Jesus calls himself **the Amen**: unwavering in his purposes and faithful to all his promises.

If religion matters at all, it matters supremely. Christ requires earnest devotion.

Yet many who profess the gospel are neither hot nor cold. They show little concern for essential matters while becoming angry and combative about minor disputes.

Christ warns such people of severe judgment.

Their indifference gives others a false picture of Christianity. Some conclude that the faith is unholy; others suppose it cannot bring real satisfaction, since its professed followers are so lifeless and quick to seek happiness from the world.

Self-conceit and self-deception are among the roots of this spiritual inconsistency.

What a difference between **Laodicea's opinion of itself** and **Christ's judgment of it**!

We must not deceive our own souls. Henry warns that **many are in hell who once believed themselves far along the road to heaven**.

Pray earnestly that God will keep us from flattering ourselves.

People grow proud as religion becomes outward and worldly.

The Laodiceans imagined themselves rich, yet were spiritually **poor**; they believed they could see, but were **blind** to their condition and danger.

They lacked the clothing of justification and sanctification. Exposed to sin and shame, they were **naked**, without the shelter of God in whom alone the soul finds lasting safety.

Christ nevertheless offered them the counsel they needed.

He revealed where genuine riches could be found and how to receive them.

They must relinquish **sin and self-confidence**, not anything truly valuable, so that they might receive the treasures he gives.

From Christ they must receive white clothing: **his righteousness counted as theirs for justification**, together with holiness and sanctification.

By yielding to his word and Spirit, they would receive sight to recognize their way and their final destination.

Let us examine ourselves by the standard of Scripture and ask the Holy Spirit to remove pride, prejudice and worldly desire.

Sinners should receive the rebukes of God's word and correcting providence as signs of concern for their souls.

Christ stands at the door and knocks through **providential events, Scripture's warnings and teaching, and the influences of his Spirit**.

Those who open to him enjoy his fellowship.

What we offer him may be a poor meal, but what he brings supplies a rich feast of **fresh grace and consolation**.

Christ overcame every temptation and conflict he faced.

Those who are made like him in his trials will also be made like him in glory, sharing his promised victory.

The final call to listen belongs not only to these seven congregations, but to **everyone**.
