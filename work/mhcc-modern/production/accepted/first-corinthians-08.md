# 1 Corinthians 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The danger of having a high conceit of knowledge. | 1–6 |
| The mischief of offending weak brethren. | 7–13 |

## Knowledge without love produces pride (verses 1–6)

Nothing proves ignorance more commonly than **a conceited opinion of one's own knowledge**.

A person may know many facts yet know nothing in the way that truly matters.

Those who become proud of what they understand are often the least likely to use it well.

Satan harms some people through **pride in intellectual powers**, just as he tempts others through bodily pleasures.

Knowledge that makes people arrogant, self-confident and dismissive of others is just as dangerous as **self-righteous pride**, even when the information itself is accurate.

Without **holy love and godly affections**, all our knowledge is worthless.

The pagans worshiped many beings they called gods or lords, some considered greater and others lesser.

But none was God in truth.

Christians know **the one God who created everything and rules over everything**.

Paul speaks of **one God, the Father**, as the Godhead who alone is the rightful object of all religious worship.

He also speaks of **one Lord, Jesus Christ**: Emmanuel, **God manifested in flesh, one with the Father and united with us**.

Christ is the appointed Mediator and Lord of all.

Through him we come to the Father, and through him the Father gives us every spiritual blessing by **the Holy Spirit's power and work**.

We must refuse religious worship not only to idols, but also to creatures wrongly treated as divine, including **saints and angels**.

Above all, examine yourselves: do you truly approach God **through faith in Christ**?

## Do not harm weaker believers through careless liberty (verses 7–13)

Eating a particular kind of food or abstaining from it does not make a person acceptable before God.

Yet Paul warns against **creating a stumbling block for a weaker believer**.

Someone with a sensitive conscience might imitate a person who knows an idol is nothing.

But instead of eating the food simply as ordinary food, the weaker believer might eat it **as an offering to the idol**, becoming guilty of idolatry.

The distinction matters.

A person possessing Christ's Spirit will love people whom Christ loved **enough to die for them**.

Injuries done to Christians are done to Christ himself.

The most serious injury is **entangling them in sin and wounding their consciences**.

Christians must be especially careful not to put others in a position where they may stumble, **even through an act harmless in itself**.

If we must guard other people's souls in this way, how much more carefully should we protect **our own**!

Avoid moving unnecessarily close to evil or even its appearance.

Many people excuse such risks in public life by arguments that sound plausible.

But no one can sin against a brother or sister **without offending Christ** and endangering their own soul.
