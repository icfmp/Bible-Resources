# Nehemiah 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews complain of grievances. | 1–5 |
| Nehemiah redresses the grievances. | 6–13 |
| Nehemiah's forbearance. | 14–19 |

## The people's grievances (verses 1–5)

People exploit one another. Those who despise the poor insult their Creator.

This is disgraceful conduct for anyone, but especially hateful when practiced by people who profess to be Christians.

We should sympathize with oppressed people and grieve over the hardships under which so many suffer.

We ought to put ourselves in their place, remembering them both in prayer and through practical help.

People who show no mercy must expect judgment without mercy.

## Nehemiah confronts the abuses (verses 6–13)

Nehemiah knew that however high, thick or strong he built Jerusalem's walls, the city would remain insecure while injustice continued inside.

The right way to reform people's conduct is to **convince their consciences**.

Those who truly fear God will not covet worldly wealth or treat brothers and sisters cruelly.

Nothing brings more disgrace upon religion than worldliness and hardheartedness in those who claim to practice it.

People who insist harshly on every one of their own rights can scarcely persuade others to give up theirs.

When reasoning with selfish people, it is helpful to contrast their conduct with the generosity of others.

But the greatest example is **Christ**, who was rich and became poor for us, so that through his poverty we might become rich (**2 Corinthians 8:9**).

The people did what they had promised.

Good promises are valuable, but **doing what was promised is better**.

## Nehemiah's restraint as governor (verses 14–19)

Those who truly fear God will not dare to act cruelly or unjustly.

Everyone holding public office should remember that its purpose is to **do good**, not to enrich the officeholder.

Nehemiah asked God in prayer to remember his service.

He was not claiming to have earned favor from God.

Instead, he trusted God to make up for what he had spent or sacrificed for God's honor.

He spoke and acted as a person conscious of his own sin.

He did not demand a reward as a debt owed to him.

He looked to the Lord who graciously rewards even **a cup of cold water given to a disciple** for Christ's sake.

Fear and love of God, together with true love for brothers and sisters, lead to every good work.

These works are proper **evidence of justifying faith**, not the price paid to obtain it.

Our reconciled God regards such people for good according to their service for his people.
