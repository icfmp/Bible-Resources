# Revelation 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A vision of God, as on his glorious throne, around which were twenty-four elders and four living creatures. | 1–8 |
| Whose songs, and those of the holy angels, the apostle heard. | 9–11 |

## The heavenly throne and its worshipers (verses 1–8)

After Christ commanded John to record the things concerning the churches, he received another vision.

He saw **a throne in heaven**, representing the Lord's universal authority.

The glorious One seated on it was not described through human features that could be copied into an image. Only surpassing brightness was revealed, pointing to the excellence of God's nature and his awesome justice.

A **rainbow** surrounded the throne, an appropriate sign of the covenant of promise established with Christ as head of the church and with all God's people in him.

Its prevailing green color suggested the renewing and refreshing character of the new covenant.

Around the throne were **twenty-four seats with twenty-four elders**.

Henry considers it **probable**, not certain, that they represent God's whole church.

Their seats signify honor, rest and satisfaction.

Their places around the throne signify nearness to God and the privilege of seeing and enjoying him.

Their **white clothing** symbolizes both the righteousness credited to believers and their holiness; their golden crowns signify glory shared with God.

Lightning and voices proceeded from the throne: solemn declarations to the church of God's sovereign will and purpose.

Before it burned **seven lamps of fire**, signifying the gifts, graces and operations of the Holy Spirit throughout Christ's churches, given according to the will of God who sits on the throne.

In the gospel church, the cleansing basin is **the blood of Jesus Christ**, which washes away all sin.

Everyone entering God's gracious presence on earth and his glorious presence in heaven must be cleansed by that blood.

John also saw **four living creatures** standing between the throne and the elders.

Henry proposes that these **seem to signify genuine ministers of the gospel**, serving between God and his people.

The features of the creatures represent **wisdom, courage, diligence and discretion**, together with holy desires that rise toward heaven.

This is Henry's proposed symbolic reading, not an identification the passage explicitly settles.

## Eternal thanksgiving and worship (verses 9–11)

All true believers attribute their **redemption, conversion, present privileges and future hopes** entirely to the eternal and most holy God.

This gives rise to the thankful and harmonious praises of the redeemed in heaven.

If we wish to imitate them on earth, let our praise be **constant rather than intermittent**, **united rather than divided**, **thankful rather than cold and mechanical**, and **humble rather than self-confident**.
