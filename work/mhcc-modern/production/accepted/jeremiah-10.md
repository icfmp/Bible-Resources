# Jeremiah 10 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The absurdity of idolatry. | 1–16 |
| Destruction denounced against Jerusalem. | 17–25 |

## The Lord alone is God (verses 1–16)

The prophet displays the **glory of Israel's God** and exposes the foolishness of worshiping idols.

Charms and other attempts to gain supernatural help or discover what will happen in the future copy **the wicked customs of pagan nations**.

Let us stand in reverence before the Lord.

We must not provoke him by giving another being the glory that belongs to **him alone**.

Yet he is ready to forgive and save everyone who **repents and believes in the name of his Son, Jesus Christ**.

Faith learns these blessed truths from **God's word**.

Knowledge claimed apart from that source, when used as an alternative authority concerning God and salvation, leads to **empty and worthless doctrines**.

## The coming destruction and the prophet's prayer (verses 17–25)

The Jews who still lived in their own land felt secure.

But sooner or later sinners will discover that **everything God has said is true**, including the warnings they dismissed as mere threats.

Submission to God sustains a believer through whatever sorrow he appoints.

But what can make the burden of **his judgment** bearable for someone who meets it with stubborn despair?

People cannot expect to prosper if they fail to take God with them **through faith and prayer in every part of life**.

The news of the approaching enemy was terrifying.

Yet human plans, however deeply considered and skillfully arranged, can be shattered in a moment.

Events often turn out **exactly contrary to what we intended or expected**.

If the Lord has guided our feet into the paths of peace and righteousness, we should ask him to enable us **to continue walking in those paths**.

Do not pray, **“Lord, never correct me.”**

Pray instead, **“Lord, do not correct me in anger.”**

We may bear the pain of **God's correcting rod**, but we cannot bear **the weight of his wrath**.

Those who restrain themselves from prayer reveal that they do not know God as they should.

People who know him **seek him and seek his favor**.

Even severe correction can become a cause for deep gratitude when it convinces sinners of **truths that bring healing**.

They will then humble themselves before the Lord.
