# Ezra 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The adversaries of the temple. | 1–5 |
| The building of the temple is hindered. | 6–24 |

## Opposition to the rebuilding (verses 1–5)

Every sincere attempt to revive true religion provokes the opposition of Satan and those under his influence.

These adversaries were the **Samaritans**, who had been settled in Israel's territory (**2 Kings 17**).

They clearly did not intend to join in worship of the Lord according to his word.

Those who discourage a good project or weaken its workers should consider whose example they are following.

## The temple work is obstructed (verses 6–24)

It is an old slander to claim that the prosperity of God's church endangers kings and rulers.

Nothing could be further from the truth.

Genuine faith teaches people to **respect and obey lawful authorities**.

But when divine commands and human laws directly conflict, **we must obey God rather than human beings**, patiently accepting the consequences.

Everyone who loves the gospel should avoid even the appearance of wrongdoing, so as not to provide needless encouragement to the church's opponents.

The world is often ready to believe any accusation against God's people while refusing to listen to their defense.

The king was misled through fraudulent reports and falsehoods.

Rulers often see and hear through other people's eyes and ears and make decisions based on descriptions of events that are inaccurate or dishonest.

But **God's judgment is entirely just**.

He sees everything as it really is.
