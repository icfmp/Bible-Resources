# 2 Samuel 17 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Ahithophel's counsel overthrown. | 1–21 |
| He hangs himself, Absalom pursues David. | 22–29 |

## God defeats Ahithophel's counsel (verses 1–21)

What an astonishing effect of divine Providence!

God blinded Absalom's judgment and influenced his heart so that he would not accept Ahithophel's advice immediately, but would also seek Hushai's counsel.

No one can successfully resist the God who can turn a person's own actions against that person and destroy a scheme through its author's mistakes and uncontrolled desires.

Ahithophel's **earlier counsel** had been followed because God intended to discipline David.

But his **later counsel** was rejected because God had no intention of allowing David to be destroyed.

The Lord can overrule every human plan.

Whatever wisdom or assistance people provide, **success comes from God alone**.

He will not allow his people to perish finally.

## Ahithophel dies; friends help David (verses 22–29)

When Ahithophel realized his counsel had been rejected, his frustrated pride became unbearable.

What breaks a proud person's heart may not even disturb a humble person's sleep.

Ahithophel recognized that Absalom's cause would probably fail.

He also feared that he himself would be punished publicly.

In despair, he ended his own life.

Henry describes this as a self-inflicted judgment driven by injured pride and fear, not as an example for others to follow.

Thus a mind filled with plans to do harm came to a wretched end.

Absalom pursued David.

But notice how God sometimes provides comfort and practical help through **strangers** when our own relatives have disappointed or betrayed us.

Our great King does not need our help.

Nevertheless, Christ promises to accept and reward acts of kindness toward the least of his brothers and sisters—those who are **sick, poor or lacking basic necessities**—as though they had been done to him personally.
