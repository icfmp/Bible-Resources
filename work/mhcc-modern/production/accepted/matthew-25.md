# Matthew 25 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The parable of the ten virgins. | 1–13 |
| The parable of the talents. | 14–30 |
| The judgment. | 31–46 |

## The wise and foolish bridesmaids (verses 1–13)

The parable draws on Jewish marriage customs to teach about the great day of **Christ's coming**.

Christians profess to attend to him, honor him and wait for his arrival.

The wise virgins represent **genuine believers** and the foolish ones represent **hypocrites**.

The most important kind of wisdom or folly concerns the condition of our souls.

Many people carry **a lamp of outward religious profession** without possessing sound spiritual knowledge or lasting conviction.

They lack the inward dispositions that **God's renewing Spirit** creates.

Good works should shine visibly before others.

But they will not shine for long without a settled, living principle of **faith in Christ and love for God and neighbors**.

All ten fell asleep.

The delay pictures the interval between a person's real or apparent conversion and Christ's coming, whether through **that person's death** or through **the final judgment**.

Christ may arrive later than our expectations, but never later than the appointed time.

Even the wise virgins kept their lamps alight **while failing to remain awake**.

Genuine Christians may grow careless.

One degree of neglect can lead to another; those who become drowsy are in danger of falling asleep.

Fear the first signs of spiritual decline.

A sudden call came to meet the bridegroom.

Even those best prepared for death should be diligent in actually preparing for it (**2 Peter 3:14**).

The day will expose the truth of everyone's condition.

The foolish virgins discovered they lacked oil.

Those who settle for outward religion without **true grace** will discover their lack sooner or later.

A religious profession may seem sufficient during ordinary life but fail in the shadow of death.

Some want to die the death of the righteous without having lived a righteous life.

**No believer can transfer saving grace to another person**.

Even those possessing the most grace have none to spare; the strongest still need more from Christ.

A soul that has neglected salvation may be overwhelmed with fear on a deathbed.

The person then tries to obtain the grace that should already have been received and exercised.

Death and judgment may arrive before that person turns sincerely to Christ.

Only those prepared for heaven **while living** will enter it afterward.

Sudden death need not rob genuinely prepared believers of their happiness.

But the door will eventually be closed.

Hypocritical confidence may survive many warnings yet fail when admission is finally denied.

An unexpected summons may startle a true Christian, whose faith can then shine brighter.

It exposes the empty light of the mere professor.

**Watch over your soul and live in reverent dependence upon the Lord every day.**

## The servants and the talents (verses 14–30)

Christ does not employ servants merely to remain idle.

Everything they receive belongs to him.

Strictly speaking, nothing is theirs independently except their sin.

The gifts Christ gives are meant to be used **in his service**.

The Spirit's gifts are given for the benefit of others.

A day of accountability is coming.

We must answer for what we have gained spiritually ourselves and how we have served others through our opportunities and advantages.

This does **not** mean that improving natural abilities **earns divine grace**.

Rather, it is a Christian's privilege and freedom to serve the Redeemer, advance his glory and benefit his people.

Christ's love moves believers to stop living for themselves and begin living for **the One who died and rose for them**.

People who believe God cannot be pleased and service to him is pointless rarely accomplish anything useful for his kingdom.

They complain that God demands more than they are able to perform and condemns them for what they cannot help.

Whatever their excuses, such people reveal dislike of **the Lord's character and work**.

The lazy servant lost what he had been entrusted with.

This may apply to temporal gifts, but even more clearly to **opportunities and means of grace**.

Those who fail to recognize God's visitation can lose opportunities that would have led them to peace.

The sentence of **outer darkness** expresses the misery of final judgment.

Here Jesus moves beyond the story's illustration to the actual judgment it signifies.

Let us neither envy unrepentant sinners nor covet their perishing possessions.

## The final judgment of the nations (verses 31–46)

Jesus described the judgment that will separate people into eternal happiness or misery.

Christ will return in **his own glory as Mediator**, as well as the Father's glory.

In this world righteous and wicked people share towns, churches and families.

The failures of some believers and the hypocrisy of some unbelievers often make them difficult to distinguish.

Death comes to both.

But on the great day **the Shepherd will separate his own people from those who do not belong to him**.

All worldly distinctions will disappear, but the distinction between **holy and unholy, righteous and wicked**, will remain forever.

God's people will inherit **a kingdom**.

Even the most glorious earthly kingdom offers only a faint comparison with their heavenly happiness.

The kingdom has been **prepared**.

The Father provided it in wisdom and power.

**The Son purchased it**.

**The Spirit prepares believers to enjoy it**, transforming them for the holiness of their inheritance.

It was prepared from the foundation of the world.

The saints were chosen for this happiness, and the happiness prepared for them, from eternity.

They **inherit** the kingdom.

An inheritance is not wages earned through personal labor.

**God makes people heirs of heaven**.

Jesus does not teach that charitable acts **purchase eternal life**.

Rather, works performed for his sake, through him, identify believers whose lives have been renewed by his Spirit.

They are **effects and evidence of grace**.

Those condemned had repeatedly been invited to come to Christ for life and rest, but refused.

They are justly told to **depart** from the One they would not approach.

Their excuses cannot reverse the judgment.

Final punishment is everlasting and cannot be changed afterward.

Life and death, blessing and curse are placed before us.

Our chosen path matters because it leads toward our final condition.
