## Those excluded from Israel's assembly (verses 1–8)

We should value the privileges of belonging to God's people, for ourselves and for our children, above every other advantage.

Under the gospel, no bodily defect, ancestor's offense or difference in nationality bars someone from the church's spiritual blessings.

But a corrupt heart can keep us from enjoying those blessings.

And the bad example of parents—or marriages that draw a family away from faith—can keep children from seeking them.

## Cleanliness in Israel's camp (verses 9–14)

Nothing offensive or unclean was to be tolerated in the Lord's camp.

If Israel had to pay careful attention to the cleanliness of the body, how much more should we preserve purity of heart and mind!

## The runaway servant, lending, vows and everyday generosity (verses 15–25)

It is honorable to shelter and protect someone vulnerable, provided the person is not deliberately engaged in wrongdoing.

People who convert to the truth should be treated with particular tenderness, so that they are not drawn back to worldly life.

We cannot honor God with our possessions unless we acquired them honestly.

What matters is not only **what we give**, but **how we obtained it**.

Where someone borrows money to engage in an activity that brings or may bring profit, Henry considers it just for the lender to share in that gain.

But people who borrow simply to obtain necessary food should be treated with compassion.

A solemn promise deliberately spoken must be fulfilled completely.

Israelites were allowed to pick and eat grapes or grain along their journey, but they were forbidden to carry any away.

This arrangement showed the abundant food Canaan would provide and made provision for poor travelers.

We should be similarly generous and hospitable, willing to give without treating every gift as a loss.

Yet we must not exploit the generosity of others or use permitted privileges as a way to take more than is right.

Faithfulness in our commitments and respect for other people's property should characterize God's people.
