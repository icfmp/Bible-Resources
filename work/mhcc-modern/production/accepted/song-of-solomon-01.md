# Song of Solomon 1 — Modern English

## The title of the song (verse 1)

This is **the Song of Songs**, superior to every other song in Henry's reading.

It is wholly concerned with the glory of **Jesus Christ** and the love between him and the people he has redeemed.

## The believer confesses sin while longing for Christ (verses 2–6)

The church, or more particularly the individual believer, speaks here as **the bride of the Messiah, the King**.

The kisses of his mouth represent **assurances of pardon**.

Those assurances fill believers with peace and joy in faith and cause them to overflow with hope through **the Holy Spirit's power**.

Gracious souls find their greatest pleasure in **loving Christ and being loved by him**.

His love is better and more desirable than any good the world can offer.

The name of Christ is no longer like perfume shut up in its container.

It is like **perfume poured out**: the gospel openly reveals the fullness and freeness of his grace.

Those whom Christ redeems and sanctifies are pictured as the young women who love and follow him wherever he goes (**Revelation 14:4**).

They ask him to **draw them**, giving spiritual life and strength through the Holy Spirit.

The more clearly we see Christ's glory, the more we recognize our inability to follow him as we should—and the more strongly we want to follow.

Notice how quickly this prayer receives an answer.

Those waiting at Wisdom's entrance are led into truth and comfort.

In the King's chamber, sorrows disappear.

Everything we enjoy spiritually comes from Christ, to whom we owe all gratitude.

We should remember his love with thanksgiving longer and more deeply than anything else.

Only **sincere love** is acceptable to him (**Ephesians 6:24**).

The daughters of Jerusalem may represent people who profess faith but are not yet established in it.

The bride is dark like **the tents of nomadic Arabs**, yet lovely as **the splendid curtains in Solomon's palace**.

Likewise, a believer is sinful and defiled by nature, yet made beautiful through divine grace and renewed in God's image.

Even after renewal, some sinful corruption remains.

Yet the believer is lovely because **accepted in Christ**.

Someone despised by the world may be precious in God's sight.

The bride's darkened appearance reflects the difficult treatment she endured.

Those belonging to the church as her mother—but not to God as Father—had acted harshly toward her.

Their demands caused hardships and made her neglect the care of her own soul.

Here is a picture of a poor and suffering woman chosen to marry a prince.

Christ's love similarly finds people burdened with sin, exhausted by labor and heavy with sorrow.

What a great change occurs when **his love becomes known to them**!

## The believer asks Christ for guidance and rest (verses 7 and 8)

How affectionate is the title given to Christ: **“You whom my soul loves.”**

Those who truly love him may approach with boldness and humble confidence.

Are God's people under the noon-day heat of outward suffering or inward spiritual conflict?

**Christ has rest for them.**

Believers want to share fully in the privileges of Christ's flock and fear straying away from him more than any other danger.

God is ready to answer their prayers.

Follow **the good old way** and the footsteps left by faithful believers.

Learn from their example and listen to the guidance of trustworthy ministers—**the under-shepherds**.

Bring those entrusted to your care; they too are welcome.

Christians should pray that God will direct their earthly work, circumstances and employment so that **their Savior remains before them at all times**.

## Christ praises the church, and the church values him (verses 9–17)

The Bridegroom praises the bride.

In Christ's eyes his believers are excellent and valuable, fitted to serve as instruments of his glory.

The ornaments used in that culture picture the **spiritual gifts and graces** Christ grants every true believer (**Song 1:10–11**).

The graces are many and depend upon one another.

The One who began this good work will also **finish** it.

The grace believers receive from Christ's fullness produces **faith, loving affections and gratitude**.

Yet **Christ himself**, not merely his gifts, is their greatest treasure.

Henry explains the word translated **“camphire”** as meaning **“atonement or propitiation”**.

Christ is precious because **he is the propitiation for our sins**.

No rival may occupy his place in the soul.

Believers desire him to remain in their hearts through **the whole night of earthly troubles**.

Christ delights in the good that his grace has produced within their souls.

Those made holy should give thanks for the grace that made them beautiful when by nature they were corrupted.

The bride's modest, humble gaze suggests **simplicity and godly sincerity**.

Her eyes are illuminated and guided by the Holy Spirit, **the blessed Dove**.

In response, the church praises Christ:

**He is the perfect original; I am only a faint and imperfect copy.**

Someone may be physically attractive but unpleasant in character.

Christ is both beautiful and delightful.

In **verse 16**, the believer praises the appointed means of grace through which faithful people enjoy fellowship with Christ.

Whether worshiping in God's courts, resting privately, doing daily work, lying sick in bed or even confined in prison, a sense of **God's presence can turn any place into paradise**.

Daily communion with **the Father, Son and Holy Spirit** gives the soul a living hope of an inheritance that cannot perish, be defiled or fade away.
