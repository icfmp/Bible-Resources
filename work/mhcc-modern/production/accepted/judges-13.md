# Judges 13 — Modern English

## The birth of Samson is announced (verses 1–7)

Israel did evil again, and God gave the people into the hands of the Philistines.

During this period of suffering, Samson was born.

His parents had long been childless.

Many remarkable servants of God were born to mothers who had waited a long time for children.

Mercies awaited patiently often become extraordinary blessings.

They also encourage others not to give up hope in God's kindness.

The angel showed that he knew Manoah's wife's affliction.

God often sends comfort at exactly the right time, when his people feel their troubles most deeply.

Samson, the coming deliverer, was to be set apart for the Lord.

Manoah's wife was convinced that the messenger came from God.

She gave her husband a detailed account of both the promise she had received and the instructions given.

Husbands and wives should share how God has met them and what they have learned about him.

In that way they can help one another grow in holy living.

## The angel gives further instruction (verses 8–14)

Blessed are those who believe without seeing, as Manoah did.

Good people care more about knowing **what duty God requires of them** than about knowing exactly what the future holds.

Our duty belongs to us; events belong to God.

He will direct those who sincerely ask him to teach them what they ought to do.

Godly parents, in particular, should ask for his help.

The angel repeated the earlier instructions.

We need great care in ordering both our own lives and our children's lives properly, remaining distinct from the world's sins and offering ourselves as living sacrifices to the Lord.

## Manoah offers sacrifice (verses 15–23)

When Manoah asked for guidance in his duty, he received an answer.

When he asked merely to satisfy curiosity, the answer was withheld.

Scripture gives us sufficient direction about our responsibilities.

But God has never promised to satisfy all our questions.

Secret matters remain beyond our knowledge, and we must accept not understanding them during this life.

The Lord's name is wonderful and mysterious.

Yet through his wonderful acts he reveals everything we truly need to know of him.

Prayer is the soul rising toward God.

Without Christ dwelling in the heart by faith, our services are only offensive smoke.

In him, they rise like an acceptable flame.

Henry sees in the angel ascending through the sacrificial flame a picture of **Christ offering himself**.

Through his own blood he entered the Most Holy Place once for all (Hebrews 9:12).

Manoah responded with terror, fearing they would die.

His wife showed greater faith.

As his suitable helper, she encouraged him.

Believers may have experienced fellowship with God through his word and prayer, recognized his gracious presence and received signs that he has accepted their service.

Those memories should encourage them when later days become dark and difficult.

God would not have begun his gracious work in their souls merely to abandon them to destruction.

His work is perfect.

We should reason as Manoah's wife did: if the Lord intended to destroy us in wrath, he would not first have granted the signs of favor he has given.

## Samson is born and begins to show God's gifts (verses 24 and 25)

The Spirit of the Lord began to move Samson while he was still young.

This showed that God had blessed him.

Where the Lord gives a special calling, he also gives his Spirit to prepare people for it.

Blessed indeed are those in whom God's Spirit begins the work of grace during childhood.

Samson drank no wine or strong drink.

Yet he was strong and courageous because the Spirit of God moved him.

Therefore, do not be drunk with wine; instead, be filled with the Spirit.
