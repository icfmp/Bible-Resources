# Esther 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews lament their danger. | 1–4 |
| Esther undertakes to plead for the Jews. | 5–17 |

## The Jews grieve over the decree (verses 1–4)

Mordecai openly acknowledged his connection with the Jewish people.

Trouble affecting God's church should weigh more heavily upon us than private misfortune.

It is especially painful to know that our own conduct has contributed to other people's suffering.

God will preserve those whose sensitive consciences expose them to danger.

## Esther decides to intercede (verses 5–17)

We naturally shrink from tasks that may cost us dearly or endanger us.

But when Christ's cause and people need our service, we must take up our cross and follow him.

Christians should be corrected when they seek personal comfort or safety at the expense of the public good.

The Persian law against approaching the king uninvited was explicit and widely known.

**It is not like that at the court of the King of kings.**

We may always come boldly to the throne of grace, expecting God's peaceful answer to faithful prayer.

Through **Jesus' blood**, we are welcome even into the holiest place.

God's Providence had arranged for the king's affection toward Esther to cool by this time, according to Henry's interpretation.

This made her faith and courage face a greater test and the king's eventual kindness more remarkable.

Haman, Henry supposes, had probably also tried to turn the king against her.

Mordecai believed that the Jewish people's cause would succeed one way or another.

Esther could therefore undertake her duty with confidence.

This was the language of strong faith, trusting God's promise even when hope seemed impossible.

Someone who tries to preserve life through sinful methods instead of trusting God while doing duty will lose it by sin.

Esther's royal position had been prepared through Providence for precisely such service.

Gratitude obliged her to act for God and his people.

Otherwise, she would fail to fulfill the purpose of her promotion.

God's wise counsel is at work in every event, directing it toward the church's ultimate good.

Each of us should consider why God has placed us where we are and seek to fulfill our calling before the opportunity passes.

Once we have committed ourselves and our cause to God, we may courageously undertake his service.

Every earthly danger is small compared with the danger of losing our souls.

Sinners who tremble before God can resemble Esther before the king: they fear entrusting themselves entirely to his mercy.

Let such a person approach with earnest prayer.

God will receive them even more graciously than Ahasuerus received Esther.

**God's cause will prevail**, and those united to it are safe.
