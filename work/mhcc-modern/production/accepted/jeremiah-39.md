# Jeremiah 39 — Modern English

## Jerusalem is captured (verses 1–10)

Jerusalem was so strongly fortified that its inhabitants believed no enemy could ever enter it.

But the people's sins provoked God to withdraw his protection.

Without him, the city was just as vulnerable as any other.

Zedekiah's eyes were put out.

A man who had shut his eyes to the **clear light of God's word** was now condemned to literal darkness.

Those who refuse to believe what God says will eventually be convinced when his words are fulfilled.

Notice the astonishing changes of Providence.

Earthly possessions and circumstances are never secure.

The Lord's judgments are just.

But whether he makes people rich or poor, neither condition will do them real good while they **cling to their sins**.

## Jeremiah receives kind treatment (verses 11–14)

God's faithful servants are the people best prepared for whatever may happen.

Even while wicked people suffer, those who belong to God may experience rescue and comfort.

They sometimes receive **more kindness from people who make no profession of religion** than from people who outwardly claim to be godly.

The Lord can raise up friends for his people, do them good and fulfill every one of his promises.

## Ebed-melech receives a promise of safety (verses 15–18)

God sent a message to assure **Ebed-melech** that his great kindness toward Jeremiah would be rewarded.

The reason for the promise was explicit: **“Because you have trusted in me,” says the Lord**.

God regards not only the outward service people perform, but **the faith and motives from which they act**.

Those who trust him while doing their duty, as Ebed-melech did, will discover that their hope does not fail even amid the greatest danger.
