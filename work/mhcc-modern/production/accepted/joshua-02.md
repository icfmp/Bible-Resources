# Joshua 2 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Rahab receives and hides two Israelites. | 1–7 |
| Rahab and the spies. | 8–21 |
| The return of the spies. | 22–24 |

## Rahab protects the spies (verses 1–7)

Faith in God's promises does not make careful action unnecessary.

Instead, faith encourages us to use the appropriate means.

God's providence guided Israel's spies to Rahab's home.

God knew there was someone there who would be faithful to them, although they could not have known it.

Rahab appears to have been an innkeeper.

Henry thinks her former reputation for immoral living is uncertain; if it was true, she had left those practices behind.

Events that appear accidental to us often serve great purposes under God's providence.

Rahab welcomed the spies despite her king and nation's hostility to Israel.

Her action was an expression of faith (James 2:25).

True faith gave her courage to rise above fear of other people and cast in her lot with God's people.

She helped them because she respected Israel and its God, not because she expected profit or intended wrongdoing.

But Henry refuses to excuse the **falsehood she told**.

Even if excuses can be suggested, we should not explain away her guilt.

Her knowledge of God's law was limited.

A similar lie told by someone possessing the fuller light of revelation would deserve serious correction.

## Rahab and the spies agree on her protection (verses 8–21)

Rahab had heard of God's miracles for Israel.

She trusted that his promises and warnings would certainly come true.

She recognized that safety lay only in submitting to him and joining his people.

This revealed the genuine principle of divine faith within her.

Notice the spies' promises.

God's goodness is described through **kindness and truth** (Psalm 117:2).

We should imitate him in both qualities.

People who intend to keep promises conscientiously will make them carefully.

The spies included necessary conditions in their agreement.

The scarlet cord recalls the Passover blood placed upon Israelite doorposts.

It pictured the sinner's safety under Christ's atoning blood and the need to seek refuge there from God's righteous judgment.

The cord Rahab used to help save the spies would also be used for her own safety.

What we employ in honoring and serving God may become an instrument of his blessing toward us.

## The spies report to Joshua (verses 22–24)

The spies returned with encouraging news.

The people of Canaan were losing courage because of Israel.

They had neither the wisdom to surrender nor the courage to resist.

A troubled conscience and fear of God's anger may terrify ungodly people without leading them to repentance.

That fear can be a warning that judgment is near.

But even the greatest sinners may still receive abundant grace.

Let them flee without delay to Christ, and all will be well.
