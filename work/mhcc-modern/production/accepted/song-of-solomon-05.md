# Song of Solomon 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Christ's answer. | 1 |
| The disappointments of the church from her own folly. | 2–8 |
| The excellences of Christ. | 9–16 |

## Christ welcomes his people's invitation (verse 1)

See how readily **Christ accepts his people's invitation**!

Whatever small measure of goodness remains in us would disappear unless he preserved it.

He also invites those he loves to eat and drink abundantly: the acts of worship in which his people honor him are **means through which he gives grace**.

## Carelessness interrupts fellowship with Christ (verses 2–8)

Churches and individual believers may, through carelessness and false security, provoke Christ to withdraw the comfort of his presence.

We should recognize our periods of spiritual slumber and unhealthy attitudes.

Christ **knocks** to awaken us through **his word and Spirit**, through difficulties and through conscience. Henry points to **Revelation 3:20**.

Even when we neglect Christ, he does not forget us. His love should move us to respond with love, including acts of self-denial; such surrender benefits us.

Careless souls make little of the Lord Jesus. We cannot send someone else to open the door for us.

We make excuses: we have no desire, no strength or no time. But these excuses amount to **taking Christ lightly**. Refusing to leave a warm bed or face a cold wind for him is a kind of contempt.

The image of the Bridegroom putting his hand to the door represents the **powerful influence of God's grace and the Spirit working upon the heart**.

The sweet-smelling myrrh on the door handles represents the believer rising above self-indulgence, seeking Christ in prayer and removing obstacles to fellowship.

Yet the Beloved has gone away. Through this loss of felt communion, Christ teaches believers to prize his gracious visits more highly.

Nevertheless, the soul still calls him **her Beloved**. The sense of desertion does **not necessarily mean despair**.

We may confess both faith and weakness: **“Lord, I believe; help my unbelief.”**

Once God awakens us, memories of ignored conviction and rejected invitations become painfully bitter.

The believer searches diligently, not only praying but also seeking Christ in the places and ways where he is ordinarily found.

The watchmen wound her. Henry notes that **some interpreters** understand this as ministers applying God's word wrongly to people with awakened consciences.

Her appeal to the daughters of Jerusalem expresses the distressed believer's desire even for the **prayers of the weakest Christian**.

An awakened soul feels Christ's withdrawal more keenly than any outward distress.

## The church praises Christ's excellence (verses 9–16)

Even people who know little of Christ may notice something beautiful in believers who bear his likeness.

When people begin to ask about his character and perfections, there is reason for hope.

Christians who know him should eagerly help others know him too.

To a heart enlightened to discern spiritual things, **his divine glory makes him altogether lovely**.

Henry interprets his white appearance as **the innocence of Christ's spotless life**, and his red appearance as **the blood shed in his suffering and death**.

The other images belong to the figurative language of their original time, depicting bodily beauty and gracious character. We may not immediately understand every allusion.

Christ will ultimately be **glorified in his saints and admired by everyone who believes**.

May his love constrain us to live for his glory.
