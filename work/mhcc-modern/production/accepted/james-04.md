# James 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Here are cautions against corrupt affections, and love of this world, which is enmity to God. | 1–10 |
| Exhortations to undertake no affairs of life, without constant regard to the will and providence of God. | 11–17 |

## Resist sinful desires and submit to God (verses 1–10)

Quarrels and conflicts arise from **corrupt desires within the human heart**.

We need to put to death the sinful passions that struggle within us. Worldly and bodily desires are like illnesses that never permit lasting contentment.

They also hinder prayer, diverting our longings away from God.

We may misuse even a genuine mercy after receiving it, because our hearts are wrongly directed.

When people ask God for prosperity, their aims may be corrupt. A prayer arising chiefly from selfish lust rather than spiritual grace rightly receives no answer.

**Worldliness is hostility toward God.** An enemy can be reconciled, but a settled attitude of hostility must itself be abandoned.

A person may possess many things in this life and remain within God's love. But the one who sets the heart upon the world and would conform to it rather than lose its friendship chooses opposition to God.

The Holy Spirit does not produce envy, greed and self-centered striving. Our fallen nature does.

The Spirit teaches us to use what we have for the good of those around us.

God's grace changes the worldly spirit into another kind of disposition.

The proud resist **God's truth with their understanding**, **God's commands with their will**, and **God's providence with their passions**. It is no wonder, then, that God opposes the proud.

The humble receive greater grace because they recognize their need, pray for help and give thanks for what they receive.

Submit **your mind to God's revealed truth**, your will to his commands, and your circumstances to his providence.

The devil continues pursuing people who yield to him. But those who put on God's whole armor and resist firmly will see him retreat.

Let sinners turn to God and seek his grace. **All sin must be mourned**, either now through godly sorrow or afterward in misery.

The Lord will comfort a person who genuinely grieves over sin and exalt one who humbles himself before him.

## Speak kindly and plan in dependence upon Providence (verses 11–17)

Let our speech be governed by **kindness as well as truth and justice**.

Christians are brothers and sisters. We must not elevate personal opinions into laws for everyone else.

The law of God judges us all; we have no authority to replace it with our own preferences.

Consider how quickly ambitious, calculating people leave God out of their plans!

Human life is brief and uncertain. We may calculate the exact minute of tomorrow's sunrise, but cannot control **when a cloud of vapor will vanish**.

Our circumstances and pleasures disappear just as quickly, yet eternal happiness or misery depends upon how we respond to God in this short span.

We may make responsible plans for ourselves, family and friends, but should always acknowledge that **our times belong to God**.

Providence frequently overturns even careful human schemes.

Submit every intention and action to the Lord's will. Worldly boasting about future success is foolish, harmful and often a path to disappointment.

**Sins of omission are as real as sins of commission**.

Not doing the good we know we should do will come into judgment just as doing known evil will.

How diligently we should avoid **neglecting prayer, meditation and self-examination**, as well as obvious outward sins!
