# Jeremiah 27 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The neighbouring nations to be subdued. | 1–11 |
| Zedekiah is warned to yield. | 12–18 |
| The vessels of the temple to be carried to Babylon, but afterwards to be restored. | 19–22 |

## The nations are told to accept Babylon's rule (verses 1–11)

Jeremiah had to prepare a **symbolic sign** announcing that the surrounding countries would become subjects of **the king of Babylon**.

God asserted his authority to distribute kingdoms according to his will.

Every earthly possession is a gift the Lord has chosen to permit.

We should therefore be **content with our portion**.

The things of this world cannot be the highest good.

God sometimes grants the largest share of worldly power or wealth to people who are **wicked**.

**Earthly authority does not prove saving grace**.

People refusing to serve the God who made them may be justly compelled to serve **enemies seeking their destruction**.

Jeremiah urged them to preserve their lives by **submitting rather than resisting**.

A meek spirit can accept even the hardest turns of Providence and make the best of a painful situation.

Many people avoid devastating judgments by **submitting to humbling circumstances**.

It is better to carry a **lighter cross God has placed in our way** than draw a heavier one upon ourselves.

The poor in spirit, meek and humble find comfort and escape many miseries that people of a proud and defiant temperament bring upon themselves.

**Obeying God's will is always in our true interest**.

## Zedekiah must submit to the king of Babylon (verses 12–18)

Jeremiah appealed to **King Zedekiah** to surrender to Babylon.

Consider the comparison.

It was wise for Judah to accept the heavy and oppressive yoke of **a cruel earthly ruler** if that submission would preserve their lives.

How much wiser is it to accept **the pleasant and gentle yoke of our Lord and Master Jesus Christ**, securing the eternal welfare of our souls?

Sinners should fear the judgment awaiting those who refuse to let **Christ reign over them**.

Why should anyone suffer **the second death**, far worse than being killed by sword or famine, when submission to the Savior brings life?

Those who encourage sinners to continue in wrongdoing **will perish with them**.

## Sacred vessels are taken away but will return (verses 19–22)

Jeremiah announced that **the bronze furnishings** of the temple would be carried away after **the golden ones**.

Everything would be transported to Babylon.

Yet the prophecy ended in a **promise of mercy**.

The time would come when the vessels would be brought back.

The church's prosperity may not return during our own lifetime.

But we must not despair.

**God's appointed time will come**.
