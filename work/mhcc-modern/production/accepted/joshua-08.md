# Joshua 8 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God encourages Joshua. | 1, 2 |
| The taking of Ai. | 3–22 |
| The destruction of Ai and its king. | 23–29 |
| The law read on Ebal and Gerizim. | 30–35 |

## God encourages Joshua again (verses 1 and 2)

Only after Israel had faithfully removed the forbidden thing that separated it from God did the Lord speak fresh words of comfort and guidance.

When God shows us how to continue our work and spiritual warfare, we have good reason to believe he has restored us to fellowship with him.

Joshua was commanded to attack Ai again.

This time the possessions of the city were not forbidden, as they had been at Jericho. There was therefore no temptation to repeat Achan's particular trespass.

Achan had grasped at possessions God forbade him to take.

He lost both the goods and his life.

Israelites who refused the forbidden things soon received permitted possessions instead.

The way to enjoy what God allows us is to refrain from what he forbids us.

No one finally loses through self-denial.

## Ai is captured (verses 3–22)

Notice Joshua's care, skill and good judgment.

Anyone seeking to persevere in spiritual conflict must not become devoted to ease.

Henry considers it probable that Joshua went alone into the valley to pray for God's blessing.

He did not seek it in vain.

Joshua did not withdraw before the work was finished.

Those who have raised their hands against spiritual enemies must not lower them prematurely.

## Ai and its king are destroyed (verses 23–29)

God, the righteous Judge, had pronounced sentence upon the Canaanites because of their wickedness.

Israel carried out that ancient judgment.

**Nothing in Israel's conduct here should be treated as a precedent for other people.**

Henry considers it likely that the king of Ai had committed exceptionally serious offenses—perhaps even blasphemy against Israel's God.

He offers this as a conjecture, not a verified fact.

## The covenant is renewed at Ebal and Gerizim (verses 30–35)

Joshua reached the mountains Ebal and Gerizim.

He did not put off renewing the covenant merely because Israel had not yet fully settled in the land and enemies still threatened it.

He followed God's instructions (Deuteronomy 11; 27).

We must not postpone covenanting with God until our worldly circumstances are settled.

No ordinary responsibility should cause us to neglect the one thing that is necessary.

The path to real prosperity begins with seeking God first (Matthew 6:33).

Joshua built an altar, and the people offered sacrifices.

Through those offerings, Israel dedicated itself to God as a living sacrifice through a Mediator.

Through Christ's sacrifice of himself, we have peace with God.

It is a great mercy when a people possesses God's law in writing.

That written law should be available in a **language people know**, so that everyone can see and read it.
