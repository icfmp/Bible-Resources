## Simeon's inheritance (verses 1–9)

Judah's people did not object when towns within their assigned territory were given to Simeon, once they understood that they had received more than was right.

If a true believer has gained an advantage improperly, even without intending it, that believer will give it up without complaining.

Love does not insist on its own interests or behave shamefully.

Where love dwells richly, it leads us to share what belongs to us with brothers and sisters who lack what they need.

## Zebulun's inheritance (verses 10–16)

The division of land among Israel's tribes fulfilled the prophetic blessings spoken by Jacob.

Whether the people chose their portions or received them by lot, their inheritance was assigned in the places Jacob had foreseen.

How trustworthy the prophetic word is! It teaches us what to believe and confirms the truths God has revealed.

## The other tribes and Joshua's own inheritance (verses 17–51)

Joshua waited until every other tribe had settled before asking for any land for himself.

He was content to have no settled place until the people were provided for.

This is a model for everyone serving in public office: place the common good before personal advantage.

Those who work hardest to benefit others should seek an inheritance in the heavenly Canaan.

It will be soon enough to enter that inheritance when they have completed all the service they can offer their brothers and sisters.

Nothing gives them greater assurance of their own inheritance than seeking to help others desire, seek and receive it.

Our Lord Jesus came to live on earth in **poverty rather than splendor**.

He provided rest for humanity while having no place to lay his own head.

Christ did not seek to please himself.

Nor did he enter his own inheritance until, through obedience even to death, he had secured the eternal inheritance for all his people.

He will not count his own glory complete until every person he has redeemed has entered heavenly rest.
