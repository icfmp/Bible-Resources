# Ephesians 1 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| A salutation, and an account of saving blessings, as prepared in God's eternal election, as purchased by Christ's blood. | 1–8 |
| And as conveyed in effectual calling: this is applied to the believing Jews, and to the believing Gentiles. | 9–14 |
| The apostle thanks God for their faith and love, and prays for the continuance of their knowledge and hope, with respect to the heavenly inheritance, and to God's powerful working in them. | 15–23 |

## Grace, peace and a faithful profession (verses 1 and 2)

Every Christian ought to be a **saint**, someone set apart for God.

Those who do not truly belong to him here will not become saints in heavenly glory.

Genuine saints are **faithful believers in Jesus Christ** who live consistently with their profession of belonging to him.

Grace means **God's undeserved love and favor** together with the spiritual blessings that flow from it.

Peace includes the inward and outward blessings that are genuinely good for us.

**Grace comes first; peace follows**. There is no true peace without grace.

Neither grace nor peace comes from any source apart from **God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ**.

Even the most mature believers need fresh supplies of the Holy Spirit's grace and should long for further growth.

## Chosen in Christ for adoption and holiness (verses 3–8)

Spiritual and heavenly blessings are the greatest blessings of all.

Possessing them, we cannot finally be miserable; lacking them, we cannot finally be happy.

These gifts flow from God's gracious decision **in Christ before the foundation of the world**.

He chose believers **to become holy**, separated from sin, dedicated to God and sanctified by the Holy Spirit.

Everyone chosen for **happiness as the final goal** is also chosen for **holiness as the appointed path**.

In love, God predestined believers to be **adopted as his children through faith in Jesus Christ**, publicly receiving the privileges of that relationship.

The reconciled, adopted, forgiven sinner therefore gives **all the praise** to the gracious Father.

His love planned redemption, did not spare his own Son, and brought believers to hear, trust and embrace his salvation.

What rich grace to provide **his own Son as our Surety** and freely hand him over for us!

This salvation does **not** encourage wrongdoing.

Instead, it reveals how hateful sin is and how seriously it deserves divine judgment.

The believer's **conduct as well as speech** should proclaim the glory of God's mercy.

## The mystery of redemption and the Spirit's seal (verses 9–14)

God made known to believers the mystery of his sovereign purpose and the way he would redeem and save them.

These truths would have remained hidden forever if the Lord had not revealed them through **his written word, the preached gospel and the Spirit of truth**.

In his own person, **Jesus Christ brought together God and humanity**, who had been separated by sin, and satisfied the wrong that caused their separation.

By his Spirit he produces **faith and love**, uniting us to God and to one another.

Christ distributes his blessings according to his gracious will.

Through his divine teaching, some are brought to see the glory of truths that others continue to reject or even mock.

What a gracious promise that **the Holy Spirit will be given to those who ask for him**!

The Spirit's sanctifying and comforting work **seals believers** as God's children and heirs of heaven.

His present blessings are **the first fruits of everlasting happiness**.

We were created and redeemed for this purpose.

Everything God has done for us aims ultimately at **the praise of his glory**.

## Paul prays for greater understanding and hope (verses 15–23)

God has stored up every spiritual blessing for us in **his Son, Jesus Christ**.

He calls us to seek and receive those blessings through **prayer**.

Even the strongest Christians still need others to pray for them.

When we hear encouraging news about Christian friends, we should respond by **praying for their continued growth**.

Genuine believers still need much **heavenly wisdom**.

Even the best of us resist God's rule at times, though submitting to him is the only path to true rest.

How often do we trade our inward peace for a brief pleasure!

If we spent less time arguing and more time praying **with and for one another**, we would see more clearly **the hope of our calling** and the riches of God's glorious inheritance.

We should long to experience the mighty power of divine grace **beginning and continuing the work of faith within us**.

It is extraordinarily difficult for a soul to trust Christ completely and place every hope of everlasting life upon **his righteousness alone**.

Nothing less than **God's almighty power** can produce such faith.

Christ supplies the needs of those who trust him and gives his blessings abundantly.

Being united to Christ himself, we share the fullness of grace and glory found in him.

How foolish to seek righteousness **apart from him**!

If we understood what we have been called to receive, we would eagerly seek Christ.

As we recognize our weakness and the strength of our spiritual enemies, we see more clearly the greatness of the power that **converts the believer and guarantees the completion of salvation**.

This knowledge should move us, through love, to live for **our Redeemer's glory**.
