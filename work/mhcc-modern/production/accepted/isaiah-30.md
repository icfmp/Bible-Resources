# Isaiah 30 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| The Jews reproved for seeking aid from Egypt. | 1–7 |
| Judgements in consequence of their contempt of God's word. | 8–18 |
| God's mercies to his church. | 19–26 |
| The ruin of the Assyrian army, and of all God's enemies. | 27–33 |

## Egypt is a false refuge (verses 1–7)

When the Jews faced trouble from one neighboring country, they repeatedly sought protection from another instead of looking upward to God.

Adding sin to sin can only deepen the disaster.

We escape the final consequences of wrongdoing by making **Christ's righteousness our refuge** and seeking **the Holy Spirit's sanctifying work**.

Human beings are inclined to depend on their own judgment even against God's warning.

But that trust brings shame and distress.

Judah spent great effort and wealth seeking Egyptian support.

Those sacrifices yielded no lasting protection.

See the danger of leaving God to pursue hopes grounded only in human power!

**The Creator is the Rock of Ages; the creature is a broken reed.**

We should neither expect too much from humanity nor trust too little in God.

Our strength lies in remaining quiet before him, humbly depending upon his kindness and submitting peacefully to his will.

## Contempt for God's word leads to ruin (verses 8–18)

The Jews were the only nation then publicly professing the Lord's name.

Yet many among them remained rebellious.

They possessed light but preferred darkness.

The prophets repeatedly restrained them through correction, keeping them from feeling comfortable in sin.

That angered the people.

Nevertheless, faithful ministers should not stop trying to awaken sinners merely because they are disliked.

God is **the Holy One of Israel**, and the people would discover what that meant.

They did not want to hear his commands or his hatred of sin.

They wanted to be left undisturbed.

But by despising God's word they destroyed the very foundations of their safety.

The nation would be **shattered like a potter's vessel**.

We should turn from our sinful paths and return to duty. That is the way of salvation.

True strength comes through **quiet confidence in God** and a mind resting upon him.

Instead, the people imagined themselves wiser than the Lord.

The plan they devised to rescue themselves became the very cause of their downfall.

Only a scattered few would escape as a warning to others.

Those who refuse to repent and seek happiness in God's favor and service merely hasten their ruin.

The Lord waits **to be gracious to all who come to him through faith in Christ**.

Happy are those who also wait upon him.

## God's mercy and guidance for Zion (verses 19–26)

God's people will one day reach **the heavenly Zion**.

Then tears will be removed forever.

Even now, greater faithfulness in prayer would bring believers more comfort as well as more holiness.

**A famine of God's word is worse than a famine of bread**.

People face temptations both to the right and to the left.

Satan continually tries to draw us into side paths.

We are blessed when a faithful minister or friend, the warnings of conscience, or the Spirit's inward work corrects us when uncertain and restrains us before we go astray.

Through repentance, people come to hate the idols they once cherished.

The Lord accomplishes this daily in converting souls by his grace and teaching them to **fear and love him**.

Areas formerly lacking the means of grace will receive abundant instruction together with the Holy Spirit's influence.

The result should be **comfort and joy** for God's people.

The growing light represents **increasing knowledge of divine truth**.

This is the light brought by the gospel, announcing healing for the brokenhearted.

## Assyria and all impenitent enemies judged (verses 27–33)

God restrains people from doing wrong.

A word is enough to direct his willing servants, while **a bridle is needed to turn rebellious enemies toward their ruin**.

The destruction of Sennacherib's army is also a picture of the future, everlasting judgment of those who persist in sin.

**Tophet** was a valley near Jerusalem.

Henry describes fires burning there to destroy offensive or dangerous things, and the hideous practice in which idolatrous Israelites **made their children pass through the fire to Molech**.

He treats this as an image of the certainty and severity of final punishment.

No oppressor can finally escape divine wrath.

Let sinners therefore **flee to Christ and seek reconciliation with him**.

Then they may be safe and blessed when the Almighty brings an end to everyone who continues in rebellion.
