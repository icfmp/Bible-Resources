## Offerings for God's dwelling among Israel (verses 1–9)

God had chosen Israel as his own special people, above all other nations.

He himself would be their King.

He therefore commanded that a royal dwelling be prepared for him among them.

This dwelling was called the *sanctuary*, the *holy place*, or the *habitation*.

There God would show his presence among his people.

Because Israel lived in tents during its journey through the wilderness, God's royal dwelling was to be a **tabernacle**—a tent that could travel with them.

The people were to supply the materials Moses needed, each person giving freely and willingly.

The best use we can make of earthly possessions is to honor God through acts of devotion and charity.

We should ask not only, “What must we do?” but also, “What *can* we do for God?”

Whatever we give him, we should give cheerfully, not resentfully.

God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:7).

We should consider money spent in his service well spent.

Yet everything done in God's service must also be done **according to his directions**.

## The ark and the mercy seat (verses 10–22)

The ark was a chest covered in gold.

It was made to hold the two tablets of God's law.

Those tablets are called the *testimony*, because through them God testified to his will.

The law was a testimony **for** Israel, guiding the people in their duty.

If they disobeyed, it would also be a testimony **against** them.

The ark stood in the Most Holy Place.

The high priest sprinkled sacrificial blood and burned incense before it.

Above it appeared the visible glory that represented God's presence.

Henry sees the ark as a picture of Christ.

His human nature was without sin and did not undergo corruption.

It was personally united with his divine nature, and through his death he made atonement for our violations of God's law.

The golden cherubim faced each other, both looking down toward the ark.

They represented the angels' attendance upon the Redeemer, their readiness to carry out his will, their presence when the saints gather, and their desire to understand the mysteries of the gospel.

The ark was covered by a golden lid called the **mercy seat**.

God was said to dwell—or sit enthroned—between the cherubim above that mercy seat.

There, like a king seated on his throne, he gave his law and listened to those who came to him in prayer.

## The table of the bread of the Presence (verses 23–30)

A wooden table covered with gold stood in the outer room of the tabernacle.

The bread of the Presence was always to be placed on it.

The table, its furnishings and their use seem to picture the fellowship the Lord enjoys with his redeemed people through his appointed means of worship.

They point to the provisions and feasts of his household.

They also picture the food believers find for their souls whenever they hunger for it.

Finally, they point to the delight God takes in his people's lives and service, which are presented before him in Christ.

## The golden lampstand (verses 31–40)

The lampstand represented the light of God's word and Spirit, given through Jesus Christ to believers in this dark world.

That light guides their worship and obedience and brings them comfort.

Compared with the glory it will enjoy in heaven, the church is still in darkness, just as the tabernacle was.

Yet God's word shines like a lamp in a dark place (2 Peter 1:19).

How dark the world would be without it!

Verse 40 gives Moses an explicit warning.

Nothing was to be left to Moses' personal preference, to the workmen's creativity or to the people's wishes.

God's will had to be followed in every detail.

Christ likewise commanded his disciples to observe everything he had instructed them (Matthew 28:20).

Let us remember that believers themselves are temples of the Holy Spirit.

God's law is written on our hearts.

We should live in fellowship with him, enjoy the feast of his appointed ordinances, and be the light of the world—if we are truly following Christ.

May the Lord help us examine ourselves in the light of this description of faith and live accordingly.
