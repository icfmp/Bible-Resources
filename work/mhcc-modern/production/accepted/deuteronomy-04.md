# Deuteronomy 4 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Earnest exhortations to obedience, and dissuasives from idolatry. | 1–23 |
| Warnings against disobedience, and promises of mercy. | 24–40 |
| Cities of refuge appointed. | 41–49 |

## Moses urges obedience and warns against idolatry (verses 1–23)

God's power and love toward Israel gave Moses many reasons to warn the people solemnly against sin.

The warnings particularly concerned Israel's national covenant, but they can also be applied to people who live under the gospel.

What purpose does a law serve if not to be obeyed?

Our obedience cannot earn eternal salvation.

But it is the only evidence that we have received God's gift of eternal life through Jesus Christ.

We are surrounded by many temptations, and corrupt desires remain within our hearts.

We therefore need to guard our hearts carefully.

People cannot walk on the right path if they walk carelessly.

Moses warned particularly against idolatry.

Anyone who thinks clearly should see how weak the temptation is.

The supposed gods—the sun, moon and stars—are creatures God has generously given to all nations.

How absurd it is to worship what he created to serve us!

We must take care not to forget the Lord's covenant.

We need vigilance, thoughtfulness and watchfulness to prevent the forgetfulness that would lead us away from faith.

## Warnings, mercy and the greatness of God (verses 24–40)

Moses appealed to God's greatness, glory and goodness.

If we truly considered the One to whom we answer, we would take our duty seriously and not dare to sin against him.

Why turn away from a merciful God who will never abandon those who remain faithful to him?

Where else could we go?

May his love and mercy bind us to him.

Moses reminded Israel that God was its sovereign and that the people owed him obedience.

Keeping his commands was also wise for their own sake.

Fear of the Lord is wisdom.

People privileged to receive divine truth and law ought to live honorably and wisely, so that God is glorified.

God hears those who call on him in faith.

He is near enough to answer their prayers with peace.

His laws are just and righteous beyond the laws of other nations.

What Israel saw at Sinai gave a foretaste of the final day of judgment, when the Lord Jesus will be revealed in flaming fire.

The people must also remember what they **heard** there.

God reveals himself through the works of creation without human language, yet their testimony reaches everywhere (Psalm 19:1, 3).

At Sinai he spoke in words the people could hear, graciously adapting himself to their weakness.

Israel's origin was unlike that of every other nation.

God's free grace explains the distinction.

We are not loved because of some merit in ourselves, but for Christ's sake.

Moses also pointed out the benefits of obedience.

At the beginning of his speech (verse 1), he had said that obedience would enable them to live and enter the land.

At its end (verse 40), he promised that obedience would lead to good for them and their children.

Israel's national prosperity was bound to its faithfulness.

Apostasy would ruin the nation.

Moses foresaw that Israel would turn from the Lord to idols.

Only people who seek God with their **whole heart** will find him to their lasting comfort.

Afflictions often stir us to seek him, and through God's grace they bring many people back to sound judgment.

When such troubles come, return to the Lord: they show what follows from abandoning him.

Consider all these reasons together, and you will see that faith is entirely reasonable.

People abandon God's rule only after abandoning sound human understanding.

## The cities of refuge and the next address (verses 41–49)

This passage introduces another address Moses would give to Israel in the following chapters.

He placed God's law before them.

It was to be the guide for their actions and the way they lived.

It was also to be a mirror in which they recognized their own natural condition.

Looking into this perfect law of liberty, they were to continue in obedience.

The instructions first given after Israel came out of Egypt were now repeated.

Moses gave them while the people were camped opposite **Beth-peor**, a Moabite center of idolatry.

Israel's recent victories offered another strong reason to obey God.

We should similarly understand our own condition as sinners and the gracious covenant to which we are invited.

That covenant reveals greater things than Israel witnessed at Sinai and provides mercies beyond what the people enjoyed in the wilderness or Canaan.

The One who speaks to us is infinitely greater than Moses.

He bore our sins on the cross and appeals to us through his dying love.
