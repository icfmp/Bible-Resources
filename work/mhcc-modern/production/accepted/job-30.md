# Job 30 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job's honour is turned into contempt. | 1–14 |
| Job a burden to himself. | 15–31 |

## Job's honor becomes contempt (verses 1–14)

Job compared his present humiliation with the respect and authority he once enjoyed.

How little reason anyone has to become ambitious or proud of earthly honors when they can disappear so easily!

How foolish to place our ultimate confidence in them!

When wicked people despise, insult or hate us, we should not become overwhelmed.

Look instead to **Jesus Christ**, who endured the hostility of sinners against himself.

## Job struggles under inward and outward suffering (verses 15–31)

Job complained bitterly.

At this point, his most persistent sin was **cherishing harsh thoughts about God**.

When inward temptations are added to outward calamities, the soul can become like a ship tossed in a storm, full of confusion and distress.

Yet how dreadful is the position of people who truly have God as an enemy!

Compared with the eternal ruin awaiting unrepentant sinners, what are even the most severe outward or inward sufferings of this present life?

Job found some comfort, though little.

He expected death to end his trials.

God might permit him to die under suffering, but his soul would be **safe and happy in the world of spirits**.

Even if no human being pities us, God both **corrects and pities his children**, as a father does.

Let us direct our thoughts more toward eternity.

There the believer's mourning will end and be replaced by joyful praise for **redeeming love**.
