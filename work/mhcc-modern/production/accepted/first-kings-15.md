# 1 Kings 15 — Modern English

## The reign of Abijam (verses 1–8)

Abijam's heart was not wholly devoted to the Lord his God. He lacked sincerity. He began well but then fell away, walking in the sins of his father and imitating his bad example even though he had seen its harmful results.

Nevertheless, David's family remained a lamp in Jerusalem. It preserved true worship there when divine truth seemed to have been extinguished everywhere else.

The Lord continues to preserve his own cause, even when people who should have served it faithfully live and die in their sins.

The Son of David will remain a light to his church, establishing it in truth and righteousness until the end of time.

Henry distinguishes **two ways of fulfilling God's law**.

The first is **legal fulfillment**: carrying out everything the law requires through one's own obedience. Only Christ—and Adam before his fall—fulfilled it in this way.

The second is **gospel fulfillment**: believing in Christ, who fulfilled the law on our behalf, and sincerely endeavoring with our whole being to obey every command of God.

God accepts this obedience from everyone who is in Christ. In that sense Scripture says that David and other believers fulfilled the law.

## Asa brings reform to Judah (verses 9–24)

Asa did what was right in the Lord's eyes. What God judges to be right is right indeed.

His reign was a period of reform.

Reformation begins by removing evil, and Asa found a great deal of evil to remove.

When idolatry was present within his own royal household, he removed it there too.

**Reformation must begin at home.**

He respected and loved his mother, but loved God more.

People who possess authority are blessed when they have hearts willing to use it well.

Yet we must not merely stop doing evil; we must also learn to do good.

We should not only throw away our sinful idols but dedicate ourselves and all we possess to God's glory.

Asa was sincerely committed to serving God. His sins did not come from a presumptuous desire to rebel.

But his alliance with **Ben-hadad** arose from unbelief.

Even genuine believers find it hard to trust the Lord wholeheartedly in urgent danger.

Unbelief makes room for worldly schemes, and one sin can lead to another.

Christians have sometimes sought help from the Lord's enemies in their disputes with their own brothers and sisters.

In this way, people who once shone brightly may find their final years clouded over.

## Nadab and Baasha rule Israel (verses 25–34)

During Asa's single reign in Judah, Israel's government passed into **six or seven different hands**.

Notice the destruction of Jeroboam's family.

Not one word of God will fall to the ground.

His warnings of judgment are not spoken merely to frighten people without consequence.

Wicked people sometimes carry out God's righteous judgments against one another.

Yet amid dreadful sins and apparent confusion, the Lord continues fulfilling his own plan.

When that plan is finally complete, his glorious justice, wisdom, truth and mercy will be admired and worshiped forever.
