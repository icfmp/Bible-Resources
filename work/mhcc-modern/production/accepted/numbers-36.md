## The inheritance of Zelophehad's daughters (verses 1–4)

The leaders of Manasseh pointed out a difficulty.

If Zelophehad's daughters married into another tribe, the land granted to them might eventually leave their ancestral tribe.

The leaders wanted to preserve God's arrangement for the inheritances and prevent future disagreements and disputes.

People who possess property should take similar care.

They ought to settle their affairs and arrange their inheritances so as to prevent quarrels among those who come after them.

## Marriage within the tribe (verses 5–12)

God answered their concern.

Those who consult his word because they want to secure their heavenly inheritance will receive not only guidance about what to do, but also his gracious acceptance of their honest search.

God would not allow one tribe to become richer at another's expense.

Each tribe was to keep its own inheritance.

Zelophehad's daughters submitted to that command.

How could they fail to marry well when God himself was directing them?

Henry draws a Christian application.

Those who belong to God's people should be especially careful about the people with whom they enter life's closest and most lasting relationships.

A true believer in Jesus ought to marry someone who also belongs to the Lord.

Our intentions and affections should submit to God's revealed will, particularly in marriage.

God's word allows genuine affection and personal preference.

But it does not approve of uncontrolled passion or an idolatrous attachment that refuses to consider the future.

A person who defies proper authority simply to satisfy desire acts against wisdom, the good of society, happiness in marriage and—most seriously—the religion of Christ.

## The closing words of Numbers (verse 13)

These are the commands God gave Israel on the plains of Moab.

Most concerned the people's settlement in Canaan, which they were now about to enter.

Whenever God's providence brings us into a new situation, we should ask him to show us the duties it carries and to enable us to perform them.

We should do **today's work today**, and fulfill the responsibilities of each place while we are there.
