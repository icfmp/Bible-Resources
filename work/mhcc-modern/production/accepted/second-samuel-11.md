# 2 Samuel 11 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| David's adultery. | 1–5 |
| He tries to conceal his crime. | 6–13 |
| Uriah murdered. | 14–27 |

## David commits adultery (verses 1–5)

Consider what led David into this serious sin.

**First, he neglected his responsibilities.** He remained in Jerusalem when his duty called him elsewhere.

When we leave the path of our duty, we place ourselves in the path of temptation.

**Second, he chose an easy, idle life.** Laziness gives the tempter an advantage.

**Third, he allowed his eyes to wander.** He had not made a covenant with his eyes as Job had—or had forgotten that promise.

Consider also the steps by which one sin led to another.

Sin is a downhill path.

Once people begin doing wrong, they often find it very difficult to stop.

Consider how much worse David's sin became because of the responsibilities and privileges he possessed.

How could he rebuke or punish others for conduct that he himself knowingly practiced?

## David tries to conceal his wrongdoing (verses 6–13)

Giving way to sin hardens the heart and provokes the Holy Spirit to withdraw.

Taking away someone's ability to reason rightly is worse than taking away that person's money.

And enticing someone to sin is worse than bringing that person into any ordinary worldly trouble.

## David arranges Uriah's death (verses 14–27)

Adultery often leads to murder.

One wicked act may be used to conceal another.

We should dread even the beginning of sin because no one knows where it may end.

Can someone who truly believes act this way?

Can a person committing such crimes really be God's child?

Henry teaches that even if God's saving grace is not finally lost in such a terrible fall, **the assurance and comfort of grace must be suspended**.

David's spiritual vigor and joy in religion were, for the time, destroyed.

No person living in such deliberate wrongdoing can reasonably claim to possess evidence that he or she is a genuine believer.

The greater a person's confidence while sunk in sin, the more that confidence is presumption and hypocrisy.

Someone resembling David **only in his transgressions** must not use David's story to reassure himself that he is merely a temporarily fallen believer.

He must first follow David in **humiliation, repentance and the other exceptional graces of his character**.

Opponents of Christianity should not say, “These are the fruits of faith.”

No.

David's crimes were the fruit of **corrupt human nature**.

We should all watch against the earliest movements of self-indulgence and keep far away from evil.

Yet with the Lord there is mercy and abundant redemption.

He will not reject a humble, repentant believer or allow Satan to snatch his sheep from his hand.

God restores his people in a way that makes his hatred of their sins clear.

In this way, no one who takes his word seriously will be encouraged to abuse the comfort of divine forgiveness.
