# Job 26 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| Job reproves Bildad. | 1–4 |
| Job acknowledges the power of God. | 5–14 |

## Job rebukes Bildad (verses 1–4)

Job mocked Bildad's answer.

His words combined irritability with too high an opinion of himself.

Bildad should have offered Job **the Almighty's consolations** instead of only warnings of terror.

Jesus Christ knows how to speak the right word to a weary person (**Isaiah 50:4**).

His ministers should not needlessly distress someone whom God has not intended to make sorrowful.

We are often disappointed by people from whom we expected help and consolation.

But **the Holy Spirit, the Comforter**, never makes a mistake or fails to accomplish his purpose.

## Job describes divine power (verses 5–14)

Job gave striking examples of God's wisdom and power in **creating and preserving the world**.

Look around at earth and water and you see divine power.

Think of the unseen depths beneath us and you can understand something of God's authority over them.

Look up at heaven and you see further displays of his might.

Through **his eternal Spirit**, who moved over the waters, and through the breath of his word, God not only made the heavens but adorned them (**Psalm 33:6**).

Yet the work of **redemption** surpasses every other marvelous work of God.

Through it we can draw near to him, taste his grace, learn to love him and delight in his commands.

The central disagreement between Job and his friends was their belief that his afflictions proved he had committed exceptionally grave crimes.

They failed to give adequate consideration to **the guilt of original sin** and God's gracious purpose in **purifying his people**.

Job himself also spoke about things beyond his understanding.

Yet his view was clearer than theirs.

He does not appear to have claimed his personal righteousness as the ground of his hope before God.

However, by continually describing his sufferings as undeserved and excessively severe, he practically contradicted what he had acknowledged about human sin.

Those complaints showed why the Lord was further humbling him.
