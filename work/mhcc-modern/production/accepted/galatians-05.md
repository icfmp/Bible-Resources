# Galatians 5 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| An earnest exhortation to stand fast in the liberty of the gospel. | 1–12 |
| To take heed of indulging a sinful temper. | 13–15 |
| And to walk in the Spirit, and not to fulfil the lusts of the flesh: the works of both are described. | 16–26 |

## Stand firm in the freedom Christ gives (verses 1–6)

Christ will not be the Savior of anyone who refuses to trust and receive him as **the only Savior**.

We must heed Paul's warning to remain firm in **the doctrine and freedom of the gospel**.

The Holy Spirit teaches true believers to await everlasting life and righteousness as **God's gift through faith in Christ**, not as payment for their own good works.

A Jewish Christian might continue observing certain traditional ceremonies or make use of the freedom not to observe them.

A Gentile believer might likewise disregard those customs or participate in them.

The decisive issue was **not trusting those practices as the basis of acceptance with God**.

No outward privilege or religious label can make a person acceptable without **genuine faith in Jesus Christ**.

True faith is active: **it works through love** for God and for fellow believers.

May we be among those who, through the Spirit, await **the hope of righteousness by faith**.

The danger does not lie in the indifferent character of some outward ceremonies.

It lies in attaching saving value to them **in place of faith in Christ**.

Without faith expressing itself in love, everything else is worthless by comparison.

## Keep running the Christian race without accepting false teaching (verses 7–12)

Christian life is like **a race**.

We must run faithfully and continue until the prize is obtained.

Simply claiming the name Christian is not enough.

Our manner of life should agree with that profession.

Many begin well but become distracted, slow down or leave the path.

Those who notice their faith weakening should ask seriously: **What is hindering me?**

The false teaching troubling the Galatians added **works of the law to faith in Christ as necessary for justification**.

Paul showed that such persuasion did not come from God but from Satan.

Churches must beware of encouraging those who follow destructive error and especially those who **spread it**.

When correcting wrongdoing, we should distinguish **teachers who mislead from people who have been misled**.

Some Jewish opponents resented the gospel's claim that Christ alone saves sinners.

If Paul had agreed that Mosaic observances must be added to faith for salvation, he and other believers might have avoided much persecution.

But even **the first traces of this corrupting leaven** had to be resisted.

People who persist in disturbing Christ's church with such teaching will face God's judgment.

## Gospel freedom is for loving service, not sin (verses 13–15)

The gospel teaches **godliness** (**1 Timothy 6:3**).

It never provides permission to indulge sin.

On the contrary, Christ's freedom gives us the strongest reasons to resist and put sin to death.

The whole moral law is summarized in the command **to love your neighbor as yourself**.

Believers should help and encourage one another.

If they instead attack one another because of different opinions, the resulting conflict opens the way for spiritual harm.

The God of love may withdraw the felt comfort of his grace; the Spirit of love is resisted, and the evil spirit who seeks destruction gains an advantage.

How much better if Christians spent less energy **biting and devouring one another** and more energy opposing sin **within themselves and their communities**!

## Walk by the Spirit and cultivate his fruit (verses 16–26)

When believers seek to live under the **guidance and power of the Holy Spirit**, sinful desires may still arise and struggle against them.

But those desires need not **rule over them**.

The Christian life involves a real conflict between remaining corruption and renewing grace.

Genuine believers long for the Spirit's grace to gain a complete and speedy victory.

Those who yield themselves to the Holy Spirit are no longer under the law **as a covenant requiring merit** or exposed to its curse.

Their hatred of sin and desire for holiness show the reality of the salvation they have received.

The **works of the flesh** are numerous and plainly sinful.

People who persist in them will not inherit heaven.

How terrible that many who profess Christianity nevertheless continue those practices while claiming to expect salvation!

The **fruit of the Spirit** describes the qualities produced by a renewed heart.

As Paul's list of sinful practices particularly includes actions that injure people and relationships, his list of spiritual fruits especially emphasizes attitudes and actions that make Christians **kind and beneficial toward one another**.

The Spirit's fruit both benefits the believer and makes Christian fellowship more joyful.

These traits give evidence of **the Spirit's guidance**.

The contrast teaches us which practices we must **resist** and which virtues we must **cultivate**.

Genuine Christians take that duty seriously.

Sin must no longer reign in their mortal bodies (**Romans 6:12**).

They seek to **put it to death**, not merely excuse it.

Christ will not acknowledge as his faithful people those who voluntarily surrender themselves as slaves to sin.

And it is not enough merely to stop doing wrong: **we must learn to do good**.

Our conduct follows the power that governs our hearts (**Romans 8:5**).

Let us labor to put the body's sinful deeds to death and to live in spiritual newness.

Do not seek vain praise, provoke one another or become envious.

Instead, cultivate more abundantly the good fruits which **through Jesus Christ bring praise and glory to God**.
