# Psalm 38 — Modern English

## Original chapter outline

| Original outline | Verses |
| --- | --- |
| God's displeasure at sin. | 1–11 |

## The burden of sin (verses 1–11)

Nothing troubles a godly heart as much as the feeling that God is angry.

We remain at peace by keeping ourselves in his love.

Yet the guilt of sin is too heavy for any person to bear. Without God's pardoning mercy, it leads to despair and destruction.

Henry sees human sickness and pain as effects of **sin's entrance into creation**: without sin there would be no suffering in the body. The whole creation groans beneath the burden of guilt and corruption.

That burden falls upon sinners either as sorrow drawing them to seek mercy or as final destruction if they remain unrepentant.

When we understand our condition, we will value and seek **the Good Physician**.

Many allow their spiritual wounds to worsen because they delay going to their merciful Friend.

During bodily illness, we should remember how our bodies have dishonored God and ask him to teach us repentance.

Even groanings too deep for words are known to the One who searches hearts and knows the Spirit's mind.

David's suffering also pictured **Christ in his agony and upon his cross**, suffering amid abandonment.

## Suffering, patience and prayer (verses 12–22)

Wicked people may hate goodness even while benefiting from it.

In his descriptions of hostile enemies, David again points toward Christ.

Other people can do us lasting harm only if they persuade us to abandon God and neglect duty.

The true believer's distress can become useful through learning to wait upon God rather than looking only to the world or personal power for relief.

Often our minds will be quieter when we pay less attention to the insults and injuries others inflict.

David suffered correction for **his own transgressions**, but **Christ suffered for our sins alone**.

Why should sinners respond with anger or impatience when God mercifully corrects them?

David was aware of sinful impulses still working within him.

When good people continually look only at their suffering, they may lose their balance; when they **keep God before them**, they remain standing.

True repentance makes us patient during correction.

For a suffering believer, nothing is more painful than the fear of being abandoned by God.

Therefore the heartfelt prayer is: **“Do not be far from me.”**

The Lord will come to help those who trust him as their salvation.
