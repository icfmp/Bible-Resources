## The queen of Sheba (verses 1–12)

This story has already been considered in **1 Kings 10**.

Yet our Savior used the queen of Sheba as an example of someone who earnestly sought him (**Matthew 12:42**).

We should not pass over the lesson.

People who know the value of genuine wisdom will begrudge no expense or difficulty involved in obtaining it.

The queen traveled far and spent much to hear Solomon's wisdom.

She learned from him to serve God and fulfill her duty, and believed that knowledge more than repaid her effort.

Heavenly wisdom is **the pearl of great price**.

Even if we surrender everything to receive it, we make a good bargain.

## Solomon's wealth and death (verses 13–31)

The imported objects listed here suggest that prosperity had led Solomon and his people to value things that were rare and interesting but **not useful in themselves**.

True wisdom and happiness always belong together.

There is no similar necessary connection between wealth and enjoyment of this life.

Let us draw near to the Savior to find rest for our souls.

Solomon ruled with unprecedented wealth, power, ease and abundance.

He reigned in profound peace for **forty years**, unlike the great kings chiefly remembered for warfare.

God fulfilled his promise to give him riches and honor beyond those of other kings.

His splendor was a faint image of the **spiritual glory of Messiah's kingdom**, whose throne stands above every throne.

Yet Solomon died and left all his wealth and power to a successor whom he knew would act foolishly (**Ecclesiastes 2:18–19**).

That was vanity and vexation of spirit.

Neither power, riches nor wisdom can prevent death or prepare the soul for it.

But thanks be to God: through Jesus Christ our Lord, he gives the true believer victory even over that dreaded enemy.
