# Jeremiah — Book introduction (Modern English candidate)

**Jeremiah** was a priest from **Anathoth**, a town in the territory of Benjamin.

God called him to be a prophet when he was very young, about **seventy years after Isaiah's death** according to Henry's chronology.

Jeremiah continued serving faithfully for about **forty years**, until Judah's sins reached their full measure and national destruction followed.

The prophecies preserved in this book are **not arranged in the order in which Jeremiah delivered them**.

Henry notes that **Benjamin Blayney** attempted to arrange them more regularly, in this order:

**Jeremiah 1–20; 22; 23; 25; 26; 35; 36; 45; 24; 29; 30; 31; 27; 28; 21; 34; 37; 32; 33; 38; 39:15–18, followed by 39:1–14; 40–44; 46–52.**

This is a report of **Blayney's proposed reading sequence**, **not** a recommendation to rearrange the canonical biblical text or its SWORD versification.

Jeremiah's prophecies chiefly concern **the idolatry and other sins of Judah**, the threatened judgments, the future **restoration and deliverance**, and **promises concerning the Messiah**.

They are especially marked by **plain and faithful rebukes**, affectionate appeals and grave warnings.
