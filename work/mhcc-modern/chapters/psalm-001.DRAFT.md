## The holiness and happiness of a godly person (verses 1–3)

To meditate on God's word is to speak to ourselves about the great truths it contains. It requires close attention and steady thought.

We must continually turn to Scripture as the rule for our actions and the source of our comfort.

Its words should be in our minds by day and by night.

There is no wrong time to meditate on the word of God.

## The character and end of the ungodly (verses 4–6)

The ungodly are the opposite of the righteous, both in their character and in their condition.

They listen to wicked counsel, walk in the way of sinners, and take their place among scoffers.

They find no delight in God's law. Their only fruit is evil.

The righteous are like useful trees that bear fruit. The ungodly are like chaff blown away by the wind—dust that the owner of the threshing floor wants removed because it has no use.

However highly they value themselves, they are of no worth in God's sight.

Every wind of temptation can drive them this way or that.

For a time, chaff may lie among the wheat. But the One whose winnowing fan is in his hand is coming, and he will thoroughly clear his threshing floor.

Those who make themselves like chaff through sin and foolishness will be shown to be chaff before the whirlwind and fire of God's wrath.

The fate of the ungodly is settled. Yet whenever a sinner recognizes his guilt and misery, he may come through Christ, the living Way, into the company of the righteous.

In Christ he becomes a new creation.

His desires and pleasures change. So do his hopes and fears, his sorrows, his companions, and the work he does.

His thoughts, words and actions are changed. He enters a new condition of life and bears a new character.

Behold what divine grace does: it makes all things new, transforming the soul into the image of the Redeemer.

How different are the character and the end of the ungodly!
