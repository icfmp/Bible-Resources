## Ruth gleans in Boaz's field (verses 1–3)

Notice Ruth's humility. Providence had made her poor, yet she willingly accepted her circumstances.

Some proud people would rather starve than take humble work. Ruth was different: she herself proposed going out to glean.

She asked respectfully for permission. We should not demand kindness as though it were a debt owed to us. Even in small matters, we should ask for it and receive it as a favor.

Ruth also showed diligence. She did not want to eat the bread of idleness. Young people especially can learn from her example. Diligence promises well for this life and the life to come.

We should not shrink from honest work. No honest labor is beneath us. Sin is beneath us, but we must never think ourselves above work to which Providence calls us.

Ruth cared for her mother and trusted Providence. God wisely orders events that seem small to us. Even what appears completely uncertain is directed toward his glory and the good of his people.

## Boaz shows kindness to Ruth (verses 4–16)

The godly and kindly greetings exchanged by Boaz and his workers show that there were faithful people in Israel.

How different this was from the coarse and corrupt talk too often heard among workers! A stranger hearing such speech would form a very different impression of a country than Ruth would have formed of Israel through Boaz and his men.

True religion teaches people to behave rightly in every station of life. It makes masters kind and servants faithful. It brings harmony into households and mutual kindness between people of different ranks.

We see this in Boaz and his workers. When he arrived, he prayed for them. They returned his blessing instead of cursing him as soon as he was out of hearing, as resentful servants sometimes do.

Things are likely to go well when masters and servants show one another such goodwill. Their kindness took the form of praying for each other.

When Boaz noticed Ruth, he asked about her and ordered that she be treated well.

Those in authority must do more than refrain from harming others themselves. They must also prevent their servants and those under their care from doing wrong.

Ruth humbly acknowledged that she was unworthy of such favor. She had been born and raised among the heathen.

It is right for us all to think humbly of ourselves and to esteem others more highly than ourselves.

And in Boaz's kindness to Ruth, let us see the kindness of the Lord Jesus Christ toward poor sinners.

## Ruth returns to Naomi (verses 17–23)

Even gleaning brings a return for honest labor. That is an encouragement to diligence.

Ruth was glad of what she had earned by her own work, and she took care not to lose it. We too should guard what we have gained for the good of our souls, as we are reminded in 2 John 1:8.

Parents should take an interest in what their children have done, as Naomi did. But they should not question them in ways that frighten or discourage them, make them dislike home, or tempt them to lie.

They should praise what is good and, where correction is needed, offer gentle reproof and caution.

Each evening we would do well to ask ourselves: Where have I gleaned today? How have I grown in knowledge and grace? What have I done that will prove truly worthwhile?

When the Lord deals generously with us, let us not wander into another field in search of happiness and satisfaction in created things. We lose divine favors when we treat them lightly.

Ruth faithfully followed her mother's directions. When the harvest ended, she stayed at home and kept her aged mother company.

Dinah went out to see the daughters of the land, and her vanity ended in disgrace (Genesis 34). Ruth, by contrast, stayed with her mother and went out only to provide for her.

Her humility and diligence ended in advancement.
