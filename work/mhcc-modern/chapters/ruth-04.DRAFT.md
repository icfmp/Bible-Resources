## The nearer relative declines to redeem (verses 1–8)

The matter depended on the inheritance laws given through Moses. No doubt it was settled in the proper legal way.

When the nearer relative learned the conditions of the arrangement, he refused.

Many respond to the great redemption in much the same way. They hesitate to embrace religion. They have heard good things about it and have nothing against it. They are even willing to speak well of it.

But they are also willing to let it go. They will not commit themselves, for fear of damaging their inheritance in this world.

So the right of redemption passed to Boaz.

Anyone who wishes to be a true Israelite, without deceit, must deal fairly and openly in every contract and business transaction.

Honesty will prove the best policy.

## Boaz marries Ruth (verses 9–12)

People readily seize opportunities to increase their property. Yet few understand the value of godliness.

These are the people the world calls wise, but the Lord calls foolish.

They neglect the interests of their souls and reject salvation through Christ because they are afraid of harming their earthly inheritance.

God, however, honored Boaz by bringing him into the family line of the Messiah.

The nearer relative feared diminishing his standing and damaging his inheritance. Yet his name, family and inheritance are now forgotten.

## The birth of Obed and the line of David (verses 13–22)

Ruth gave birth to a son through whose descendants thousands upon thousands would be born to God.

As an ancestor of Christ, she also became an instrument in the happiness of all who would be saved through him—Gentiles like us as well as those descended from the Jews.

Her life bore witness to the Gentile world that God had not completely forsaken them. In due time they too would become one with his chosen people and share in his salvation.

Prayer to God accompanied Ruth's marriage, and praise to God accompanied the child's birth.

How sad that godly language is not used more often among Christians—or that, when it is used, it can become an empty formality!

Here we learn how David descended from Ruth.

In time, Bethlehem in Judah would witness wonders greater still than those recorded in this book.

There, the seemingly outcast child of another poor and desolate woman of the same family line would appear. Though he seemed so lowly, he would direct even the counsels of the Roman ruler of the world and draw princes and wise men from the East to his feet, bearing gold, frankincense and myrrh.

His name will endure forever, and all nations will call him blessed.

In that promised Seed, all the nations of the earth will be blessed.
