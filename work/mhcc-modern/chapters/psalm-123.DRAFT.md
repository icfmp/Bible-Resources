## Looking to God for mercy under contempt

Jesus has taught us to look to God in prayer as our Father in heaven.

Whenever godly people pray, they lift up their souls to him. This is especially important when they are in trouble.

We ask God for mercy and hope that he will show it. We keep waiting for him until it comes.

A servant watches the master's directing hand, expecting instructions about the work to be done.

The servant also looks to that hand for provision. Servants depend on their master or mistress for food at the proper time.

So we look to God for our daily bread and for sufficient grace. We receive these gifts with gratitude.

Where else could we turn for help but to our Master?

We also look to his protecting hand.

If a servant is mistreated while doing the master's work, who should put matters right but the master himself?

Then there is God's correcting hand. Where else should sinners turn but to the One who has struck them? They humble themselves beneath his mighty hand.

Finally, we look to his rewarding hand.

Hypocrites seek their reward from the world. True Christians look to God as both their Master and the One who rewards them.

God's people often find little mercy from others. Their comfort is that the Lord himself is merciful.

They have endured mockery and contempt in the past. They endure it now and may expect it again in this world.

Such treatment is painful. But God's servants should not complain that they are treated as his beloved Son was treated.

When trials leave us close to despair, let us look to Jesus. Through faith and prayer, let us entrust ourselves to God's mercy.
