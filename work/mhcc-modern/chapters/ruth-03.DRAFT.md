## Naomi gives Ruth instructions (verses 1–5)

Marriage ought to provide as much rest as anything on earth can offer. It should establish lasting affection and a bond for life.

That is why marriage should be entered into with great seriousness, earnest prayer for guidance and God's blessing, and careful regard for his commands.

Parents should advise their children wisely in this important matter, with particular concern for the good of their souls.

Let us always remember: what is best for our souls is best for us.

Naomi's advice may seem strange to us, but it followed the laws and customs of Israel. If it had appeared to involve wrongdoing, Naomi would not have recommended it.

By law and custom, Ruth—who had now embraced the true religion—had a rightful claim upon Boaz. Widows customarily asserted such a claim (Deuteronomy 25:5–10).

But this event was not recorded for people in other times to imitate, nor should it be judged simply by modern customs.

And if there had been anything wrong in the proposal, Ruth had too much virtue and good sense to accept it.

## Boaz acknowledges the duty of a kinsman (verses 6–13)

What would be improper in one age or country is not necessarily improper in another.

As a judge of Israel, Boaz could tell Ruth what she ought to do. He could also explain whether he had the right to redeem, and what steps and customary rites were needed for her to marry either him or another man.

Boaz deserves the highest praise for his conduct.

He did not try to take advantage of Ruth. He did not look down on her as a poor stranger without support, or suspect her of bad intentions.

He spoke honorably of her as a virtuous woman and made her a promise. When morning came, he sent her away with a gift for her mother-in-law.

Yet he made his promise conditional. A nearer relative existed, and the right of redemption belonged to that man first.

## Ruth returns to Naomi (verses 14–18)

Ruth had done everything it was right for her to do. Now she needed to wait patiently for the outcome.

Boaz had taken responsibility for the matter, and she could trust him to manage it well.

How much more reason do true believers have to entrust their cares to God! He has promised to care for them.

Our strength is to sit still (Isaiah 30:7).

This account can encourage us to place ourselves in faith at Christ's feet. He is our near Kinsman, for he has taken our nature upon himself. He has the right to redeem us.

Let us ask him to guide us: “Lord, what will you have me do?” (Acts 9:6).

He will never blame us for coming to him at the wrong time.

And let us earnestly seek that same rest for our children and friends, so that it may go well with them too.
