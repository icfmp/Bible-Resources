## Paul's gratitude for Philemon's faith and love (verses 1–7)

Faith in Christ and love for him should unite believers more closely than any outward relationship unites people in the world.

Paul remembered his friends specifically in his private prayers.

We too should remember Christian friends often, according to their needs. Let us carry them in our thoughts and hearts before God.

Differences of opinion or practice in matters that are not essential must not diminish our affection for those who love the truth.

Paul wanted to know whether his friends' graces were genuine, growing and bearing fruit: whether they trusted Christ and loved both him and all the saints.

Philemon's kindness brought joy and comfort to Paul and to others.

They therefore longed to see him continue in goodness and bear even more fruit, to God's honor.

## Love rather than authority; the changed Onesimus (verses 8–14)

There is nothing beneath our dignity in stooping to make a request—even when we have the right to command.

Paul appealed out of love, rather than exercising his authority, on behalf of Onesimus, who had been converted through his ministry.

The name *Onesimus* means “profitable” or “useful.” Paul acknowledged that Onesimus had once been unprofitable to Philemon, but he quickly pointed to the change that had made him useful.

Ungodly people are unprofitable because they fail to fulfill the great purpose for which they exist.

What a wonderful difference conversion makes! Those who once did evil can now do good; those once useless can become useful.

Faithful servants are a treasure to a household. They take their time and responsibilities seriously, acting faithfully in everything entrusted to them.

But the prospect of doing useful work elsewhere must never become an excuse to neglect our duties or fail in obedience to those over us.

A great evidence of true repentance is returning to the duties we once neglected.

Before his conversion, Onesimus had run away and harmed his master. Having recognized his sin and repented, he was now willing and eager to return to his responsibilities.

We rarely know what purposes God may have in allowing people to move elsewhere or undertake new ventures, even when their motives are wrong.

We can each recall plans formed in ungodliness which, had God not overruled them, could have brought us to ruin.

## Receive a repentant brother (verses 15–22)

When speaking about sin against God, we must never minimize its evil. But we should deal with a repentant sinner as God does, covering the sin rather than continually exposing it.

People whose character has been transformed often become a blessing to everyone around them.

Christian faith does not cancel our duties to other people. It teaches us how to carry them out rightly.

Those who truly repent are willing to acknowledge their faults, especially when they have harmed another person. No doubt Onesimus had done so when he came to repentance under Paul's ministry.

The fellowship of believers does not abolish distinctions of property.

Here one person freely takes responsibility for a debt incurred by another, so that the wrongdoer may be spared the punishment due to his wrongdoing.

This points to Christ, who willingly bore the punishment of our sins so that we might receive the reward belonging to his righteousness.

Philemon was Paul's son in the faith, yet Paul appealed to him as a brother.

Onesimus was a poor enslaved man, yet Paul pleaded for him as earnestly as if he were asking for something of great personal importance.

Christians should seek to bring joy to one another. The world brings them trouble; among fellow believers they should find comfort and gladness.

When any of our blessings are taken away, our trust and hope must remain in God.

We should diligently use the means available to us and, even when no other help is at hand, continue steadfastly in prayer.

Prayer is powerful, but it does not earn the blessings it receives.

And even if Christians never meet again on earth, the grace of the Lord Jesus will remain with their spirits. Soon they will meet before his throne and forever admire the riches of redeeming love.

Onesimus's example should encourage even the worst sinners to return to God. But it is shameful to use his example as an excuse for continuing in evil.

Many die in their sins, while others become still more hardened.

Do not resist the conviction you feel today, lest it never return.

## Final greetings and the blessing of grace (verses 23–25)

Believers have often enjoyed God's presence most deeply while suffering together for him.

Grace is the best blessing we can desire for ourselves or anyone else. Paul begins and ends his letter with it.

All grace comes from Christ. He purchased it and gives it.

What more do we need for happiness than the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ with our spirit?

Let us choose now what we would choose at the moment of death.

When that moment comes, people are ready to turn away from the world and value even the smallest portion of grace and faith more than a kingdom.
