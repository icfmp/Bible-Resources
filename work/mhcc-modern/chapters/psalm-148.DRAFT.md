# Psalm 148 — Modern English working draft

## The heavenly world is called to praise (verses 1–6)

Living in this dark and sinful world, we know little about the heavenly world of light.

But we do know that above us there is a world of blessed angels, continually praising God.

The psalmist longs for God to receive the highest praise. As we join in it, we show our fellowship with the spirits above who are still praising him.

The heavens and everything they contain declare God's glory.

They call us to glorify the Creator and Redeemer of the universe alongside them—not only by what we say, but by what we do.

## The earthly world and God's people are called to praise (verses 7–14)

Even in this dark and troubled world, God receives praise.

The forces of nature, however strong or violent, do only what God appoints for them.

People who rebel against his word can be more violent than stormy winds. Yet even they ultimately fulfill what he has determined.

Look across the earth: its mountains and hills, some barren and others fruitful. Everywhere we can find reasons to praise God.

Surely creatures endowed with reason should devote themselves to that praise.

Let people of every rank join in, great and small alike.

Let us show that we belong to his holy people by praising his name continually.

For he is not only our Creator but also our Redeemer. He has made us a people near to him.

“The Horn of his people” may point to Christ, whom God has exalted as Prince and Savior.

Christ is the defense and the praise of all his saints, and he will remain so forever.

Redemption reveals God's indescribable glory. It is the source of all our hope and joy.

May the Lord forgive us, teach our hearts to love him more, and lead us to praise him better.
