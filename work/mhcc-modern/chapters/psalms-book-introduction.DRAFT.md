# Psalms — Book introduction (Modern English working draft)

David wrote most of the psalms. Some were clearly composed by others, while the authors of some are uncertain.

Yet all were written under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. No part of the Old Testament is quoted or referred to more often in the New.

Every psalm either points directly to Christ—his person, character and offices—or helps believers turn their thoughts toward him.

The psalms give words to the believer's heart.

They speak for those mourning over sin, longing for God or rejoicing in him. They speak for those weighed down by suffering, struggling against temptation, or celebrating the hope or experience of deliverance.

They also express admiration for God's perfections, gratitude for his mercies, meditation on his truths and delight in serving him.

In all these ways, they provide a God-given standard of spiritual experience by which we can examine ourselves.

Their value is immense, and we will find them increasingly useful as true religion grows stronger in our hearts.

Through the words of the psalmists, the Holy Spirit helps us to pray.

If we become familiar with the psalms, they can help us bring whatever we need to the throne of grace, whether in confession, petition or thanksgiving.

Whatever holy feeling stirs within us—desire or hope, sorrow or joy—we can find words here to express it: sound words that cannot rightly be condemned.

Through the centuries, the church has used the language of this inspired book to offer its prayers and praises before God's throne of grace.
