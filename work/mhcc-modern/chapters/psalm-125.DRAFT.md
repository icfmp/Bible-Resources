## God keeps those who trust in him (verses 1–3)

A mind resting on God will be truly steady.

God's people are like Mount Zion: firm and unshakable.

The mountain is held up by God's providence. But this holy mountain is upheld by something even greater—his promise.

Believers cannot be driven away from their trust in God. They remain in the grace that guarantees their everlasting place in glory.

When they entrust themselves to him, he keeps them safe from their enemies.

Mountains may crumble, and rocks may be moved. But God's covenant with his people cannot be broken, nor can his care for them cease.

Their trials will not last longer than the strength God gives them to bear them.

The rod of wicked people may strike the righteous. It may touch their bodies, property, freedoms, families, reputation or anything else in their outward lives.

But it cannot reach their souls.

And even when that rod falls upon them, it will not remain there forever.

The Lord will make all these things work together for their good.

The wicked can become an instrument of correction, but not a sword that destroys God's people.

Even this rod of correction will not remain upon them, lest they begin to distrust God's promise and imagine that he has abandoned them.

## Pray for the upright; the wicked will perish (verses 4–5)

God's promises should stir us to pray.

The path of holiness is straight. There are no twists or evasions in it.

The way of sinners is crooked. They change their intentions, turn aside and seek to deceive others.

Disappointment and misery will meet them in the end.

Those who remain faithful to God's ways may suffer on the road, but their end will be peace.

Their Savior pleads for them, securing God's sustaining power and preserving grace.

Lord, count us among them both now and forever.
