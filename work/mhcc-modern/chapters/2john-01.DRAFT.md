## John greets the elect lady and her children (verses 1–3)

True religion turns polite greetings into genuine expressions of respect and love.

An older disciple deserves honor. How much more an aged apostle who has led others in following Christ!

The letter is addressed to a noble Christian woman and her children. It is good when the gospel reaches people of high standing too, for God calls some of them.

Families need encouragement and guidance in the love and responsibilities of home.

Those who love truth and godliness in themselves ought to love them in others. Christians loved this woman not for her social standing, but for her holiness.

Where true religion has taken root, it will remain forever.

The apostle asks for grace from the divine Persons of the Godhead. Grace is God's favor and goodwill, the source of every good thing.

Every spiritual blessing given to sinful people is an act of grace.

He also asks for mercy: free pardon and forgiveness. Even those who already enjoy abundant grace still need forgiveness continually.

And he asks for peace: a quiet spirit, a clear conscience, and confidence in reconciliation with God, together with such outward well-being as truly serves our good.

These blessings are sought in truth and love.

## John rejoices in their faith and love (verses 4–6)

It is good to learn true religion from childhood. Children may also be loved for their parents' sake.

The apostle was delighted to see children following the faithful example of their parents, and likely in their turn to support the gospel.

May God bless such families more and more, and raise up many others to follow their example!

What a contrast with those who pass unbelief, ungodliness and vice on to their children.

Our walk is true, and our conduct right, when it agrees with God's word.

The command that Christians love one another can be called new because the Lord Jesus declared it. Yet in its substance it is an old command.

To obey God's commands is also to show love for our own souls.

Perhaps the apostle foresaw that Christian love would decline, along with other departures from the faith. That may explain why he urged this duty so often and so earnestly.

## Beware of deceivers (verses 7–11)

The deceiver is identified by his error concerning the person or office of the Lord Jesus.

Such a person is a deceiver and an antichrist. He misleads souls and attacks the glory and kingdom of Christ.

We should not be surprised to meet opponents of Christ's name and dignity today. They existed even in the apostles' time.

The more deceivers and false teachings abound, the more watchful Christ's disciples must become.

How sad when great progress made in Christ's school is lost!

To receive the full reward, we must remain faithful to Christ and steadfast in our religion to the end.

Holding firmly to Christian truth joins us to Christ and therefore also to the Father, for they are one.

We should be equally wary of those who do not remain in Christ's doctrine and those who break his commands.

Those who neither profess nor teach the truth about Christ as the Son of God, and salvation through him from guilt and sin, must not be welcomed or encouraged as teachers of the Christian faith.

Yet this warning is no excuse for harshness toward Christians who disagree with us on lesser matters.

We must show them kindness and a gracious spirit, while holding firmly to the essential truths about Christ's person, his atonement, and the holy salvation he gives.

## John hopes to meet them (verses 12–13)

The apostle leaves much for a personal visit.

Writing can strengthen and comfort others. But meeting face to face often does so even more.

Christians should preserve their fellowship in every appropriate way, and that fellowship should bring mutual joy.

We already find much of our present joy in communion with the saints. Through it, we also look ahead to everlasting happiness.
