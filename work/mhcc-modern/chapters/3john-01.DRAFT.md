## Gaius is commended for faith and hospitality (verses 1–8)

Those who are loved by Christ will love their fellow believers for his sake.

Prosperity of the soul is the greatest blessing we can enjoy on this side of heaven.

Grace and health are wonderful companions. Grace can put good health to fruitful use.

Yet a spiritually rich soul may live in a weak body. In such circumstances, grace must also be exercised through patient submission to God's providence.

We may rightly wish and pray that those whose souls prosper will also enjoy healthy bodies. Then their grace may shine through even greater opportunities for service.

How many professing Christians need the apostle's words turned around! Their health and circumstances prosper, but we must earnestly pray that their souls would flourish in the same way.

True faith expresses itself in love.

Those who have received kindness should speak well of it. The people whom Gaius helped could not help telling the church what they had experienced.

Good people rejoice when the souls of others prosper. They are glad to hear of another believer's grace and goodness.

Just as godly parents rejoice when their children live well, so faithful ministers rejoice to see their people live in ways that honor their Christian profession.

Gaius looked beyond minor differences between serious Christians. He freely helped all who bore Christ's likeness and carried out his work.

He acted with integrity, like a faithful servant.

Those who are truly faithful can hear praise without becoming proud. Whatever good is commended in them leads them back to the foot of Christ's cross.

Christians ought to ask not only what they are obliged to do, but also what good they have an opportunity to do.

Even the ordinary tasks and kindnesses of life should be done in a godly spirit, as service to God and for his glory.

Those who freely proclaim Christ's gospel should receive practical help from believers whom God has given the means to provide it.

We may not all be able to preach the gospel ourselves, but we can still welcome, support and encourage those who do.

## Beware the pride of Diotrephes; follow what is good (verses 9–12)

We must watch both our hearts and our words.

Diotrephes was filled with pride and ambition.

It is wrong to neglect doing good ourselves. It is worse to prevent others from doing good.

Warnings and counsel are more likely to be received when they are given with love.

Follow what is good. The person who gladly practices what is good shows that he is born of God.

Those who do evil may boast of knowing God, but such claims are empty.

We must not copy proud, selfish people whose actions serve bad purposes, even when they hold important positions or wield great power.

Instead, let us imitate God and walk in love, following the example of our Lord.

## Demetrius, Christian fellowship and peace (verses 13–14)

Here we see the character of Demetrius.

A good name in the gospel and a good reputation among the churches are worth more than worldly honor.

Few people are praised by everyone. Sometimes it would even be a bad sign if they were.

Happy are those whose character and actions commend them both to God and to people.

We should be willing to speak up for such Christians. It is especially valuable when those giving the recommendation can appeal to the judgment of people who know the person well.

Speaking face to face often saves time, trouble and the misunderstandings that letters can cause.

Christians have good reason to be glad when they can meet one another.

The closing blessing is peace: may every kind of happiness attend you.

Those who hope to live together in heaven should gladly greet one another here on earth.

By seeking the company of such Christians and following their example, we may enjoy peace within ourselves and live peaceably with fellow believers.

Our fellowship with God's people here will be pleasant, and we may look forward to being counted among them in everlasting glory.
