## Humility and the quiet trust of a child

The psalmist did not aim at great things. He wanted to be content with whatever circumstances God gave him.

Humble believers do not think of themselves as highly as others might think of them.

When God's love rules the heart, it overcomes selfish love. A proud heart usually shows itself in a proud manner.

Knowing God and knowing our duty is learning great enough for us.

Wisdom teaches us not to interfere in matters that are not ours to handle.

The psalmist had learned to accept every condition the Lord placed him in.

He had become as humble as a child at the age of weaning, no longer reaching for great things.

Like a child completely dependent on its mother or nurse, he placed himself entirely at God's disposal.

We too must become like little children (Matthew 18:3).

Our hearts crave the things of this world. We cry out for them and become attached to them.

But when God's grace makes a soul holy, it begins to wean that soul away from such desires.

At first the weaning child is irritable and distressed. Soon it no longer cries for milk and can digest stronger food.

The converted soul learns in the same way to be quiet when something it loved is lost, or when a hope is disappointed.

It can remain at peace whatever happens.

When our circumstances do not suit our wishes, we must learn to bring our wishes into harmony with our circumstances.

Then we become easier to live with, both for ourselves and for those around us. Our souls become like a weaned child.

From his own experience, the psalmist urges all Israel to put its hope in God.

In every trial, it is good to hope in the Lord and quietly wait for his salvation.
