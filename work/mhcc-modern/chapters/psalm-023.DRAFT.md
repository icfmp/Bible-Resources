## Confidence in God's grace and care

“The Lord is my shepherd.”

With these words believers express their satisfaction in the care of the great Shepherd of the universe, the Redeemer and Preserver of humanity.

They can rejoice that they have a Shepherd—and that their Shepherd is the Lord himself.

Picture a gentle, harmless flock feeding in green pastures under the watchful, skillful and tender care of its shepherd.

That is an image of believers brought back to the Shepherd of their souls.

Even the greatest worldly abundance is a dry pasture to the ungodly, who enjoy only what pleases their senses.

But for the godly, who taste God's goodness by faith in everything they receive, even a little of this world's goods can be a green pasture.

The Lord gives peace and contentment of mind, whatever our circumstances.

When we enjoy the green pastures of the church's ordinances, we should not be content merely to pass through them. We should remain and feed there.

The consolations of the Holy Spirit are the quiet waters beside which God's people are led. They are streams flowing from the Fountain of living water.

Only those who walk in the paths of righteousness are led beside these waters of comfort.

The path of duty is the truly pleasant path. Righteousness works toward peace.

Yet we cannot walk in those paths unless God leads us into them and continues leading us along them.

Discontent and distrust come from unbelief, and an unsteady walk follows.

So let us trust our Shepherd's care simply and listen to his voice.

The valley of the shadow of death may represent the most severe suffering or the darkest providence the psalmist could ever face.

Death also lies like a dark valley between the part of Christ's flock still on earth and the part already gone to heaven.

But the words themselves lessen its terror. It is only *the shadow* of death.

The shadow of a serpent cannot sting. The shadow of a sword cannot kill.

The valley is deep, dark and muddy. Yet valleys are often fruitful, and even death bears the fruit of comfort for God's people.

It is a valley we *walk through*. Christ's sheep will not be lost there. They will reach the mountain on the other side.

Death may be the king of terrors, but it is not so to Christ's sheep.

As they come to die, God will rebuke the enemy. He will guide them with his rod and uphold them with his staff.

The gospel contains enough comfort for believers even in death, and beneath them are the everlasting arms.

The Lord's people feast at his table on the rich provisions of his love.

Neither Satan nor wicked people can destroy their comfort while they are anointed with the Holy Spirit and drink from the ever-full cup of salvation.

Past experience teaches believers to trust that God's goodness and mercy will follow them throughout their lives.

They desire and resolve to find happiness in serving him here, and they hope to enjoy his love forever in heaven.

Even now, the Lord can make any situation pleasant through the anointing of his Spirit and the joy of his salvation.

But those who wish to enjoy the blessings of his house must also remain faithful to its duties.
