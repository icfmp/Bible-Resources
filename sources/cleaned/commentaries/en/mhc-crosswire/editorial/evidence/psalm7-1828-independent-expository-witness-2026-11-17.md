## Additional independent published historical witness

Compared these **publicly rendered** sources by passage sequence and subject:

1. [CCEL: Psalm VII, verses 1–9](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhc3.Ps.viii.html): exposition following the KJV psalm text, with the subsection beginning “II. He makes a solemn protestation” and continuing through the three-part discussion of David's innocence and self-imprecation, including a Latin verse quotation and its English rendering.
2. [Wikisource: *An Exposition of the Old and New Testament* (1828), Psalms](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/An_Exposition_of_the_Old_and_New_Testament_%281828%29/Psalms), under **PSALM VII**: scan-linked, proofread transcription of an **undated Barrington & Haswell reprint in the 1828 Towar & Hogan typographic lineage**, not a separately verified original 1828 physical copy. Wikisource's edition introduction explicitly reports corrected wording in the later reprint (including Psalm 7), so the two printings must not be silently conflated. It reproduces this same ordered **II.** exposition sequence, the Latin and translated verse, the charges against David, his restraint toward Saul, and the three consequences of his solemn appeal.

The [validated scanned-page transcription at page 216 (DJVU image 224)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:An_Exposition_of_the_Old_and_New_Testament_%281828%29_vol_3.djvu/224) contains the end of this **II.** exposition and start of **III.** Its transcription status is *validated*, but the page image itself has not been independently proofread line-by-line in this checkpoint. See also [Wikisource's edition note](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/An_Exposition_of_the_Old_and_New_Testament_%281828%29).

**Confirmed narrower conclusion:** the material in the CCEL Psalm 7 discussion is **commentator's substantive exposition already present in a nineteenth-century printed-edition lineage**. The large anomaly is **not plausibly explained as merely a missing KJV Psalm title**. This finding strengthens the priority for the original same-start CrossWire text investigation.

**Important differences:** the CCEL rendered reading around the Latin quotation does **not** present every connecting phrase in exactly the form visible in the 1828 transcription (for example, the 1828 reading introduces the post-quotation sentence with “Happy indeed, when”). The displayed punctuation and editorial capitalization also vary. No full-text case-sensitive token alignment, full 569-word paragraph identity, scan-page-by-scan-page proofreading, or edition stemma has been performed. Do **not** assert that the *exact CCEL paragraph* is identical to the 1828 printing.
