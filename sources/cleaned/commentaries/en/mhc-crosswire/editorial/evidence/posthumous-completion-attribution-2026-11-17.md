# Full MHC authorial attribution — Henry's work and the fourteen posthumous continuators

**Date:** 2026-11-17  
**Status:** source-level historical attribution research, not an approved metadata change.  
**Scope:** [MHC full commentary intake #285](https://github.com/icfmp/Bible-Resources/issues/285), [draft PR #286](https://github.com/icfmp/Bible-Resources/pull/286); distinct from the abridged MHCC.

## Important attribution boundary

The anonymous contemporary preface to the last volume (published 1721) calls the Epistles and Revelation continuation **posthumous** and records that ministers worked from Matthew Henry's transcribed exposition and family/congregation notes. Its account distinguishes the **historical part of the New Testament (Gospels and Acts)**, the work Henry had completed and left ready for the press, from the remaining epistolary part prepared by others.

Source: [CCEL full MHC Volume VI, Preface](https://ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhc6/mhc6.vi.html). The **named table following** the anonymous preface is a *later editorial attribution* explicitly credited there to J. B. Williams's *Memoirs* (p. 308); it must **not** be misidentified as a list of contributors appearing in the original 1721 preface.

An independently published scholarly annotated bibliography examines the **1721 posthumous edition**, Henry's prior 1715 historical NT volume, and a note by **Isaac Watts**, preserved in an 1797 magazine and later repeated by Orme and Williams. It explicitly identifies **fourteen** named continuators rather than an undocumented shorthand of “thirteen.” Source: [*Matthew Henry: The Bible, Prayer, and Piety*, Chapter 15, bibliography §3.1–3.2, note 11](https://www.uecf.net/books/matthew-henry-the-bible-prayer-and-piety/chapter19.html).

## Book-group attribution as recorded in the later editorial table

| Continuator (reported attribution, not a modern signed authorial manuscript) | Assigned biblical books |
| --- | --- |
| John Evans | Romans |
| Simon Browne | 1 Corinthians |
| Daniel Mayo | 2 Corinthians; 1–2 Thessalonians |
| Joshua Bayes / Bays (historical spelling varies) | Galatians |
| Samuel Rosewell | Ephesians |
| William Harris | Philippians; Colossians |
| Benjamin Andrews Atkinson | 1–2 Timothy |
| Jeremiah Smith | Titus; Philemon |
| William Tong | Hebrews; Revelation |
| Samuel Wright (“S. Wright”) | James |
| Zechariah Merrill (“Zec. Merrill”) | 1 Peter |
| Joseph Hill | 2 Peter |
| John Reynolds | 1–3 John |
| John Billingsley | Jude |

The table has **14 distinct named persons** covering Romans through Revelation, and does **not** assign Acts to these continuators. This is a historical *attribution of compilation/completion responsibility*; it does not prove that every thought or sentence in these books originated with the attributed person, nor that no words were derived directly from Henry's own pre-death notes. The scholarly witness notes that Romans was especially extensively prepared in Henry's hand, but its final chapter commentary was prepared for publication by John Evans.

## Consequences for faithful resource metadata and anomaly review

1. In future source credits, do **not** silently label the full commentary text as authored solely by Matthew Henry. A candidate description should distinguish his primary authorship through Acts and the fourteen continuators in the posthumous Epistles–Revelation section, with exact edition and attribution provenance.
2. The original **Acts 17:22–31** 235-word source/stock anomaly belongs to the portion **historically attributed to Henry himself**, not to the fourteen posthumous continuators; its source/stock collation should preserve that scope.
3. Different editions and 1721/1811/1828 reprints can differ in editing and preparation. This source-level attribution does **not** establish CCEL XML identity with the 1721 first publication or grant permissions over CCEL's electronic markup and annotations.
4. Proposed module name, copyright wording, catalogue credits and metadata must await independent exact-edition rights review and user/editorial decision. No original commentary text or metadata has been changed here.
